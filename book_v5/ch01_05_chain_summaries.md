## Chapter 1
Daniel Mercer — wearing his Brentwood Hardware shirt and carrying about
forty dollars to his name — trips in a hardware store parking lot at
7:15 a.m. and blacks out, waking moments later in an unfamiliar stubble
field with no roads, power lines, or cell service. He walks toward a
settlement of mud-walled buildings where the inhabitants wear belted
tunics, carry hand-forged tools, and speak a language he cannot
identify, and no one can understand him. From a hilltop he spots a vast
city in a valley and recognizes a massive, fully intact, newly built
amphitheater — the Colosseum — which he knows only from pictures as a
ruin, forcing him to accept that he has somehow traveled back to ancient
Rome. Before he can process this, four armed soldiers in leather armor
come up the hill, surround him, and march him down toward the city with
his hands behind his neck, leaving him with no way to explain himself.

## Chapter 2
The soldiers march Daniel, hands tied with rope, into the town and lock
him in a stone-and-brick cell with a single barred window and a heavy
plank door. Alone, Daniel turns on his phone — now at 98% battery —
switches it to airplane mode, and uses the Notes app to record the date,
"ROME 98 AD??," and personal details he fears forgetting, including
fragments of his mother's phone number and home address; he also takes a
selfie, leaving two photos on the phone total. One guard returns to
inspect the phone and Daniel's clothes but cannot puzzle out either, and
leaves without taking anything. Daniel then lies on the cell floor
thinking through what skills or knowledge he might use to survive,
concluding he has little of practical value but decides he can at least
demonstrate literacy, numeracy, and map-drawing, and will claim to be a
foreigner from the far north rather than reveal the truth. Near dark, a
higher-ranking soldier with a stripe on his shoulder brings Daniel a
bowl of grain mush, cuts the rope from his wrists, exchanges names with
him, and departs — leaving Daniel eating alone in the dark.

## Chapter 3
Daniel is brought before a senior official — a middle-aged man in a
red-hemmed tunic with a clerk present — who interrogates him in Latin,
which Daniel cannot understand. When the official dismisses him with the
word "Auferte," Daniel seizes a last chance by miming the act of
writing, and the clerk reluctantly hands over a wax tablet and stylus.
Using his memorized knowledge of world geography, Daniel draws a map of
the Mediterranean and then, carried away by two days of being ignored,
extends it to show the full coast of Africa (closed at the south),
India, China, and finally two unmistakable landmasses across the western
ocean — the Americas. The official and clerk are visibly shaken, the
clerk immediately begins copying the map, and the official sends the
optio to fetch someone described as both a healer and a foreigner — a
person elsewhere in the town who may be able to communicate with Daniel.
Returned to his cell without being re-tied, Daniel resolves to use his
phone's last 11% battery to study the real coastlines from his offline
map app so he can draw a more accurate version when the inevitable next
map is demanded.

---

## Chapter 4
A physician named Heras arrives and examines Daniel physically — pulse,
eyes, hands — before attempting conversation first in Latin, then
switching to Greek. Daniel cannot follow sentences but catches isolated
Greek-rooted words like *pou* (where), *therm*, and *photo*, which
become his only verbal toehold. The two communicate mainly through a
wax tablet, where Daniel demonstrates Hindu-Arabic numerals and place
value, impresses the physician despite fumbling a long-division problem,
and redraws his world map including the two unmistakable western
landmasses — this time deliberately leaving their interiors empty of
habitation to limit what he reveals. The physician copies Daniel's
numeral "7" into his own notes and keeps it, and afterward arranges for
Daniel to be moved from the cell to a workshop loft with a pallet and
blanket — better conditions but still captivity with an implied guard.
That night Daniel's phone battery finally dies completely — taking his
camera roll, map app, and notes with it — and he lies in the dark
unable to write any record of what has happened.

## Chapter 5
Daniel, now a year into his captivity, slowly acquires Latin through
immersion and humiliation — with Stichus the water-boy as an unwitting
first teacher and Heras as his main intellectual anchor — while making
a formal introduction with Heras only months after they first met. His
attempt to convey germ theory to Heras over a workshop boy's infected
arm is flatly dismissed as dangerous folk-superstition, but he salvages
the encounter by reframing hygiene as cleanliness — convincing Heras to
boil water and wash his hands — and the boy survives; Heras quietly
adopts the practices while never conceding the underlying explanation.
Daniel also produces crude soap through rendered tallow and wood-ash
lye, raises the danger of lead pipes and defrutum cooked in lead vessels
(Heras politely ignores it), and privately notes that tomatoes,
potatoes, corn, and chocolate exist somewhere across the western
landmasses he once sketched for the magistrate — a direction, not yet
a plan. The chapter ends with Daniel lying in the loft feeling warm air
rise through the floor tiles and realizing that a cloth bag filled with
hot air would visibly lift off the ground — cheap, his alone, and
undeniable to any eye — and he falls asleep for the first night in a
year without reaching for his dead phone.
