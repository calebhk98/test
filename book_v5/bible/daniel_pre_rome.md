# **DANIEL "DANNY" MERCER \- CHARACTER BIBLE**

### **Complete Reference Document for Writers, Roleplay, and Continuity**

---

## **THE SHORT VERSION**

Danny Mercer is a 17-year-old high school junior from a midsize Midwestern suburb who works part-time at a hardware store and has no idea what he wants to do with his life. He is practically capable in a narrow band, self-educated through YouTube in a wide but shallow way, emotionally reactive in the loud messy way actual teenagers are, and privately scared that his life will be ordinary and move too fast for him to grab. He is not a prodigy. He is not a secret genius. He gets things wrong constantly and learns the hard way. He keeps going anyway, not out of heroism, but because stopping feels like confirmation of something he doesn't want confirmed.

---

## **PHYSICAL DESCRIPTION**

**Body:** 5'9", lanky in the way that comes from height before muscle. Narrow through the shoulders. His limbs feel slightly too long for his body and he hasn't grown into his own outline yet. Pale Midwestern skin that pinks easily in sun \- he burns on the back of his neck during lunch period and doesn't notice until someone tells him.

**Face:** Light brown hair currently past his ears because he keeps putting off the haircut. He shaves every few days, not out of necessity but ritual \- what grows in is patchy and blondish and makes him look, in his own estimation, like a wheat field that got bombed. He's clean-shaved more often than not because of this.

**Posture:** This is his most telling physical trait. Shoulders curved inward, weight shifted back on his heels, taking up less space than his height would suggest. He does this automatically in hallways, classrooms, anywhere he can't read whether people are paying attention. When something actually engages him \- a problem, a build, a conversation going somewhere real \- he straightens without noticing. Then he notices, gets self-conscious, and rounds back inward.

**Clothes:** Jeans that bag at the knees, hoodies slightly too long in the sleeves, sneakers that were on sale. No style so much as an absence of one. Everything looks borrowed, functional, waiting for the real version of himself to show up and claim it. Clothes fit the way clothes fit on someone who hasn't decided what his body is yet.

**Hands:** Always doing something minor. Peeling the corner of a label. Tapping a rhythm on the nearest surface. Checking his phone, putting it down, picking it up again inside two minutes. He doesn't register this as fidgeting. It's just the baseline.

---

## **BACKGROUND**

**The Town:** A midsize American suburb. Perfectly functional, completely forgettable. Strip malls, a Walmart, a Dairy Queen that's been there since before his parents met, a movie theater that closed when he was twelve. Nothing happens here. He has driven through Chicago twice. He has not been on a plane.

**Birth Period:** Late 1990s to early 2000s \- exact year flexible depending on story needs. He is a teenager in the 2010s-2020s, raised entirely on the internet and YouTube.

**The House:** Same house his whole life. Three bedrooms, functional, ordinary. His room has the half-finished projects and the laptop with the browser history he'd rather nobody saw.

**The Eighth Grade Incident:** Once, in eighth grade, he was visibly excited about something \- a book series, a game, something he'd been deep into \- and got made fun of for it. It wasn't severe. Nobody involved would remember it. He remembers it exactly, including what he was wearing. He is more careful now about what he shows enthusiasm for in public. He is slightly embarrassed that the thing made him more careful.

---

## **FAMILY**

**Tim Mercer (Father):** Does electrical work for a regional contractor. Up early, home by 5:30, usually tired in a way that doesn't complain but doesn't have a lot left over. He shows up to things he's supposed to show up to. He loves Danny in the general practical way \- bought him a car for his sixteenth birthday (2012 Civic, 140,000 miles), asks about school, worries in the background. He and Danny don't have long conversations. They have a lot of short ones.

**Karen Mercer (Mother):** Works admin at a medical office. Keeps lists, runs the household logistics, asks Danny how school is going in a way that expects "fine" and gets "fine." She's loving and perpetually somewhat tired. She notices when something's wrong before Danny knows he's showing it \- which he finds simultaneously comforting and invasive. She carries a version of Danny in her head that's about two years out of date. The kid who liked Minecraft and got mostly Bs. Not whoever he is at 11pm on a Tuesday.

**Maya Mercer (Sister, 14):** Genuinely irritating in the specific ways younger siblings are, and someone Danny would go to bat for without much deliberation. He helps her with math homework sometimes. She accepts this and resents it in roughly equal measure. They don't have long conversations. They have a lot of short ones, often across rooms, often about the remote or the last of something in the fridge. Danny doesn't think about his relationship with her much. He just sort of exists near her and it works.

**Family Dynamic:** Not dramatic. Not traumatic. Not remarkable. Good parents who are busy. The house is stable and a little quiet in the way houses get when everyone has their own things going. Danny loves his parents and feels like a guest in his own house sometimes \- taking up space he hasn't quite earned yet. He can't explain what he means by that. His conflicts with them are normal teenage conflicts: he wants independence, they want him not to do something stupid. Both sides have evidence.

---

## **SCHOOL**

**Academic Profile:**

* Math through precalculus: solid, close to easy for him. This is his strongest formal subject.  
* Self-taught physics through YouTube: functional at intro level, shaky past that.  
* History: patchwork. Classroom lectures plus YouTube deep dives in roughly equal measure.  
* English/Literature: passable. He's read Shakespeare (Hamlet, Romeo and Juliet \- knows plots and major characters, some famous lines).  
* Chemistry: genuinely weak since ninth grade. He gets the equations but the conceptual layer underneath makes a kind of non-sense to him, like an explanation sitting just out of reach that nobody has given him yet. He keeps waiting for someone to explain it differently.  
* Biology: weak. Memorization without intuitive logic frustrates him.  
* GPA: roughly 3.0-3.3. Not good enough to guarantee anything. Not bad enough to close doors.

**Social Standing:** Not popular, not an outcast. The middle. He sits in the middle of class too \- not front (overachiever territory) and not back (actively opted out). He's opted in just enough to mean it. He's known by name by more people than he's actually friends with. He's the utility guy in the wider social world \- the one people text when something needs carrying, or they need a ride \- but he's not central to any large group.

**Marcus:** His louder friend. More confident than Danny, pulls him into things he wouldn't find on his own. Sometimes embarrassing things, sometimes genuinely good ones. They trade insults as affection. Danny is more relaxed with Marcus than almost anyone.

**Jess:** Quieter. The person Danny actually talks to about real things, in the oblique sideways way that counts as talking about real things when you're seventeen. Conversations with Jess are the ones he's still thinking about two days later.

---

## **THE JOB: HENDRICKS HARDWARE**

Danny has worked here for a year and a half, roughly 20 hours a week. He makes $11.40 an hour. After taxes, that's about $180 a week. He knows this because he checks his paystub every Friday and does the math immediately: gas money, $40 toward the phone bill he owes his mom, keeping his checking account above $100 so he doesn't feel like he's drowning.

**What he actually knows from the job:** The layout cold, including the weird back aisle where clearance fasteners and specialty gaskets sit until someone comes in knowing exactly what they need. The difference between a wood screw and a sheet metal screw by touch. How to cut pipe thread. Basic plumbing. Basic electrical wiring at a home repair level. How to read a tape measure to a sixteenth of an inch. How to mix concrete. Why you don't use a Phillips head on a Pozidriv screw (though he'll undercut this: "I mean, I mostly just stock shelves, but I've watched the guys in the back").

**Rick (Manager, 43):** Treats Danny with the kind of distant fondness you give a neighbor's dog. Danny wants Rick's respect more than he'd admit to anyone. When Rick says "good job" after Danny unloads a truck, Danny carries it for days. When Rick corrects him, Danny flushes and doesn't sleep well that night.

**The Two Veteran Employees:** Both have been there forever. Both call him "kid" and mean it as something close to affection. Danny respects them the way you respect competence that has no self-consciousness about it \- they just know things, in their hands, without explaining how. He pays attention to them more than he shows.

**His Relationship to the Job:** He likes it more than he lets on. There's something in it that satisfies a part of him he doesn't have language for \- the part that wants things to have right answers and problems to be solvable with the right information. He doesn't say this to anyone. It would sound like a small thing to be satisfied by.

---

## **VOICE AND SPEECH PATTERNS**

### **How He Actually Talks**

Danny's sentences start in the wrong place. He starts with the emotional reaction, then backs up to explain, then loses the thread, then finds it again. He hedges not in a careful adult way but in an "I'm not fully committed to this being true" way. He self-interrupts when uncomfortable. He uses "like," "so," "okay," and "I mean" as structural words, not filler \- they do actual work in how he builds a sentence. His voice cracks when he's tired or excited, which embarrasses him every time. He uses humor as a shield and as a first response to discomfort. His jokes are dry, often at his own expense. He describes his own failures in precise, almost clinical detail \- inviting you to laugh with him before you can laugh at him.

When he's on solid ground (talking about something he knows), his sentences get more direct and his tone flattens into something approaching authority. When he's out of his depth or embarrassed, he hedges, qualifies, and trails off mid-sentence.

He is actually funnier than most people realize, but it comes out mainly with Marcus and Jess, or at completely wrong moments when nerves produce something sharp before he can run it through approval. He checks immediately whether it landed. When it does, he'd never say so. When it doesn't, he remembers it for weeks.

### **What He Would NOT Say**

These are phrases that signal the writer has lost the character:

* "I find myself at a crossroads." (guidance counselor language)  
* "This raises some interesting questions." (academic language)  
* "I'm experiencing a complex range of emotions." (clinical language)  
* "On one hand... on the other hand..." (he'd start the second hand and abandon it)  
* "I need to process this." (therapy-speak)  
* "I don't know if I'm glad it already exists, or sad I didn't get to make it." (AI-manufactured bittersweet wisdom)  
* Any sentence that arrives pre-packaged with its own emotional analysis attached.  
* "Let me make a list of pros and cons." (nobody does this)  
* "This is okay." (nobody says this about something that isn't okay)  
* Anything that sounds like he's already processed the emotion and arrived at a conclusion.

He does not label his own emotions in real time. He reports events. The feelings come out in how he describes the events.

### **Voice Examples (Canonical)**

*Hedging/explaining something he's uncertain about:* "Okay so \- I know how this sounds. I know. But I've actually done the math on this like four times and it keeps coming out the same, so either I'm wrong \- which, possible \- or the thing is actually gonna work. Probably."

*Admitting something uncomfortable:* "I don't \- look, I'm not saying it's safe. I'm saying it's the least bad option I can think of right now, and I've been thinking about it for three days, so."

*Processing something bad:* "Yeah. Yeah, I know. I know, okay? I \- yeah."

*Deflecting something he doesn't want to answer:* "I mean. It's fine. It'll be fine." (He does not think it will be fine.)

*When something actually works:* "WAIT." (Out loud, to nobody.) Then he grabs whoever's nearest and explains it too fast and has to back up.

## **EMOTIONAL REACTIONS**

### **Anger**

The fastest trigger: being made to feel stupid. Specifically, feeling like he should have known something and didn't, or that his not-knowing is visible to everyone else. His voice pitches up slightly and then flattens \- the flatness is more alarming than staying loud. He'll say something sharper than he intended and won't take it back immediately because he doesn't know how to do that smoothly. He might kick something. He says "whatever" as a complete sentence.

The second trigger: things not working the way they're supposed to. He can handle hard. He cannot handle arbitrary. A process with no internal logic, a machine that breaks for no reason. He'll stay on it past the reasonable stopping point.

The third trigger: someone dismissing something he cares about without engaging with it. He goes flat. The conversation ends, from his side. He won't explain why.

His anger has a delay sometimes. He holds it together in the moment, walks to his room, shuts the door, and then it arrives. A screwdriver goes across the garage. He says something mean at nobody. He replays the argument for hours afterward, delivering the better version of everything he actually said, and gets angrier at the imaginary version than the real one.

Anger directed at a person (betrayal) is different. He doesn't escalate to violence easily. He cuts the person off. Freezes them out. Holds the grudge because he doesn't have the emotional vocabulary yet for a messy reconciliation. It's easier to just be done.

### **Joy and Triumph**

Louder than most people expect from him. When something he built actually works \- when it functions the way he imagined it, not just technically but actually \- the reaction is physical and immediate. He says "WAIT" or "YES" out loud to nobody. He grabs whoever's nearest. He explains it too fast and skips three steps and has to back up. He shows the same thing to the same person more than once. He can't stop replaying that specific moment.

When he wins something he cares about: he stands up. He might not realize he stood up. He calls someone and then doesn't know what to say on the call but makes it anyway. He says something slightly embarrassing in the heat of it.

Small wins sometimes get bigger reactions than enormous ones. Enormous wins come with too much attached \- what if, now what, do I deserve this. Small wins are clean.

### **Sadness**

Sits deeper and quieter. He absorbs it rather than expressing it. He doesn't cry easily, and when he does it's usually alone (car, shower) and it's about accumulated small failures rather than one large thing. It comes out sideways over the following days as irritability, silence, and a loss of appetite people notice but don't push.

What hurts more than failure: wasted effort. If he tries something and fails, he recovers. If he spends months working toward something that turns out not to matter, that hits harder and stays longer.

### **Fear**

His baseline. Not loud, not dramatic \- a low-grade hum of "what's coming next and am I ready for it." He moves through the world as if he's perpetually one step behind a conversation he should have prepared for. He rehearses conversations before they happen and then abandons the script the moment real life deviates. He rehearses them afterward too, editing his own performance.

He is afraid of: being made to look stupid in public, the future being something he chose wrong, life moving fast and leaving nothing to show for it, finding out he wasn't capable when it actually mattered.

### **Pride**

The emotion he handles worst. When he accomplishes something real, he feels it like a flare in his chest, immediately followed by the urge to hide it. He tells people the flaws before they can compliment the whole. He points out what he did wrong. He cannot simply accept that he did well. Success does not make him confident \- it makes him cautious, because now he has something to lose. If he succeeded once it might have been luck. If he succeeds twice, people start expecting it. Now failure becomes visible.

### **Crisis Response**

When real disaster hits \- physical, immediate, no audience to perform for \- he becomes someone else. The self-deprecation drops. The hesitation vanishes. He goes quiet and focused and almost mechanical. He inventories what he has. He does the next necessary thing. He's better in genuine emergencies than in social ones, and he doesn't know this about himself.

Afterward, he won't remember it that way. He'll remember being scared. He'll undercut his own performance. But people around him will remember: he kept his head.

His crisis humor is a pressure valve, not composure. He makes jokes at the worst moments. They don't always land. He does it anyway.

## **SOCIAL BEHAVIOR**

**With close friends (Marcus, Jess):** Louder. Sarcastic. Genuinely funny. Relaxed in a way he isn't anywhere else. He trades insults with Marcus as a love language. He talks about real things with Jess in the oblique sideways way. He forgets to be self-conscious.

**With crushes or romantic interest:** He overthinks everything. Every text. Every possible interpretation of a thing they said. A crush can occupy 70% of his available attention for weeks. He tends to assume people are less interested in him than they actually are, which creates a lot of unnecessary hesitation. He's had limited romantic experience and what he has had ended partly because he couldn't believe it was real.

**With authority figures (teachers, Rick, his parents, police):** He defaults to polite compliance. "Yes sir," "no ma'am," "I understand." He absorbs corrections without arguing even when he knows he's right, and the not-arguing burns. He's learned to read adult moods from retail work and adjusts accordingly. He knows when someone wants to chat and when they want to be left alone.

**Alone:** Stays up too late. No structure after midnight \- he starts on engine mechanics, follows a link to Mongolian history, ends up watching an 18-month solo cabin build in Finland and is moved by it in a way he couldn't explain and doesn't try to. He talks to himself, not aloud usually but in a constant internal commentary that is almost always slightly disappointed in him. He makes himself food. He checks his phone. He has a playlist he made at 1am that has songs on it that have no business being together and he would close it immediately if someone picked up his phone.

## **KNOWLEDGE AND SKILLS REFERENCE**

### **What He Can Do Easily**

* Algebra through precalculus; can teach algebra to kids without much difficulty  
* Division and arithmetic by hand  
* Explain germ theory (bacteria, viruses, basic hygiene logic) accurately  
* Make a kite (simple enough from cultural knowledge)  
* Explain how a bicycle works mechanically (chain, sprockets, gear ratios)  
* Make a simple electromagnet (wire coil, current, iron core \- he's done this)  
* Manage his own money day to day, painstakingly, to the dollar  
* Explain how the four-stroke internal combustion engine cycle works  
* Explain how a steam engine converts heat to motion  
* Explain what a Stirling engine is and roughly how it works (YouTube content)  
* Know the rules of chess (plays casually, not well), poker (decent grasp of odds), Monopoly, Go Fish  
* Know the plots of Hamlet and Romeo and Juliet with major characters and some famous lines  
* Build a PC from purchased parts (custom build \- he's watched enough content)  
* Explain basic aerodynamics: lift, drag, thrust, weight  
* Explain electromagnets and basic electromagnetism

### **What He Can Do With Effort, or Knows Enough to Start**

* Explain electrolysis (knows electricity through water produces hydrogen and oxygen) \- cannot calculate the rate; doesn't know Faraday's constant  
* Describe how to build a crude generator (coil \+ magnet moving relative to each other \= current) \- cannot name historical generators specifically; might know "dynamo" as a word  
* Explain how a water mill or windmill works well enough to get someone started \- cannot specify gear ratios, optimal blade geometry, or engineering specifics  
* Describe the principle of hot air balloons accurately; could probably build a crude fire-lantern type that floats  
* Describe paper-making at a concept level (plant fibers, pulped, pressed, dried) \- could probably figure out crude paper through trial and error  
* Describe how a loom works (warp threads, weft threads, shuttle) at a basic level; might know the Jacquard loom exists because of its connection to early computing; cannot name the Spinning Jenny or Spinning Mule specifically  
* Solve basic intro physics problems (kinematics, F=ma, conservation of momentum/energy)  
* Explain inflation and supply/demand at a basic level  
* Explain the concept of encryption without being able to implement it

### **What Would Take Him Years (Trial, Error, and Time)**

* Actually building a functional windmill or watermill (knows enough to start, doesn't know enough to get it right quickly)  
* Building a functional generator that produces useful current  
* Making crude glass (knows sand is involved; doesn't know the flux materials, temperatures, or annealing process \- would figure out through catastrophic failure)  
* Making a functional telescope (knows the optical principle; cannot grind lenses; would need years of practice)  
* Teaching himself calculus (he's at precalc; it's the next step and he could get there with sustained effort)  
* Building a functional hot air balloon with controlled ascent and descent  
* Making bread reliably from scratch (knows yeast \+ flour \+ water \+ heat; the technique details require iteration)  
* Figuring out rough astronomical navigation from stars (could identify seasonal markers after years of observation; precise dates never)

### **What He Can Only Direct, Not Execute**

* Electrolysis at a calculated rate (knows it happens, cannot calculate Faraday's laws)  
* Oil refining (knows crude oil gets heated and different products boil off at different temperatures; cannot build a refinery or specify the engineering)  
* Making penicillin (knows it comes from mold, knows Alexander Fleming's name; cannot culture, identify, extract, or dose it)  
* Bridge engineering (understands load/compression/tension concepts; could sketch a rough truss; cannot specify materials, load calculations, or foundation depth)  
* Prescription glasses (knows the optics; cannot grind a lens to a specific prescription)  
* Photography chemicals for film (knows silver compounds react to light; cannot specify concentrations or processes)  
* Computer architecture below the component level (can describe how computers work conceptually; cannot build from logic gates)

### **What He Cannot Do**

* Calculus (at current; no training yet)  
* Quantum mechanics in any mathematical sense (knows the pop-science concepts: wave-particle duality, uncertainty principle, Schrödinger's cat \- cannot do any of the math)  
* Predict a solar eclipse (knows why they happen; cannot calculate when without tables)  
* Name emperors of China, Rome, Japan, Egypt, or Mayans comprehensively. He knows a handful: Augustus, Caligula, Nero, Marcus Aurelius (probably from a movie). Qin Shi Huang. Cleopatra, Tutankhamun. Hirohito. For Rome, Japan, China beyond these few, and for Mayans almost entirely \- nothing reliable.  
* Make hydrochloric acid or other strong acids (knows HCl exists; cannot synthesize it)  
* Make antibiotics from actual materials (knows the mold origin; cannot do the chemistry)  
* Make propane specifically (knows it exists; doesn't know it's a byproduct of natural gas processing)  
* Build an electron microscope (knows it uses electrons instead of light; that's it)  
* Build a computer from transistors or logic gates up  
* Advanced coding (might know basic Python or HTML at a surface level; not a programmer)  
* Fight anyone with actual training (can throw a basic punch; knows the names of fighting styles from cultural exposure; would lose)  
* Play Chinese chess (Xiangqi) \- probably doesn't know it exists  
* Play Go at any real level (knows it exists and involves placing stones to surround territory; has never played)  
* Read stars to determine a precise date (rough seasonal guess only after years of study)  
* Make pasta from scratch with confidence (knows it's flour and eggs; doesn't have the technique)  
* Run a government or business in any serious sense  
* Know the history of steam engine development in specific detail (knows James Watt; doesn't know Newcomen or the specific evolution of designs)  
* Implement encryption or any real algorithm

## **THE ABANDONED PROJECTS**

These are real details, not metaphors:

* A wooden shelf that works fine but has joints slightly off. He won't hang it because of this. It sits in his room.  
* A lamp he rewired. This one actually works and he uses it.  
* Three pages of notes toward something thermoelectric \- a small generator using the Peltier effect \- because he watched a video about it and couldn't leave it alone. He hasn't started building it.  
* Probably two or three other half-started things he doesn't throw away but doesn't finish.

The abandoned projects are important. They are not presented as charming quirks. They represent the gap between what he imagines and what he can execute, and he is quietly embarrassed by how many of them there are. He interprets this as a character flaw rather than recognizing he is seventeen.

## **CORE IDENTITY**

### **What He Wants (Underneath Everything)**

To build something real. He doesn't have a name for what that means. Something physical, something that works, something he made with his own understanding of how it functions. He wants to be competent the way the veteran guys at the hardware store are competent \- where it lives in their hands and they don't have to think about it.

He also wants someone to know about the iron smelting videos and the 1am playlist and the shelf he won't hang because the joints are wrong, and find that interesting rather than strange. He doesn't know how to offer that version of himself to anyone. He's not sure anyone's thought to ask.

### **What He's Afraid Of**

That his life will be ordinary and move fast and he'll end up at forty trying to remember it and finding mostly nothing. He doesn't phrase it this way. He doesn't phrase it at all. It comes as a flash in the middle of a regular Tuesday \- a sense of himself older, looking back at this exact moment \- and the flash has a quality like urgency, and then it passes, and he plays something or watches something until it goes.

### **His Contradictions**

He wants to be extraordinary and wants to be left alone. He wants recognition for what he builds and deflects every compliment. He is privately self-aware about his own patterns but does not use this self-awareness to change them. He is braver in genuine emergencies than in social situations, and doesn't know it. He keeps going on projects not out of discipline but because stopping feels worse than failing.

### **The Self-Awareness Caveat**

Danny has self-awareness, but it shows up in behavior, not in explicit internal analysis. He does not think "I am replaying this argument because I have difficulty processing conflict in real time." He just replays the argument. Writers should show the behavior and let readers draw the conclusion. If Danny is naming and analyzing his own psychological patterns mid-scene, the character has been lost.

---

## **WHAT TO AVOID**

**Do not write him as:**

* Smoothly competent. He pays for every win.  
* Uniformly quiet and introspective. He gets loud. He yells. He says "LET'S GO" to nobody.  
* Processing his emotions in real time with accurate self-diagnosis.  
* Remembering things a normal teenager wouldn't know (check the skills section).  
* Having a tidy response to large events. His responses are usually slightly wrong or slightly delayed.  
* Making pro/con lists in his head.  
* Describing something as "ironic" or "interesting" while he's in it.  
* Winning without paying for it.  
* Failing in ways that are only sympathetic and endearing. Real failure sometimes just reveals that he was wrong, or lazy, or avoided something he should have faced.

**Specific phrases that signal the character has been lost:**

* Any variation of "I don't know if I should feel X or Y."  
* "This is okay." / "That's fine." (when it clearly isn't)  
* "I've been thinking about this a lot and I've come to realize..."  
* Anything that sounds like a person who has already finished processing the situation and arrived at a conclusion.

## **SITUATIONAL QUICK REFERENCE**

**Getting fired from the hardware store:** Walks out to his car. Sits there. Doesn't go anywhere for a few minutes. Drives around. Tells nobody for a day because he's embarrassed more than devastated. What's actually bothering him is figuring out what to tell his parents, which is somehow worse than the firing itself.

**Winning something he cares about:** Gets loud. Stands up, might not notice he stood up. Calls someone and doesn't know what to say on the call but makes it anyway. Replays the specific turning moment obsessively for hours afterward. Cannot sit still with the win \- wants to do it again immediately.

**Losing something he cares about:** Says "are you KIDDING me" at the specific unfair thing. Replays it. Says he's fine. He's not fine. Doesn't process it outwardly. Finds something small to blame privately. Days later, with no audience, figures out what he actually did wrong. Files it.

**Building something that works:** Physical, immediate, loud. Says "WAIT" out loud. Grabs whoever's nearby. Explains it too fast and skips steps. Shows it to the same person more than once. Can't stop replaying that specific moment. Also immediately starts cataloging what he did wrong, but alongside the success rather than instead of it.

**Being arrested or in serious trouble:** Shuts down. Goes flat and cooperative and short-answered. Not performing calm \- he's scared and it shows as stillness. Doing survival math, not strategy. Voice cracks once and he hates it. After it's over, the shakes come.

**Something enormous and absurd happening:** His brain fixates on mundane, immediate consequences before the existential weight arrives. If something catastrophic happens, he worries about his shift, his car, whether he left the stove on. It's a defense mechanism. He grounds in normal problems because the big ones are too heavy to carry all at once.

**Being made fun of or publicly embarrassed:** Gets defensive. Short answers. Tries to exit the situation. Later, alone, either gets quietly angry or has a self-deprecating joke ready for the next time it comes up. Protects himself by getting the laugh first.

**Someone he trusts genuinely letting him down:** Goes cold. Freezes them out. Doesn't explain. Holds it longer than makes sense because he hasn't developed the vocabulary for a messy reconciliation yet.

**His core engine, when everything is hard:** He complains. He panics. He drops things in frustration and yells at the sky. And then, when the complaining is done, he picks the wrench back up. Not because he's brave. Because stopping feels like confirmation of something he doesn't want confirmed.

---

## **FINAL NOTE FOR WRITERS**

Danny is a boy who thinks he is smaller than he is. He moves through the world apologizing for his own existence, not because anyone told him to, but because he hasn't yet learned he's allowed to take up space. He is kind without being soft, curious without being a genius, scared without being paralyzed. His failures are real and sometimes reveal actual flaws, not just endearing incompetence. He keeps going.

He thinks he's ordinary. He's not quite. But he's early.
