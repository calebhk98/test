# Verbal Tic Fingerprint — ch01-ch05

Compiled from five independent Sonnet tic-finders, one per chapter.
This is a comparison checklist for whether subsequent chapters match
the established voice.

---

## Sentence-opener distribution (across all 5 chapters)

| Opener | Density |
|---|---|
| **"I [verb]"** | ~22-35 per chapter (DOMINANT — 25-40% of all openers) |
| **"He / She [verb]"** | 8-20 per chapter |
| **"The [noun]"** | 8-12 per chapter |
| **"Then"** | 5-7 per chapter |

## Recurring constructions

1. **"X and X and X" triadic lists** — universal. Sometimes ending in a comma-fragment instead of a third noun.
2. **"Which, X" parenthetical wrap-ups** — heavy in ch05 ("which, fair"); present in all.
3. **Trailing fragment after a longer sentence** — universal.
4. **Short declarative pivot after build-up** — universal.
5. **Parenthetical "which" mid-sentence aside** — heavy in ch02.
6. **Trailing appositive that undercuts** ("a B-minus in the one art class they ever made him take drew this map") — ch03.
7. **Low-grade correctio variants** ("None of it was magic. None of it stopped a spear.") — present but explicitly banned in style guide.

## Present-tense general-truth slips (canon style)

Every chapter contains 3-4. Examples:
- "Everybody knew this building." (ch01)
- "Some things you just know." (ch02)
- "Nobody draws Brazil from memory." (ch03)
- "You can't un-show a thing." (ch04)
- "Hot air is lighter. Hot air climbs. The gods don't send it up. It just goes." (ch05)

These are in-voice and required by the style guide. The narrator slipping to present-tense for a universal aside is part of the voice signature.

## Modern teen voice markers (always on)

- **"Yeah"** as agreement/deflection — never "yes"
- **"Okay"** as standalone — ch01, ch04
- **"I - "** / **"I mean"** interrupted self — universal in dialogue
- **Hedges** ("kind of," "maybe," "I think," "I don't know," "probably")
- **Self-deprecating dry humor** — universal
