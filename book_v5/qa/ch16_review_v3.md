## 3. Prose Tics — Zero-Tolerance Sweep

### 3a. Correctio / "Not X, it's Y" (ZERO allowed)

**ONE INSTANCE FOUND — BLOCKER:**

- L61: "she had stopped being a child I was hiding and become a problem I was hiding, **which is a different and worse kind**"

This is a correctio pivot in the "not X, it's Y" family: the construction frames the second thing as a correction/revision of the first, using the contrast as rhetorical emphasis. The logic is "not [child-hiding], but [problem-hiding — different and worse]." The structure performs the contrast rather than stating it.

**Fix:** Trust the second clause alone. "She was eighteen or nineteen that summer and the problem I was hiding had outgrown the child I had been hiding." Or simply cut the trailing qualification entirely: the reader already knows hiding a problem is worse; the contrast does not need labeling.

**No other correctio instances found.** Line 113's "it wasn't a win" is permitted — it describes a practical outcome, not a correctio pivot. Line 21's "He did not care that a machine had made it or that I had made the machine. He cared that it cost two asses" is a parallel construction, not a negation-definition pivot; it passes.

### 3d. Narrator stepping outside time / future-vantage (ZERO allowed)

**ONE CANDIDATE — BORDERLINE / REVISE:**

- L89: "The future, as it happened, was waiting at Mettius's house with a cup of watered Falernian and four years of resentment, and it had read its Strabo again."

**Ruling: BORDERLINE — leans banned.** The phrase "as it happened" signals the narrator arriving from a later vantage to frame the upcoming scene as an ironic reversal ("I said the future / actually the future was here"). It does not declare significance with the banned formula ("I did not know then…") but it does perform dramatic irony by announcing that what Daniel called "the future" was already present. A close reader would experience it as the narrator stepping outside the scene's timeline.

The style guide permits "close-retrospect that reports what Daniel knew or felt at the time" but bans "declaring significance from a future vantage point." This line is doing the latter — it is telling the reader the chapter will contain an ironic reversal before it arrives.

**Fix:** Drop the framing sentence or rewrite as: "Macer sent me back to the salon, and I went more willingly than I once would have." The scene that follows earns its weight without an announced introduction.
