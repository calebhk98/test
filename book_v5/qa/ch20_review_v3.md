## 3. Prose Tics — Zero-Tolerance Sweep

### 3a. Correctio / "Not X, it's Y" (ZERO allowed)

**VIOLATION FOUND: 1**

- [x] **FOUND: L37** — `"He laughed. Not the polite kind. The real kind, that came from his chest."`

This is the classic correctio form: define X by negating it, then pivot to Y. "Not the polite kind. The real kind." It is the exact construction named in PROSE_PATTERNS_TO_AVOID §1.1 and §2.6 as zero-tolerance.

**Fix:** Replace with the direct fact. Options:
- "He laughed — his chest laugh, not the dry social thing." (No: still em dash variant)
- "He laughed from the chest."
- "He laughed, and it was the real kind, the one that shook his shoulders."
- Or: describe the laugh's physical quality without the negation pivot at all.

**Secondary candidate for review: L73**
`"the worst part was not that he was cheating me. He wasn't cheating me."`
This is a milder instance — the first sentence sets up a negation, the second confirms it, and the paragraph then states the actual truth. It's correctio-adjacent but the pivot is softer (both halves are negative; the positive statement follows as a new sentence). Flagging as SHOULD FIX: rewrite to state the actual fact directly without the setup negation. Example: "He had made me look at the number she was, and the number was high because of everything I'd taught her."

## 7. Verdict

**REVISE**

### Blocking issues (must fix before next use)

1. **ZERO-TOLERANCE CORRECTIO — L37** — `"He laughed. Not the polite kind. The real kind, that came from his chest."` — Classic correctio construction; banned in all forms. Fix: state the fact directly — "He laughed from his chest" or describe the physical quality of the laugh without negation-and-pivot.

2. **ONE-SENTENCE PARAGRAPH DENSITY — 36.5% (31 of 85 paragraphs)** — More than double the 15% cap (≈13 max). The device is deployed as default dialogue punctuation rather than reserved emphasis. Consolidate the short-exchange dialogue pairs ("And Tyche." / `"Tyche," he said, and smiled, "keeps your tally."`; "I sat."; "What?"; "Tyche." / "I'm not thanking you." — several of these can run into the adjacent paragraph without loss of rhythm, and doing so will restore the emphasis that the few genuinely isolated sentences deserve).

3. **AGE ARITHMETIC: "nine years" conflicts with Tyche age twenty-four — L159, L171** — "I'd kept it back from everyone for nine years" (L159) and "I had spent nine years not saying *at home*" (L171) imply a chapter date of ~107 AD. But Tyche born ~85 AD and stated to be twenty-four places the chapter at ~109 AD. The outline specifies ~108-109 AD; Tyche's twenty-four was the required V2 fix. Fix: change "nine years" to "eleven years" in both instances (L159 and L171).

### Revise items (should fix)

4. **"looked at me" over cap — L19, L137, L173 (count: 3, cap: 2)** — All three instances are charged and earn their place individually, but the count is one over. L173 is the most dispensable: `"At home," she said, and looked at me, and filed it, and didn't ask.` The "looked at me" can be cut without loss: `"At home," she said, and filed it, and didn't ask.`

5. **L73 correctio-adjacent** — `"the worst part was not that he was cheating me. He wasn't cheating me."` — Not a hard zero-tolerance violation but the negation-setup structure is the pattern the rule targets. A brief, class-marked line — even two to four words — would fulfill his V2 function as the book's lower-register voice and make him a more active witness to a scene about freedom that directly concerns his own past experience. This chapter's opening is functional, but flagged per the global budget. Borderline — the chapter is otherwise strong enough that the opening does not undermine it, and this is a SHOULD FIX rather than a blocker.
