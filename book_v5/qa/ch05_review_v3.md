### 3d. Narrator stepping outside time / future-vantage (ZERO allowed)

Two candidates found:

**CANDIDATE 1 — L53:** "To the end of that winter and **probably the end of his life** he thought my invisible animals were a superstition..."

Ruling: **PERMITTED.** This is close retrospect — the narrator is an old man writing this from decades later; he is reporting what he observed/inferred, hedged with "probably." The construction does not perform dramatic irony or announce future significance. It reports a state that Daniel could reasonably infer at the time and knows more firmly from the vantage of memoir. Compare to the permitted form: "I know now that X." This is within the retrospective frame.

**CANDIDATE 2 — L135:** "...and it would turn out I didn't know the half of how much I didn't know."

Ruling: **BORDERLINE — FLAG FOR WRITER'S DECISION.** The phrase "it would turn out" is the narrator arriving from future experience to annotate the present moment. It is not dramatic-irony-by-declaration in the full banned sense (it does not assign weight to the moment or announce its importance), but it is structurally the same as "I did not know then what it would cost me." The context is self-deprecating rather than portentous, which softens it, but the construction is technically in the banned category. The line reads: "I didn't know how to build it. I didn't know what it cost or what it weighed or whether the cloth would hold or burn, and it would turn out I didn't know the half of how much I didn't know." A clean fix: "I didn't know the half of what I didn't know" — present the ignorance directly without the future-tense annotation.

**Result:** [x] One borderline instance: L135 "it would turn out." Recommend revision (not a hard block if coordinator rules it's within tone, but a clean cut is available and preferable).
