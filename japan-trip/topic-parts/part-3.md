## Nature and landscape

| What | Day | Address | Website | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Yoyogi Park | 5 | Yoyogikamizonocho, Shibuya-ku, Tokyo (nearest station Harajuku; address unverified) | https://www.tokyo-park.or.jp/park/yoyogi/ | Tokyo's largest and most famous city park, wide open lawns beside Meiji Jingu's forest. Free, flat paved and grass paths throughout, fully stroller-friendly. This is where the day's midday rest block, packed lunch and the Sunday rockabilly dancers all happen. |
| Odaiba Marine Park | 8 | 1-4 Daiba, Minato-ku, Tokyo 135-0091 | https://www.gotokyo.org/en/spot/39/index.html | A flat waterfront park and small artificial beach on Tokyo Bay, facing the Rainbow Bridge and a half-scale replica Statue of Liberty. Free, wide paved promenade throughout, no stairs, a quiet early-morning start before the crowds build. |
| Chureito Pagoda / Arakurayama Sengen Park | 11 | 3353-1 Arakura, Fujiyoshida-shi, Yamanashi | https://www.city.fujiyoshida.yamanashi.jp/page/13101.html | A five-story vermilion pagoda on a hillside above Lake Kawaguchi, framed with Mt. Fuji behind it - the single most photographed view in the Fuji Five Lakes region. The viewing platform is reached by 398 stone steps or a longer slope path, both carrier-only; one adult carries one infant up while the other two adults and the stroller wait at the flat lower shrine grounds. Free entry, no booking needed. |
| Oishi Park | 12 | 2525-11 Oishi, Fujikawaguchiko-machi, Minamitsuru-gun, Yamanashi 401-0305 | https://www.fkchannel.jp/naturelivingcenter/oishipark/ | A free lakeside park and flower garden on Lake Kawaguchi's north shore, laid out to frame Mt. Fuji across the water without any stair climb. Go in the morning, the reliable window for a clear mountain before afternoon cloud builds. Flat gravel and grass paths, fully stroller-friendly, with a small cafe and restrooms on site. |
| Lake Kawaguchi sightseeing cruise | 12 | Funatsuhama Pier, Kawaguchiko, ~10 min walk from Kawaguchiko Stn (street address unverified) | https://www.fujikyu.co.jp/en/leisure/leisure15.html | A 20-minute loop around Lake Kawaguchi passing under the Kawaguchiko Ohashi bridge and around Unoshima Island, a water-level angle on Fuji different from the park or ropeway view. ¥1,200/adult, infants free. Boarding is via a short gangway with a handrail, not wheel-on, but an adult can carry a folded stroller aboard. |
| Kawaguchiko Tenjozan Ropeway | 12 | 1163-1 Azagawa, Fujikawaguchiko-machi, Minamitsuru-gun, Yamanashi 401-0303 | https://www.mtfujiropeway.jp/en/ | A cable car climbing about 400m up Mt. Tenjo in under three minutes to a summit deck with a panorama of Mt. Fuji across the full width of Lake Kawaguchi. ¥1,000/adult, infants free. Both stations are step-free and the cabin takes a folded stroller or a carrier-worn infant; morning is the more reliable window for the mountain. |
| Oshino Hakkai | 13 | Oshino, Minamitsuru-gun, Yamanashi 401-0511 | https://oshino-navi.com/en/ | A cluster of eight spring-fed ponds filled with Mt. Fuji's own filtered snowmelt, the free public path between them being the actual draw. One of the eight, Nigori-ike, sits inside a separate paid garden with its own admission (+¥300). Flat, paved, stroller-friendly throughout, and busiest by late morning. |
| Yuigahama Beach | 7 | Yuigahama, Kamakura, Kanagawa (nearest station Yuigahama, Enoden; address unverified) | https://visit.trip-kamakura.com/things-to-do/yuigahama-beach/ | Kamakura's town beach, a wide stretch of gray-brown sand facing Sagami Bay. Free, an easy wind-down after two temples, flat sand approach from the road, stroller-passable to the sand's edge; carrier or being carried is easier once actually on the sand. |
| Arashiyama Bamboo Grove | 17 | Sagaogurayama Bamboo Grove path, Ukyo-ku, Kyoto, near Arashiyama Stn (Hankyu) / JR Saga-Arashiyama (address unverified) | https://kyoto.travel/en/areas/saga-arashiyama/ | A several-hundred-metre path cut through a dense grove of towering bamboo, one of the most photographed natural scenes in Japan. Free, always open, no ticket, best in the first hour after sunrise before tour groups arrive. The path is flat packed gravel and stroller-fine, with no shade or seating along it. |
| Togetsukyo Bridge and riverside | 17 | Ukyo-ku, Kyoto, over the Katsura/Hozu River, near Arashiyama Stn (Hankyu) (address unverified) | https://kyoto.travel/en/destinations/togetsukyo-bridge/ | "Moon Crossing Bridge," Arashiyama's postcard landmark, the current span dating to 1934. The wide river and forested hillside behind it are the real draw. Free to walk, flat pavement, open riverside space for a toddler to stretch their legs off the stroller. |
| Philosopher's Path | 16 | Tetsugaku-no-michi, Sakyo-ku, Kyoto, Nanzenji to Eikan-do stretch, near Keage Stn (Tozai Line) (address unverified) | https://kyoto.travel/en/destinations/philosophers-path-tetsugakunomichi/ | A stone path along a canal between Nanzenji and Ginkaku-ji, named for 20th-century Kyoto University philosopher Nishida Kitaro, who reportedly walked it daily for meditation. Free, flat, and quiet compared to the temple crowds; late-October color is only just starting to turn at the canal's edge. |
| Yanaka Cemetery | 6 | Yanaka, Taito-ku, Tokyo (nearest station Nippori; address unverified) | https://www.tokyo-park.or.jp/reien/yanaka/ | A large, tree-lined Edo-era cemetery of over 7,000 graves laid out in quiet gridded lanes lined with cherry trees, more a peaceful historic walk than a somber one. Free; paths are paved but uneven in places, so a carrier is the better call than the stroller. |
| Nara Park (deer) | 20 | Nara Park (district; address unverified) | https://www.pref.nara.lg.jp/site/park/index.html | Home to roughly 1,200 wild sika deer that have wandered the park freely for over a thousand years, historically protected as messengers of the local Shinto gods. Free to watch; ¥200 per pack of rice crackers to feed them by hand. The deer are pushy once food is out, so keep the pack out of a toddler's reach and hold crackers at adult height. |
| Momijidani Park | 25 | Miyajima-cho, Hatsukaichi-shi, Hiroshima, at the Miyajima Ropeway base station (address unverified) | https://www.miyajima.or.jp/english/spot/spot_momiji.html | A free, shaded maple valley park at the base of the Miyajima Ropeway, benches throughout. It doubles as the day's midday rest block: one adult naps with the stroller and one infant here while the other two adults ride the ropeway. |
| Meriken Park | 28 | 2 Hatobacho, Chuo-ku, Kobe 650-0042 | https://www.feel-kobe.jp/en/attractions/detail_1006.html | A flat harbourside park facing Kobe Port Tower, with the "Be Kobe" sign as the current photo spot. One corner holds the Port of Kobe Earthquake Memorial Park, a 60m section of quay wall left cracked by the 1995 Great Hanshin-Awaji Earthquake. Free, fully stroller-friendly, Nankinmachi a short walk inland. |

## Rural and small-town Japan

| What | Day | Address | Website | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Oshino Hakkai | 13 | Oshino, Minamitsuru-gun, Yamanashi 401-0511 | https://oshino-navi.com/en/ | A preserved farming village of thatched-roof buildings and water wheels built around eight spring-fed ponds of startlingly clear Mt. Fuji snowmelt. The free public path between the ponds is the draw; flat, paved, stroller-friendly throughout, busiest by late morning. |
| Saiko Iyashi-no-Sato Nenba | 13 | 2710 Nenba, Saiko, Fujikawaguchiko-machi, Minamitsuru-gun, Yamanashi 401-0332 | https://saikoiyashinosatonenba.jp/en/ | A reconstructed thatched-roof (kayabuki) farming village on Lake Saiko's shore, rebuilt after a 1966 landslide destroyed the original hamlet; about 20 restored buildings house small craft workshops, food stalls and a folk museum. ¥500/adult, infants free. Flat, unpaved-but-firm paths, stroller-manageable with some care at the thatched-building thresholds. |
| Yanaka Ginza and Yanaka Cemetery | 6 | Yanaka, Taito-ku, Tokyo (nearest station Sendagi or Nippori; address unverified) | https://www.yanakaginza.com/ | An old-fashioned shotengai of roughly 60 shops - senbei, menchi-katsu, cat-themed goods - that survived Tokyo's postwar rebuilding largely intact, paired with the adjoining tree-lined Edo-era cemetery. Free to browse; the shopping street is flat and stroller-friendly outside peak hours, the cemetery's paths are paved but uneven, better with a carrier. |
| Chushojima / Fushimi sake district | 22 | Canal streets around the Gekkeikan Okura Sake Museum, Chushojima, Fushimi-ku, Kyoto (district; address unverified) | https://kyoto.travel/en/see-and-do/fushimi.html | A free canal-side walk past rows of white-plastered, black-trimmed sake brewery warehouses, some dating to the Edo period, when this was the transshipment point for sake and rice moving between Kyoto and Osaka by boat. Entirely flat, paved and stroller-friendly, with benches along the water and none of central Kyoto's crowds. |

## Streets, lanes and canal walks

| What | Day | Address | Website | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Neighbourhood walk | 14 | Shijo-Omiya, Kyoto, near Omiya Stn, Hankyu Kyoto Line (address unverified) | - | A free, unplanned walk around the hotel's neighbourhood to get oriented before the ten-night Kyoto stay begins - no set route or paid stop, just enough to find the nearest konbini and bus stop. Flat city streets, fully stroller-friendly, deliberately light after a transfer-heavy morning. |
| Gion district walk | 18 | Hanamikoji-dori, Gion, Higashiyama-ku, Kyoto, near Gion-Shijo Stn (Keihan) (address unverified) | https://kyoto.travel/en/areas/gion-kiyomizu/ | Kyoto's most famous geiko and maiko entertainment district, a working quarter since the Edo period, lined with narrow-fronted wooden machiya townhouses. Several private side-lanes ban tourist photography, with fines reported up to ¥10,000 for photographing geiko or maiko without consent. Flat and stroller-fine; the real geiko/maiko traffic happens after dark, past infant bedtime. |
| Shirakawa Canal | 18 | Shirakawa Canal, Gion, Higashiyama-ku, Kyoto, near Gion-Shijo Stn (Keihan) (address unverified) | - | A narrow, willow-lined canal past traditional teahouses, one of Gion's most photographed corners and quieter than Hanamikoji-dori itself. Free, flat stone walkway alongside the water, stroller-fine. |
| Pontocho evening lantern walk | 18 | Pontocho, Nakagyo-ku, Kyoto, between Sanjo-dori and Shijo-dori, one block west of the Kamogawa (address unverified) | https://www.japan.travel/en/spot/79/ | One of Kyoto's five historic geiko districts: a single pedestrian lane about 500m long and only 2-3m wide, lined with machiya, hanging lanterns and around 80 restaurants. Free to walk through after dark; the priciest restaurants refuse first-time or foreign walk-ins (ichigen-san okotowari), so the plan walks through only. The riverside kawayuka dining platforms are dismantled by the end of September, so this October visit sees the plain alley rather than the summer platforms. Narrow and crowded in the evening, carrier over stroller. |
| Kamo riverbank walk (in kimono) | 23 | Kamo River near Gojo, Shimogyo-ku, Kyoto (district; address unverified) | https://www.japan.travel/en/spot/1183/ | The Kamo River's grassy, paved banks near the kimono shop, today's photo backdrop while the three adults are dressed for the day. Free, and the plan stays on the paved path throughout since geta sandals are slower and stiffer than sneakers on anything uneven; stroller-friendly for whichever infant isn't being carried. |
| Dotonbori canal walk | 27, 29 | Dotonbori, Chuo-ku, Osaka (district; nearest station Namba, address unverified) | https://osaka-info.jp/en/spot/dotonbori/ | Osaka's best-known nightlife strip, a canal-side promenade lined with oversized illuminated signs, the animated Glico "running man" billboard the classic photo spot. Free, flat and stroller-workable along the main promenade, though crowded and slow-moving after dark. |
| Canal-side brewery walk, Chushojima | 22 | Canal streets around the Gekkeikan Okura Sake Museum, Chushojima, Fushimi-ku, Kyoto (district; address unverified) | https://kyoto.travel/en/see-and-do/fushimi.html | A free stroll along the old Fushimi river port's canal, past rows of white-plastered sake brewery warehouses with black wooden trim, some dating to the Edo period when this was the transshipment point for sake and rice moving between Kyoto and Osaka by boat. Entirely flat, paved and stroller-friendly, with benches along the water. |
| Higashiyama lanes, Sannenzaka and Ninenzaka | 18 | Sannenzaka and Ninenzaka slopes, Higashiyama-ku, Kyoto, near the Kiyomizu-michi bus stop (address unverified) | https://ja.kyoto.travel/komafuda/show.php?id=2239&lang=en | Two connected stone-paved lanes climbing to Kiyomizu-dera, lined with preserved wooden shopfronts and teahouses - the closest thing to a preserved Edo-period streetscape in central Kyoto. Free to walk. The paving is old, uneven flagstone on a slope, stepped and steep in places, so carrier rather than stroller. |
