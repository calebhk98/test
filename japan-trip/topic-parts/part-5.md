## Theatre and performance

| What | Day | Address | Website | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kabukiza single-act (hitomakumi) | 2 | Kabukiza, 4-12-15 Ginza, Chuo-ku, Tokyo 104-0061 (single-act entrance and box office, building's left side, 4th floor) | https://www.kabukiweb.net/ | A hitomakumi ticket buys standing/bench-row admission to just one act of Kabuki's four-hour daily programme rather than the whole show, for a fraction of the price - the practical way to see this centuries-old, highly stylized theatre form (all-male cast, live shamisen, a raised walkway through the audience) without committing an entire evening to it. About ¥2,500 including a ¥1,000 refundable-deposit English subtitle tablet, which solves the language problem the Japanese-only earphone guide doesn't. Tickets are sold same-day only, cash only, at a separate entrance, no advance booking. |
| Tokyo Rockabilly Club dancers | 5 | Yoyogi Park, Harajuku entrance plaza, Shibuya-ku, Tokyo (same plaza as the park entrance; exact address unverified) | - | A free, unticketed open-air gathering: crews of 1950s-styled greasers, leather jackets and pompadours, meet most Sunday afternoons at the plaza by Yoyogi Park's Harajuku entrance to dance to rock and roll off a portable sound system, a tradition running since the 1980s. There is no official site for it and no fixed schedule - it is weather-dependent and not guaranteed, so a cold or rainy Sunday can mean nobody turns up. Free to watch, viewable from the sidelines or a stroller, commonly starting early-to-mid afternoon. |

## Transport as an experience

| What | Day | Address | Website | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sumida River water bus | 4 | Hamarikyu Pier, Chuo-ku, Tokyo, to Asakusa Pier, Taito-ku, Tokyo | https://www.suijobus.co.jp/en/ | A roughly 40-minute Tokyo Cruise boat ride under 13 bridges down the Sumida River, turning what would otherwise be a dull subway transfer into scenic downtime that lands a five-to-ten-minute walk from the hotel. ¥1,000/adult on top of the day's other transport. Reconfirm the route is running before relying on it - Tokyo Cruise's fleet has faced extended service suspensions, and Hamarikyu boarding specifically has been withdrawn in the past. |
| Enoden coastal railway | 7 | Kamakura Station, Kamakura, Kanagawa (line runs to Fujisawa) | https://www.enoden.co.jp/en/ | A single-track local line that hugs the Kamakura coastline, running past Yuigahama Beach close enough to see the surf from the window - the ride itself, not just the stops it serves, is the point. ¥800 for the "Noriorikun" 1-day unlimited pass, which covers the hops between the Great Buddha, Hase-dera and the beach. Boarding queues at Kamakura Station can run 30-45 minutes on a fine holiday weekend. |
| Kawaguchiko Tenjozan Ropeway | 12 | 1163-1 Azagawa, Fujikawaguchiko-machi, Minamitsuru-gun, Yamanashi 401-0303 | https://www.mtfujiropeway.jp/en/ | A cable car that climbs about 400m up Mt. Tenjo in under three minutes to a summit deck with an unbroken panorama of Mt. Fuji across the full width of Lake Kawaguchi - worth the fare for the view alone, the single best elevated vantage point on the Kawaguchiko leg on a clear day. ¥1,000/adult round trip, infants free. Both stations are step-free and the cabin takes a folded stroller or a carrier-worn infant. |
| Lake Kawaguchi sightseeing cruise | 12 | Funatsuhama Pier, Kawaguchiko, ~10 min walk from Kawaguchiko Stn (street address unverified) | https://www.fujigokokisen.jp/contents/en/ | A 20-minute loop around Lake Kawaguchi on the red sightseeing boat, passing under the Kawaguchiko Ohashi bridge and around Unoshima Island for a water-level angle on Mt. Fuji different from the park or the ropeway view. ¥1,200/adult, infants free. Boarding is via a short gangway with a handrail, not wheel-on, but a folded stroller can be carried aboard. |
| Mishima -> Kyoto Shinkansen | 14 | Mishima Station, Shizuoka, to Kyoto Station, Kyoto (Tokaido Shinkansen) | https://global.jr-central.co.jp/en/ | The trip's one full intercity transfer leg: an unreserved Hikari covering Mishima to Kyoto in 1h50, at cruising speeds around 285 km/h. ¥10,780/adult; infants ride free and the stroller is exempt from the oversized-baggage reservation rule. The ride itself is smooth, seated downtime after a morning of buses and transfers, a contrast to the walking-heavy days on either side of it. |
| Sagano Romantic Train (Torokko) | 17 | Torokko Saga Station, adjacent to JR Saga-Arashiyama Station, Ukyo-ku, Kyoto (exact street address unverified) | https://www.sagano-kanko.co.jp/en/ | A slow, open-window sightseeing train on a former freight line, running one-way through the Hozugawa gorge with forested cliffs and river rapids close alongside the tracks - a genuinely different view of Arashiyama than any of its temples give. ¥880/adult; reserve ahead, since departures run roughly hourly and seats sell out on weekends. Boarding is a small step up from a low platform, manageable with the stroller folded rather than wheeled on. |
| Fushimi Jikkokubune canal boat | 22 | Boarding dock by Bekkan Terrace, Chushojima, Fushimi-ku, Kyoto (address unverified) | https://kyoto-fushimi.or.jp/fune/ | A round trip in a jikkokubune, the flat-bottomed wooden cargo boat that once carried rice and sake down these canals to Osaka, passing the backs of the brewery warehouses and through the Miyuki-bashi lock. It explains why a sake industry grew here at all - Fushimi was a river port before it was a brewing town, and the canal was the road. ¥1,900/adult, about 55 minutes, seated throughout. Reservations are online-only, no phone or email, closing at midnight two days before the date; it does not run Mondays. |
| Kyoto -> Hiroshima Shinkansen | 24 | Kyoto Station, Kyoto, to Hiroshima Station, Hiroshima (Sanyo Shinkansen) | https://www.westjr.co.jp/global/en/train/shinkansen/tokaido-sanyo-shinkansen/ | An unreserved Hikari/Sakura run of 1h40 on the Sanyo Shinkansen, the segment's first of two big intercity legs. Walk-up fare is ¥10,770/adult, but this leg is covered by the JR Kansai-Hiroshima Area Pass bought the same morning, which pays for itself almost entirely on this one ride. Infants ride free and the stroller travels without a reservation. |
| JR Miyajima ferry (round trip) | 25 | Miyajimaguchi Pier, Hatsukaichi, to Miyajima Pier, Hatsukaichi, Hiroshima | https://jr-miyajimaferry.co.jp/en/ | A 10-minute crossing that passes closest to Itsukushima Shrine's floating torii gate of any of the island's ferries, so the approach by water is itself part of the sight rather than just a way to reach it. ¥400 round trip walk-up, but covered by the JR Kansai-Hiroshima Area Pass. Infants ride free, same as on JR trains. |
| Miyajima Ropeway | 25 | Momijidani Station, Miyajima-cho, Hatsukaichi (address unverified) | https://miyajima-ropeway.info/english/ | A two-stage cable car climbing most of the way up Mt. Misen, Miyajima's sacred mountain, ending at a viewpoint over the Inland Sea and its islands - a scenic gondola ride substituting for the stone-stepped summit trail, which is carrier-only and unsuited to two adults managing two under-twos on steep stairs. ¥2,000/adult round trip. Only 2 of 3 adults go up, one carrying the toddler, while the third rests with the stroller and the other infant at the base station. |
| Hiroden streetcar | 24, 26 | Hiroshima (citywide network; boarding points include Hiroshima Station and Genbaku Dome-mae) | https://www.hiroden.co.jp/en/ | One of the last big tram networks left running in Japan, with more than 35km of track and a fleet that deliberately keeps older cars in service rather than retiring them - some surviving pre-war units still run alongside newer low-floor trams on the same lines. A flat citywide fare of ¥220 per ride makes it the easy, cheap way to move between Peace Memorial Park, Shukkei-en and Hiroshima Castle; infants ride free. |
| Hiroshima -> Shin-Osaka Shinkansen | 27 | Hiroshima Station, Hiroshima, to Shin-Osaka Station, Osaka (Sanyo Shinkansen) | https://www.westjr.co.jp/global/en/train/shinkansen/tokaido-sanyo-shinkansen/ | The segment's second big intercity leg, an unreserved Hikari/Sakura covering Hiroshima to Shin-Osaka in 1h30. Walk-up fare is ¥9,710/adult, again covered by the JR Kansai-Hiroshima Area Pass bought on Day 24, using up the pass's last day of validity. Infants ride free. |
| Tombori River Cruise | 29 | Tazaemonbashi Pier, by the Don Quijote store, Dotonbori, Chuo-ku, Osaka (pier location; no street address) | https://www.ipponmatsu.co.jp/cruise/tombori.html | A 20-minute narrated boat ride along the Dotonbori canal with a live guide, passing underneath the same illuminated signs - including the Glico running man - already seen on foot, but from the water and after dark, when the signs are lit. ¥2,000/adult, infants free. It runs on scheduled hourly and half-hourly departures rather than a walk-up guarantee, and boarding is via a short ramp or steps at the pier. |

## Views and observation decks

| What | Day | Address | Website | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tokyo Skytree Tembo Deck | 2 | 1-1-2 Oshiage, Sumida-ku, Tokyo 131-0045 | https://en.tokyo-skytree.jp/ | The first observation deck (350m) of the tallest structure in Japan (634m total), with glass-floor sections and a 360-degree view over the Kanto plain - Mt. Fuji is visible on a clear day. ¥2,500/adult; advance timed tickets are recommended to skip the counter queue. Elevators throughout, fully stroller and infant-friendly. |
| Tokyo Tower Main Deck | 4 | 4-2-8 Shibakoen, Minato-ku, Tokyo (address per site research; postal code unverified) | https://en.tokyotower.co.jp/ | The Main Deck (150m) of Tokyo's original 1958 broadcast tower - lower and more central than Skytree, giving a different classic view of the city rather than a redundant one. ¥1,500/adult, elevator-served, no stairs. Worth doing on its own terms even a few days after visiting Skytree. |
| Landmark Tower Sky Garden | 9 | 2-2-1 Minatomirai, Nishi-ku, Yokohama, Kanagawa 220-0012 | https://www.yokohama-landmark.jp/skygarden/ | The 69th-floor observation deck of what was, for decades, Japan's tallest building, reached by a high-speed elevator in about 40 seconds, with sweeping views over Minato Mirai's harbor skyline and Yokohama Bay (Mt. Fuji visible on a clear day). ¥1,200/adult. Fully indoor and stroller/infant-friendly. |
| Chureito Pagoda viewpoint | 11 | 3353-1 Arakura, Fujiyoshida-shi, Yamanashi Prefecture | https://en.fujiyoshida.net/ | A five-story vermilion pagoda on a hillside above Lake Kawaguchi's basin with Mt. Fuji rising directly behind it - the single most photographed view in the Fuji Five Lakes region. Free, reached via 398 stone steps or a longer, gentler zig-zag slope path, both carrier-only with no ramp or elevator anywhere on the route. |
| Kawaguchiko Tenjozan Ropeway summit | 12 | 1163-1 Azagawa, Fujikawaguchiko-machi, Minamitsuru-gun, Yamanashi 401-0303 | https://www.mtfujiropeway.jp/en/ | The summit deck reached by the ropeway above, with an unbroken panorama of Mt. Fuji across the full width of Lake Kawaguchi - on a clear day the best elevated vantage point on the Kawaguchiko leg. ¥1,000/adult round trip, infants free. Ride late morning to keep the wait short before midday crowds build. |
| Miyajima Ropeway summit | 25 | Shishiiwa Station, Mt. Misen, Miyajima, Hatsukaichi (address unverified) | https://miyajima-ropeway.info/english/ | A viewpoint over the Inland Sea and its islands reached by the two-stage cable car, standing in for the stone-stepped walk to Mt. Misen's actual 535m summit, which this trip skips. ¥2,000/adult round trip. Only 2 of 3 adults go up, one carrying the toddler, while the third rests with the stroller at the base station. |
| Hiroshima Orizuru Tower | 26 | 1-2-1 Otemachi, Naka-ku, Hiroshima 730-0051 | https://www.orizurutower.jp/en/ | A modern tower directly across from the Atomic Bomb Dome, with an open-air rooftop deck looking back over Peace Park and the city, plus a spiral ramp down through a glass wall where visitors fold and drop a paper crane into the atrium below. ¥2,200/adult, a deliberately lighter, hands-on stop right after the Peace Memorial Museum. Elevator access throughout, fully stroller-accessible. |
| Tsutenkaku Tower | 29 | 1-18-6 Ebisuhigashi, Naniwa-ku, Osaka 556-0002 | https://www.tsutenkaku.co.jp/ | A 103m steel observation tower first built in 1912 and rebuilt in 1956, the centerpiece of the retro Shinsekai entertainment district - a smaller, more old-fashioned stand-in for Osaka's newer towers, and this trip's own "city from above" stop. ¥900/adult, elevator-served; infants free. |
| Shibuya Scramble Crossing and Hachiko statue | 5 | Outside Shibuya Stn Hachiko Exit, Shibuya-ku (address unverified) | https://www.gotokyo.org/en/spot/78/index.html | The famously chaotic five-way pedestrian crossing where up to 3,000 people cross at once when the light changes, next to the bronze statue of Hachiko, the dog who waited at the station for his deceased owner for years. Free, a five-minute "see it to believe it" stop rather than a place to linger - best watched from the station walkway or a window rather than from inside the crowd with a stroller. |
| Kawaguchiko Tenjozan Panoramic Ropeway | 12 | 1163-1 Azagawa, Fujikawaguchiko-machi, Minamitsuru-gun, Yamanashi 401-0303 | https://www.mtfujiropeway.jp/en/ | The same cable car and summit view as above, climbing about 400m up Mt. Tenjo in under three minutes to a deck overlooking the full width of Lake Kawaguchi and Mt. Fuji beyond it. ¥1,000/adult round trip, infants free. Morning is the reliable window for a clear view of the mountain before afternoon cloud builds. |
