## Markets and street stalls

| What | Day | Address | Website | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nakamise-dori | 2 | 2-3-1 Asakusa, Taito-ku, Tokyo 111-0032 | https://www.senso-ji.jp/ | The covered approach street to Senso-ji, lined with snack and souvenir stalls leading to the Kaminarimon lantern gate. Free to browse, grounds always open. It's genuinely too dense for a stroller by mid-morning, so use the carrier here. |
| Ameyoko | 3 | Ameyoko market, Taito-ku, Tokyo (address unverified) | https://www.ameyoko.net/ | A street market running between Ueno and Okachimachi stations, born out of a postwar black market and now mixing cut-price clothing and cosmetics with seafood stalls and street food. This visit is a food stop - takoyaki, pan-fried gyoza, yakitori skewers eaten standing. Free to browse. |
| Yanaka Ginza | 6 | Yanaka, Taito-ku (nearest station Sendagi or Nippori; address unverified) | https://yanaka-ginza.com/ | An old-fashioned shotengai of roughly 60 small shops - senbei crackers, menchi-katsu stalls, cat-themed goods - that survived Tokyo's postwar rebuilding largely intact. The best "old Tokyo streetscape" browsing outside Asakusa, and much less crowded. Free to browse, flat and stroller-friendly outside peak weekend hours. |
| Toyosu Market (outer area) | 8 | Toyosu Market outer area, Koto-ku (nearest station Shijo-mae; address unverified) | https://www.shijou.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/ | The outer, publicly accessible ring of Tokyo's wholesale fish and produce market, grazed here for breakfast - a grilled scallop skewer, tamagoyaki, a few pieces of nigiri, hojicha tea - stall by stall rather than at a sit-down sushi counter. Entry is free; public hours run roughly 5am-5pm and the market closes on scattered days, so check before going. |
| Kobo-ichi, To-ji | 15 | To-ji, 1 Kujocho, Minami-ku, Kyoto 601-8473 | https://toji.or.jp/en/ | A monthly temple market: a flea-and-food market tied to a temple's own history rather than staged for tourists. Kobo-ichi falls on the 21st of every month, marking the death anniversary of Kobo Daishi, and fills To-ji's grounds with roughly 1,000 stalls of antiques, vintage kimono, ceramics, plants and genuine street food. Free to enter, published hours roughly 8:30-17:30; open gravel approach paths take a stroller, but the packed stall rows call for a carrier. |
| Nishiki Market | 15 | Nishikikoji-dori, Nakagyo-ku, Kyoto 604-8054 | https://www.kyoto-nishiki.or.jp/en/ | A roughly 400m covered arcade of about 130 shops trading since the Edo period as "Kyoto's Kitchen" - fresh seafood, pickles, knives, tea and skewered snacks. Free to walk, and the best way to taste a range of Kyoto specialties without a sit-down meal, though several stalls now post signs asking visitors not to eat while walking. The covered floor is stroller-workable but narrow and can be shoulder-to-shoulder at midday. |
| Tenjin-ichi, Kitano Tenmangu | 19 | Kitano Tenmangu, Bakuro-cho, Kamigyo-ku, Kyoto 602-8386 | https://kitanotenmangu.or.jp/ | Kitano Tenmangu's own monthly temple market, the same kind of centuries-old, fixed-date flea-and-food market as To-ji's Kobo-ichi, held on the 25th of every month to mark the death anniversary of the god of learning. Up to around 1,000 stalls fill the shrine grounds and surrounding streets with antiques, plants, kimono and street snacks. Free to enter, published hours roughly 6:00 until the stalls wind down; the shrine's open paths take a stroller, but the packed aisles are carrier-only. |
| Nankinmachi (Kobe Chinatown) | 28 | Nankinmachi, Sakaemachi-dori, Chuo-ku, Kobe 650-0023 | https://nankinmachi.or.jp/ | One of Japan's three historic Chinatowns, a compact grid of streets given over to pork buns, ebi mayo buns and char siu skewers eaten standing. Free to walk, a short stroll inland from Meriken Park. This is a market now aimed squarely at visitors rather than a residential Chinatown's own daily shopping. |
| Kuromon Ichiba Market | 29 | Kuromon Ichiba Market, 2-4-1 Nipponbashi, Chuo-ku, Osaka 542-0073 | https://kuromon.com/en/ | A roughly 580m covered market of about 170 stalls, historically a wholesale fish and produce market feeding local restaurants. Most stalls today sell grill-it-and-eat-it single portions - nigiri, grilled wagyu skewers, tamagoyaki, oysters on the half shell - priced for visitors rather than a neighborhood greengrocer, which is the honest reason costs run higher than they look. Free to enter and browse. |

## Shopping

| What | Day | Address | Website | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nakamise-dori | 2 | 2-3-1 Asakusa, Taito-ku, Tokyo 111-0032 | https://www.senso-ji.jp/ | Senso-ji's covered approach street, about 250m of souvenir and snack stalls running from the Kaminarimon gate to the temple itself. Free to browse. Genuinely too dense for a stroller by mid-morning, so plan on the carrier. |
| Ameyoko | 3 | Ameyoko market, Taito-ku, Tokyo (address unverified) | https://www.ameyoko.net/ | A street market between Ueno and Okachimachi stations mixing cut-price clothing and cosmetics with seafood stalls and street food, a legacy of its postwar black-market origins. Free to browse. |
| Akihabara | 3 | Sotokanda, Chiyoda-ku, Tokyo - Mandarake Complex, 3-11-12 Sotokanda; Super Potato, Kitabayashi Bldg 3F-5F, 1-11-2 Sotokanda | https://www.mandarake.co.jp/ (Mandarake); https://www.superpotato.com/ (Super Potato) | Tokyo's "electric town": Radio Kaikan and Gachapon Kaikan for capsule toys, Mandarake Complex's eight floors of manga, figures and Gunpla, Super Potato's retro-game floors, and Yodobashi Camera. Free to browse. Ground floors are stroller-workable in short bursts; Mandarake's upper floors and Super Potato's 3F-5F run on narrow stairs and a small lift, so one adult peels off there while the stroller waits below. |
| Kappabashi Kitchenware Street | 6 | Kappabashi Kitchen Street, Nishi-Asakusa, Taito-ku (specific studio address unverified) | https://www.kappabashi.or.jp/en/ | Tokyo's wholesale restaurant-supply street, about 15 minutes on foot from Ueno - the place where the uncannily realistic plastic food displayed in restaurant windows across Japan is actually made and sold, alongside knives, crockery and every other piece of a restaurant kitchen. Free to browse. A hands-on plastic food-sample workshop here (see Crafts) needs adult hands for the hot wax. |
| Takeshita Street + Laforet Harajuku | 5 | Takeshita-dori, Jingumae, Shibuya-ku, Tokyo 150-0001; Laforet Harajuku, 1-11-6 Jingumae, Shibuya-ku, Tokyo 150-0001 | https://www.laforet.ne.jp/en/ | A 350m pedestrian street of roughly 130 shops - teen fast fashion, crepe stands, purikura booths - the most recognizable image of Harajuku. It is narrow and genuinely packed by early afternoon, worse on weekends, so this is carrier rather than stroller terrain. The old street-level Lolita and gothic Lolita cosplay gathering on the nearby Jingu-bashi bridge faded years ago; what survives of that fashion is retail, in boutiques inside Laforet - Angelic Pretty, Baby the Stars Shine Bright, and the gothic-leaning Alice and the Pirates. Free to walk through; Laforet is open 11:00-20:00 with no fixed holidays. |
| Shibuya PARCO | 5 | Shibuya PARCO, 6F, 15-1 Udagawacho, Shibuya-ku, Tokyo 150-0042 | https://en.shibuya.parco.jp/ | One mall floor sharing three fandoms: Pokemon Center Shibuya, the Jump Shop (Shonen Jump merchandise), and Nintendo Tokyo (official Nintendo goods, no console games sold). Free to enter all three. Elevator-served and fully stroller-friendly, a good weatherproof stand-in for the day's other Shibuya stops. |
| Nishiki Market + Daimaru depachika | 15 | Nishikikoji-dori, Nakagyo-ku, Kyoto 604-8054 (Daimaru Kyoto depachika basement, Nakagyo-ku) | https://www.kyoto-nishiki.or.jp/en/ | Kyoto's 400-year-old covered food arcade - seafood, pickles, knives, tea and skewered snacks from about 130 shops - paired here with the basement food hall (depachika) of the neighboring Daimaru department store for a bento top-up. Free to browse. Several Nishiki stalls now ask visitors not to eat while walking; the covered floor is stroller-workable but narrow and crowded at midday. |
| Omamori charms + goshuincho stamp | 15 | Fushimi Inari Taisha stalls, Fushimi-ku, near JR Inari Station (address unverified) | https://inari.jp/en/ | Omamori are small charms sold at shrine and temple stalls, each blessed for one specific wish - safe travel, health, good luck. A goshuincho is a blank accordion-fold book carried between sites to collect goshuin, a priest's hand-brushed calligraphy stamp marking the visit, traditionally a pilgrim's record rather than a souvenir. A five-minute stall stop, about ¥500 per charm, not a destination in itself. |
| Sannenzaka/Ninenzaka | 18 | Sannenzaka and Ninenzaka slopes, Higashiyama-ku, near the Kiyomizu-michi bus stop (address unverified) | - | Two connected stone-paved lanes climbing toward Kiyomizu-dera, lined with preserved wooden shopfronts, teahouses and souvenir stores - the closest thing to a preserved Edo-period streetscape in central Kyoto. Free to walk, best early or late for fewer crowds. The paving is old, uneven flagstone on a slope: hard going for a stroller, easier carried or walked slowly by hand. |
| Kuromon Ichiba Market | 29 | Kuromon Ichiba Market, 2-4-1 Nipponbashi, Chuo-ku, Osaka 542-0073 | https://kuromon.com/en/ | A covered market of about 170 stalls, once a wholesale fish and produce market and now dominated by grill-it-and-eat-it single portions - nigiri, wagyu skewers, tamagoyaki, oysters - priced for visitors rather than a neighborhood greengrocer. Free to enter and browse. |
| Den Den Town | 29 | Nipponbashi/Ota Road, Naniwa-ku/Chuo-ku, Osaka (district, no single address) | - | Osaka's own anime and hobby-shop district, a roughly 1km strip along Sakaisuji and the parallel Ota Road with over 150 shops - Animate, Yellow Submarine, Super Potato, K-Books and other figure, Gunpla, card and doujinshi stores. It shares some chains with Akihabara but reads smaller, less crowded and more collector-hobbyist. Free to browse; flat, wide shopping street, stroller-workable outside peak hours. |
| Dotonbori/Shinsaibashi | 27, 29 | Dotonbori canal, Chuo-ku, Osaka (canal-side pedestrian strip, no single street address); Shinsaibashi-suji, Chuo-ku, Osaka | http://www.dotonbori.or.jp/en/ (Dotonbori); https://global.shinsaibashi.or.jp/en/ (Shinsaibashi) | Osaka's best-known nightlife and shopping strip: Dotonbori's canal-side promenade of oversized illuminated signs, most famously the Glico running man, feeding straight into the covered Shinsaibashi-suji arcade of fashion and department stores. Free to walk. Level and paved throughout, stroller-workable on the main routes, but expect dense, slow-moving evening crowds on both. |
