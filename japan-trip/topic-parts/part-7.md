## Festivals and seasonal events

| What | Day | Address | Website | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jidai Matsuri | 16 | Kyoto Gyoen (the park, not the walled Gosho), Kamigyo-ku, Kyoto 602-0881 | https://www.heianjingu.or.jp/ritual/jidaimatsuri/ | Kyoto's "Festival of the Ages," held every 22 October since 1895 - in 2026 that is a Thursday, the date this visit falls on. About 2,000 participants march in accurate historical costume spanning Kyoto's 1,000+ years as capital, processing from the Imperial Palace area to Heian Jingu roughly 12:00 to 14:30. Watched free from inside Kyoto Gyoen's wide gravel paths, no grandstand seat bought (paid seats run about ¥5,000-15,500). Two phoenix-crested portable shrines near the procession's end bring gagaku court music, audible free from the roadside. |
| Tokyo Rockabilly Club dancers | 5 | Yoyogi Park, Harajuku entrance plaza, Shibuya-ku, Tokyo (exact address unverified) | - | A free, unticketed open-air dance gathering going back to the 1980s: crews in full 1950s greaser style, leather jackets and pompadours, dance to rock and roll off a portable sound system most Sundays. Welcoming to onlookers and viewable from the sidelines or a stroller. Weather-dependent and not guaranteed, commonly starting early-to-mid afternoon and running an hour or more. |
| Kobo-ichi market | 15 | To-ji, 1 Kujocho, Minami-ku, Kyoto 601-8473 | https://toji.or.jp/mieku/ | A monthly temple market on the 21st of every month, marking Kobo Daishi's death anniversary, running unbroken for generations rather than staged for tourists. Roughly 1,000 stalls fill To-ji's grounds beneath its five-story pagoda with antiques, vintage kimono, ceramics, plants, and street food. Free entry, roughly 8:30-17:30; the wide approach is stroller-workable but the packed stall aisles favor a carrier. |
| Tenjin-ichi market | 19 | Kitano Tenmangu, Bakuro-cho, Kamigyo-ku, Kyoto 602-8386 | https://kitanotenmangu.or.jp/en/ | Kitano Tenmangu's own monthly market, held on the 25th to mark the death anniversary of Sugawara no Michizane, god of learning. Up to around 1,000 stalls fill the shrine grounds and surrounding streets with antiques, plants, kimono, and street snacks. Free, roughly 6:00 until stalls wind down in the afternoon; the shrine's open paths take a stroller, the packed aisles want a carrier. |
| Midosuji "Hikari no Kyoen" illumination | 28 | Midosuji Avenue, between the Hanshin-mae and Namba-nishiguchi intersections, Chuo-ku, Osaka | https://www.hikari-kyoen.com/en/midosuji/index.html | Osaka's main boulevard strung with LED displays for about two months each winter, part of the citywide Osaka Hikari-Kyoen light festival. This visit catches opening night, 3 November. Free to walk, no ticket, evening hours only, on wide flat paved sidewalks - stroller-friendly, though opening night draws a crowd so keep to the outer edge of the path. |
| Culture Day (Bunka no Hi) | 28 | National Museum of Art, Osaka, 4-2-55 Nakanoshima, Kita-ku, Osaka 530-0005 | https://www.nmao.go.jp/en/ | A national holiday, 3 November, on which the National Museum of Art, Osaka waives its usual roughly ¥430 adult admission - this is the one Osaka museum confirmed free that date, not a citywide policy, and separate from Kansai Culture Day around 15-16 November, which falls after the trip. The entire exhibition space is underground behind a cluster of curved steel entrance pylons; fully lift-served, no stairs-only stretches, closed Mondays. |

## Things aimed at the toddlers

| What | Day | Address | Website | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sumida Aquarium | 2 | Tokyo Solamachi, 1-1-2 Oshiage, Sumida-ku, Tokyo 131-0045 | https://en.sumida-aquarium.com/ | An indoor aquarium inside the Skytree Town mall, built around an open-topped penguin and fur seal pool you can walk right up to, plus jellyfish tanks and a touch pool. Compact and low-stimulation rather than spectacular - a good indoor stop for toddlers after Senso-ji's crowds. ¥2,900/adult, fully stroller-accessible with elevators and on-site changing facilities. |
| Ueno Zoo | 3 | 9-83 Uenokoen, Taito-ku, Tokyo 110-8711 | https://www.tokyo-zoo.net/en/ueno/ | Japan's oldest zoo, opened 1882, on paved, gently sloped paths inside Ueno Park. **There are no pandas**: the last two, twin cubs Xiao Xiao and Lei Lei, were flown back to China in late January 2026, leaving Japan without a single giant panda for the first time since 1972 - don't build up "the pandas" beforehand. What's actually there instead: Asian elephants, western lowland gorillas, Sumatran tigers, polar bears, and capybara, plus a five-storey pagoda inside the zoo grounds. ¥600/adult, stroller-friendly throughout, infants free. |
| Yoyogi Park | 5 | Yoyogikamizonocho, Shibuya-ku, adjacent to Meiji Jingu (nearest station Harajuku; address unverified) | https://www.tokyo-park.or.jp/park/yoyogi/ | Tokyo's largest and most famous city park, wide open lawns beside Meiji Jingu's forest. Free, unstructured downtime - good for toddlers to run on grass after a morning of gravel paths and shrine etiquette. Flat paved and grass paths throughout, fully stroller-friendly. |
| teamLab Planets | 8 | Toyosu 6-1-16, Koto-ku, Tokyo 135-0061 | https://www.teamlab.art/e/planets/ | A barefoot, walk-through digital art museum: visitors wade ankle-deep through a mirrored water room and move through immersive projection-mapped spaces. Genuinely unlike anything else on the trip. ¥5,300/adult; bring a full change of clothes since guests get wet to the knee, and strollers are left at entrance lockers, the space navigated on foot or in the carrier. |
| Inokashira Park + small zoo corner | 10 | Inokashira Park, Musashino-shi/Mitaka-shi (nearest station Kichijoji or Inokashira-koen; address unverified) | https://www.tokyo-zoo.net/en/inokashira/ | A large pond-centered park adjoining the Ghibli Museum, known for swan-shaped paddle boats and a small, free-to-enter zoo corner with a paid small-animal and elephant enclosure (¥500/adult). An easy fallback if the Ghibli Museum lottery doesn't come through. Paved paths around the pond, stroller-friendly. |
| Nara Park deer | 20 | Nara Park (district; address unverified) | https://narashikanko.or.jp/en/feature/deer | Roughly 1,200 wild sika deer that have wandered Nara Park freely for over a thousand years, historically protected as messengers of the local Shinto gods. They are not fenced and will bow their heads for a cracker, a learned begging cue - but they push, butt, and nibble at clothing once they smell food, worth knowing with a toddler at their eye height. Free to watch; ¥200 per pack of crackers to feed them by hand. |
| Miyajima Public Aquarium | 25 | 10-3 Miyajima-cho, Hatsukaichi, Hiroshima 739-0588 | https://www.miyajima-aqua.jp/ | A mid-size public aquarium a ten-minute walk from Itsukushima Shrine, built around Seto Inland Sea marine life plus a penguin and sea lion show and a hands-on touch pool. A good pressure-release stop after two temple/shrine visits rather than a world-class aquarium. ¥1,420/adult, indoor, climate-controlled, and flat-floored - the easiest stroller stretch of the day. |
| Miyajima deer | 25 | Miyajima, Hatsukaichi (district; address unverified) | https://www.miyajima.or.jp/english/nature/nature_animal.html | About five hundred wild sika deer that roam Miyajima island freely, considered sacred in Shinto belief. Like Nara's, they are not fenced and are famous for "bowing," a learned cue for the shika senbei crackers sold around the island for about ¥200 a pack - but they push and nose at food and bags, so keep crackers out of a toddler's reach and hands empty once they're gone. |
| Anpanman Children's Museum & Mall | 28 | 1-6-2 Higashikawasakicho, Chuo-ku, Kobe 650-0044 | https://www.kobe-anpanman.jp/en/ | A two-zone complex built around Anpanman, the toddler-recognized superhero: a free ground-floor mall with a themed bakery, food court, and souvenir shops, and a paid 2nd-floor museum of play structures, a stage show, and soft-play elements sized for small bodies. **Unlike almost every other site on this trip, admission is charged from age 1** - roughly ¥2,000-2,500 per person - so both toddlers here are paying visitors, not free infants. A web ticket for a specific date and time is required. Indoor, stroller-parked-at-door, fully step-free. |
| Deer senbei at Nara Park | 20 | Nara Park (district; address unverified) | https://narashikanko.or.jp/en/feature/deer | Packs of unglazed rice crackers sold by park vendors let you hand-feed Nara's wild deer, many of whom genuinely bow their heads for one. The deer get pushy once they know you're holding food, sometimes butting or nibbling at clothing, so keep the pack out of a toddler's reach and show empty hands once it's gone. ¥200 per pack, ¥600 for three. |
