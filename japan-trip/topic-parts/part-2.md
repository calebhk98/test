## Imperial and court history

| Heian Jingu | 16 | 97 Okazaki Nishitennocho, Sakyo-ku, Kyoto 606-8341 | https://www.heianjingu.or.jp/ | A shrine built in 1895 as a 5/8-scale replica of the original 8th-century Imperial Palace's main ceremonial hall, marking the 1100th anniversary of Kyoto's founding as capital. | Jidai Matsuri gagaku music | 16 | Kyoto Gyoen (the park, not the walled Gosho), Kamigyo-ku, Kyoto 602-0881 | https://www.heianjingu.or.jp/ritual/jidaimatsuri/ | Two phoenix-crested mikoshi (portable shrines) near the end of the Jidai Matsuri procession are accompanied by gagaku, Japan's ancient court music, played on instruments largely unchanged since the Heian period. Free to hear from the roadside inside Kyoto Gyoen, no extra stop or ticket needed. It is heard alongside the day's free procession viewing, not sought out as a separate stop. |
| Kyoto Imperial Palace (Gosho) | 19 | 3 Kyoto Gyoen, Kamigyo-ku, Kyoto 602-0881 | https://kyoto-gosho.kunaicho.go.jp/en/visit | The former residence of Japan's emperors from 1331 until the 1868 move to Tokyo, the walled and gated compound inside Kyoto Gyoen park. A 2016 policy change dropped the old reservation requirement, so entry is now free and walk-in, 9:00-16:20, closed Mondays. Visitors see the grounds and the exteriors of the Shishinden and Seiryuden from outside their fences; there is no access to building interiors. |
