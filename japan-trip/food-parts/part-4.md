| Houtou | 12 | Hoto Fudou (chain, exact branch unconfirmed), Fujikawaguchiko area, Yamanashi | http://www.houtou-fudou.jp/ | ¥1,400/adult, approximate (the day's lunch cost ¥4,200 for three adults) | ~750 kcal, approximate | Yamanashi's own noodle dish, thick, flat, hand-cut wheat noodles, wider than udon, simmered directly in a hearty miso broth with kabocha squash and root vegetables, so the noodles thicken the broth rather than being served separately. It comes to the table in one large iron pot, still simmering, and is eaten hot straight from it with everyone ladling their own bowlful. Nothing raw, no fish, and the soft-cooked squash and noodles are easy to cut small for a toddler once cooled. |
| Yudofu | 17 | Restaurant unverified, Saga-Arashiyama, Ukyo-ku, near Tenryu-ji | - | ¥1,000/adult, approximate (the day's lunch cost ¥3,100 for three adults) | ~700 kcal, approximate | Simple blocks of soft tofu simmered gently in a pot of plain kombu (kelp) broth at the table, kept warm over a small burner, and lifted out piece by piece with a ladle or chopsticks to dip in a soy-based sauce with grated ginger and spring onion. Originally a Buddhist temple dish, it is deliberately plain rather than richly flavoured, and it is the speciality of this exact Arashiyama temple district. Vegetarian at its base, though the dipping sauce may contain fish stock, and the soft, plain tofu is one of the easiest dishes in Japan to share with a toddler once cooled. |
| Obanzai | 18 | Obanzai restaurant, Gion, Higashiyama-ku (restaurant name unverified) | - | ¥1,200/adult, approximate (the day's set dinner cost ¥3,600 for three adults) | ~800 kcal, approximate | Kyoto's home-style cooking: a set of several small vegetable-forward side dishes served together on individual small plates rather than one main course, typically simmered vegetables, a little tofu, pickles, and a small piece of grilled or simmered fish, all seasonal and lightly seasoned. It is what Kyoto households actually eat day to day, closer to a home-cooked spread than restaurant food built for visitors. Nothing raw and no strong spice, and the small varied portions make it easy to pick out soft, plain pieces for a toddler. |
| Kaiseki dinner | 13 | Lakeland Hotel Mizunosato, 1070 Kodachi, Fujikawaguchiko, Yamanashi | https://www.mizunosato.jp/en/ | ¥1,980/adult, the ryokan's half-board dinner add-on rate (a full a la carte kaiseki elsewhere typically costs several times this) | ~750 kcal, approximate, across all courses | The formal multi-course Japanese dinner, served here as the ryokan's evening meal: a small seasonal appetiser first, then sashimi (thin-sliced raw fish served cold with soy sauce and wasabi), a grilled dish, a simmered vegetable dish, tempura (battered, deep-fried vegetables or seafood), then rice with pickles and miso soup, finishing with a piece of seasonal fruit. Courses arrive one at a time over an hour or more rather than all at once, following set seasonal presentation rather than an a la carte menu. It contains raw fish in the sashimi course, and the length of the meal is worth planning around infant bedtime; the cooked rice, simmered vegetables and miso soup are fine to share with a toddler once cooled, the sashimi is not. |
| Kasujiru | 22 | Restaurant near the Gekkeikan Okura Sake Museum, Chushojima, Fushimi-ku (address unverified) | - | ¥1,100/adult, approximate for the full set meal (the day's lunch cost ¥3,300 for three adults, including rice, pickles and fish; the soup itself is a small part of that) | ~350 kcal, approximate, for the soup alone | A thick, savoury soup made from sake kasu, the soft white paste of fermented rice and yeast left behind once sake is pressed out, simmered with pork and root vegetables such as daikon and carrot in a base similar to miso soup. It has a distinct, faintly boozy aroma from the sake lees, though most of the alcohol cooks off in simmering, and it is served hot in a bowl as part of a set meal. It is Fushimi's own dish, tied to this district's sake breweries and not a standard menu item elsewhere in Japan; given the residual alcohol, it is not recommended for infants or toddlers. |
| Anago-meshi | 25 | Omotesando shopping street, Miyajima-cho, Hatsukaichi (street stalls, address unverified) | - | ¥2,000-3,000/adult, normal menu price, approximate (the day's ¥4,200 lunch for three adults also included grilled oysters) | ~650 kcal, approximate | Grilled saltwater conger eel (anago) laid over a bowl of steamed rice and brushed with a sweet soy glaze, Miyajima's own speciality and a different fish from unagi, the freshwater eel more familiar abroad - anago is lighter and milder in both flavour and texture. It is served hot and fully cooked, not raw, usually in a lacquered box or bowl. The flaky, boneless fish is easy to break into small pieces for a toddler once cooled. |
| Hiroshima-style okonomiyaki | 25 | Okonomimura, 5-13 Shintenchi, Naka-ku, Hiroshima 730-0034 | http://www.okonomimura.jp/ | ¥900/adult, approximate (the day's dinner cost ¥2,700 for three adults) | ~750 kcal, approximate | A savoury griddle dish built up in distinct layers rather than mixed together: a thin, crepe-like batter base goes down first, then a tall mound of shredded cabbage, then pork, then a nest of fried yakisoba noodles pressed in as its own layer, then a fried egg on top, the whole stack finished with a thick, slightly sweet brown sauce. Okonomimura is a purpose-built building of stacked okonomiyaki counters, three floors of small individual stalls each with its own griddle, so the dish is cooked to order right in front of diners rather than in a back kitchen. It is eaten hot straight off the griddle with a small metal spatula, contains egg and wheat, and the layered cabbage and noodles cook down soft enough to cut into small toddler bites. |
| Osaka-style okonomiyaki | 28 | Dotonbori food stalls, Chuo-ku, Osaka (individual stall addresses unverified) | - | ¥900-1,200/adult, normal menu price, approximate (the day's ¥3,200 dinner for three adults also included takoyaki to share) | ~450 kcal, approximate, for a shared portion | Osaka's version of the same "grilled as you like it" pancake, but here the shredded cabbage, batter, egg and chosen fillings are all mixed together in a bowl first and poured onto the griddle as one thick pancake, with no separate noodle layer the way Hiroshima's version has. It is flipped once, finished with the same sweet-savoury brown sauce plus a drizzle of Kewpie mayonnaise and a scatter of dried bonito flakes on top, then cut into wedges and eaten hot at the table. It contains egg and wheat, plus whatever filling is chosen (often pork or seafood), and the soft cooked pancake is easy to cut small for a toddler once cooled. |
| Kushikatsu | 29 | Kushikatsu Daruma, 1-6-8 Dotonbori, Chuo-ku, Osaka 542-0071 | https://www.kushikatu-daruma.com/ | ¥1,770/adult, approximate (the day's dinner cost ¥5,300 for three adults) | ~750 kcal, approximate, for a full skewer set | Bite-sized pieces of meat, seafood and vegetables, here pork, chicken, lotus root, quail egg and cheese, individually skewered, breaded and deep-fried, then dipped once into a shared sauce pot on the counter. The house rule is strict and enforced: no double-dipping, a skewer already bitten never goes back into the communal sauce, so extra sauce is scooped up with the free shredded cabbage on the counter instead. It is served hot and crunchy, eaten by hand straight off the skewer, and contains wheat and egg; the plain fried pieces are fine for a toddler once the sauce is wiped off and the skewer removed. |
