# TOPIC 1 - FESTIVALS AND THE "ANIME SCENE" (7 Oct – 5 Nov 2026)

## The straight answer on fireworks: no

**There is no hanabi taikai that lines up with where this party actually is, on the
date they are actually there.** Every real 2026 autumn fireworks event near the
five bases falls either before arrival, after departure, or while the party is
in a different city entirely. This is a season problem, not a planning miss:
Japan's hanabi taikai season is essentially July–August, when 200+ major
displays run nationwide; only a handful of small autumn/winter shows exist at
all, and none of them sit inside this trip's footprint.

| Event | 2026 date | Where | Why it doesn't work |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tamagawa Fireworks Festival (Setagaya) | Sat 3 Oct [VERIFIED, tokyocheapo.com] | Tokyo, Tama River | Party arrives Narita 7 Oct - this is 4 days before arrival |
| Naniwa Yodogawa Fireworks Festival (38th, moved to Oct in 2026 for the Osaka Expo) | Sat 17 Oct [VERIFIED, kobebrighten.com, osakascene.com] | Osaka, Yodogawa riverbed | Party isn't in Osaka until 3–5 Nov, more than 2 weeks later; on 17 Oct they're in Kawaguchiko |
| Minato Hanabi (Kobe, 5 nights) | Mon 19 – Fri 23 Oct, 18:30-18:50 nightly [VERIFIED, kobebrighten.com] | Kobe, Meriken Park | The closest real near-miss - see below |
| Itsukushima Underwater Fireworks | - | Miyajima | **Cancelled for 2026** [per japaneventcalendar.com] |
| Tokyo Bay Grand Fireworks Festival | Sat 24 Oct, 17:30-19:00 [VERIFIED, tokyocheapo.com/realjapanguide] | Tokyo Bay, Harumi Pier | Party leaves Tokyo for Kawaguchiko on 16 Oct - this is 8 days after they're gone |
| Tsuchiura Fireworks Festival (one of Japan's "big three" competitions) | Sat 7 Nov [ESTIMATE, per search] | Ibaraki, near Tokyo | Trip ends 5 Nov - 2 days after departure |
| Odaiba Rainbow Fireworks | 5, 12, 19, 26 Dec [VERIFIED, tokyocheapo.com] | Odaiba, Tokyo | December, a month after departure |

**The one genuine near-miss: Kobe Minato Hanabi, 19–23 Oct.** During those five
nights the party is based in Kyoto (Days 15–17, Toyoko Inn Shijo-Omiya). Kobe is
reachable - about 55 min on the JR Special Rapid to Kobe-Sannomiya, then a
10-min hop to Motomachi and an 8-min walk to Meriken Park - but the show itself
is only 20 minutes (18:30-18:50), the JR Kansai-Hiroshima Area Pass isn't active
yet (it starts Day 24), so the whole trip is paid walk-up fare, and a realistic
round trip from the Shijo-Omiya hotel runs 2.5-3 hours door to door. That means
leaving around 16:30-17:00 and getting back after 20:00 - well past this
itinerary's consistent ~19:00-19:30 infant bedtime, on top of a full day already
run at the Kyoto pace. It is priced below as a rejected Tier 3 option, honestly,
because it is the only real fireworks display this trip's calendar and geography
can reach at all, but it does not fit two children under two.

**Bottom line for the traveller:** the trip's own window was chosen for weather,
cost, and typhoon-avoidance reasons (see 00-overview.md), and that window happens
to sit in the calendar gap between hanabi taikai season (July-Aug, with a
handful of stragglers into mid-October) and winter illumination season
(Nov 20 onward). Nothing in this pass changes that; it is exactly the tradeoff
the brief anticipated ("fine with none, but want it if possible").

## Robes: yukata and kimono in the street

**Jidai Matsuri, Day 16 (Thu 22 Oct 2026), already in the plan, is genuinely the
best answer available.** Date confirmed [VERIFIED, japan-guide.com,
selfguidejapan.com]: ~2,000 participants in accurate historical costume spanning
Kyoto's entire 1,100+ years as capital, processing from the Imperial Palace to
Heian Jingu. Nothing else in this window comes close to that concentration of
people in traditional dress, and the itinerary already has it costed at ¥0
(free street viewing from inside Kyoto Gyoen). No change proposed here.

**Shichi-Go-San lands after departure - verified.** The formal date is Sunday
15 November 2026 [VERIFIED, multiple sources incl. web-japan.org]. The trip ends
5 November, ten days earlier. **However**, this is softer than a hard miss:
families do not wait for the exact date. Multiple sources confirm visits happen
"on a convenient day between mid-October and late November," concentrated on
weekends [ESTIMATE, general consensus across sources]. That means at any big
shrine this itinerary already visits on a Sunday or Saturday - Meiji Jingu on
Day 5 (Sun 11 Oct), Fushimi Inari on Day 15 (Wed, less likely), Kasuga Taisha on
Day 20 (Mon, less likely), Itsukushima Shrine on Day 25 (Sat 31 Oct) - there is a
real, if unguaranteed, chance of seeing a few families with 3- and 7-year-old
girls in kimono or 3- and 5-year-old boys in hakama getting shrine blessings
weeks early. This is not schedulable and shouldn't be advertised as a booked
experience, but it's honest upside worth naming, concentrated most on the two
Sundays/Saturdays above.

**Kimono rental (Day 18, already in the plan) remains the right way to
participate rather than watch** - the brief's own framing is correct. Worth
adding one honest note: October is actually a comfortable month for it,
better than summer's heat/humidity for walking in full kimono and geta sandals.

**Gion Odori (Nov 1-10 2026, Gion Higashi geiko/maiko dance) - rejected, date
mismatch.** [VERIFIED, japancheapo.com] The 67th Gion Odori runs 1-10 November
at Gion Kaikan, two daily shows (13:30, 16:00). The Kyoto stay ends Day 23
(Thu 29 Oct) - the show starts two days after the party leaves Kyoto. A real
near-miss, not fixable without moving the Kyoto stay later, which would eat
into the pre-foliage-premium timing 00-overview.md deliberately built around.

## Yatai: the food-stall answer

The brief is right that yatai cluster at festivals and temple market days, not
continuously. Two real, exact-dated monthly temple markets land inside days the
itinerary already spends in the right neighbourhood - this is the strongest
finding in this whole pass.

**Kobo-ichi at To-ji - 21st of every month - is Day 15 (Wed 21 Oct 2026).**
[VERIFIED: monthly market on the 21st, commemorating Kobo Daishi's death
anniversary - discoverkyoto.com, japan-experience.com, kyotostation.com] Hours
roughly 5:00-16:00, stalls mostly open by 8:00-9:00. Reported stall counts vary
by source (several hundred to ~1,200), covering antiques, kimono, plants, and
genuine street food. To-ji sits about a 15-minute walk south of Kyoto Station -
and Day 15's existing schedule already routes through Kyoto Station twice by
bus (out at 08:35, back at 11:50) on the way to and from Fushimi Inari. This is
close to a free add: fold a stop at To-ji into the return leg instead of going
straight back to the hotel, and eat the day's lunch from the market's own food
stalls instead of the separately-priced inari-zushi shop.

**Tenjin-ichi at Kitano Tenmangu - 25th of every month - is Day 19 (Sun 25 Oct
2026).** [VERIFIED: monthly market on the 25th, commemorating Sugawara no
Michizane's death anniversary - mykyotomachiya.com, basekyoto.com] Hours
6:00-16:00, reportedly up to ~1,000 stalls in good months, explicitly noted for
food stalls: takoyaki, okonomiyaki, warabimochi, Uji tea, grilled bamboo shoots.
Kitano Tenmangu sits roughly 2km / a short bus ride from Kinkaku-ji, which Day
19 already visits (11:35-12:15) right before the day's stop at Ryoan-ji.

Both markets are open-air shrine-precinct events on wide paved/gravel grounds -
genuinely different crowd dynamics from Kurama Fire Festival's single narrow
street (which the itinerary correctly already excludes on Day 16) or Jidai
Matsuri's curbside crush. Browsing is self-paced, exits are everywhere, and
neither requires committing to a fixed viewing spot. Stroller-workable in
principle, though both are genuinely crowded market floors (uneven footing in
places, narrow gaps between stalls) - the carrier is the safer call for the
densest aisles, same logic the itinerary already applies to Nishiki Market.

**Tori-no-Ichi (Otori shrine rooster-day markets) - verified, both 2026 dates
fall after departure.** [VERIFIED: 7 and 19 November 2026 - en.japantravel.com,
livejapan.com] Famous for kumade (decorated bamboo good-luck rakes) and dense
yatai food rows around Otori Shrine (Iriya/Minowa, not Asakusa itself). The
trip ends 5 November - the first 2026 date is two days after departure. No fix
available; this one is a clean miss, not a near-miss.

**Existing yatai coverage, for context (no change needed):** Nishiki Market
(Day 15), Ameyoko (Day 3 lunch - takoyaki, gyoza, yakitori already), Toyosu
Market outer stalls (Day 8 breakfast), Yanaka Ginza (Day 6), Nankinmachi (Day
28), Kuromon Ichiba (Day 29) already give the trip a lot of stall-grazing.
What was missing specifically was a stall cluster tied to an actual matsuri/
shrine-festival date rather than a permanent market - which is exactly what
Kobo-ichi and Tenjin-ichi supply.
