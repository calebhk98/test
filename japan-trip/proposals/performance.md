# Proposal: Live performance - theatre, music, light shows

## The honest bottom line, up front

**Kabuki works, cheaply, but only as an adults-only peel-off.** Kabukiza's single-act
ticket (hitomakumi) is the real answer to "how is Japanese theatre different from
American theatre" - it costs almost nothing, needs no advance booking, and the English
subtitle tablet solves the language problem. But the evidence on whether small children
are even allowed into the single-act section is inconsistent (see below), and
regardless of the official rule, a hushed single-scene auditorium with no aisle access
mid-act is a bad match for a 12-month-old and a 20-month-old. **The honest plan is a
split: 1-2 adults go alone for 30-90 minutes while the third adult and both infants do
something else nearby or back at the hotel.** That is not a failure of the idea, it is
the correct shape for it.

**Bunraku in Osaka is the better whole-party fit, and it lines up with the itinerary
almost too well to pass up.** The National Bunraku Theatre's Kinshu (Autumn) run goes
Oct 31 - Nov 23, 2026 [VERIFIED, ntj.jac.go.jp], which covers the trip's entire Osaka
stay (Days 27-29, Nov 2-4). The theatre sits one minute from Kintetsu Nipponbashi
Station [VERIFIED, National Bunraku Theatre listing], which is the same block as
Kuromon Ichiba Market, already Day 29's lunch stop. Metre-tall puppets worked in full
view by three visible puppeteers each is legible without any Japanese, in a way spoken
kabuki dialogue is not, and the National Theatre's own FAQ does not set a minimum age -
it only says a fussy child may be asked to step out, with lobby monitors as a fallback
[VERIFIED, ntj.jac.go.jp/qa/]. The real constraint is cost and duration, not an entry
ban: ¥6,500/adult per part, uniform seating [VERIFIED, October 2026 press listing], and
each part runs 2h20-3h15 - too long to bet on two toddlers sitting through it.

**Noh/kyogen is real, beginner-oriented, and priced out of this budget.** Kyoto's
IMAGINE NOH series (Oct 14, 22, 28, Nov 5, 2026) is a 90-minute kyogen-plus-noh excerpt
built specifically for first-timers with English introductions and a post-show Q&A -
exactly the "shorter and more physical" shape the traveller wants - but it is
¥23,000/adult [VERIFIED, imagine-noh.com]. One adult alone would burn the entire
remaining discretionary budget. Rejected on cost alone, not on fit.

**Music and light stay minor, as asked, and mostly resolve to "already in the plan."**
The gagaku court music that already accompanies the two ceremonial mikoshi in the Day
16 Jidai Matsuri procession answers the "shrine music" wish for free, with no schedule
change. Every other Tokyo/Kobe illumination worth naming opens after Nov 13 or in
December [VERIFIED, multiple 2026-27 illumination listings] - after this trip departs
on Nov 5 - so Midosuji (already Day 28) remains the only illumination that is actually
open during the trip. Big-name J-pop and idol concerts sell out to fan-club lottery
entrants months ahead and are not realistically bookable by a visitor with a fixed
mid-trip date; the walk-in "idol experience" tours that do exist are late-evening,
standing-room, adult-oriented venues, a poor match for this party. Both rejected
honestly rather than padded in.
