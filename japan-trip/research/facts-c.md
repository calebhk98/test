## GAP 1 — Kawaguchiko → Kyoto routing (highest priority)

**Baseline to beat:** direct Kawaguchiko–Kyoto/Nagoya-area highway bus, reportedly ~11h — not usable with two infants. Four alternatives costed below; adult fares are per-person, one-way.

### (a) Bus to Mishima + Tokaido Shinkansen — RECOMMENDED

- **Kawaguchiko/Fujikyu Highland → Mishima Station**: "Tokkyu Mishima・Kawaguchiko Liner" (特急 三島・河口湖ライナー), jointly operated by **Fujikyu City Bus / Fujikyu Yamanashi Bus** [VERIFIED, exists — bus.fujikyu.co.jp/highway/detail/id/29, fujikyucitybus.com]. **All seats reserved** (no standing/lap-only travel; book at Mishima, Fujiyama Highland, or Kawaguchiko ticket windows or online).
  - Fare: **¥2,700 window / ¥2,500 online (web-discount)** per adult [VERIFIED, bus.fujikyu.co.jp/highway/detail/id/29].
  - Duration: **~80–90 min** (site) / "about 1h30m" per FAQ, can run longer in traffic [VERIFIED, same source].
  - Frequency: hourly-pattern departures confirmed from the Mishima side at **07:20, 08:20, 09:20 … 17:20** (11 daily departures) [VERIFIED, NAVITIME timetable page for this line]. Kawaguchiko-side departures follow the same roughly-hourly pattern (exact Kawaguchiko-origin times not independently pulled this session — treat count as **[ESTIMATE ~10–11/day]** by symmetry with the Mishima side).
- **Mishima → Kyoto**, Tokaido Shinkansen, unreserved (自由席):
  - Hikari: **~1h50m**, unreserved fare **¥10,780**; reserved **¥11,310** [VERIFIED, ekitan.com shinkansen fare/time pages].
  - Kodama: **~2h40m**, same unreserved fare band (Kodama and Hikari share the same base fare+basic-charge structure on this route; only Nozomi carries a surcharge) [VERIFIED duration; fare parity is standard JR fare structure, ESTIMATE on the exact Kodama figure since only the Hikari number was pulled directly].
- **Totals (adult, one-way):** fare **¥2,500–2,700 (bus) + ¥10,780 (shinkansen, Hikari, unreserved) ≈ ¥13,280–13,480**. Duration door-to-door: **~80–90 min bus + connection buffer at Mishima (allow 20–30 min) + ~1h50m Hikari ≈ 3h40m–4h10m total**, well under half the direct 11h bus. **1 transfer** (at Mishima Station, cross-platform-ish — bus terminal to JR station, a short walk).

- **Totals (if it ran):** fare **≈ ¥4,500 + ~¥5,600 ≈ ¥10,100** (cheapest of the four); duration **≈ 4h20m bus + connection + 35–50 min shinkansen ≈ 5.1–5.5h total**, **1 transfer**.
- Verdict: cheapest fare on paper but (i) **not confirmed to run on a Tuesday**, (ii) a single 4h20m bus leg is a *harder* stretch for two infants than (a)'s much shorter 80–90 min bus + a shinkansen leg where they can move around, feed, and use the aisle/vestibule. **Not recommended** even setting aside the schedule problem.

### Recommendation

**Route (a): Kawaguchiko → Mishima by highway bus, then Mishima → Kyoto by unreserved Hikari.** It has the shortest total door-to-door time (~3h40m–4h10m vs 5+ hours for every alternative), only one transfer, the shortest single continuous ride for the infants (80–90 min reserved-seat bus, guaranteed seats), and lets you buy Green/wider unreserved shinkansen seating on the day rather than being locked into a 3x/day (c) or weekend-only (d) schedule. Cost (~¥13,300–13,500/adult) is essentially tied with the cheaper-but-worse options.

**Stroller/luggage handling:**
- The Mishima liner bus has an **under-floor luggage hold** typical of Japanese highway coaches — check large suitcases there; keep the stroller foldable and board with it if you can fold it in the aisle, or check it in the hold with the driver's help.
- On the Tokaido Shinkansen, the **oversized-baggage rule** (JR Central, applies to Tokaido/Sanyo/Kyushu/Nishi-Kyushu Shinkansen) requires reserving an "Oversized Baggage Space Seat" **only** for items whose three dimensions sum to **160cm–250cm**; a **¥1,000 fee applies if such an item is brought aboard without a reservation** [VERIFIED, railway.jr-central.co.jp/oversized-baggage/]. **Strollers, wheelchairs, and sports/musical-instrument cases are explicitly exempted from this reservation requirement regardless of their size** [VERIFIED, same source via search aggregation] — so your stroller(s) travel free with no advance booking needed, though on an unreserved car you may need to fold/stow it near your seats or in the end-of-car luggage racks yourself.

## GAP 2 — Shinjuku → Kawaguchiko highway bus, infant seat policy

- **Route/operator:** Keio Dentetsu Bus and Fujikyu jointly (共同運行, "京王バス・フジエクスプレス"/富士急バス group) [VERIFIED, bus.fujikyu.co.jp/highway/fujisan/shinjuku-kawaguchiko/]. Departs Busta Shinjuku.
- **Fare (adult, one-way):** **¥2,000 online/web-discount** up to **¥2,200–¥2,650 window**, depending on exact drop-off point on the route; Kawaguchiko Station itself is at the **¥2,000 (web) / ¥2,200 (window)** end of that band [VERIFIED, bus.fujikyu.co.jp + bushikaku.net aggregator].
- **Duration:** **~1h45m** (site states ~1h35–45m depending on source), varies with traffic [VERIFIED, bus.fujikyu.co.jp; bushikaku.net].
- **Frequency:** confirmed to run multiple daily departures on a well-served, high-demand tourist route, but an exact daily departure count could not be pulled from a blocked timetable page this session — **[ESTIMATE: roughly 15–20+ round trips/day in high season, since it is one of the most heavily used Tokyo–Fuji routes]**. Reserve in advance during your Oct 7–16 Tokyo stay/Oct 17 travel day, as Sports Day (Oct 12) creates a 3-day-weekend demand bump per facts-a.md.
- **Child/infant seat policy — operator's own wording** [VERIFIED, fujikyucitybus.com/highwaybus/qa.html, and consistent with a second Fujikyu-group FAQ page found]:
  - **"幼児が座席を専用する場合は運賃が必要です"** — "A fare is required if an infant/toddler (yoji, age 1–6) occupies a seat of their own."
  - **Lap-riding is discouraged for safety**: **"高速バスでは、安全上の観点から、膝の上のご乗車はおやめいただくようお勧めしております"** — the company recommends against carrying a child on your lap on the highway bus.
  - **Car seats cannot be used/fitted**: **"当社ではチャイルドシートの使用は出来ません"**.
  - **Capacity rule**: bus seating capacity is legally fixed: **"乗車定員に含まれることから、乳幼児を同伴されるお客さまで、ご人数分の座席指定席をお持ちでないお客さまは、ご乗車をお断りさせていただくことがございます"** — a party traveling with infants/toddlers **without a reserved seat for every person** (infants included) can be **refused boarding** if the bus is full.
  - **Age bands** (group-wide Fujikyu convention): adult (otona) = 12+ (junior-high and up); child (kodomo) = 6–<12 (elementary school); infant/toddler (yoji) = 1–<6 (pre-school); baby (nyuji) = under 1. **Child (kodomo) fare = half the adult fare** (rounded up to the nearest ¥10); when a yoji/nyuji occupies their own seat, the **child (half-price) fare structure is the applicable charge** by pattern across Fujikyu-group routes [VERIFIED wording + general fare band; the explicit "yoji pays exactly half" statement is by pattern/cross-reference rather than a single quoted sentence — **[ESTIMATE on the exact fare tier applied to a seated yoji, though "must have a paid/reserved seat" is directly VERIFIED]**].
- **Practical read for your party:** with a ~20-month-old (yoji) and a ~12-month-old (nyuji), the operator's own guidance is that lap-riding is discouraged and a seat-less infant risks the whole party being denied boarding if the bus is full — **book a paid seat for the 20-month-old** (functionally can sit independently) and strongly consider booking one for the 12-month-old too, both for safety (no car seats allowed) and to guarantee boarding.
- **Likely total cost, 3 adults + 1 lap infant (12mo, free/no seat) + 1 seated toddler (20mo, child/half fare):**
  - 3 adults × ¥2,000 (web) = ¥6,000
  - 1 toddler seat × ~¥1,000–1,100 (half of ¥2,000–2,200, rounded up) = **~¥1,000–1,100**
  - 12-month-old: free if genuinely lap-held, **but this contradicts the operator's own safety guidance** and risks a boarding refusal if seats are tight — **budget a 5th paid seat (~¥1,000–1,100) as the safer real-world plan**.
  - **Realistic total: ¥7,000–8,100 one-way** for the party (¥6,000 if you gamble on one true lap infant and get seats reserved for the rest; ¥8,100 if you buy all 5 seats).

## GAP 6 — Kyoto, Oct 22, 2026

### Jidai Matsuri (時代祭)

- **Date confirmed for 2026: Oct 22** (rescheduled to Oct 23 only if it rains) [VERIFIED, multiple Japanese sources via search].
- **Route/timing:** Procession (神幸列/时代行列) departs **Kyoto Gyoen (Imperial Palace Park)** around **09:00** for a ceremony at the Palace's Kenreimon Gate at **10:30**, the main parade departs Kenreimon Gate at **12:00 noon**, proceeds via Karasuma-dori/Oike-dori/Kawaramachi-dori/Sanjo-dori/Jingu-michi (approx.) to **Heian Jingu Shrine**, arriving roughly **14:30** [VERIFIED, kyoto.travel official event page]. Total parade route takes about **2 hours** to walk from the Palace to Heian Jingu.
- **Scale:** ~2,000 costumed participants spanning 8 historical eras from Meiji back to the Heian period — Japan's largest historical-costume parade.
- **Paid seating:** Yes, official paid grandstand seats are sold along **Kyoto Gyoen, Oike-dori, and Jingu-michi**: **front-row general seats ¥6,500**, **2nd-row-back general seats ¥4,500**, with guided/narrated premium seats above that [VERIFIED, kyoto-city official ticket page via search].
- **Free viewing for a family with a stroller:** the parade is free to view anywhere along the public route outside the ticketed grandstand sections — the most family-workable free spots (per general Kyoto-travel guidance) are the **wide, flat, tree-lined paths inside Kyoto Gyoen itself near the parade's start**, and **Jingu-michi near Heian Jingu's giant torii** close to the finish, both of which have more open pavement/less curb congestion than the narrower Oike-dori/Kawaramachi-dori downtown stretch. Arrive at least 45–60 min early for a curbside spot with stroller space; there is no fee to simply stand along the route.

### Kurama Fire Festival (鞍馬の火祭) — same night, honest assessment

- **Date:** also **Oct 22, 2026**, and it is explicitly **NOT postponed for rain** ("雨天決行、順延なし") — it happens rain or shine on that exact date, unlike Jidai Matsuri [VERIFIED, search-aggregated Japanese sources for the 2026 event].
- **Timeline:** torch-lighting rituals from around **18:00**, the climax (shimenawa-giri / mikoshi-togyo, when massive burning pine torches are carried through the narrow village) around **22:00**, procession concludes at the Otabisho around **23:00** — this is a **very late-finishing event**, well past normal infant bedtime and into last-train risk territory.
- **Access constraint:** the only realistic way in/out is the **Eizan Railway Kurama Line**, a small single-track mountain line with genuinely limited capacity; **passenger restrictions/entry limits ("乗車制限・入場規制") are routinely imposed** when crowding peaks [VERIFIED, search-aggregated]. **All car parking near Kurama is closed for the event** — there is no driving fallback.
- **Assessment for a 12-month-old and a 20-month-old: not advisable.** Reasons: (1) the event runs into an 11pm+ finish with a single narrow mountain rail line as the only exit, creating a real risk of being stuck in a slow-moving, tightly packed crowd well past a reasonable infant bedtime; (2) it is a genuine **fire festival** — large burning pine-bough torches (some over 100kg, carried by hand) move through a narrow street lined with spectators, with real ember/heat/smoke exposure at close range, which is a materially different risk profile from a normal costume parade; (3) reported crowd-control measures (entry limits, one-way flows) mean a stroller is a serious liability and you may not be able to leave quickly if an infant needs a diaper change, feed, or is overwhelmed by noise/smoke/heat; (4) unlike Jidai Matsuri (daytime, wide boulevards, optional paid seating with clear sightlines), Kurama's viewing is standing-only in a **narrow single street** with the crowd pressed close to open flame. **Recommendation: see Jidai Matsuri in the afternoon and skip Kurama that night with this age group** — if any adult in the party is determined to see it, treat it as a solo/adults-only side trip after the infants are settled for the night elsewhere, not a family outing.

## GAP 7 — Miyajima

- **Ferry fare (2026):** both operators (**JR West Miyajima Ferry** and **Miyajima Matsudai Kisen**) charge identical passenger fares: **¥200 one-way / ¥400 round-trip, adult** [VERIFIED, monomoney-living.com 2026 guide]. IC cards (ICOCA, Suica, PASMO, etc.) accepted.
- **Miyajima Visitor Tax:** **¥100 per person**, charged **once, on the outbound (to-island) trip only**, introduced October 2023 [VERIFIED, monomoney-living.com; corroborated by city of Hatsukaichi's own tax page found in search]. **Round-trip total with tax: ¥500/adult** (¥400 ferry + ¥100 tax, tax charged once).
- **Visitor-tax exemptions:** **children of pre-school age (未就学児, i.e. under ~6)** are exempt — both your infants (12mo, 20mo) qualify and pay **¥0 tax** [VERIFIED]. Also exempt: school-trip students/staff (with certificate, university groups excluded) and holders of a disability certificate — **note their accompanying caregiver still must pay the tax** [VERIFIED, same source].
- **Itsukushima Shrine admission:** **Adult ¥300**; high-schoolers ¥200; elementary/junior-high ¥100; **pre-school-age children (未就学児) are free** [VERIFIED, multiple corroborating sources via search] — so both your infants enter free, only the 3 adults pay ¥300 each (¥900 total). A combined ticket with the shrine's treasure hall (宝物館) is ¥500 adult / ¥150 elementary-junior-high, same free-under-6 policy implied.
- **Stroller navigability:**
  - Ferry itself: **stroller can be wheeled straight on and stay with you**; the only inaccessible part is the **upper deck (stairs only)**, used for viewing the floating torii gate from above — skip that if you need to stay with the stroller, or take turns [VERIFIED, search-aggregated firsthand-report sources].
  - **JR Miyajimaguchi Station to the ferry pier**: the connecting underpass has an **elevator**, so it's stroller-accessible without needing to lift the stroller on stairs [VERIFIED, same source set].
  - **On Miyajima island itself**: the main approach — **Omotesando shopping arcade** — is wide, mostly paved/covered, and stroller-friendly; general island streets near the shrine and waterfront are described as mostly paved and manageable [VERIFIED, multiple firsthand-report blogs].
  - **Itsukushima Shrine's own boardwalk/corridor** is a raised wooden walkway — generally passable but check current tide/maintenance conditions, as some threshold boards can be uneven for wheels; not independently confirmed step-by-step this session.
  - **Mount Misen (弥山)** — the mountain/hiking trails behind the shrine — is **explicitly NOT stroller-friendly on any of its route options** [VERIFIED, search-aggregated] — skip Misen with infants, or go via the ropeway and carry the child instead of pushing.
  - **A local caution specific to Miyajima**: the free-roaming sacred deer leave droppings on the paved paths, which can end up on stroller wheels — several local guides suggest **using a rental stroller on-island** (available at multiple points including Miyajima Aquarium) rather than your own, for this reason [VERIFIED, search-aggregated].
