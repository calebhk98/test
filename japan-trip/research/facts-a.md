## 4. Infant / Child Fare & Admission Rules

**JR Group rail (shinkansen + local) [VERIFIED, multiple corroborating sources incl. JRailPass, japan-experience.com, jrpass.com, JR Central official, accessed 2026-09-04]:**
- Infants under 1 year old: always free, no limit tied to seat use (unless occupying their own reserved seat).
- "Young children" (満1歳〜6歳未満, i.e., ~age 1 up to before elementary school, roughly ages 1–5): travel **free** when accompanied by a fare-paying adult or child — **but only up to 2 free young children per fare-paying passenger**; a 3rd young child (or a young child who is given/needs their own reserved seat) is charged the **child fare**.
- Children aged 6–11 (elementary school age): pay **half the adult fare** for base fare, limited-express charge, and reserved-seat charge; Green Car surcharges are full adult price.
- **Direct relevance to your party:** 3 adults + 2 infants (ages ~1 and ~1.7) = both children qualify as "young children," and with 3 fare-paying adults in the party you have ample allowance (up to 6 free young children total) — **both infants ride JR trains completely free**, including shinkansen, as long as they don't each need their own reserved seat. If you want each infant to have a guaranteed reserved seat/seat of their own (recommended for a 20-month-old who won't lap-sit for 4 hours), that specific child would then be charged child fare for that reservation.

**Tokyo Metro / Toei Subway:** children under 6 ride free with a paying adult [VERIFIED, japan-experience.com/aaa.com, accessed 2026-09-04]. Ages 6–11 use a child ticket (half fare, typically rounded).

**Osaka Metro:** children under 6 ride free with a paying adult; the "up to 2 free young children per adult" rule mirrors JR practice [VERIFIED general policy, though the specific "2 per adult" cap was drawn from a general Japan-transit source rather than an Osaka Metro-specific page — treat the cap number as ESTIMATE, the "under 6 free" core rule as VERIFIED]. Ages 6–11 pay half fare on weekdays; some sources note free travel for that age band on weekends/holidays [ESTIMATE — worth confirming on Osaka Metro's own site before relying on it].

**Kyoto city bus / subway:** children under 6 ride free with a paying adult guardian; if accompanied by a non-guardian adult, the free allowance is capped at the first 2 toddlers, with a 3rd+ charged the child fee [VERIFIED, Kyoto City official transport guide (city.kyoto.lg.jp), accessed 2026-09-04]. Ages 6–12 pay a reduced child fare (half adult, rounded up to ¥10).

**Highway buses (Fujikyu / Willer / JR Bus Kanto) [VERIFIED policy shape, Willer FAQ page, accessed 2026-09-04]:**
- Infants **who cannot sit independently**: ride **free** on a parent's lap (no separate seat).
- Children **under 6 who can sit independently**: generally **must have their own paid seat** (child fare) for safety/seatbelt reasons, even though under 6 — this differs from the rail/subway "free under 6" norm.
- Age 6+: always requires own seat at child fare, seatbelt required.
- **Practical read for your trip:** your ~12-month-old will very likely qualify as lap-infant-free; your ~20-month-old is a toss-up — if she can sit independently/is asked to use a seatbelt, Willer/Fujikyu will likely require a paid child seat for her on the Shinjuku–Kawaguchiko and any Kawaguchiko–Kansai legs. Budget for one child fare on highway buses as a conservative assumption.

**Museum/temple/garden admission for under-6s [VERIFIED pattern from multiple official and aggregator sources, accessed 2026-09-04]:** the overwhelming norm across Japanese museums, temples, and gardens is **free admission for young children**, commonly bundled into a "free under 18" or "free under high-school-age" policy at national museums (e.g., Tokyo National Museum, Kyoto National Museum outdoor areas). Some city-run museums have recently extended free admission up to age 18 as a promotional policy. **Practical assumption: budget $0 for both infants at essentially all standard museum/temple/garden admissions** — this is a genuine, verified budget win versus adult-per-head pricing assumptions.

**Domestic ferries (Miyajima ferry, JR West):** [VERIFIED, jr-miyajimaferry.co.jp via search, accessed 2026-09-04] Adult one-way ¥200; **up to 2 preschool children travel free per fare-paying adult**, and children under 12 months are free regardless. Comparable private operators (Matsudai Kisen, Setonaikaikisen) apply the same "1 free preschool child per adult, infants always free" structure. **The Miyajima Island Visitor Tax (¥100/person, charged separately on arrival) exempts preschool children** [VERIFIED, same source set].

**Strollers — practicality assessment:**
- **Elevators:** widely available at major stations, especially in Tokyo and at all shinkansen stations, but not always intuitively signed; smaller/older regional stations can lack them or require a longer walk to find the correct one [VERIFIED directional claim, japantravel.com/thetokyochapter.com, accessed 2026-09-04]. Station staff will open gates/direct you if asked. The free NAVITIME Japan Travel app can filter routes for elevator/barrier-free access.
- **Stroller rental:** available — e.g., "Babycal" (JR East-affiliated, pickup at Tokyo, Shinjuku, Ueno, Shinagawa, Ikebukuro, Maihama stations; ~¥1,500/12hr) and "StrollerTrip" (Combi-brand, can deliver to your accommodation) [VERIFIED, thetokyochapter.com, accessed 2026-09-04].
- **Recommendation from sources:** bring a **small/compact umbrella-style stroller** rather than a wide double stroller — many passageways, shop aisles, and some station elevators are narrow, and a wide double stroller will be a repeated friction point [VERIFIED directional advice, multiple parent-travel-blog sources]. With 2 infants, consider one compact single stroller + a baby carrier for the second child rather than a double stroller, especially for the Kyoto temple-heavy days (gravel paths, stairs at many temples are common and not stroller-friendly regardless of elevators).
- **Major sites requiring folded strollers:** many temples, shrines, and some museum special-exhibit areas require strollers to be folded/checked at the entrance (uneven stone paths, tatami areas, stairs) [ESTIMATE/general pattern from parent-travel-blog consensus — no single authoritative citywide rule found; verify per-site, e.g., Fushimi Inari and Kiyomizu-dera involve significant stairs/slopes that are difficult with any stroller].

**Cheapest combination for your exact route, 3 adults (both infants ride free throughout per §4) — the math:**

Your itinerary's only genuine long-distance JR shinkansen need, given the routing (Tokyo→Kawaguchiko and Kawaguchiko→Kyoto both by highway bus, not shinkansen), is the **Kyoto → Hiroshima → Osaka** leg. This changes the pass calculus significantly from the "classic Golden Route" case:

- *Option A — Point-to-point only:* Kyoto→Hiroshima (¥10,770) + Hiroshima→Shin-Osaka (¥9,710) = **¥20,480/adult** × 3 = **¥61,440 total** (~$396 at ¥155/$1), plus you'd separately pay cash/IC-card fares for any Kyoto local trains, Nara day trip, Miyajima ferry, etc.
- *Option B — JR Kansai-Hiroshima Area Pass (5-day):* **¥17,000/adult** × 3 = **¥51,000 total** (~$329 at ¥155/$1) for the *pass alone*, which already covers both required shinkansen legs (¥20,480 worth) **and** up to 5 days of unlimited regional JR travel across Kansai + Hiroshima — e.g., a Nara day trip, Kyoto↔Osaka hops, and the JR Miyajima ferry — all included at no extra cost. **This is cheaper than buying the two point-to-point tickets alone**, before even counting the extra regional trips it unlocks.
- *Option C — Nationwide 21-day JR Pass:* ¥100,000–105,000/adult × 3 = **¥300,000–315,000 total** (~$1,935–$2,032). Drastically not worth it for this itinerary: because the Tokyo↔Kawaguchiko↔Kyoto legs are done by highway bus rather than rail, you never use the pass's biggest value driver (the expensive Tokyo–Kyoto shinkansen run), so you'd be paying for ~20 unused pass-days to save on ~¥20,480/adult of actual shinkansen fare.

**Recommendation: skip the nationwide JR Pass; buy the JR Kansai-Hiroshima Area Pass (5-day, ¥17,000/adult) timed to cover your Kyoto→Hiroshima→Osaka transition window (roughly Oct 29–Nov 2), and pay cash/IC-card (Suica/ICOCA/Pasmo) for everything else** — Tokyo Metro/Toei local travel, Kyoto city buses, and the two Kawaguchiko highway-bus legs. Estimated 3-adult rail-pass total: **~¥51,000 (~$329)**, versus ¥300,000+ (~$1,935+) for a 21-day nationwide pass — a difference material to a $9,000 total budget.
