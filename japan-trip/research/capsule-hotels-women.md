# Women-only capsule/pod hotel alternatives

Verified alternative for each of the three capsule-hotel nights, for a woman in the party who wants a property with no men in the building at all, rather than a mixed property with separate men's and women's floors.

All three verified options below are genuinely women-only for the whole property (not a floor within a mixed building). Distances and travel times are estimated from public transit schedules and walking-distance figures published by the operators or by Tokyo Metro / Hankyu / Osaka Metro; they are not measured door to door.

---

## Tokyo, Day 5 night: Akihabara BAY HOTEL

- **Property**: Akihabara BAY HOTEL, part of the BAY HOTEL Group. The group's Tokyo Ariake location is also women-only, but Akihabara BAY HOTEL is the branch relevant here.
- **Address**: 44-4 Kanda-Neribeicho, Chiyoda-ku, Tokyo 101-0022
- **Official website**: https://www.bay-hotel.jp/akihabara/eng/ (Japanese site: https://www.bay-hotel.jp/akihabara/)
- **Evidence it is women-only**: The operator's own English page states directly: "We are a female only capsule hotel located in Akihabara," "Capsule hotel only for women," and, in the FAQ, "No. This hotel is for women only." The whole building is the hotel, not a floor within a mixed property.
- **Minimum age**: Elementary-school-age children and younger cannot stay at all, regardless of accompaniment. Middle-school-age guests and older may stay with a signed parental consent form (downloadable from the hotel's own site). Quote from the official FAQ: "中学生以上のお子様は親権をお持ちの保護者の同意書が必要となります。"
- **Nightly rate (2026 shoulder-season estimate)**: No date-stamped quote was found on the operator's own site (rates are only shown live in their reservation system). Aggregator listings (Jalan, Rakuten Travel) show a published range of roughly ¥4,500 to ¥18,600 per person before tax (about ¥4,950 to ¥20,500 with tax), i.e. roughly $29 to $132 at ¥155 = $1. Treat this as an estimate, not a quote for specific autumn/spring 2026 dates.
- **How to get there from Hotel Livemax Higashi-Ueno**: This is easier than expected because the base hotel's nearest station, Iriya, and Akihabara are both stops on the Tokyo Metro Hibiya Line, with no transfer needed. Walk from the hotel to Iriya Station (a few minutes), ride the Hibiya Line three stops (Iriya to Ueno to Naka-okachimachi to Akihabara, roughly 6 to 8 minutes), then walk about 2 minutes from Exit 2 of Akihabara Station to the hotel. Total door to door is roughly 20 to 25 minutes, similar to or slightly easier than reaching GLANSIT AKIHABARA (4-4-6 Sotokanda), since both properties sit in the same Akihabara station area and GLANSIT's address is a comparable walk from the same station. This is not a harder swap.
- **What it is like**: A classic no-frills capsule hotel, not a designer pod or a spa property. Capsules are a standard 206 cm x 100 cm x 100 cm, 130 beds total, with a women-only basement lounge, shower rooms (no bath/onsen), coin laundry on each floor, and wristband-key floor access for security. Amenities (room wear, towels, toothbrush, hairdryer) are provided; it is a budget-to-mid capsule stay, comparable in spirit to GLANSIT, not an upgrade to a design hotel.

### Tokyo candidates rejected
- **Centurion Ladies Hostel Ueno Park**: Would have been an excellent match, directly by Shinobazu Pond in Ueno and very close to the base hotel, but multiple independent sources (including a Tokyo lodging-history site cataloguing it as "旧・センチュリオンレディースホステル上野公園," i.e. "former") indicate it stopped operating around 2020. No current official Centurion page or live booking listing for it could be found. Do not use it; it appears closed.
- **Nadeshiko Hotel Shibuya**: Genuinely women-only and well-documented (nadeshiko-hotel.jp), but it is in Shibuya, on the far side of central Tokyo from Taito-ku, roughly 30+ minutes away by train with a transfer. Rejected for distance, not for lack of verification.

## Kyoto, Day 16 night: MAJA HOTEL KYOTO

- **Property**: MAJA HOTEL KYOTO (women-only capsule hotel). It is a standalone brand, not a women-only branch of a larger mixed chain.
- **Address**: 92 Tsuchiya-cho, Yanaginobamba-dori Rokkaku-agaru, Nakagyo-ku, Kyoto 604-8112 (the hotel occupies floors 2 through 4 of the building)
- **Official website**: https://maja-hotel.com/
- **Evidence it is women-only**: The operator's own site states: "MAJA HOTEL KYOTO, the capsule hotel for female-use only." This is a fully women-only property (all guest floors), not a men's/women's split within one building.
- **Minimum age**: Per the operator's own site: "Minors under the age of 16 are not allowed to stay in the hotel. Minors under 18 years of age staying by themselves will need the consent of their parents."
- **Nightly rate (2026 shoulder-season estimate)**: Not date-stamped on the operator's own site (payment/booking is handled through the reservation system, not listed as a flat rate on the marketing pages). A dated guest review on Jalan for a January 2026 stay reported roughly ¥6,000 to ¥7,000 per adult including tax for a no-meal plan; a November 2025 booking on the same aggregator showed roughly ¥12,000 to ¥13,000 per adult. That is a wide range (about $39 to $84 at ¥155 = $1), reflecting normal capsule-hotel dynamic pricing; treat it as an estimate bracket rather than a fixed quote.
- **How to get there from Toyoko Inn Kyoto Shijo-Omiya**: Walk to Hankyu Omiya Station, ride the Hankyu Kyoto Line one stop to Karasuma Station (about 2 to 3 minutes), then walk about 8 minutes from Exit 13 to the hotel (per the operator's own access page). Total is roughly 12 to 15 minutes. This is a bit longer than reaching GLANSIT KYOTO KAWARAMACHI, which is one stop further down the same Hankyu line at Kawaramachi and a short walk from that station (roughly 8 to 10 minutes total) - so this swap is somewhat harder to reach, by perhaps 3 to 5 minutes, but stays on the same train line with no transfer, and is well within a normal evening walk.
- **What it is like**: A modern, design-forward pod hotel, not a classic bare-bones capsule and not a spa/onsen property. It was designed by Finnish industrial designer Harri Koskinen with triangular-roofed wooden capsule units, Marimekko textiles, Airweave mattresses, dimmable lighting, a guest lounge, and an on-site Café Aalto (a Finnish-design cafe) for breakfast. Two capsule tiers are offered: smaller "sleep-in" huts (2 sq m, 40 of them) and slightly larger "walk-in" huts (3 sq m, 20 of them).

### Kyoto candidates rejected
- **THE POCKET HOTEL Kyoto Shijo-Karasuma** and **Anshin Oyado Premier Kyoto Shijo-Karasuma**: Both have a dedicated women's floor (with card-key access), but in each case it is a floor within a mixed-gender building, which is exactly the arrangement the existing GLANSIT/First Cabin picks already provide. They do not qualify as "no men in the building at all" and were rejected on that basis, not for being unverifiable.
- No Kyoto branch of Nadeshiko Hotel or of the BAY HOTEL Group was found; those chains do not currently operate in Kyoto.

## Osaka, Day 27 night: a-STYLE Shinsaibashi

- **Property**: a-STYLE Shinsaibashi (完全女性限定ラグジュアリーカプセル, "completely women-only luxury capsule"), operated by ACN Co., Ltd., on the 8th floor of the ACN Shinsaibashi Building. This is a standalone brand, not a women-only branch of First Cabin, 9h, or another mixed chain.
- **Address**: 4-12-10 Minamisenba, Chuo-ku, Osaka 542-0081 (8F, ACN Shinsaibashi Building)
- **Website status - important caveat**: The operator's own dedicated site (astyle-shinsaibashi.jp) no longer resolves to hotel content; it now serves unrelated content, indicating the domain has lapsed. **No live operator-owned website could be verified for this property.** The address above is corroborated by ACN's own opening press release (distributed via Dreamnews, a press-release wire, and quoting ACN directly) and independently by the trade press (Traicy) and travel-agency listing pages (JTB, Kinki Nippon Tourist), which agree on the address and floor. The property still appears on multiple booking platforms with 2026 dates (Trip.com, Agoda), suggesting it is still operating, but this could not be confirmed on the operator's own current site. Treat the "still open" status as reasonably likely but not fully verified to the same standard as the Tokyo and Kyoto picks.
- **Evidence it is women-only**: ACN's own 2016 opening press release states it is "関西初！完全女性専用カプセルホテル" ("Kansai's first completely women-only capsule hotel"), staffed entirely by women. Every floor of the property is women-only; it is not a mixed building with a women's section.
- **Minimum age**: Unverified. No age policy was found for this property on any source, official or aggregator.
- **Nightly rate (2026 shoulder-season estimate)**: No date-stamped operator quote available (site is down). Secondary sources describe rates as roughly up to about ¥5,000 per person for a basic capsule (about $32 at ¥155 = $1), but this is not date-stamped and should be treated as a rough floor rather than a reliable 2026 shoulder-season figure.
- **How to get there from Toyoko Inn Osaka Namba**: Ride the Osaka Metro Midosuji Line one stop from Namba to Shinsaibashi (about 2 to 3 minutes), then walk about 3 minutes to the hotel. Total is roughly 10 to 12 minutes including the walk to the platform inside Namba Station. This is harder to reach than First Cabin Midosuji Namba, which sits inside the Namba Midosuji Building a short walk from the Namba base hotel with no train ride at all - so this swap trades a same-station stay for one subway stop plus a short walk.
- **What it is like**: Billed as a "luxury capsule" rather than a classic bare capsule or a spa property - amenities described in press coverage include upgraded in-capsule fittings, POLA-brand skincare amenities, hair straighteners and facial steamers in a shared powder area, and hands-free/luggage-forwarding service. It reads as a step up in finish from a basic capsule hotel, though not to the level of a designer property like MAJA in Kyoto.

### Osaka candidates rejected
- **Grand Sauna Shinsaibashi**: Has a women-only floor and women-only sauna/bath access, but the building itself is mixed (men occupy other floors). Rejected because it is the same floors-within-one-roof arrangement as the existing GLANSIT/First Cabin picks.
- **Cabin & Capsule Hotel J-SHIP Osaka Namba**, **Capsule Hotel Asahi Plaza Shinsaibashi**: Both widely recommended for women, but both are mixed buildings with separate men's/women's floors, not wholly women-only properties. Rejected on the same grounds.
- No Osaka branch of Nadeshiko Hotel, the BAY HOTEL Group, or MAJA was found; those chains do not currently operate in Osaka. No women-only branch of First Cabin or 9h (Nine Hours) was found anywhere; First Cabin's "women's areas" are floors/zones within mixed properties, and 9h's dedicated women's branches (Woman Shinjuku, Woman Kanda) are both in Tokyo, far from Namba, and irrelevant to Osaka.
