# Capsule hotel options for the solo-adult night, one per city

Three properties, one per base city, each confirmed on the operator's own website to
accept both men and women on physically separate, security-gated floors, and each
confirmed to bar children aged 12 and under (so both infants are barred, which is the
point). Prices are not date-stamped 2026 quotes; every operator uses dynamic pricing
with no fixed rate published for a specific October or November 2026 date, so the
figures below are estimates built from published reference prices and recent guest
receipts, clearly marked as such.

## Tokyo: GLANSIT AKIHABARA

- **Address:** 4-4-6 Sotokanda, Chiyoda-ku, Tokyo 101-0021
  Verified on the operator's own site (glansit.jp): "GLANSIT AKIHABARA / 〒101-0021
  東京都千代田区外神田4-4-6".
- **Official website:** https://glansit.jp/akihabara/ (English version at
  https://glansit.jp/en/akihabara/)
- **Nightly rate for one person:** Unverified as a date-stamped quote. Guest receipts
  reported in reviews and travel-booking sites range from about ¥3,800 to ¥5,200 for a
  Standard Capsule and up to roughly $105 (about ¥16,300) for a Superior/Private
  Capsule. A reasonable shoulder-season (Oct-Nov 2026) planning estimate is **¥6,000 to
  ¥9,000 per person** for a Standard or Private Capsule. Treat this as an estimate, not
  a quote.
- **Gender policy: both, on segregated floors.** Confirmed on the operator's own FAQ
  page: "男女のフロアが分かれていますか？ はい、それぞれ男性専用フロア・女性専用フロ
  ア..." ("Are the floors split by gender? Yes, there is a men-only floor and a
  women-only floor, and for security reasons guests cannot enter any floor besides
  their own and the shared floors"). The property also has a Twin Capsule room for two
  of the same gender, and explicitly states mixed-gender pairs cannot book it together.
  Public baths are also split: men's bath basement level 1, women's bath level 10.
- **Minimum age:** Guests aged 12 and under are declined ("12歳以下のお子様はご遠慮い
  ただいております"), and unaccompanied minors aged 13-18 need signed parental consent
  at check-in. Confirmed on the operator's own FAQ page.
- **Getting there from Hotel Livemax Higashi-Ueno:** Iriya Station, the hotel's nearest
  stop, is on the Hibiya Line. Akihabara Station is also on the Hibiya Line, three
  stops south (Iriya to Ueno to Naka-okachimachi to Akihabara), no transfer, roughly
  8 minutes' ride at the ¥180 minimum fare. From Akihabara Station, GLANSIT is a
  3-minute walk from the JR Denki-gai (electronics-town) exit, or 5 minutes from the
  Hibiya Line's own exit 3.
- **What it's like:** A modern designer capsule property (2023-era build), the Tokyo
  branch of the BAGUS Group's GLANSIT brand. Wood-panelled, plant-accented lounges,
  smart-lock check-in, two capsule tiers (Standard and a slightly larger Private
  Capsule with its own locker and side desk), free drinks, and full public baths on
  two of the gender-specific floors. Closer to a First Cabin or Nine Hours-style
  boutique pod than a smoky 1980s salaryman capsule.

## Kyoto: GLANSIT KYOTO KAWARAMACHI

- **Address:** 380-2 Komeyacho, Kawaramachi-dori Shijo-agaru, Nakagyo-ku, Kyoto-shi,
  Kyoto 604-8026
  Verified on the operator's own site (glansit.jp): "〒604-8026 京都府京都市中京区河
  原町通四条上る米屋町380-2".
- **Official website:** https://glansit.jp/kyotokawaramachi/ (English version at
  https://glansit.jp/en/kyotokawaramachi/)
- **Nightly rate for one person:** The operator's own price page lists an "overnight
  plan" reference price of ¥2,800 for a 17-hour stay (17:00 check-in to 10:00
  check-out), but the same page states plainly this is a reference figure and the
  actual price varies ("表示金額は参考価格です"). Third-party booking sites show real
  guest rates from about ¥5,940 up to roughly ¥13,000+ on weekends, averaging in the
  ¥6,000-9,000 band on a weeknight. A reasonable shoulder-season planning estimate is
  **¥6,000 to ¥9,000 per person**. Treat this as an estimate, not a quote.
- **Gender policy: both, on segregated floors, plus a mixed-use floor.** Confirmed on
  the operator's own FAQ page: "男女のフロアが分かれていますか？ はい、それぞれ男性
  専用フロア・女性専用フロア・男女共用フロアに分かれております" (men-only floor,
  women-only floor, and a mixed-use floor, each behind its own security gate).
- **Minimum age:** Same chain-wide policy as Akihabara: guests aged 12 and under are
  declined, unaccompanied minors 13-18 need parental consent. Confirmed on the
  operator's own FAQ page.
- **Getting there from Toyoko Inn Kyoto Shijo-Omiya:** The base hotel sits about 1
  minute from Omiya Station on the Hankyu Kyoto Line. GLANSIT Kyoto Kawaramachi is 1
  minute's walk from Kawaramachi Station, the eastern terminus of that same Hankyu
  Kyoto Line, 2 stops from Omiya with no transfer (roughly a 5-minute ride, ¥170
  minimum fare). It is also 3 minutes' walk from Gion-Shijo Station on the Keihan Main
  Line.
- **What it's like:** A modern designer capsule hotel, right in the Shijo-Kawaramachi
  entertainment district (the operator's own site calls it Kyoto Prefecture's largest
  nightlife area) and a block from the Kamo River. Black-bamboo and wood-lattice
  interiors, smart check-in kiosks, sleep-engineered mattresses in every capsule, a
  women-only powder lounge and terrace lounge, and no large communal bath, just shower
  booths (a step down from Akihabara's public baths, but still well above hostel-grade
  capsule hotels).

## Osaka: First Cabin Midosuji Namba

- **Address:** Namba Midosuji Building 3F, 4-2-1 Namba, Chuo-ku, Osaka-shi, Osaka
  542-0076
  Verified on the operator's own site (en.first-cabin.jp): "Namba Midosuji Building
  3F, 4-2-1 Namba, Chuo-ku, Osaka-shi, Osaka 542-0076".
- **Official website:** https://en.first-cabin.jp/hotels/midosujinanba/
- **Nightly rate for one person:** Unverified as a date-stamped quote. Third-party
  booking aggregators show recent rates around $83/night (about ¥12,900 at ¥155=$1),
  described as a good-value 2-star rate for Osaka, though First Cabin's First Class
  cabins typically run cheaper than that headline figure and the aggregator price may
  reflect a Premium cabin or a surcharge date. A reasonable shoulder-season planning
  estimate for a First Class (standard single) cabin is **¥7,000 to ¥11,000 per
  person**, excluding Osaka's local accommodation tax. Treat this as an estimate, not
  a quote.
- **Gender policy: both, on segregated floors.** Confirmed on the operator's own
  facility page, which states plainly: "The building is divided into a male-only area
  and a female-only area," and lists the actual cabin count: 33 First Class cabins on
  the men's side, 19 on the women's side, plus 7 Premium Class (private, lockable)
  rooms open to both men and women, including a "Premium twin room that can be used by
  women with peace of mind." First Cabin's chain-wide FAQ separately confirms every
  location splits into men's and women's floors with shared mixed lounges.
- **Minimum age:** Chain-wide policy confirmed on the operator's own FAQ and rules
  pages: guests aged 12 and under are not permitted, and a guest aged 13-18 staying
  alone must submit a signed parental consent form at check-in.
- **Getting there from Toyoko Inn Osaka Namba:** The base hotel (per its own official
  listing) sits at 2-3-9 Namba, Chuo-ku, Osaka 542-0076, about 2 minutes' walk from
  Exit B6 of the Kintetsu/Hanshin Namba Station. First Cabin Midosuji Namba is
  directly connected to the Osaka Metro Namba Station concourse, 1 minute from Exit
  13. Both addresses sit inside the same sprawling Namba station complex (Nankai,
  Kintetsu, Hanshin, and Osaka Metro all converge there), so this is a walk within
  that complex rather than a train ride; the exact walking time is unverified but is
  very unlikely to exceed 10 minutes given both entrances open onto the same station
  concourse.
- **What it's like:** First Cabin is the aviation-themed capsule chain (fittings styled
  after first- and business-class airline cabins), a notch more upmarket than a
  classic salaryman capsule and comparable in spirit to Nine Hours or The Millennials.
  This branch adds a fitness gym overlooking Midosuji avenue, a large public bath with
  sauna and cold plunge on top of the standard shower booths, and a powder room. First
  Class cabins are 2.1m cubes with a semi-double bed, TV, side table and a small lockable
  safe (the cabin door itself cannot lock, by Japanese lodging law); Premium cabins are
  private, lockable rooms for 1-6 guests.

## Properties considered and rejected

- **Nine Hours (all locations near these three cities).** Nine Hours is a well-known
  designer capsule chain and was the first candidate checked for all three cities
  (Nine Hours Asakusa in Tokyo, Nine Hours Kyoto near Kawaramachi Station, and Nine
  Hours Namba Station in Osaka's Namba Parks). All three location pages now return a
  hard 404 on the operator's own site (ninehours.co.jp), and the current sitemap and a
  slug-by-slug check confirm nine hours currently operates only Nagoya Station,
  Suidobashi, Shinjuku-North, Hamamatsucho, Ningyocho, and Sendai. None of the three
  useful-looking locations still exists as of this check (September 2026); older blog
  posts and aggregator listings describing them are now stale. Rejected for all three
  cities on that basis.
- **Centurion Cabin & Spa / Centurion Ladies Hostel (Ueno, Kyoto).** These properties
  turned up repeatedly in Ueno and Kyoto searches, but the Ueno branch is explicitly
  branded "Ladies Hostel" and is women-only, and Centurion's other branches skew the
  same way. Rejected because the party needs one property that also accepts the men
  who will rotate into the solo room.
- **Anshin Oyado (Tokyo network).** A reputable spa-capsule chain with a published
  location list, but its own site lists Shinjuku, Akihabara (Kanda), Shimbashi as
  men-only and Ginza-Shiodome, Shinjuku-Ogikubo as women-only, no mixed-gender branch
  near Ueno/Asakusa. Rejected for lacking a single property that serves both genders.
- **Asakusa Riverside Capsule Hotel.** Frequently recommended in travel blogs as a
  women-friendly capsule hotel right in Asakusa, but no genuine operator-owned domain
  could be found, every result was a third-party reseller site
  (intokyohotels.com, hotelsoftokyo.com, tokyo-hotels-stay.com, etc.), so its address
  and gender policy could not be verified on the operator's own site as the brief
  requires. Rejected on verification grounds, not on the merits.
- **First Cabin Kyoto Nijo Castle.** A live, verifiable First Cabin branch (7-4
  Marutamachi-dori, Higashi-Horikawa-dori, Nakagyo-ku, Kyoto-shi, Kyoto 604-0076, 8
  minutes' walk from Nijojo-mae Station on the Tozai Line or Marutamachi Station on
  the Karasuma Line), with the same confirmed both-gender, segregated-floor policy as
  the Osaka branch. Passed over in favor of GLANSIT Kyoto Kawaramachi only because
  Kawaramachi sits on the same Hankyu Kyoto Line as the Omiya base hotel with no
  transfer, while Nijo Castle would need a transfer to the subway system. Worth
  keeping as a backup if Kawaramachi is sold out.
