## 2. Profile Adherence (profiled characters appearing in range)

### DANIEL — [no blocker]
Strong adherence to the "fallible, non-prodigy" mandate. The bible's DON'Ts ("smoothly competent," "remember things a normal teen wouldn't," "win without cost") are actively inverted across the range.
- Fallible / often wrong: blows the long-division in front of Heras — "I subtracted wrong... 'okay, it goes in more times than that, you absolute idiot, count it'" (ch04). Can't recall saltpeter or the boiling-point-altitude rule (ch02). Forgets pi past 3.14159 (ch02).
- Knowledge is shaky and YouTube-grade, not encyclopedic: "the half-memory of a video, swarming dots under a microscope I'd seen once on a screen" (ch05); map drawn "for a friend to win a bet, fast and confident and fundamentally a lie in the details" (ch03).
- Never wins without cost: germ pitch fails and is dangerous (ch05); balloon costs a year of burned cloth and two burned arms (ch06–07); the public demo "going up had only made me visible. A man can be visible and dead in the same hour" (ch08); ch11 ends with a man burned for life. This is exemplary adherence.
- Voice: dry, self-deprecating, modern references that die unheard, swears ("Sherlock lives," ch03; airplane-mode joke, ch02). Matches profile.
- Hardware-store competence (not engineering) is honored: "no equations... a memory of smoke" (ch06).
- MINOR continuity note (not a profile deviation): the glossary bans "okay/OK" in *Roman dialogue* but allows Daniel to *think* in modern terms. Daniel says "Okay" aloud to himself in ch01 (twice) — this is to the empty field, not in Roman dialogue, so it is within the rule. Flagging only so the line-edit pass confirms intent. [MINOR]

### HERAS — [no blocker]
Excellent adherence to "dry skeptic who punctures him."
- Punctures the germ pitch exactly as the profile demands: takes the stylus "gently, and he took me apart," reframes to bad air/humors/the gods' wrath, warns it is "witch-talk" (ch05). This is the profiled "punctures the germ pitch" beat landed precisely.
- Allergic to enthusiasm / dry irony: "You are counting at it. There is a difference" (ch07); "the most useless miracle I have ever seen" (ch07); "the air had carried the fire very well" (ch06); "Now make it not ugly, and you will have done something, and not before" (ch10).
- Refuses to be impressed, then is honestly moved once — "There were no strings... He was telling himself the thing he had just seen, out loud, because he did not yet believe his own eyes" (ch07) — which matches the arc "skeptic -> reluctant believer in method (not magic)."
- Treats Daniel as a gifted, infuriating student; prices his mind off the division error and never re-prices down for the bad Latin (ch05). Matches profile.
- Freedman physician from Pergamon, Greek + Latin: confirmed (ch04, ch09 "a Greek physician of Pergamon"). Switches to Greek to find a toehold (ch04). Adherence solid.
- MINOR: Profile says he "Quotes Hippocrates and Epicurus." In range he invokes Hippocrates/humoral theory in substance (ch05) but no on-page Epicurus quotation yet. Not a deviation — opportunity for later chapters. [MINOR]

### MACER (Titus Flavius Macer) — [no blocker]
Strong adherence and correct canon timing.
- CANON timing honored: he is the unnamed off-page owner who deals only through a steward and authorized the demo (ch07–08), and Daniel does NOT meet him face-to-face or hear his name until ch09 ("They walked me across the city... 'This is the Thulean'"; first named in ch09 chapter title and the Celer-intro in ch11). Matches bible exactly.
- Took Daniel on cheaply as a speculative oddity: "I bought you for less than I pay for a season's mules" (ch09). Matches "took Daniel on cheaply."
- Voice: blunt, transactional, mean-funny, contemptuous, respects money/results. "I am not a fool and I am not generous, those are the same sentence" (ch09); "I have liked mules" (ch09); "Smaller fire next time. Or a longer basket. Work it out" (ch11). Matches.
- Uses "the Thulean"/"boy": "First time he used it. He liked it" (ch09); "you will guess wrong about which is which... and that, boy, is the actual deal" (ch09). Matches profile's "Calls Daniel 'the Thulean' or 'boy'."
- Casual slaveholding as friction with Daniel: the peculium speech (ch09); the Pamphilus collar scene (ch10). Matches.
- Does NOT free Daniel, and explicitly says he *cannot* ("It is not mine to give... a citizen is made by men a great deal higher than me," ch09) — this correctly preserves the bible's canon that freedom comes from the emperor in ch17. Good guard against premature payoff. Adherence solid.

### TYCHE — [no blocker]
Strong adherence.
- ~14, kitchen/errand slave in Macer's household, assigned to fetch/carry for Daniel (ch09: "Tyche... Yours. Fetch and carry. She does not run"). Matches profile.
- "Frighteningly good with numbers once shown": learns long division and place-value in a single pass — "She'd watched me do it slow and wrong and slow and right, exactly the one time, and she'd kept it" (ch10). Matches "quick, watchful, frighteningly good with numbers once shown."
- Terse, guarded, survival-caution voice growing sharp: "city-quick, no slowing it for the foreigner" (ch10); catches Helena's padded bill (ch10); "Bad?" (ch10). Matches.
- The "DON'T romanticize / no saccharine gratitude" guard is respected: she gives "nothing back," her enslavement is shown as a leash with two ends (ch09), and Daniel's lie-to-protect-her ("the surest way to get Tyche sold," ch10) keeps it unsentimental. Good.
- Found-family / explicitly NOT romance is preserved in range (no romantic coding). Adherence solid.

### CRISPUS (Lucius Vettius Crispus) — [no blocker]
Strong adherence on introduction.
- ~40, vain, beautifully kept man of letters, salon darling: "around forty, beautifully kept... his hair oiled into careful waves" (ch10). Matches.
- Function — patronizes-then-resents, probes contradictions, seeds the fraud charge: "From a country no one can reach. With marvels no one quite saw fly... I do admire the symmetry" (ch10); "The gods, perhaps, expressing an opinion of the new philosophy." Matches "spreads the charge of fraud" and "clever and not always wrong."
- Voice: elegant, allusive, cutting, performs erudition, Greek tags, Strabo: cites Pytheas of Massalia and "Strabo... calls our friend Pytheas the greatest liar who ever lived" (ch10) — this is the profile's literal "Strabo citations (he is the skeptic who says Thule may not exist)." Excellent, near-verbatim alignment.
- Uses "Hyperborean," finishes Callimachus in Greek. Matches "Greek tags." Adherence solid.

### CELER (Quintus Marcius Celer) — [no blocker]
Strong adherence.
- ~35, equestrian serving officer, practical/ambitious, frontier-hardened: "maybe thirty-five... a face that had been out in weather for fifteen years"; named in ch11. (Bible says ~35; on-page "maybe thirty-five" — consistent.)
- Function — first to see the balloon's military value: asks "How far down the river could that be seen" — "a question you ask about a watchtower" (ch11). Matches "the man who first sees the balloon's military value."
- Voice: clipped, competent, gallows-humored, no patience for court games. "Not yet" (ch11); "The men I bury are the ones who weren't... Don't love it" (ch11). Matches.
- "A friendship with a soldier's bluntness" + foreshadowing of cost: "afraid of someone you've started to like" (ch11); the Naso burning is the early on-ramp to the profile's "where Daniel first sees his gifts kill people" (here, maim). Good setup; Celer himself is intact, consistent with "decide in outline" for his own death. Adherence solid.

### AULUS VIBENIUS — [no blocker]
Strong adherence.
- ~55, senior haruspex, Etruscan-coded liver-reader, shrewd about the politics of belief. Named in ch07 ("Aulus Vibenius. Do not point at him"). On-page reading entrails at the gate (ch07) and at the demo (ch08).
- Function — can bless as gods' favor OR brand as impiety; Daniel must frame so the gods get their due: the ch08 verdict gives "the crowd both doors and stood in the middle of them... whichever way it went now, he had read it true." This is a precise dramatization of the profiled ambiguity. Matches.
- Voice: grave, indirect, never shows his hand. Matches. Adherence solid.

### TRAJAN — [no blocker]
Cameo only, offstage, not named on-page (ch07): "a big plain soldier in a clean toga walking on his own two feet," "modestly." Matches the profile's "soldierly, practical, affable... keep him mostly a distant force." Correct restraint. (Not named yet — fine for a crowd-glimpse.)

## 3. Unprofiled characters with REAL roles (flag for bible)

- **The optio** [MAJOR — recommend a short bible note]. He is the single most present secondary figure of ch02–ch04 (detains, feeds, cuts Daniel's bonds, exchanges names, escorts him repeatedly) and the emotional first-contact beat of the whole opening. He is never named on-page and has no profile. Given his weight in the first quarter, the bible should either name him (glossary soldier stock, e.g. one of the listed names) and give a one-line note, or explicitly mark him as a deliberately-unnamed transitional figure who exits. Right now his status is ambiguous.
- **The steward** [MINOR]. Recurring functional presence ch08–ch11 (Macer's mouthpiece, assigns Tyche, vets the tablets). Unnamed by design seems intentional, but worth a one-line bible note confirming he stays anonymous.
- **Stichus** [MINOR]. Real recurring helper across ch05–ch08 (the rag, the demo coal-pot, comic foil). Drawn from glossary slave-stock, so usage is legal, but he carries enough screen time that a one-line tracker entry would help continuity (age ~10, water-boy, "does not like Daniel" early).
- **The kitchen woman** [MINOR]. Recurring friction figure (ch06–07), explicitly kept nameless in-text ("I'd never gotten a name out of"). Intentional; just noting.
- **Pamphilus** [MINOR]. Recurring ch10–11 (collared cloth-cutter; the collar scene is a thematically loaded beat). Glossary stock; fine, but a tracker line would help.
- **Naso (Titus Aelius Naso)** [MINOR]. Not recurring but a *costed casualty* (first flier, permanently maimed) that the book will likely reference again. Glossary stock name used correctly; recommend logging him so later callbacks stay consistent.
- **Onesimus** [MINOR]. One-scene accountant foil (ch10). Glossary lists him as "Tiberius Claudius Onesimus"; text uses bare "Onesimus." Fine for a walk-on. See naming note.

## 4. Naming inconsistencies / consistency notes

- [MINOR] **Onesimus**: glossary stock is "Tiberius Claudius Onesimus"; ch10 uses single-name "Onesimus." Consistent with the glossary's own note that slaves/freedmen often go by one name, so not an error — just confirm the full *tria nomina* if he ever reappears formally.
- [MINOR] **Mettius**: ch10 names the salon host "Mettius"; glossary equestrian stock has "Marcus Mettius Rufus." Same person presumably, shortened. No conflict, but log the full name if reused.
- [MINOR] **Daniel's rendered name**: glossary specifies Romans call him "Danihel" or "the Thulean." Text uses "Danihel" (ch05) and "the Thulean" (ch08+) — consistent. The optio and others mangle "Daniel" early (ch02–03), which matches the glossary's "Romans render it awkwardly." Good.
- No same-person-two-names errors and no misspellings of principal names detected across ch01–11. Heras, Macer, Tyche, Crispus, Celer, Vibenius, Stichus, Pamphilus, Davus, Naso, Helena all spelled consistently.
