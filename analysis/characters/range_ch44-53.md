## 2) PROFILE ADHERENCE (per profiled character)

### Daniel / Marcus Ulpius Danihel — [OK] (with one notable evolution, no deviation)
- Bible arc terminus ("an institution-builder racing his own mortality"; the second fear —
  "dying with everything still in his head" — becoming the larger) is delivered exactly. ch44:
  "A man wants to be the one who taught the world to count and also wants the world to count
  without thinking of him, and he cannot have both." ch48: "the success of a thing you make to
  outlast you is the exact measure of how little it needs you."
- Voice intact to the end: dry, self-undercutting, modern reference no one gets, never
  monologuing the loneliness directly (bible: "Show the loneliness; never have him monologue
  it"). ch50 the lost first-language song and the "no one in the room I could ask" deliver the
  loneliness obliquely, as instructed.
- "NOT a prodigy / never wins without cost": honored hard. The clock, the steam-engine bridge,
  the microscope, electricity, the sea-crossing — all explicitly his FAILURES, left blank in the
  books (ch49, ch50). ch47: "I could not make the catch." The moral weight of ch45 (his figures
  reckon the falling price of war-captives; his drains/survey serve the razing of Judaea) is the
  bible's "his casual" cost-of-invention theme at full maturity.
- Death (ch50): dies of pulmonary edema ("the wet"), narrating to the last sentence which breaks
  off mid-line ("the next one is"). Tyche present. Consistent with bible (dies before Tyche).
- NAME: "Marcus Ulpius Danihel" used/consistent (ch49 citizenship tablet). [OK]

### Tyche — [OK] (strongly on-model; the bible's headline instruction is met)
- Bible: "grows sharp, funny, fierce about the work… Eventually freer-tongued than is safe…
  has her own anger and her own agenda… survivor who outlives Daniel and carries the ladder
  forward." ALL delivered.
- Sharp/fierce + unforgiving: ch47 "she does not entirely forgive me for the years she read it
  as a slave who could be sold for knowing it, and that unforgiveness is part of why she is the
  best keeper a thing could have. She owes the books no flattery." This is the bible's "her own
  anger" rendered precisely, and the anti-saccharine instruction ("don't romanticize her
  enslavement or make her gratitude saccharine") is actively honored.
- Outlives Daniel: confirmed — present at his deathbed (ch50), and ch51 references her still
  alive, "very old, a freedwoman who was his clerk fifty years… teaching it to a slave-girl she
  has bought." [OK — exactly the bible's "epilogue link / carries the ladder forward."]
- Found-family, NOT romance (bible DON'T): preserved cleanly. The deathbed (ch50) and the
  box-burial scene (ch49) are sibling/comrade intimacy, no romantic coloring.
- [OK] No deviations. This is the closest-to-bible portrayal in the range.

### Marcia — [OK]
- Bible: "runs accounts and the business side… more nerve than most men… a genuine partner,
  not a prize… unsentimental and real… her own agenda for the enterprise and any children."
- Delivered: she finishes the legal deed of trust on her deathbed (ch48), closing the gap "she
  had spent thirty-five years teaching me to find," and dictates succession ("Tyche was to be
  obeyed… I was to be humored and ignored"). Unsentimental to the end: "it was never of love,
  which would have sounded like a man pricing a thing he was afraid of losing." Exactly the
  bible's tone.
- Death ch48 (liver). Bible left her fate open; dying mid-late is consistent and well-motivated.
- [OK]

### Macer — [OK] (death lands on the bible's flagged chapter)
- Bible: "blunt, transactional, funny in a mean way, respects results and money… Calls Daniel
  'the Thulean' or 'boy' long past when it fits… casual slaveholding a recurring friction… does
  NOT free Daniel (freedom comes from the emperor)… possible late reconciliation OR rupture."
- All on-model at the deathbed (ch48): "You still here, Thulean… Outlived the lot of them…
  The clever ones go first." Mean-dry to the last; does arithmetic on Daniel's press profits and
  calls him "a sentimental fool" for overpaying. The will leaves only the ring, inscribed "to the
  Thulean, who will know what it cost" — never even names him. This is a sharp, earned, NON-
  saccharine close that resolves the relationship as ambivalence rather than tidy reconciliation,
  consistent with bible's "a man Daniel must outgrow without destroying."
- Death: ch48, age 96 (bible flag "~ch48" — precise). [OK]

### Heras — [OK] (used as absence)
- Already dead at range start ("a year and more in the ground," ch45). Bible wanted his death
  "to land mid-late book" as "a real loss" — honored: ch45 makes his absence the engine of a
  whole section ("I tried to take it to Heras and he was a year and more in the ground… the use
  of him, and it was gone"). His function (the skeptic who makes Daniel say what he means,
  refuses to grant permission) is explicitly eulogized. ch47/ch50 establish his medical line
  continues via Zoticus. [OK]

### Hermes — [OK]
- Referenced as the steel-chokepoint thread that never resolved (bible: "keeps chasing
  repeatable steel and mostly fails, which is honest and bitter"). ch45: "not from Hermes in
  thirty years… it will not come in my life." Dead by ch46 (fund pays his heirs). On-model. [OK]

### Celer — [OK]
- Referenced only, dead long before the range. Bible flagged his death as "where Daniel first
  sees his gifts kill people" and possible death "in Dacia or the Parthian war." Here he is part
  of the guilt-litany ("Celer under my hands," ch47/48) — died of a wound-flux under Daniel's
  failed care, consistent with the bible's intent (a cost Daniel carries). [OK]

### Crispus — [OK] (clever afterlife use)
- Bible: "elegant, allusive, cutting… a long-running antagonist… clever and not always wrong."
  ch51 resurrects him posthumously through his coined phrase "the barbarian's heaven by ladder"
  / "Prometheus with a printing-frame" — the religious-impiety charge that forced the contest's
  dedication to Minerva. This honors "not always wrong" (the ladder-past-the-gods framing is
  genuinely the book's thesis) and "could be neutralized, co-opted, or destroyed" — the charge
  is neutralized by buying off the gods. Good payoff of a profiled antagonist. [OK]

### Hadrian — [OK]
- Bible (real-historical): "intellectually voracious, prickly, dangerous… receptive to long-term
  projects in some moods, suspicious in others… the succession resets every relationship."
- Delivered: the ocean/western-exploration program is HIS vanity ("a restless emperor's wish to
  have done a thing no Caesar had done," ch46), and his death ch46 resets everything — the
  stay-at-home Antoninus lets the sea program lapse. ch46 recalls the Strabo-baiting first
  meeting and his keeping Daniel's origin "like a coin he had not yet decided to spend" — fully
  consistent with the dangerous-patron read. The Apollodorus exile/death is invoked correctly
  (ch46: "Apollodorus could have met me as a peer and was dead, and partly of this man"). [OK]

### Apollodorus / Trajan — [OK]
- Both real-historical, dead, used as memory. Apollodorus = the lost peer; his clash-with-Hadrian
  fate is canon-correct and even surfaces in ch51 ("died badly for the sin of speaking his mind
  to one who outranked him in everything save mind"). Trajan = the prior reign, the source of
  Daniel's name. No issues. [OK]

## 3) IMPORTANT CHECKS

### A) ch51 interlude narrator — was a Pliny cameo planned? [FLAG — MINOR/EDITORIAL]
- The bible (02_characters.md, REAL HISTORICAL FIGURES) explicitly earmarks **Pliny the Younger**
  for exactly this slot: "perfect for a Roman-document interlude — a letter mentioning the strange
  Thulean. Cameo only."
- ch51 IS that letter-interlude — but the narrator is an INVENTED Roman, **Gaius Norbanus Rufus**,
  writing to **Sextus Caecilius Priscus**, dated "the consulship of Clarus and Cethegus." Pliny
  is NOT used.
- Assessment: This is a deliberate departure from the bible's suggestion, not an error of fact.
  And it is well-motivated: by the dramatic date (a generation after Daniel's death, two reigns
  on), the historical Pliny (d. ~113 AD) would be long dead, so he could not plausibly narrate a
  retrospective on a man who outlived Trajan AND Hadrian. The invented Norbanus solves the
  chronology the real Pliny could not. RECOMMEND: log this as a conscious override of the bible
  note so the bible/canon-log no longer implies Pliny fills this slot. No prose problem.
- [FLAG] is editorial/continuity-log only — the chapter itself is clean and on-theme (it is a
  superb skeptic's-eye recap; even quotes the worn-smooth keeper-chain rumor and the buried glass).

### B) "Ulpia" vs "Procula" — different people, or conflated? [OK — CLEARLY DISTINCT]
Verdict: clearly DIFFERENT people. No conflation in the prose. Evidence:

- **ULPIA** = Daniel and Marcia's biological DAUGHTER.
  - First appears ch44 (the survey "in Ulpia's clean copying").
  - Explicitly Marcia's daughter: ch46 "She was twenty that summer… She had understood… She had
    got that from her mother"; ch47 "She was raised by Marcia and Tyche… even her father's";
    ch48 "Ulpia had the survey." Her brother is Lucanus (ch49–50).
  - Works "under a borrowed name because the law would let her do it no other way" (ch46) — i.e.
    she is freeborn but barred as a woman from holding a counting-house in her own name. Her
    cognomen/the "Ulpia" name ties to Daniel's nomen Ulpius (ch46 "grave the way her cognomen
    promised").
  - Last appears ch50, at her father's deathbed. She does NOT appear in the epilogue.

- **PROCULA** = the epilogue CLIMBER, a full generation later (ch52 only).
  - "She was twenty, that spring… Her mother had been bought into the storeroom as a girl and
    freed in the great widow's [Marcia's] will and had married a freedman of the press, and so
    Procula had been born free into the smell of lamp-black" (ch52).
  - So Procula is NOT Daniel's descendant at all: she is the daughter of a storeroom slave whom
    Marcia freed. Her cipher-teacher was "old Vibia," and her craft-cousin is Saturius. She solves
    the escapement/clock that Daniel and Felix and Saturius all failed at — the literal "begin
    where I failed" payoff.
  - She "did not know the man who had laid the bottom of it… She knew the name they all knew, the
    Thulean" (ch52) — i.e., she has no personal/family connection to Daniel, underscoring she is
    a different lineage from Ulpia.

- The two names never co-occur in any chapter (Ulpia: ch44–50; Procula: ch52 only), are separated
  by Daniel's death (ch50) and the ch51 interlude, and are given distinct parentage, distinct
  generations, and distinct functions (Ulpia = blood-heir/survey-keeper who declines no role;
  Procula = unrelated craft-line keeper who climbs the next rung). NO conflation risk. [OK]
  - NOTE for continuity: the keeper-CHAIN is deliberately matrilineal-by-craft, NOT by Daniel's
    blood. Ulpia (blood) keeps the survey but the cipher-keeper line runs Tyche → Chloe → (a
    storeroom girl/press boy) → ... → Vibia → Procula. Daniel's blood line (Lucanus) explicitly
    OPTS OUT (ch49, ch50). This is thematically intentional ("the ladder… would not check a man's
    birth before it let him up").

## 4) IMPORTANT CHARACTERS MISSING FROM THE PROFILES — [FLAG]

The following carry real dramatic weight in the final act/epilogue yet have NO bible profile.
For a final-act QA these are the headline gaps:

- **Ulpia** [FLAG — HIGH]: Daniel's daughter, a major presence ch44–50, the survey-keeper and one
  of the two dictation-scribes who finishes the encyclopedia. The bible's Marcia profile notes
  "her own agenda for the enterprise and any children" but never names or profiles the children.
  Ulpia deserves a profile (or at least a canon-log entry: parentage, "borrowed name" legal
  device, survey custodianship, fate).
- **Lucanus** [FLAG — HIGH]: Daniel's SON; the advocate who declines the keeper role — the
  emotional core of ch49–50 (the blood line that opts out so the craft line can be open to all).
  Completely absent from profiles. Needs a canon-log entry at minimum.
- **Procula** [FLAG — HIGH]: the epilogue protagonist who solves the escapement (the book's final
  triumph). Unprofiled (expected for a late-introduced epilogue figure, but flagged per task).
  Parentage/keeper-oath/clock-solution should be logged.
- **Tyche's keeper-chain (Chloe, Vibia, the unnamed two)** [FLAG — MED]: Chloe (ch46–50) and Vibia
  (ch52) are load-bearing links in the survival-of-knowledge spine. Chloe is freed in Marcia's
  will and teaches the next two; Vibia teaches Procula. The chain is the whole point of ch49 and
  the epilogue. Worth logging the chain explicitly: Tyche → Chloe → ... → Vibia → Procula.
- **Vitalis** [FLAG — MED]: runs the press across ch44–50 and CASES THE LEAD COPY of the
  encyclopedia (ch49) buried under the press-room stone — the object the ch53 future literally
  rests on. Unprofiled; recurring across many chapters.
- **Felix** [FLAG — MED, CARRYOVER]: From glossary slave stock but recurs from the mid-book
  contest through ch52 as the named heir of the steam-pot failure-thread; arguably promoted past
  "walk-on." (Already flagged in the ch12–22 range file as a [MINOR]; by ch52 he is a named link
  in the multi-generation craft-failure chain and is worth a canon-log line.)
- **Pamphilus** [FLAG — CARRYOVER]: first freedman, recurring since early book; dies ch48 with a
  genuine emotional beat. Consistent with the prior range file's [FLAG]. Still unprofiled.
- **Gaius Norbanus Rufus** [FLAG — MINOR/LOG]: the ch51 narrator standing in for the bible's
  intended Pliny cameo (see §3A). Should be logged as the deliberate substitute.

Lower-priority unprofiled walk-ons (Zoticus, Bituitus, Theophanes, Eudemus, Saturius, Priscus,
Tertia) are appropriately minor and drawn within glossary conventions; no action needed beyond
optional canon-log mentions for Zoticus (Heras's medical line) and Bituitus (the Garonne yard).
