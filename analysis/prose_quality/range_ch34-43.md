# Prose Quality Audit: ch34-43

Scope: ch34 ("From Scratch") through ch43 ("Family"). First-person past, narrator Daniel.
Criteria: /home/user/test/Bad writing.txt + bible/06_style_guide.md.
Tags: [BLOCKER] / [MAJOR] / [MINOR]. Recurring tics flagged.

General note: the prose is high quality and largely renders rather than labels. The
dominant problem is not crude AI filler but a small set of *recurring stylistic tics*
that, repeated across ten chapters, become a signature: (1) the "stayed where I set
them / had a far side" closing refrain, (2) the gnomic last-line aphorism, (3) the
"not X, it was/it is Y" correctio (which the style guide explicitly bans), and (4) a
sameness of scene-architecture (negotiation -> aphorism -> count the loads). The grief
in ch42 is mostly rendered well; one beat tips into announcement. Details below.

---

## 1. BAD WRITING.TXT CATEGORIES

### Outline-notes-as-prose (describing the function of a scene rather than rendering it)
[MAJOR] Recurring. Daniel repeatedly narrates the *purpose/shape* of a beat instead of
playing it.
- ch36 L11: "a thing that is only more of the same does not come with an escort of two."
  - Acceptable as deduction, but it shades into labeling the scene's function.
- ch36 L97-98: "the way to die in front of this man was to defend a thing he had already
  half-seen through. So I gave him a third of it and let him keep the suspicion, because
  a man allowed his suspicion stops digging for the rest." This is a tactic explained as
  a rule, i.e. the planning note read aloud rather than dramatized.
- ch37 L83-89: the Theophanes exchange is then *re-explained* in full ("That was all of
  it. That was my word to Hadrian... hedged so far down it was barely a word at all"),
  labeling what the scene already showed.
- ch40 L13-17: "This is the way the thing was ordered, because the order was the whole
  of it. You climb a ladder by the bottom rung." An outline of the book's structure
  delivered as exposition. [MINOR] (it is framed as a deliberate didactic interlude).

### Negative narration (naming what isn't happening / silence)
[MAJOR] Recurring tic. The text repeatedly names absence and silence.
- ch34 L104: "Tyche had been quiet, building something behind her eyes."
- ch34 L131-133: Tyche "said nothing, but the corner of her mouth did the thing..."
  (ch34 L25 has the near-identical "the corner of her mouth did the thing that is the
  closest she comes to a smile").
- ch35 L131: "Marcia looked at me across the wax and did not ask the loud question...
  She asked the quiet one." Naming the unasked question.
- ch36 L27: "There was nothing to say to it that would not have been the loud thing, and
  we did not do that to each other."
- ch37 L103-105: "She had heard me come back... and had asked nothing, and she asked
  nothing now."
- ch42 L69: "I did not say anything." / L97: "I did not say it."
- ch43 L121: "She never asked again." / "She did not press."
This is a stylistic mannerism (the "loud thing"/"quiet thing" contrast) that recurs in
ch35, ch36, ch42, ch43. Individually fine; as a repeated structural move it flattens.

### Telegraphing twists ("unexpected," "surprising")
[MINOR] Mostly avoided. Closest offenders:
- ch35 L19: "It was not announced as the emperor, which is how I should have known." A
  mild retrospective tell, but earns itself by being Daniel's self-rebuke, not the
  narrator flagging a twist for the reader.
- ch43 L93: "The roots came up, when they came up, sideways" -- pre-labels the coming
  beat as significant.

### Narrator stepping outside time (retroactive annotation from the future)
[MAJOR] Recurring. The style guide explicitly forbids "little did I know" vantage. Daniel
mostly tells it as-felt, but slips:
- ch36 L133: "Men would die against it eventually, in good hulls, sailing toward an
  empty shore that was not empty... Or they would never sail at all... I could not tell
  which, and I had aimed it anyway." Future-annotation of consequence. (Borderline:
  framed as his present speculation, but the prophetic register is exactly the banned
  vantage.)
- ch37 L75: "It was the only gift either of us ever gave the other... and I have it."
  Narrator reaching from the future to weight the object.
- ch41 L87: "each carrying off a piece of a country... that none of them would ever
  miss, because you cannot miss a thing you were never told you were carrying." Outside-
  time annotation of what the children will never feel.
- ch35 L5: "A man higher than Eudemus had asked the question Eudemus had waited a year to
  be asked" -- compresses time and annotates significance.

### Announcing emotional weight (punchy declarative depth)
[MAJOR] Recurring, and it is the book's signature closing move.
- ch34 L137: "It was the smallest I had been since the cell, and the strange thing was
  the strength it gave me, the way going to ground gives an animal back its hearing."
- ch38 L63 opens: "I want to be exact about what happened in me there, because it was not
  a flash and it was not a grief, though there was grief under it." Announces the
  emotional event then describes it from outside.
- ch42 L33: "I sat there with my hands full of nothing to fix, and I want to say that was
  easier, and it was not. It was its own thing." Declaring the feeling's category.
- ch43 L37: "I sat at the foot of the table and ate the salt pork and was as happy as a
  man gets, which is the trouble." The "which is the trouble" tag announces the weight.

### Dramatic irony by declaration
[MINOR] Largely architectural (the Apollodorus/Hadrian thread is built, not declared).
One slip:
- ch35 L127-129: the Apollodorus material is partly *stated* as irony ("Apollodorus did
  not know it, or knew it and did not believe it could matter") rather than left for the
  reader to hold. Borderline; mostly earned by ch37 paying it off.

### Exposing subtext (naming what should stay beneath)
[MAJOR] Recurring. The narrator frequently names the thing the behavior should imply.
- ch37 L17: "He was not my friend, and Heras knew it, and used the word because there was
  no other for what Apollodorus was to me." Naming the relationship's subtext directly.
- ch42 L77: "I understood that it was a clay copy of a living man, that I had spent my
  whole life being kept honest by a Greek who would not be impressed." The hooked-stroke
  metaphor's meaning is spelled out rather than left to land.
- ch43 L121: "which was the gentlest thing anyone could have done to me and the one I
  could thank no one for." Names the emotional content the scene already delivered.

### Dialogue that delivers information (characters saying exactly what they mean)
[MAJOR] Recurring, concentrated in ch38-39 and the Heras deathbed (ch42). Much dialogue
is functional/thesis-bearing rather than evasive.
- ch38 L57-61: Marcia delivers a fully-articulated thesis about Daniel's mortality and
  mis-built legacy: "you are going to die with all of it still living only in your head
  and your hands, and your head is starting to go, and your hands will follow." Reads as
  the author's argument, not deflecting human speech.
- ch39 L13: Marcia explains the entire burial-club legal mechanism cleanly: "A club can
  hold property. A club can take a gift. A club can pay wages out of rents and never
  die..." Pure info-delivery (tricolon, see AI tells).
- ch42 L75: Heras's deathbed speech ("When I am gone you will guess, and it will sound
  like knowing...") states the theme outright. Moving, but it is the character naming the
  book's subtext rather than circling it.
Note: Hadrian's dialogue in ch35-36 is the *counterexample* and the strongest writing in
the range -- he deflects, tests, traps, and talks around. That contrast shows the others
could be pushed sideways too.

### Beat-tag cliches (nod/sigh/chuckle/shrug as punctuation)
[MINOR] Largely avoided; gestures are usually specific (Marcia squaring tablet edges,
Apollodorus letting a plumb-bob settle). Watch items:
- "the dry up-from-the-chest laugh" recurs: ch41 L85, ch42 L61. Becoming a signature beat
  for Heras -- specific, but now repeated verbatim enough to read as a tag.
- ch43 L131 / repeated: Macer "turned the gold ring on his finger with his thumb." Used
  as character punctuation; fine once, flagged as the kind of move that can ossify.

### Stacked incoherent imagery
[MINOR] Rare; imagery generally coheres (ladder/rungs, plumb-line, the box, counting
loads are sustained controlling images). No clear blocker. Minor:
- ch37 L117 stacks "a bob on a string knows one true thing... and it does not bend, and
  it has never once... been wrong, and it cannot fly" -- piling clauses; coherent but
  over-extended.

### Scene closings that restate the takeaway
[BLOCKER] The single most recurring structural problem. Most scenes/chapters end by
summarizing their own meaning, frequently via the "counting/numbers stayed put" refrain.
- ch34 L143: ends "went out to keep my appointment with a man who was not my friend."
- ch35 L137-138: "I counted the loads through. Then, because my hands would not quiet, I
  counted them again, and they came out the same, and that was the one thing in the whole
  day that had." Restated takeaway.
- ch36 L141-142: "I closed the lid on the nothing inside... where the numbers stayed
  where I set them and did not have a far side." Near-identical move.
- ch37 L119: ends on the books being "the one place in Rome I had built where a man could
  put a true thing and have it stay." Takeaway appended.
- ch38 L109-110: "'I know,' said Ulpia Severa, and did the next line... She would just do
  the next line." Restated.
- ch39 L96: "I could not have read it close enough to spoil my own pleasure in not
  needing to." Meaning appended.
- ch42 L99: "the water in the cup went cold while I worked, and I drank it cold rather
  than get up for more." (This one is GOOD -- ends on concrete image.)
The "numbers/loads stayed where I set them" closing recurs across ch34, ch35, ch36 and
is a clear recurring tic.

### Fake/vague details (category labels standing in for specifics)
[MINOR] Rare; Rome is well-grounded (garum, lamp-soot, pearwood, posca, wool grease,
pepper and rope-tar). Sensory grounding meets the style guide. Occasional abstraction:
- ch34 L7: "the Roman for never" / "a fine waiting" (ch35 L21) -- mild category labels,
  but used wittily.

### Repetition across scenes (re-explaining established dynamics)
[MAJOR] Recurring. The text re-establishes Tyche's silence, Marcia's edge-squaring, the
map-in-the-box, and Daniel's failing eyes more than the reader needs.
- Marcia "lined the edge of the tablet with her finger" / "put the books square": ch34
  L23, L101; ch35 L9; recurs as her established tic but is re-described each time.
- Daniel's failing eyes are re-explained in ch38 L37-43, then re-stated ch39 L73, ch40
  L81, ch41 L11, ch42 L39, ch43 L57. By ch43 the reader has it; re-explanation persists.
- The map/box ("the size and shape of the thing no longer in it," ch36 L141; "the box...
  where I am not allowed to know it lives," ch34 L25, ch36 L17) re-explained repeatedly.

### Uniform hedging ("perhaps," "it seemed," "in some ways")
[MINOR] Notably, the in-world books deliberately hedge (the "hooked stroke," ch40), which
is earned and thematic. In the narration proper, hedging is mild and varied. Watch: "I
think" / "I think" clusters (ch35 L127; ch42 L65, L87) and "almost" ("almost a grin,"
"almost like approval," "almost smiled" ch36 L37) recur enough to soften several beats.

## 2. AI TELLS

### Gnomic / aphoristic closings
[BLOCKER] Recurring -- the strongest AI signature in the range. Nearly every section or
chapter lands on a portable epigram:
- ch34 L57: "It is the only interesting part."
- ch34 L91: "afraid of the wind." (carrying a coal cupped)
- ch35 L138: "that was the one thing in the whole day that had."
- ch37 L65: "What I am for is to be the man who said so."
- ch37 L73: "Go fold something."
- ch38 L89: "You are being the man another man reads by a lamp in six hundred years."
- ch41 L57: "to be glad and to be afraid, which feel, when they come together, almost
  exactly the same."
- ch41 L83: "a fire carried only by men who needed to see the far end would have gone out
  in the first generation that did not."
- ch42 L78: "that is the only correction that ever actually worked on me."
- ch43 L139: "and slept."
The cumulative effect: the reader is trained to expect a wisdom-button at each scene
break. Recommend thinning by half.

### Short-sentence tricolons
[MAJOR] Recurring rhythmic tic.
- ch34 L7: "The portico... The inner room... The message left and not answered."
- ch35 L21: "A pair of architects... A Greek I took for a rhetor... We did not speak."
- ch39 L13: "A club can hold property. A club can take a gift. A club can pay wages..."
- ch41 L17: "Vitalis treated it as a craft... Chloe treated it as a door... Ulpia treated
  it as a problem." Three-clause character sort.
- ch42 L31: "I had knelt in churned dirt... and I had sat five nights... and I had stood
  in a yard..." Tricolon of past deaths.

### "Wasn't X. It was Y." correctio / epanorthosis (STYLE GUIDE EXPLICITLY BANS THIS)
[BLOCKER] Recurring and a direct violation of Bad writing.txt L61 and style guide L35.
- ch34 L71: "Not warmth. The recognition one careful man gives another..."
- ch36 L65-67: "'Homer's Ocean'... 'No, Caesar.'... 'A width. Water with a far side.'"
- ch38 L63: "it was not a flash and it was not a grief, though there was grief under it.
  It was the way a sum comes out..."
- ch42 L33: "This was not that. There was no arithmetic in it." then "It was its own
  thing."
- ch42 L77: "I had thought... that it was a clever solution... I understood that it was a
  clay copy of a living man." (was-X / was-actually-Y)
- ch43 L3: "I have no Latin word for it and no English one either."
- ch37 L47: "He was not ashamed... There was a small flat pleasure."
These are textbook correctio. The style guide forbids the construction outright.

### Templated openings / closings
[MAJOR] Recurring. Chapters open on a gnomic thesis sentence and close on a quiet beat,
the same shape repeatedly:
- Opening thesis lines: ch34 L3 "The man who flew was no use to me anymore, so I went
  looking for work as the man who could add." ch38 L3 "The accounts of Aquitania came
  back to me in the spring with a column I could not read, and for three days I blamed
  the press." ch40 L3 "The first page took me eleven days, and the first page was the
  easy one, because the first page was counting." ch41 L3 "The hardest part of teaching a
  language with no speakers is that there is no one to be wrong in front of." ch43 L3.
  All follow the formula: [aphoristic claim], because [paradoxical reframing].
- "I want to be exact / I want to be plain / I want it on the record" framing recurs: ch35
  L113, ch37 L79, ch38 L63, ch42 L31. A templated honesty-signal.

## 3. PREDICTABILITY / TELEGRAPHED BEATS

[MAJOR] Several beats are clearly signposted before they land:
- ch35-37 Apollodorus arc: ch35 L91 plants the "go and paint his gourds" recognition with
  heavy weight; ch37 then delivers exile/death exactly as flagged. The fall is
  pre-announced ("you are handing him the morning," ch37 L67) so the death (ch37 L93-95)
  carries no surprise. Telegraphed by design, but the inevitability is stated rather than
  dramatized.
- ch38 failing-eyes reveal is over-prepared: "I had been holding the work farther off for
  a year" (L9) defuses the discovery before it happens.
- ch41 L63 "they would carry it all and they would never carry the one thing" pre-states
  the ch41/ch43 emotional payoff (the untranslatable sleet word, the unfinished song).
- ch43 song beat is a near-repeat of ch41 sleet-word beat: both = "Ulpia notices a gap in
  the homeland material, Daniel half-confesses, she gracefully declines to push." The
  second (ch43 L99-121) is predictable having read the first (ch41 L65-71). Recurring
  structural beat.

### ch42 -- IS HERAS'S GRIEF RENDERED OR ANNOUNCED?
Mostly RENDERED, and well. The strongest grief writing in the range is the closing
(ch42 L95-100): Daniel drinks the boiled water, the retort "came up in me, shaped and
ready," "there was the whole length of the empty bench, and the answer had nowhere to
go." This is grief enacted through concrete behavior (the unsayable retort, asking the
empty room in Greek, drinking the water cold). Excellent -- no announcement.

However:
- [MAJOR] ch42 L91: "I am not going to write what it was to watch it [the pyre]." This is
  negative narration of grief -- announcing the withholding of the feeling. It is framed
  thematically (Heras taught him not to narrate grief), but it still *states* "I won't
  tell you how much this hurt," which is announcing weight by refusing to. Compare the
  superior enacted version 8 lines later.
- [MINOR] ch42 L33 "I want to say that was easier, and it was not" announces the emotional
  category before the scene earns it (see above).
- [MINOR] The deathbed speeches (L51-75) render Heras's character beautifully but lean
  info-delivering/thesis-stating (see dialogue category). The grief is in the room; the
  theme is occasionally spoken aloud rather than left under.

Verdict on ch42: grief is predominantly placed inside the reader (the empty-bench close
is a model of the technique the Bad writing doc asks for). One announcement slip (L91)
and the usual thesis-forward dialogue keep it from being clean.

## SUMMARY OF RECURRING TICS (priority order)
1. [BLOCKER] Gnomic aphoristic scene/chapter closings -- nearly every break.
2. [BLOCKER] Scene closings restating the takeaway, esp. the "numbers/loads stayed where
   I set them / had no far side" refrain (ch34, ch35, ch36).
3. [BLOCKER] "not X, it was Y" correctio -- direct style-guide violation, recurring.
4. [MAJOR] Templated openings (aphorism + paradoxical "because" reframing).
5. [MAJOR] Exposing subtext / announcing emotional weight ("which is the trouble").
6. [MAJOR] Repetition across scenes (failing eyes, map-box, Marcia's edge-squaring).
7. [MAJOR] Info-delivering thesis dialogue (Marcia ch38-39, Heras deathbed) -- contrast
   Hadrian, who is the model of human/evasive speech.
8. [MAJOR] Narrator stepping outside time (ch36 L133; ch37 L75; ch41 L87).
9. [MINOR] Tricolons; "dry up-from-the-chest laugh" hardening into a tag; uniform soft
   "almost/I think" hedging.

Strongest writing: Hadrian's testing dialogue (ch35-36); the ch42 empty-bench grief
close. These show the book can render without labeling -- the fix is to trust that mode
and cut the appended wisdom-buttons.
