### Repeated phrases / motifs (intentional vs overused)
- **"wait for the bubbles / the water to boil"** — deliberate recurring refrain in
  ch28/29/30 (war chapters), used as a steady-floor image. Intentional and effective,
  but it closes ch28, ch29, AND ch30 on essentially the same image. [MINOR] Three
  consecutive war chapters ending on "the water to boil" is the single most
  repetitive cross-scene beat in the range; ch30's brief is "ends poised," which it
  does, but the closing fire/boil image is now thrice-used in a row.
- **Celer's buckle/strap "worked his thumb / the way Macer worked his ring"** —
  deliberate object-grief motif, ch29→30→31→32→33, explicitly handed down to Lucanus.
  Strong and intended; not overused.
- **"the box under the floor / two countries that cannot write to each other"
  (phone + citizenship tablet)** — ch24, ch27, ch31, ch33. Intentional thread.
- **[MAJOR — the one genuine range-level overuse] Gnomic "which is the X-est thing
  there is / the only kind that Y" aphoristic close-out cadence.** This appears in
  nearly every chapter (ch23 L49/L79/L111; ch24; ch25; ch27 L93; ch33 L23/L63/L79/L93)
  and is the prose's default way of landing a paragraph. Each instance lands and QA
  cleared each individually, but cumulatively across eleven chapters it is the
  closest thing to an AI "announcing-the-takeaway" mannerism in the range. Flag for a
  cross-chapter pass to vary the paragraph-ending rhythm.
- **"high ground / they come for you on high ground"** (ch24) and **"smell on a
  coat / beard smells of smoke" (ch31/32/33)** — both well-controlled, not overused.
