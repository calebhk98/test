# Historical Accuracy & Divergence — First Half (ch01–30, ~98–117 AD)

Scope: prose chapters ch01–ch30; checked against `bible/03_timeline.md`,
`bible/05_world_rules.md` (rule 8), `research/history_facts.md`, and
`bible/04_tech_schedule.md`. The first half runs from Daniel's arrival (spring
98) to Trajan's death (Aug 117) and the news of Hadrian's contested accession.

Note on chapter/timeline mapping: the prose is somewhat MORE compressed than the
beat-sheet in `03_timeline.md`. The Forum/Column dedication, the Hadrian cameo,
and the impiety crisis (timeline beats "ch31–32") actually land in ch25–27, and
the Parthian War + Trajan's death (timeline "ch32–36") land in ch28–30. So
ch01–30 already covers the entire 98–117 span. This is a packaging difference,
not a factual error.

## PART 1 — ACCURACY OF THE REAL HISTORY THE BOOK LEANS ON

Verdict: the era detail is correct throughout. No wrong dates, no anachronisms
presented as real, and the one famous "error" (China/gunpowder) is deliberately
placed in Daniel's fallible memory, not in the narrative voice. Itemized:

1. **Nerva's death / Trajan's accession (98).** Correct and well used. ch01
   timeline + prose put the arrival in spring 98 with the emperor ABSENT —
   ch07's adventus scene and `03_timeline.md` both have Trajan still on the
   Rhine/Danube frontier through 98. Matches `history_facts.md` (~27/28 Jan 98;
   Trajan does not rush to Rome). The "emperor physically absent from the
   capital in 98" point is exploited exactly as the research flagged.

2. **Trajan's adventus (99).** ch07 dramatizes it correctly: enters Rome on
   foot, a deliberately modest entry, greeting senators as equals as a contrast
   to Domitian ("modestly, modestly" passed through the crowd). Accurate.

3. **First Dacian War (101–102).** ch12–14. Trajan crosses the Danube;
   Apollodorus builds bridging; war ends in a client-king peace with Decebalus
   keeping his head and most of his hills, the forts torn down, engineers/
   standards/deserters returned, "friend of Rome" status. This is the correct
   real outcome of the first war (an intermission, not annexation). Accurate.

4. **Apollodorus of Damascus & the Danube bridge.** ch12 shows the FIRST-war
   timber-on-cofferdam bridging (pile-driving, cofferdams, dewatering) — correct
   technique. ch18 then correctly distinguishes the SECOND-war permanent **stone
   bridge at Drobeta** (stone piers, timber superstructure, army crossing on the
   march). The two-stage treatment matches the real record. Apollodorus
   characterized accurately as the emperor's engineer.

5. **Second Dacian War + Sarmizegetusa 106 + Decebalus.** ch18. Larger army,
   legions pulled from Rhine/East; crossing on the stone bridge; siege of the
   hill-fort capital (murus dacicus described correctly); the water supply cut as
   a siege method (matches `history_facts.md` §7, "cutting the water supply (how
   Sarmizegetusa fell)"); Decebalus commits suicide (cuts his own throat) rather
   than be led in a triumph, head and right hand carried to Rome. All correct.
   Dacia annexed as a province. Correct.

6. **Dacian gold / the games.** ch18–19. Enormous gold loot floods Rome and pays
   for an extraordinary span of games and a donative. The prose is careful NOT to
   nail the inflated ancient figure (123 days / 107) into the narrative voice;
   ch19 says "a span of days I didn't believe when somebody first said the
   number," which mirrors the research's own [EST]/"treat as inflated" flag on
   the 123-days figure. Good handling — the dubious number is kept as rumor.

7. **Trajan's Forum & Column (112–113).** ch26. Forum, basilica with gilt-bronze
   roof, libraries flanking the Column, the spiral relief narrating the Dacian
   wars, captured-Dacian statuary, the equestrian statue. Column still being
   finished on the lower turns. The detail that the Column "survives today" /
   outlasts the province is delivered through Apollodorus's mouth as prophecy and
   is historically apt. Accurate, and thematically deepened (the relief as
   official memory that omits Daniel's dead soldier).

8. **Parthian War (113– ), Armenia/Mesopotamia, Ctesiphon (116), Persian Gulf.**
   ch28–29. Armenia annexed/king deposed (ch28: "Armenia's ours now… made it a
   province"); the supply line driving east; **Ctesiphon taken** and the army
   reaching the **bitter southern sea / Persian Gulf**, with Trajan's famous
   wistful remark about wishing he were younger to cross over after Alexander
   (ch29) — this is a real recorded anecdote, well placed. The rear-area
   **revolts/Kitos War (115–117)** are rendered correctly as a diaspora rising
   plus Mesopotamian revolt that cuts the overreached supply line. Daniel, as a
   limited observer, hears it "wrong" (as madness/treachery), which is exactly
   how the research frames a Roman's-eye view. Accurate; honest about the POV.

9. **Trajan's death (8 Aug 117, Selinus in Cilicia).** ch30. Dies of a stroke at
   the coastal town of Selinus while withdrawing west; the "eighth day of the
   month"; news takes ~a fortnight to reach Daniel up-country. Matches
   `history_facts.md` exactly (117, 8 Aug, Selinus, stroke, withdrawing).
   Accurate.

10. **Hadrian's accession (117).** ch30. Hailed at Antioch by the Syrian
    legions; the deathbed adoption announced but widely doubted, with the
    suspicion that Plotina (the wife) and the Guard prefect at Selinus engineered
    it, and that the freedman who announced it then conveniently died. This
    tracks `history_facts.md` precisely (the [DEBATED] deathbed adoption,
    Plotina + Attianus). Correctly kept as rumor Daniel can't verify.

11. **Hadrian cameo (ch26).** The bearded gourd-loving architect Apollodorus
    despises ("go and paint his gourds," domes "like cut melons") is unmistakably
    Hadrian, and seeds the real historical Apollodorus–Hadrian enmity (Hadrian
    later exiles/executes him) without naming him. Foreshadowing is accurate and
    earned.

### Deliberate "errors" correctly located in Daniel, not in history
- **China/gunpowder (ch21).** Daniel believes the Seres "had fire-powder for
  centuries" by ~100 AD and uses it to rationalize releasing the secret — then
  the narrative voice explicitly flags it as probably his own misremembering
  ("I'd done the thing I always did, slid a fact two or three hundred years out
  of its place"). This is exactly the confident-mistake the schedule
  (`04_tech_schedule.md` Phase C) and world rule 3 require. Not a book error.
- **Misc. half-memories** (boiling-point lapse rate, the "third powder"
  saltpeter, pi digits, the clock escapement he cannot reconstruct, copper
  springs) are all rendered as Daniel's fallible recall, never as fact. Consistent
  with rule 3.

### Material-culture / institutional accuracy (sampled, all correct)
Wax tablets + stylus; papyrus vs. emerging codex; Latin hard-c and v→w; Greek as
the language of medicine/learning; miasma + humors + divine-wrath disease theory
(germ idea maps onto NOTHING — ch05); haruspicy/augury and the priestly colleges;
the manumission mechanics (informal *inter amicos* vs. full *manumissio* before a
magistrate; freedman takes patron's praenomen+nomen, so Marcus Ulpius Danihel);
imperial freedman status and what it permits (own property, sue, make a will,
marry); riveted slave collars; the salutatio/clientela; senatorial vs. equestrian
property and dress (broad vs. narrow stripe; Macer wears none); the grain dole
restricted to citizens; the night cart-ban; the marching camp dug nightly; the
flux killing more than battle; *posca*; the *peculium*. All match
`history_facts.md`. No anachronism spotted.

One micro-note (not an error): ch28 narrates Armenia's annexation as essentially
immediate ("between one new moon and the next"), which compresses the real
campaign but is delivered as a soldier's offhand summary, so it reads as POV
shorthand rather than a claimed fact.

## PART 2 — DIVERGENCE ASSESSMENT (vs. world rule 8: "history bends but does not
break on a whim")

### What actually diverges in 98–117
The honest finding: **the macro-history does not diverge at all in this span.**
Every dated public event happens on schedule and with its real outcome. What
changes is the *texture* of Roman life at the low/cheap end, driven by one
transplant. The introduced elements, in order of penetration:

1. **Hindu-Arabic numerals + zero + place value (ch03–04, 16, 24).** The single
   real spread. Critically, it spreads ONLY into low/cheap/commercial use —
   fish-sellers' slates, factors' day-books, schoolroom times-tables — and is
   explicitly REJECTED at the prestige level: "shopkeeper's marks," kept off
   contracts, tombstones, milestones (ch16). This is the correct, plausible
   shape: it saves an afternoon, so the suspicious merchant class adopts it; the
   elite won't put "Syrian shopkeeper" marks on anything dignified. The timeline
   keeps the monumental/official adoption for LATER (the ch44 milestone-with-zero
   ledger entry, ~135 AD), so in the first half the numerals stay believably
   secondhand and capped.

2. **Germ framing + boiling + washing + sand/charcoal filtration (ch05, 14–18).**
   Spreads as practice, not theory. Daniel can never prove the "little ones," gets
   them rebranded as Greek "clean water" wisdom by Heras, and the framing stays
   fragile, mockable, and (ch27) nearly fatal as an impiety charge. He keeps
   saying "the gods send them" so it maps into religion. Correctly limited and
   dangerous.

3. **Block printing (ch10, 16, 19, 24).** Stays gray, crooked, one-page-at-a-time,
   wasting one sheet in four; prints cheap copies of EXISTING texts, not new
   knowledge, plus the contest tales. No movable type. Honest cap.

4. **Hot-air balloon (ch06–13, 17, 18, 28).** The signature spectacle. Unmanned
   works; manned KILLS (Naso maimed, ch11; Sabinus dies, ch13). Daniel nails the
   lid shut and refuses to put a man up again, so it stays a tethered observation
   eye useful only in still air. The tech never escapes its real limits.

5. **The contest / cultural dreaming (ch16, 19, 24).** Seeds a generation to
   dream of machines (eagle-chariots, the no-rowers steamship, the moon-as-
   country, the ladder-to-the-stars). The clay-train toy in ch24 shows the dream
   drifting into folk culture. This is "divergence in heads," explicitly a
   century-long crop Daniel won't live to harvest.

6. **Gunpowder + the bursting cannon (ch21).** The most consequential leak. By
   the end of the first half the powder is "loose" — powerful men have SEEN it
   throw a stone across a river, and roughly know what it eats. BUT it is gated on
   steel that does not exist, so it's a demonstrated principle, not a deployed
   weapon. Cost paid in Geta's hands/eye.

7. **Steel, clock, optics, rails, plow/rotation (ch10, 15, 21, 23).** ALL gated
   or defeated. Steel: "we can't read the heat," gorgeous one batch, sugar the
   next — unsolved. Clock: the escapement is forgotten, only a lying water-clock.
   Optics: a dim, rainbow-fringed 2x toy sold to the rich as a fraud. Rails: works
   perfectly, loses to "slaves are cheaper" (ch23). Plow/rotation: muted,
   unobserved. These are the load-bearing brakes that keep the world from leaping.

### Is the divergence gradual / earned? — YES, strongly.

- **Macro-events are untouched.** Rule 8 says real events "happen roughly on
  schedule early; Daniel's influence accumulates and begins to deflect things
  later." In ch01–30 NOTHING real is deflected. The DIVERGENCE LEDGER in
  `03_timeline.md` confirms this: the first logged divergence is the Ocean
  Program at ch36 (~121 AD, Hadrian's reign) — i.e., entirely after the first
  half. The book honors its own rule: the first half is set-up; the bend starts
  in the second half.

- **Daniel's own influence is shown actively reinforcing real history, not
  bending it.** ch18 has him cut Sarmizegetusa's water — i.e., his knowledge
  HELPS produce the real outcome on schedule. ch18 explicitly raises the rule-8
  meta-question and refuses to resolve it: "Had it ended that way. Was I
  remembering it or watching it… I had no way to know, no number, no second world
  to lay beside this one." This is the cleanest possible dramatization of "bends
  but does not break": the protagonist himself can't tell if he's changed
  anything, and structurally he hasn't yet.

- **Every introduced tech is throttled by cost, material reality, social
  friction, or death.** Numerals capped at low-status use; printing stays crude;
  rails beaten by slave economics; steel/clock/optics fail; powder gated on
  steel; the balloon kills and is shelved. This is exactly the "nothing just
  works" discipline of rules 3–4 and the tech schedule, and it's what keeps the
  pace from running hot.

- **The [STORY] elements are kept plausibly secondhand and limited.** The map
  (ch03) plants two continents across the western ocean — the single biggest
  potential lever — but it's drawn by a mute prisoner, copied as a curiosity, and
  goes into a drawer; it does NOTHING for the whole first half except sit as a
  Chekhov's gun (the western-ocean idea only stirs in Daniel privately in ch28,
  and he recoils from acting on it). Thule is unfalsifiable and challenged by a
  Strabo-reader (Crispus). Daniel does not know the future precisely and IS
  blindsided by Trajan's death (ch30), satisfying rule 8's final clause directly.

### Pace verdict: ABOUT RIGHT, arguably even on the conservative side.

For a single high-school-level transplant over ~19 years, the empire's trajectory
is bending **slowly and believably** — if anything the book errs toward caution,
which suits the premise. By 117 the visible changes are: a better numeral system
loose among shopkeepers, some cleaner water and wound-care practices, a crude
press, a popular story-prize, and a demonstrated-but-unbuildable cannon. That is a
plausible "dent," not a transformed empire. No technology has leaped its real
material ceiling; no battle, conquest, succession, or death has been altered. The
divergence is overwhelmingly *cultural and low-status* (how people count, what
children dream), which is precisely the kind of change one clever outsider could
plausibly seed and could NOT control or accelerate. The genuine accelerants
(gunpowder, the western-map lie) are present but deliberately leashed: powder
gated on absent steel, the map dormant. The structure front-loads the cheap
unlocks and honestly strands the dreams on steel/chemistry/precision, so the world
is primed to diverge in the second half without having broken in the first.

Minor watch-items (not problems, just things the back half must honor):
- Gunpowder is "loose" by 117. The ledger/back half must keep it gated on steel so
  it doesn't quietly become a deployed weapon off-page.
- The numerals' jump to OFFICIAL stone is correctly reserved for ~135 (ledger
  ch44); the first half must not be retconned to have put them on anything
  monumental.
- The western-ocean lever is planted but unused; its first real deflection is
  correctly placed at ch36/~121, i.e., post-Trajan.

## BOTTOM LINE
(a) Accuracy: clean. Every real anchor (98 accession, 99 adventus, 101–102 First
Dacian War, Apollodorus's bridges, 106 Sarmizegetusa/Decebalus, Dacian gold/games,
112–113 Forum & Column, 113– Parthian War, 116 Ctesiphon/Gulf, Aug 117 Selinus
death, contested Hadrian accession) is present and correct. No anachronisms in the
narrative voice; the famous China/gunpowder error is deliberately Daniel's.
(b) Divergence: the macro-timeline does not diverge at all in 98–117 — by design,
per rule 8 and the DIVERGENCE LEDGER, whose first entry is post-Trajan (ch36). What
changes is texture (counting, hygiene, cheap print, cultural dreaming), all capped
and earned; the real accelerants are present but leashed. Pace is about right,
leaning conservative — exactly what a single HS-level transplant should plausibly
manage.
