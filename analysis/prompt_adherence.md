# Prompt Adherence Audit — *The Long Way Home*

Research-only audit of how the finished manuscript
(`/home/user/test/book/manuscript/The_Long_Way_Home.md`, ~197k words, 53 chapters)
follows the two source prompts:

- `/home/user/test/HS student Ancient Rome.txt` (premise + tech/idea list)
- `/home/user/test/Bad writing.txt` (prose rules)

Line citations below are line numbers in the single-file manuscript unless noted.
Note: the book deliberately uses **in-world vocabulary** ("the figures" for numerals,
"the little ones" for germs, "the Thulean" for the MC's cover), so literal greps for
modern terms ("numerals", "germ", "printing press", "cipher") return zero and must be
searched by period phrasing.

## B. THEME & PLOT-SHAPE ADHERENCE

**Overall: very high fidelity to the *spine* of the prompt, lower fidelity to the
*breadth* of the gadget wishlist.** The book chose depth and honesty over the
prompt's "include basic ideas for all kinds of technologies" maximalism.

- **MC only HS-competent, makes real mistakes (not a prodigy).** Fully delivered.
  Garbled long division and public humiliation (L356-358, L472); cannot remember
  saltpeter or the boiling-point-altitude rule (L140); gets filter layers wrong
  twice (L1546); the germ pitch nearly gets him killed (L450); springs, the clock
  escapement, and useful optics all defeat him (L1570, L2608-2622, L2652-2672).
  This is the prompt's central thesis and the book honors it relentlessly.

- **Anti-slavery is structural and "not accidental."** Delivered. He attacks it
  through economics, not speeches: rail carts meant to make porters uneconomic
  (which *lose* to the fact slaves are cheaper, L2702), and he learns the collar
  economics the hard way (L952-972, "slaves don't cost wages... don't quit...
  don't talk"). He buys and frees Pamphilus (L1930-1942) and finds freeing
  "smaller than I'd dreamed" (L2036). The bible states it outright (premise L24-26).
  Honest and unsentimental, matching the prompt's intent.

- **Literacy push.** Delivered via printing of cheap times-tables and books
  (L1684), the numerals spreading on fish-sellers' slates (ch16), and the contest
  sheets being hand-copied by the illiterate poor (L2170).

- **Loneliness / "only one who knows" spine.** Delivered powerfully and repeatedly:
  "the loneliest I'd been since the phone died" (L452); cannot share the song /
  his mother's voice fading (L2774); "the only thing worse than being stuck at the
  bottom of the staircase is being the only one who knows the staircase is there"
  (L2814); Heras as "one person on earth who heard past the accent" (L472). This is
  arguably the book's strongest delivery of a prompt theme.

- **Phone dying early.** Delivered. 98% at arrival (L18), airplane-mode joke in the
  cell (L136), rationing the battery, camera-roll grief (L392); the dead "glass"
  becomes a relic kept under the floorboard. Matches the "phone dies within two
  weeks, then human memory only" constraint.

- **Cover story.** Delivered (Thule / far-country scholar, never the future;
  L158-160).

- **Writing contest seeding sci-fi imagination.** Delivered and made a load-bearing
  theme-engine (ch19), explicitly designed to outlive him; the "ladder to the
  stars" contest entry becomes the trigger for the impiety prosecution (ch27,
  L3094) — a smart escalation the prompt only gestured at.

- **Ocean-redirection lie.** Delivered (ch36), with the extra honesty that he isn't
  even sure there's landfall within a year's sail, turning the "calculated lie"
  into a genuine moral knot (L3212, L3578).

- **Encyclopedia-as-ladder for after death.** Delivered as the climax of the
  book's structure (Parts Six-Seven), in English cipher, ordered simple-to-complex,
  "a ladder, not a shortcut" (bible + L2310, L2704-2706).

- **Trajan -> Hadrian rebuild.** Delivered (Parts Four/Five; Trajan's death, the
  power "Vacuum" ch31, re-establishing everything with Hadrian ch35, ocean gambit
  with Hadrian ch36). Matches the prompt's "re-establish every relationship from
  scratch."

- **Epilogue: someone begins to climb.** Delivered (ch52-53, "A Generation Later" /
  "The Far Whisper"); the final image is a pilot logging a true sounding off an
  unknown green shore "she did not know what it meant. No one did yet" (L5528) —
  an earned, concrete close.

### Where the book under-/over-delivers vs. the prompt
- **Under-delivers on gadget breadth.** The prompt's long bait list (appliances,
  submarines, carriers, 3D printers, maglev, jets, rockets, wristwatches, tea,
  compound bows, heated floors, ball bearings, soap, nitroglycerin) is mostly
  **absent**. The book trades the prompt's "name dozens of impossible things"
  maximalism for a tight set of deeply worked items. This is a defensible artistic
  choice but it is a real divergence from the prompt's explicit wish to "mention
  things they can not build at all" across many categories.
- **Over-delivers on honesty/failure and theme.** The humbling beats, the moral
  cost (his sanitation/powder knowledge *wins sieges and kills*, L2014-2036), and
  the loneliness are richer than the prompt sketched.
- **Tone shift on ambition.** The prompt's jokey "satellites by 300AD, go big or
  go home" is deliberately replaced by sober "I moved the bottom rungs," consistent
  with the bible's chosen tone but tonally different from the source idea.

## C. BAD-WRITING-RULES COMPLIANCE (book-wide scan)

### Em dashes (—)
**Compliant in prose.** Total em-dash occurrences: **8**, and **all 8 are in the
PART-divider headers** ("## PART ONE — Arrival & Survival", etc., L6/596/1552/2844/
3484/4380/5110/5436). **Zero em dashes appear in the actual prose.** The author used
commas and normal hyphens throughout, as the rules demand. (If strict, the 8 header
em dashes technically violate the "no —" rule and could be swapped for "-" or ":".)

### "not X, but Y" / "not just X but Y" / "it wasn't X, it was Y" (correctio)
**Largely compliant; a handful of mild instances.**
- The classic "not (just/only) X, but Y" pattern: roughly **6** matches, and most
  are not true correctio (they're ordinary "not in a single session, but in my head"
  type clauses). Examples: L140, L1568 ("not rust in a wound, but"), L3592, L3768
  ("Not a great man, but"), L4732, L5096.
- "it was X, it was Y" / "it wasn't X, it was Y" emphatic restatement: ~**11**
  matches (e.g. L72 "it was clean, it was", L202 "it wasn't dead yet, it was", L2438
  "it wasn't a sound, it was"). A few of these are genuine correctio (defining by
  negation then asserting), e.g. L202 and L2438. This is light for a 197k-word book
  and well within tolerance, but a strict pass would flag the L202/L2438-type lines.

No "not only... but also" stacked constructions of the egregious kind were found.

### Conjunction-led sentences (So, / And, / But,) — prompt *wanted* these
**Compliant / embraced.** Sentence-initial coordinating conjunctions are used
heavily and deliberately, which is what the prompt requested (syndeton; "So" as
consecutive conjunction). Counts of ". And/But/So/Or/Yet [word]" sentence starts:
And ~150+, But ~75+, So ~45+, plus Or/Yet. The narrative voice leans on "So I did
the only thing left" (L454), "And it was cheap" (L498), etc. This is a clear,
intentional match to the prompt's instruction.

### Other "bad writing" patterns (spot impression)
The first-person retrospective voice does sometimes "narrate from outside time"
(e.g. "I have spent years now hearing the bag described as wide as a house", L736)
and annotate significance, which brushes against the "Narrator Stepping Outside
Time" rule — but this is structurally a memoir/confession told by an old man, so the
retrospection is a chosen frame rather than accidental telegraphing. Beat-tag
clichés ("he chuckled", "furrowed brows") were not prominent in sampling. Imagery
tends to be cumulative and concrete (the gray crooked Homer line, mutton-grease
seams, the niter beds) rather than stacked-incoherent. Overall the prose rules are
followed closely.

## Bottom line
The manuscript is a **faithful, deepened adaptation of the prompt's spine and themes**
and a **selective, honesty-first subset of its tech wishlist**. It nails the central
argument (ordinary teen, real mistakes), the anti-slavery-by-economics attack, the
loneliness, the phone death, the Thule cover, the cannon-that-bursts, the contest,
the ocean lie, and the English-cipher encyclopedia ladder. It drops most of the
prompt's broad "name every impossible gadget" bait (appliances, subs, carriers,
maglev, rockets, wristwatches) and several specific small techs (soap, nitroglycerin,
compound bow, heated floors, ball bearings, tea, Romeo & Juliet), and notably
under-dramatizes the **water-wheel mills/looms/spinning** industrial-revolution
engine. Prose-rule compliance is strong: no em dashes in prose, conjunction-led
sentences embraced, correctio nearly absent.
