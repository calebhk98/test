## LOAD-BEARING THREADS

### 1. The phone relic — its final fate — **CLOSED**
- Opened ch01 (98%, the proof of where he is from); dies ch04 ("rendered plainly").
- Kept as relic, recurs ch12, ch17, ch20, ch24, ch28, ch33, ch44, ch48.
- **Payoff DECIDED and LOGGED in ch49:** the dead glass + Macer's ring + the
  citizenship tablet (`Marcus Ulpius Danihel`) sit in a floorboard box Tyche has
  guarded 50 years. Daniel rules it cannot be a "rung" (only a danger / witch's
  relic), refuses to pass it to Tyche, and orders it **buried with him** in the
  wrappings; the ring's worth goes to the craft-failure fund.
- Lands physically in **ch50** (death): the last image is the glass "warm and
  smooth and heavy in my hand that will not call anyone," back in the stubble field.
- The outline's ch49 instruction ("buried with him? given to Tyche? decide and
  LOG") is satisfied explicitly. **Fully closed.**

### 2. The English cipher — **CLOSED**
- Named ch20 ("homeland script"); taught ch40–41; the encyclopedia written in it.
- Secured ch49 (3 copies + lead-cased buried copy + open partial-Latin door off the
  Argiletum; keeper-chain written into the collegium deed: each keeper teaches ≥2).
- **Pays off in the epilogue (ch52):** Procula reads the cipher, decodes Daniel's
  marked-but-unsolved escapement note, and **builds the working clock escapement**
  Daniel never could. Ch53: the cipher's "rule of the box" is in every pilot's log.
  Exactly the "Plant long" promise (cipher seeded III, pays off in epilogue). Closed.

### 3. The ocean-map lie (Thule / western lands) — **CLOSED**
- Seeded ch03 (draws two western continents); identity-map ch03–04.
- Germinates ch28; **deployed ch36** ("The Ocean Gambit") to Hadrian: uninhabited
  western lands a year's sail off, reachable only with ships Rome lacks → redirect
  ambition to sea/shipbuilding/navigation.
- Ship program crawls forward ch37, ch44 (western slipway at the Garonne).
- **Pays off ch53:** a ship crosses, using the marine-chronometer "box," and makes
  landfall — and the land is **inhabited** (fires, boats, people), quietly
  correcting the "uninhabited" lie honestly. The "lie's long fuse" lands. Closed.

### 4. The writing contest's recurring young entrant — **CLOSED**
- Contest launched ch19; the outline asks for "a particular young entrant or story
  that recurs later."
- **The named entrant is FELIX** (intro ch19): a literate slave who dreams the
  steam ship; Daniel awards him the prize, which buys toward his freedom.
- Felix recurs as the standing name of the steam dream: ch39 (Daniel visits aged
  Felix; he still chases the self-feeding pot), ch44, ch45, ch46, ch47 (the
  escapement box willed to "Felix's workshop / craft-failure fund"), ch49 ("a Felix

- The contest itself becomes an institution (ch39 endowed via Minerva) and is still
  held in the epilogue (ch52–53). Recurring named entrant + payoff: closed.

### 5. Vibenius's debt / omen owed after the trial (ch27) — **PARTIALLY CLOSED**
- Opened ch27: Vibenius lets the liver read "sound" and saves Daniel; Daniel notes
  he now holds "a debt I could not see the figure of," deliberately unreadable.
- Carried forward as anxiety ch30 (will his house "lean" in the new reign?), ch31.
- **Resolved by DEATH, not by collection:** ch39 states "Vibenius was four years
  dead by then," and Daniel reflects that he built the contest "half to honor the
  hook he had set in me and half to slip out of it," with "no one left who could say
  whether it counted as the lie coming true or the truth put to use."
- This is a deliberate non-payoff: the debt is **never called**; its meaning stays
  permanently ambiguous (matching the "ambiguous omen" design). Thematically closed,
  but the literal debt is dissolved by his death rather than discharged — so the
  concrete "what is owed" is left unanswered **on purpose**. Mark **partially
  closed** (intentional open question, not a forgotten thread).

### 6. Crispus's ultimate fate — **PARTIALLY CLOSED**
- Antagonist II–IV: needling (ch10), fraud charge (ch16), the impiety prosecution
  he architects (ch25, ch27).
- After ch27 he loses (the gods "declare for the barbarian"). He resurfaces as a
  named threat in the succession terror (ch33 — "a man... who has wanted you in a
  net for fifteen years"), but the prose does **not depict him acting** then, and
  gives him **no on-page death or downfall scene.**
- His only "final" status is reported obliquely in ch51 (the post-mortem Roman
  letter): his coined phrase "the barbarian's heaven by ladder" outlives him; the
  writer notes the phrase keeps its ring "long after the man who said it is proved a
  fool or a knave." Mentioned again ch35 (Hadrian wields the same charge).
- So Crispus's *idea* outlives him and he is implied dead by ch51, but he has **no
  dramatized fate** — he simply drops out after ch33. **Partially closed** (faded,
  not resolved on-page).

### 7. Scaeva's ultimate fate — **PARTIALLY CLOSED**
- Opened ch25–27: senator who spends weight to save Daniel at the trial, making
  Daniel "Scaeva's man," holding the sulfur secret and an unfilled "point the powder

- The danger this poses is examined ch28, ch30, ch31 ("I'm not sure whose man Scaeva

  death, no later reckoning, and the "point the powder" IOU is simply never called.
  The relationship is closed; the man's own end is **never shown.** Partially closed.

## NOTES ON CLASSIFICATION

- The three PARTIALLY-CLOSED character threads (Vibenius, Crispus, Scaeva) are all
  "soft" closures: the *relationship/danger* each posed is resolved (debt dissolved
  by death; antagonist defeated then faded; patron sheds Daniel), but none of the
  three gets a dramatized personal end, and the Vibenius and Scaeva IOUs are
  literally never collected. For Vibenius this is plainly intentional (the ambiguity
  is the point); for Crispus and Scaeva it reads more as the men simply dropping out
  of a long book than as a deliberate open question.
- The six DANGLING items (horse collar, spinning wheel, water power, soap,
  distillation, and the "all tracks lead to Rome" line) are **tech/bible-level
  intentions, not chapter-brief "Plant:" lines.** None was seeded into a
  `chapter_list.md` brief, so the narrative never opens them as on-page promises;
  they dangle relative to the *design documents* rather than relative to a setup the
  prose itself made. **Distillation is the strongest miss**, because the master
  outline's own Part III summary names it as an accomplishment.
- Every thread that was actually seeded as a chapter "Plant:" line pays off, with the
  intentional ambiguities noted above.
