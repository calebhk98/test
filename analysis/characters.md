# Character Audit — *The Long Way Home*

Scope: roster completeness + profile adherence. Research only; no story files edited.
Sources: prose (`manuscript/The_Long_Way_Home.md`, 53 chapters, ~197k words) cross-checked
against `bible/02_characters.md`, `07_glossary.md`, `08_canon_log.md`, `03_timeline.md`.
Severity tags: **[BLOCKER]** / **[MAJOR]** / **[MINOR]** / **[NOTE]**.

---

## SECTION 1 — ROSTER COMPLETENESS

### 1a. Characters with real page-time / plot weight NOT profiled in `02_characters.md`

The character bible (`02_characters.md`) was written as an up-front planning doc and was
**never updated** as the prose introduced and developed characters. As a result a large
number of recurring, load-bearing figures established in the canon log appear in the prose
but have **no profile entry**. The bible profiles 11 principals + 6 historical figures and
stops there.

- **[MAJOR] ULPIA SEVERA** — Daniel & Marcia's daughter (introduced ch38, recurs ch39-50;
  "Ulpia" appears 50x in prose). She is a major late-book character: fierce at figures,
  cipher-keeper candidate, the daughter-question thread (ch41), reads Daniel's failing eyes'
  work aloud (ch43-47), holds the books and the African survey after Marcia dies. The audit
  brief explicitly asked whether the daughter is profiled. **She is not.** Neither is the son
  **Lucanus** (Marcus Ulpius Lucanus, ch26+, the law-track child who rejects the workshop,
  34x). The bible's Marcia entry mentions "any children" abstractly but no child is profiled.

- **[MAJOR] TYCHE'S status as a top-tier principal under-served by her own profile** — she IS
  profiled, but see 1b/Section 2; flagged here because her late-life role (runs the whole
  enterprise ch34+, sole cipher reader, sits Daniel's deathbed ch50) far exceeds the
  "apprentice/copyist" scope the profile sketches.

- **[MAJOR] PAMPHILUS** — collared porter bought and freed by Daniel (first person he frees,
  ch10/18), then lifelong freedman/client at the yard through ch48 (71x). Thematically central
  to the slavery thread. **Not profiled.**

- **[MAJOR] FELIX** — the planted epilogue-climber seed (ch19, ch39, ch44, ch52 backstory);
  the craft-failure-fund beneficiary whose failures feed Procula's clock solution. Coordinator
  flagged him "IMPORTANT - track him." **Not profiled** (26x).

- **[MAJOR] PROCULA** — the ch52 epilogue protagonist who cracks the escapement / builds the
  first working clock; the entire ladder thesis pays off through her. **Not profiled.** (See
  the critical naming issue in 1c — Procula is a DIFFERENT person from Ulpia.)

- **[MINOR] Apprentice / keeper-chain figures, none profiled:** VITALIS (press apprentice,
  ch39+, 18x), CHLOE (Bithynian girl, cipher keeper, freed in Marcia's will, ch41-52, 21x),
  ZOTICUS (Heras's successor physician, ch42-50, 14x), EUDEMUS (fiscus clerk, the route to
  Hadrian, ch34-45, 9x), THEOPHANES (ch37, 6x), "old Vibia" (Procula's cipher teacher, ch52),
  SATURIUS (craft-fund failure, ch52). These are the institution/keeper-chain cast — the
  machinery of the book's back half — and the bible has no concept of them.

- **[MINOR] Named victims of Daniel's inventions, none profiled:** NASO (burned recruit ch11,
  recurs as a wordless conscience-figure through ch29, 23x), SABINUS (the man who dies in the
  manned-balloon disaster ch13 — the moral pivot of Part II), GETA (powder-accident, ch21,
  maimed, kept on lifetime wages, 14x). Sabinus in particular is one of the most important
  named characters in the book (his death defines Daniel's no-manned-flight vow and recurs as
  late as ch26/29) yet exists nowhere in the bible.

- **[MINOR] Recurring craftsmen/staff:** HERMES IS profiled. But STICHUS (coal-boy → grown
  yardsman, ch05-31, 23x), EROS (letterer/pressman, ch16-48, 33x), ONESIMUS (senior accountant
  resisting the new figures, ch10-20, 14x), DEMETRIOS (lens grinder, ch23-26), MAXIMUS
  (quarry/rail demo, ch23) are unprofiled recurring figures. Most are legitimate walk-ons drawn
  from the glossary name-stock; STICHUS, EROS, ONESIMUS arguably cross into "recurring."

- **[NOTE] Minor patrons/hosts** drawn correctly from stock: METTIUS (salon host, renamed from
  "Vibius" per canon log to avoid Vibenius clash), CAEPIO (Marcia's dead first husband),
  SCAURUS (Manius Aemilius Scaurus, ch27 trial host), CAESIUS (Lucanus's law mentor), BITUITUS
  (Gaulish yard-master). Correctly handled as walk-ons; no profile needed.

**Verdict 1a:** The bible is materially incomplete as a roster of who-matters. The daughter
(Ulpia Severa), Pamphilus, Felix, Procula, Naso, Sabinus, and the entire keeper-chain are
unprofiled despite carrying significant plot. This is a documentation gap, not a prose defect —
the prose itself develops them consistently via the canon log.

### 1b. Profiled characters who barely appear / never pay off

- **[MAJOR] PLINY THE YOUNGER** — profiled in the bible as a "perfect... cameo" (a Roman-document
  interlude letter mentioning the Thulean). The interlude (ch51) WAS written — but the letter is
  from invented characters **Gaius Norbanus Rufus** to **Sextus Caecilius Priscus**, NOT Pliny.
  Pliny appears **0 times** in the prose. The profiled cameo never happened; a different vehicle
  replaced it. (The bible flags it "cameo only," so low harm, but it is a profiled figure who
  never pays off.)

- **[MINOR] PLOTINA** — profiled as "engine of the contested succession." In prose she appears
  **once**, named only in passing inside Macer's rumor-dump (ch30/31); she is otherwise "the
  wife." Consistent with the bible's "use for intrigue" intent but very thin.

- **[MINOR] ATTIANUS** — profiled alongside Plotina; appears 3x, all as offstage rumor (the
  Four Consulars killings). Functions exactly as the bible intends (offstage engine) but never
  a scene character.

- **[NOTE] APOLLODORUS, HADRIAN, TRAJAN** all pay off fully despite "mostly offstage" framing —
  no issue.

### 1c. Naming inconsistencies

- **[BLOCKER — for the bible/timeline, not the prose] "Ulpia" vs "Procula" are TWO DIFFERENT
  CHARACTERS, and the timeline conflates them.** The audit brief and `03_timeline.md` treat the
  daughter as "a daughter 'Ulpia'/'Procula'" as if these were two names for one person. They are
  not. In the prose:
  - **Ulpia (Severa)** = Daniel & Marcia's freeborn daughter (b. ~118), ch38-50.
  - **Procula** = the ch52 epilogue climber, ~20 in ~190 AD, two generations later, explicitly
    **freeborn daughter of a freedwoman** "bought into the storeroom... freed in the great widow's
    will," NOT Daniel's blood (prose line 5444). The canon log (ch41, ch52) is explicit that the
    climber is deliberately NOT a blood descendant.
  - The `03_timeline.md` DIVERGENCE LEDGER (line 179-181) says "the ladder Daniel laid and
    **Procula** began... descended from **Procula's** escapement" — using Procula in a context
    (the daughter / the survey handoff) that the ch46 entries assign to **Ulpia**. This is a
    genuine cross-reference error in the timeline doc that could mislead a reader/editor into
    thinking they are one character. **The prose itself keeps them correctly distinct.** Fix is
    in the timeline/bible, not the manuscript.

- **[NOTE] Daniel's citizen name is consistent.** Locked at ch17 as **Marcus Ulpius Danihel**
  (12 full-form occurrences; "Danihel" 14x; zero instances of the variant spelling "Daniel" in
  the formal name — the canon-decision spelling "Daniel" was correctly superseded by "Danihel").
  Informally he stays "Daniel"/"the Thulean" throughout, and Macer keeps "Thulean/boy" to the
  end (ch48) — exactly per profile. No drift.

- **[NOTE] Tyche's legal name handled correctly.** Coordinator decision: law makes her
  **Ulpia Tyche**, she resents it, signs only "Tyche." Prose uses "Tyche" 172x and "Ulpia Tyche"
  **0x** — i.e. the legal form never surfaces on the page. This honors "she is 'Tyche' on the
  page always," though note the formal name never appears even in the legal/contract contexts the
  decision allowed (minor; arguably an under-use rather than an error).

- **[NOTE] Documented near-clashes resolved correctly in prose:** salon host Vibius→**Mettius**
  (vs haruspex Vibenius); press apprentice Onesimus→**Vitalis** (vs accountant Onesimus); law
  mentor Sabinius→**Caesius** (vs dead soldier Sabinus); ch27 host →**Aemilius Scaurus** (freeing
  "Caecilius" for the ch51 letter). All clean in the prose.

## SECTION 2 — PROFILE ADHERENCE (per major character)

### DANIEL (Marcus Ulpius Danihel) — narrator
- **Voice:** dry, self-deprecating, modern references no one gets, swears — consistent. Fakes
  confidence publicly, privately terrified ("worth something, here, meant a thing other men
  decide what to do with," ch08).
- **The "DON'T"s — held:** *Not made smoothly competent.* He flubs long division under pressure
  (ch04), gets the filter layer order wrong twice (ch14), clings to a half-remembered
  "three-quarters" powder ratio and burns pinches all winter (ch21), cannot build the escapement
  (ch23), grinds the quadratic's sign for "most of a morning" at 50 (ch40). *Never wins without
  cost:* the balloon maims Naso (ch11) and kills Sabinus (ch13); powder maims Geta (ch21); the
  impiety win costs a heifer + permanent priestly hook (ch27); the rail demo fails on economics
  (ch23). *Doesn't remember things a normal teen wouldn't:* memory explicitly decays (lost the
  high-altitude boiling rule, the steam mechanism, outer-planet order — ch02/ch40).
- **VERDICT: CONSISTENT.** Model adherence to the fallible-non-prodigy mandate.

### HERAS — Greek physician / skeptic & conscience
- Punctures the germ pitch on miasma/humors grounds, warns it is witch-talk, then quietly adopts
  what works (ch05). Quotes Epicurus/Hippocrates ("Epicurus, who has the great advantage of being
  dead and therefore unable to put men in baskets," ch05). Dry irony throughout
  ("the gods, perhaps, expressing an opinion of the new philosophy," ch08). Allergic to
  enthusiasm; "the future belongs to plumbers" (ch16). Dies of old age ch42, skeptical to the
  end, leaving the "be your own skeptic" charge.
- **VERDICT: CONSISTENT.** Exactly the dry skeptic/conscience the profile demands.

### MACER (Titus Flavius Macer) — patron
- Blunt, transactional, mean-funny; calls Daniel "boy"/"Thulean," compares him to mules
  repeatedly ("I have liked mules," ch09; "I bought you for less than a season's mules"). Owner →
  patron → client-relationship after ch17, fond-but-exploitative ("I find I like you. Do not let
  that comfort you"). Does NOT free Daniel; gouges him on Tyche's price (38,000 HS, ch20). Spends
  nothing to protect Daniel at the ch27 trial; tells him to "make yourself small... I will not
  burn for you" (ch33) — the cage/shield duality intact. Mean-funny to the literal end ("you
  sentimental fool"; wills Daniel only the ring, "who will know what it cost," ch48).
- **VERDICT: CONSISTENT.** Strongest single voice in the book.

### TYCHE — slave → freedwoman
- Terse/guarded young ("I can't... It's not yours to take off," ch09), grows sharp and fierce.
  **The "DON'T"s — held:** enslavement is NOT romanticized and gratitude is NOT saccharine — she
  explicitly refuses to thank him ("I'm not thanking you," ch20) and delivers a full, angry
  indictment: "what it was, every day, was you deciding how much of me Rome got to see... You
  don't know what it is to make yourself small on purpose every single day" (ch20). She has her
  own agenda and terms (real wage, her name on nothing of his, the court date kept). **The
  relationship is kept non-romantic** — the man/freedwoman gossip is rendered as a *danger to
  her* (ch20) and a discomfort Daniel navigates, never a romance. She outlives him and holds his
  hand at death reciting figures (ch50).
- **VERDICT: CONSISTENT.** All three DON'Ts honored squarely.

### HERMES (Gaius Julius Hermes) — blacksmith / steel
- Terse, physical, proud, scornful of theory ("Tell me one number... one I can do twice," ch15).
  "The fire is the master" recurs and hardens "into a kind of prayer he muttered while he worked"
  (ch15/ch21) — perfectly the man who chases repeatable steel and mostly fails. Casts the
  burst-cannon (ch21), cuts off Pamphilus's collar (ch18), funded by the failure-endowment (ch39).
- **VERDICT: CONSISTENT.**

### CELER (Quintus Marcius Celer) — army officer
- Clipped, competent, gallows-humored ("soldiers die," ch13; "mind your drain," ch29). Champions
  the balloon (ch11), takes the blame for Sabinus and bans manned ascents (ch13-14), the
  soldier's-bluntness friendship intact. **Dies ch29** of sepsis from a wound Daniel fails to
  save — the profile's "a cost the book can spend... where Daniel first sees his gifts kill
  people" realized (with the wrinkle that the *first* such cost was Sabinus, a private soldier,
  not Celer — an intensification, not a deviation).
- **VERDICT: CONSISTENT.**

### CRISPUS (Lucius Vettius Crispus) — intellectual antagonist
- Elegant, allusive, performs erudition; Strabo/Pytheas/"Hyperborean" (ch10), the fraud charge
  built on true facts (ch16), escalates fraud → impiety, coins "the barbarian's heaven by ladder"
  (ch24/25) and presses it as the accuser at the ch27 reckoning. Clever and not always wrong, as
  specified. Defeated but alive and hardened (ch27); his phrase survives into the ch51 legend.
- **VERDICT: CONSISTENT.**

### VIBENIUS (Aulus Vibenius) — haruspex / religion
- Grave, indirect, never shows his hand. Locks eyes ch07, pronounces the deliberately ambiguous
  omen ch08, weighs Daniel in silence ch16, lays out the impiety trap "the way a man who reads a
  liver lays the marks out" (ch25), and at the reckoning leaves it forever unknown whether he
  believed, was bought, or made a cold call ("He never once showed his hand," ch27). "It will do
  for the room" (ch27) is pitch-perfect. Dies offstage ~125 (his unpaid debt lapses).
- **VERDICT: CONSISTENT.**

### SCAEVA (Publius Cornelius Scaeva) — political danger
- Smooth, gracious, lethal underneath; speaks in favors/obligations. The sulfur-customs favor is
  a trap that maps Daniel's weak point ("I had handed him a map of where my walls were thin and
  called it small talk," ch25). Tips the ch27 room with senatorial weight, making Daniel publicly
  "Scaeva's man." Leash "handed to me with fruit" (ch28). Abandons Daniel by cold withdrawal, not
  denunciation, after Trajan dies (ch33).
- **VERDICT: CONSISTENT.**

### MARCIA — partner / wife
- Flat, blunt, transactional, sums-first ("everything is a toy until it makes money," ch22).
  A genuine partner, not a prize: refuses a wage for a profit-share, has her own agenda (secure
  her widow's estate, build the enterprise to outlive Daniel), proposes the marriage herself,
  catches the fraud Tyche missed (ch22), runs the enterprise from childbed (ch25) and from the
  shadows under her own name when Daniel must vanish (ch33-34). Unsentimental to the end; grief
  at her death (ch48) rendered through absence. The contubernium→citizen-marriage beat lands as
  specified.
- **VERDICT: CONSISTENT.**

### TRAJAN (historical) — distant force
- Soldierly, practical, a builder who likes engineers; fixed on the flux that kills more men than
  battle, impressed by honesty over flattery, "doing cold sums about men's lives" (ch17). Mostly
  a distant force; the ch17 audience is the single personal meeting (canon-confirmed). His death
  (ch30) blindsides Daniel.
- **VERDICT: CONSISTENT.**

### HADRIAN (historical) — the reset
- Greek-bearded, voracious, prickly, performs erudition (baits the Thule charge with
  Strabo/Pytheas), a ledger-keeper who "destroys men who make him feel small," repeats Daniel's
  good lines back as his own and watches Daniel let him (ch35). Co-opts the ocean vision
  ("The far shore is mine to find. Thule is yours to keep lying about," ch36). The succession
  resets every relationship (Part V). The Apollodorus enmity pays off (ch37).
- **VERDICT: CONSISTENT.**

### APOLLODORUS (historical) — peer/rival
- The one mind who can meet Daniel as a peer; wary mutual recognition, NOT friends. "A clever toy
  that does one thing" (ch12, reused ch13/24/26) and "a demonstration is a lie you tell on a good
  day" (ch12) land as the profile's signature lines. Pride/inflexibility planted hard ("you draw
  the picture first and ask the stone to agree," ch37) and is his undoing — the historical clash
  with Hadrian (exile/death) is used as the "late thread" the profile names. Daniel tries and
  fails to save him (ch37).
- **VERDICT: CONSISTENT.**

## OVERALL VERDICT

**Profile adherence: essentially clean.** Every one of the 14 major characters checked stays in
voice, function, and arc, and the bible's explicit DON'T notes (Daniel never smoothly competent
or cost-free; Tyche never saccharine, her enslavement un-romanticized, the relationship
non-romantic) are all honored with textual evidence. No voice drift or out-of-role behavior found
at the major-character level. This is a high-fidelity execution of the character bible.

**Roster completeness: the bible is the weak link, not the prose.** `02_characters.md` was not
maintained past initial planning, so it omits profiles for many figures the prose makes
load-bearing — most notably the **daughter Ulpia Severa**, plus **Pamphilus, Felix, Procula,
Sabinus, Naso, Geta**, and the entire **keeper-chain** (Vitalis, Chloe, Zoticus, Eudemus, etc.).
The canon log (`08_canon_log.md`) is the de-facto complete and accurate character record.

**One true cross-reference error to fix (in docs, not prose): [BLOCKER]** `03_timeline.md`
conflates **Ulpia** (Daniel's daughter) with **Procula** (the non-blood epilogue climber two
generations later). The prose keeps them correctly distinct; the timeline's divergence-ledger
wording should be corrected to attribute the survey handoff to Ulpia and the escapement to
Procula, and to make clear they are different people.

**Profiled-but-unrealized:** Pliny the Younger (profiled cameo) never appears — the ch51 Roman-
letter interlude was instead given to invented correspondents (Norbanus Rufus / Caecilius
Priscus). Low harm; bible flagged it optional.
