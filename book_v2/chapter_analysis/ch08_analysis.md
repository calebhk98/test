## 4. Monologue / Info-Dump Check
Vibenius's speech to the crowd after the bag comes down is the primary extended speech:
- "He said the sky had been shown a thing today. He said the gods send signs in many ways, in the liver and in the flight of birds, and that a fire that rose of itself and turned in the air and came down upon the city was a sign, and that he had read it. He let that sit. He looked at me, the burned hands, the wrong clothes under the borrowed tunic, the foreign face, the whole of what I was, and he said the foreigner from the cold edge of the world had sent fire up to the gods and the gods had let it rise, which no god does for a thing the gods hate. And then, in the same breath, in the same even voice, with no change in his face at all, he said that the gods had also turned the fire back down upon the rooftops of Rome, and that a wise city would consider, carefully, whether a gift had been shown to it or a warning."

This is narrated summary rather than direct speech (note the indirection: "He said...," "He let that sit," "he said the foreigner..."). It is a narrator paraphrasing an extended speech, not block-quoted dialogue. The extension is narratively necessary (Vibenius's ambiguous verdict is the chapter's pivot). The "whole of what I was" is a mild tic (see Section 6). No formal monologue violation, but the passage runs long on Vibenius's logic. A single physical beat mid-speech (what Daniel or the crowd does while Vibenius speaks) could break the static delivery the change notes flagged as a concern for ch09.
