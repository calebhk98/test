## 3. Scene-Ending Audit

The chapter has four sections separated by "---":

- **Section 1 ending** (first ---): "That was the trouble with the high ground. You could see everything from up there. You just couldn't tell who was up there with you." → WISDOM BUTTON. "You just couldn't tell who was up there with you" is a gnomic restatement of the section's theme. The chapter-title image (high ground) is packaged into a portable metaphor. Needs fixing.

- **Section 2 ending** (second ---): "I bought the train. I gave him more than he asked and told him to keep the patter, it was a good patter, and he thought I was a soft fool and I let him. / I have it still. It sits where I can see it. A clay engine that pulls nothing, made by a man who copied a dream printed by my press from a tale entered in my contest by a boy who will never know my name, sold to me as a marvel from a country that does not exist by a man who will go to his grave certain that somewhere in the cold north it is true. / That is what a rung looks like, I think, when you are standing on the one below it. It looks like a toy somebody made up." → WISDOM BUTTON. The last sentence ("That is what a rung looks like...It looks like a toy somebody made up") is a packaged aphorism. The image of the clay train itself is the stronger exit; the gloss removes the reader's discovery.

- **Section 3 ending** (third ---): The girl tells Daniel "it had to be, so the ones above could see it." Daniel: "I think about that more than I should." → WISDOM BUTTON / REFLECTION CLOSE. Daniel explicitly annotating his own ongoing meditation on the girl's line is a form of gnomic ending. Also: the girl's line ("it had to be, so the ones above could see it") comes close to a child delivering the scene's thematic capstone — see Section 5 below.

- **Section 4 / chapter ending**: "...in the loudest, fullest, emptiest moment of my whole strange life, and listened to the city's small fires, and waited, the way you wait on high ground, for whatever was coming up the slope." → WISDOM/IMAGE HYBRID. "Waited, the way you wait on high ground, for whatever was coming up the slope" completes the chapter's central metaphor. The change notes specifically say "ensure V2 does not append an explanatory clause" to the high-ground metaphor. The ending as filed appends a subordinate clause to the image rather than closing on the bare image. The final sentence is close to correct — "waited...for whatever was coming up the slope" — but the full sentence before it ("the loudest, fullest, emptiest moment of my whole strange life") is gnomic summary. Recommend cutting to: "I lay down beside her on the high ground I'd spent twelve years climbing to, and listened to the city's small fires, and waited for whatever was coming up the slope." (Cut "in the loudest, fullest, emptiest moment of my whole strange life" and the "the way you wait on high ground" elaboration.)

**Wisdom-button count for this chapter:** 4 (Sections 1, 2, 3, and chapter ending all close with gnomic statements). This far exceeds the limit of 1 per chapter and is the chapter's primary prose problem.

## 5. Voice & Character Issues
- **The girl's line:** "She told me, with the flat certainty of the young, that it had to be, so the ones above could see it." — This is a child giving what might be the chapter's thematic capstone line. The girl's insight (the torch must be big so those above can see it) directly illuminates the chapter's theme of the ladder. Daniel then says "I think about that more than I should" — which is Daniel explicitly canonizing the child's line as the day's deepest utterance. Per the character guide (RULE 5), "a child must not get the scene's thematic capstone line" and "Daniel must not canonize it as the day's deepest utterance." The girl-with-the-ladder is a named child character, not just a walk-on. Her line and Daniel's explicit statement that he thinks about it more than he should violates the rule. **Flagged as a voice issue.** Options: give the reflection to Daniel directly, or let the girl's line stand but remove Daniel's explicit endorsement ("I think about that more than I should" is the canonization).
