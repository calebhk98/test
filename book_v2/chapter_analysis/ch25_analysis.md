### 2c. Narrator stepping outside time (future vantage)
- "I have always held those two things in the same hand, because I learned that winter that a man comes for you most gently in the season when you have most to lose." — The opening sentence announces the chapter's meaning from a future vantage: "I have always held those two things in the same hand" declares the narrator's permanent retrospective connection of the two facts (son + senator). And "I learned that winter that a man comes for you most gently..." reports the lesson derived from the winter, from the narrator's future perspective. This is the banned construction: the narrator arrives from the future to announce the significance of what is about to happen. BLOCKER.

The "the way you show a horse the bridle before you show it the bit" applies Daniel's present understanding (at the time of narration) to the past event. This is permitted close-retrospect.

## 3. Scene-Ending Audit

The chapter has four sections separated by "---":

- **Section 1 ending** (Scaeva leaves): "The bearers took the weight up onto their shoulders all together, a small grunt and a lurch, and the box of him went off down the wharf, and I stood at my own gate and felt the specific coolness of a particular man having decided he needed me, and meaning to have me, and being patient, and rich, and old in the trade of having people." → QUASI-WISDOM BUTTON. "Old in the trade of having people" is a packaged characterization that summarizes rather than images. The physical action (litter departing, grunt and lurch) is stronger; the final clause interprets the moment rather than rendering it. Borderline.

- **Section 2 ending** (Crispus at the baths): "And he went off to the cold water, and I sat in the heat I no longer felt, having won the room and lost the afternoon, because he had what he had come for, which was me on record knowing the word." → QUASI-WISDOM BUTTON. "Having won the room and lost the afternoon" is a chiastic summary-aphorism. The physical image (I sat in the heat I no longer felt) is interrupted by the explanatory clauses.

- **Section 3 ending** (Vibenius at the gate): "And he went out through the gate on foot, an old man on the river stones in the cold, no litter, no name given, leaving me holding a debt with no figure on it, a thing I could not pay down by a denarius the way I had thought to pay Scaeva, a question filed against my name eleven years ago and not yet answered, and now, by his own telling, soon to be called." → IMAGE CUT (mostly good). Ends on the concrete image of the priest walking away and Daniel holding a figureless debt. The final clause ("a question filed against my name eleven years ago and not yet answered, and now, by his own telling, soon to be called") is close to wisdom-button but stays more concrete/situational than gnomic. The image of the old man on the river stones is the best image cut in this section.

- **Chapter ending** (last section, no "---"): "...and stood in the warm lamplight of the house I had built on the high ground and listened, under the women's voices, for feet on the slope." → IMAGE CUT (good). The chapter ends on a physical sensory act (listening for footsteps), in domestic space. No gnomic summary. Clean.

**Wisdom-button count for this chapter:** 2 (Sections 1 and 2 closings); Sections 3 and chapter ending are clean. Within the 1-per-chapter limit technically, these are two, but the chapter ending is clean. Still one over limit.
