## 2. Banned Pattern Violations

### 2a. Correctio / "not X, it's Y" (ZERO TOLERANCE)
- "It was not pain, exactly." — This is a textbook correctio construction. The sentence continues: "It was the wind going out of me and not coming back when I called for it." Together these two sentences enact the full banned pattern: "It was not pain, exactly. It was [description of what it actually was]." This is a BLOCKER. ### 2c. Narrator stepping outside time (future vantage)
- "That is the step that told me." (Opening sentence, referring to the dished step on the wharf) — This is the narrator, from his later vantage, assigning significance to the step. The sentence introduces the chapter's action by flagging this particular step as the one that "told" him something. This is mild future-assignment of importance — the narrator arrives with foreknowledge that this step will be significant. Borderline; the chapter is entirely retrospective memoir and the narrator immediately describes what happens, so it does not generate dramatic irony by withholding. However, the construction "that told me" is a significance-flag. Compare the style guide's "I did not know then what that meeting would cost him" (banned) versus "I know now that the meeting cost him more than he showed" (permitted). "The step that told me" operates like the permitted version: the narrator acknowledges retrospective significance without spoiling a plot beat. Acceptable.
