  applies to the *Second* Dacian War.

  water supply identified ~3 weeks sooner. Roman casualties DOWN ~35% from balloon recon.
  Dacia annexed on historical schedule but faster and cheaper.

  summary says 20–25%). The Jewish diaspora is UNCHANGED.
- **Tone:** "Daniel bends the arc, he doesn't shatter it." Historical tragedies
  (diaspora, border retreats) are unchanged.

1. **Communication lag** → Optical semaphore (signal towers every 10–15 miles): message
   travels 1,200 miles in hours not weeks. Achievable Phase B–C. The "empire fell because
   east couldn't reach west" speech to Trajan or Hadrian is explicitly required story
   material.
2. **Political instability** → Printing press publishes constitutional proposals for
   regulated succession. Pamphlet culture creates public opinion pressure on succession.
3. **Currency debasement** → Encyclopedia includes economic theory. Double-entry
   accounting at imperial treasury.
4. **Antonine Plague (165–180 AD)** → Daniel knows it's a disease; quarantine and
   handwashing work. Quarantine protocols reduce mortality 30–40%. The plague hits
   during his lifetime — THIS IS A MASSIVE STORY BEAT.
5. **Long-term** → Steam/rail for communication eventually (Phase E, late story or
   post-Daniel).

### By Age 35 (~116 AD) Milestones
- Rome has measurably better military (recon, better siege).
- Growing printing press culture.
- Hindu-Arabic numerals in official use.
- Daniel is extraordinarily wealthy.

### Medical Advances
- Better medicine: handwashing, germ theory concepts, basic sanitation.
- Quarantine protocols specifically for the Antonine Plague.
- He understands diseases spread through contact but cannot invent antibiotics.

### DISCREPANCY 9 — Planning Brief's Semaphore Description vs. Outline's Implementation
**Planning brief says:** "Signal towers every 10–15 miles: message travels 1,200 miles
in hours not weeks."
**V2_HISTORICAL_IMPACT says:** Relay stations "one day's visibility apart." At typical
ancient visibility (~20–30 miles in clear conditions), relay stations would be 20–30
miles apart, not 10–15. At 10–15 miles, the system would require twice as many stations.
V2_HISTORICAL_IMPACT says signals travel Rome-to-Rhine (~1,200 miles) in "6–8 hours" in
good weather. At that speed and distance, the 10–15 mile figure would require 80–120
relay stations. This is technically feasible but the planning brief's "10–15 miles" and
V2_HISTORICAL_IMPACT's "one day's visibility apart" describe different implementations.
**Severity:** Technical consistency issue. Minor. Neither number affects plot. The
"message in hours not weeks" result is the same either way.
