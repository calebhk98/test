## WHAT THE PLANNING DOCS SAY

### Required Story Beats (from V2_STORY_PLOT_NOTES.md Section 10)

1. **(Part I) Discovery moment** — Daniel loses money betting on wrong faction. Terni lapilli temporal vertigo: child scratching a tic-tac-toe grid in dirt. Daniel recognizes a game that will not exist for 1,500 more years. One compact paragraph.
2. **(Part I or early II) Sportsbook begins** — Before balloon contract solidifies. Small scale, no premises. Expected-value math rendered on the page. House edge: implied probabilities across all outcomes exceed 100%; Daniel keeps the margin.
3. **(Part II or III) First card game introduction** — Three-layer card design: pip count (visual recognition) + Roman numeral + Hindu-Arabic numeral on every numbered card. Deliberately designed for three audiences simultaneously. Game sequence as hidden math curriculum: Go Fish / War (recognition only) → 21/Blackjack (addition required) → more complex games. Instruction sheets shipped with each deck.
4. **(Part III) First formal casino** — Small, legal fiction, Macer-covered. Rough characters trying to muscle in. "Technically illegal; magistrate family has standing invitation."
5. **(Part IV) Poker introduction** — Bluffing mechanic causes cultural friction. Impiety accusation (hiding your true self from the gods).
6. **(Part IV or V) Flashcards — Tyche's accident** — Tyche invents them while teaching reading. Daniel recognizes and commercializes. Full product lines: numeral cards to merchants, letter cards to children.
7. **(Part V, optional) Lottery government partnership** — Scaled with printed numbered tickets.
8. **(Part V, optional) Spinning wheel / proto-roulette** — Bias is real; payout odds adjusted. Eventually triggers precision bearing manufacturing.

### Additional Planning Doc Specs

- **Sportsbook mechanics:** Daniel is the house. Implied probability across all outcomes deliberately set above 100%. He cannot lose in the long run if volume is sufficient. This is his "superpower" — expected value.
- **Card design rationale:** Three simultaneous audiences — illiterate (pips), Latin-literate (Roman numerals), numerically advanced (Hindu-Arabic). Not decorative; pedagogical.
- **Math curriculum sequence:** The game ladder is deliberate. Go Fish and War require only visual matching. 21 requires addition. Players are learning arithmetic without knowing it.
- **Chess:** Chaturanga does not yet exist in 98 AD. Daniel introduces it with pieces renamed for Roman military (legions, cavalry, etc.). **Delayed commercialization** — he notices the game has spread organically before he thinks to monetize it.
- **What already exists (Daniel refines, does not invent):** Latrunculi (Daniel introduces "Thulean variant" refinements), Tabula/backgammon (Daniel introduces modern ruleset), Terni lapilli / tic-tac-toe (already exists; temporal-vertigo beat only), Lottery (already exists under Augustus/Nero; Daniel scales with printed tickets and government partnership).
- **What Daniel invents from scratch:** Playing cards, chess (Roman variant), bingo, dominoes, poker, proto-roulette, flashcards (via Tyche).
- **Bingo:** Press-native product. Unique printed cards; no two identical. Caller + crowd format. Tavern entertainment sold to tavern keepers through the press. Does not exist in Rome.
- **Dominoes:** Printable or carvable. Numerical matching. Press product.
- **Cards wear out:** Repeat-customer model. Two price tiers: cheap paper for common use, linen-backed premium.
- **Casino political question:** Technically illegal under Roman law. Magistrate cover is a fragile arrangement. Rough characters attempt to muscle in.
- **Chess annual championship tournament** — Named in planning docs as a downstream monetization beat.
- **Roulette → precision manufacturing:** The commercial incentive to eliminate wheel bias creates a demand for precision bearings; this feeds the manufacturing arc.
- **Flashcard product lines:** After Tyche's invention, Daniel funds production of numeral cards (for merchants) and letter cards (for children) as separate commercial lines.
