# Master Outline — the spine

Seven parts plus a coda and epilogue. ~53 chapters, ~3,000 words each. The book's
argument: an ordinary teen with a normal modern education could bend history; the
bottleneck is courage, credibility, language, money, and order-of-operations, not
genius. Daniel is average-to-good and gets things wrong constantly.

## Part goals (what each part must accomplish)
- **I. Arrival & Survival (98).** Strand him, make survival the only goal, prove
  the world is real and lethal, get him from mute prisoner to "useful curiosity"
  via the map and the numerals. Plant the germ framing and the balloon decision.
  End: he commits to the balloon because it is cheap, his alone, and undeniable.
  [V2] Part I must also establish: lead pipes noticed and partially acted on (Daniel
  switches his own household, fails at scale); soap made on-page (tallow + ash lye);
  Eratosthenes demonstration with two sticks; Roman gambling culture discovered
  (dice loss, terni lapilli child, sportsbook idea forming); the first food crisis
  and his private horror; double-entry bookkeeping seeded in Macer's accounts.

- **II. The Man Who Flew (99-102).** The unmanned balloon makes him un-ignorable;
  Macer's patronage gives protection-and-a-cage; the workshop spreads the first
  techs; the army wants flight; the manned balloon kills, and war shows Daniel the
  price of his gifts. End: he returns from the Danube changed, asking how far to go.
  [V2] Part II must establish: stirrups observed and design provided to Celer during
  army contact; crossbow spanning lever sketched and pitched to Celer (the training-
  time argument, not the power argument); kite recon system operational by late 100
  for the First Dacian War (stable delta kite with tethered observer platform);
  first card game introduced at a workshop gathering (Go Fish or War, mixed company);
  sportsbook operation running through an intermediary (the expected-value edge,
  rendered without spelling it out); First Dacian War history measurably changed by
  kite recon (two ambushes avoided, ~900 fewer Roman dead).
  [V2] The flight-ban reframe: after Sabinus's death (ch13-14), the vow is stated
  on the page as "I won't put untrained people into an untested design" — a safety
  protocol, not an abandonment of flight. Within 5-7 years Daniel returns to unmanned
  and kite-class glider work, stating the distinction explicitly.

- **III. How Far to Go (103-110).** He pushes hard - steel, the bursting cannon,
  the press, the story contest, freeing Tyche, distillation - wins citizenship and
  Trajan's favor, gains Marcia, and runs face-first into the chokepoints (steel,
  optics, the clock, the un-adopted rail carts). End: a powerful, lonely high
  point; the hubris-or-duty question named aloud.
  [V2] Part III divergence checklist (all must appear by end of Part III / ~110 AD):
  - Stirrups in use by at least one cavalry unit (established in Part II, confirmed
    spread here: "the new foot-loops" referenced as spreading standard practice)
  - Soap sold/distributed from Daniel's workshop (even if niche adoption)
  - Rag paper produced (even if quality imperfect) and in use at the press by 110
  - Double-entry bookkeeping in Macer's accounts; at least one trade partner using
    Hindu-Arabic numerals without Daniel present
  - Handwashing discipline demonstrably reducing infection in one institutional
    setting: a specific military dispatch Daniel reads should note the contrast
    between cohorts using "the barbarian's water discipline" vs. those that don't
  - Magnetic compass in prototype by ~108 AD, demonstrated to Heras or a naval contact
  - Printing press has produced 500+ copies of one text circulating beyond Rome by 110
  - One small but real divergence from real history on-page: the trade route opened
    by compass enabling open-sea navigation; or a siege outcome changed by Daniel's
    engineering input
  - Volta pile demonstrated (at minimum as shock-and-electrolyze demonstration)
  [V2] Part III also seeds: heliocentrism stated aloud in conversation with Heras
  or the educated circle ("the old Samian was right"); fire prevention pitched to
  magistrates after any significant fire (with realistic political resistance shown);
  first formal casino operation (small, Macer-covered, attached to workshop space);
  early gambling empire phases established; germ theory put to a public test in an
  urban epidemic set-piece (dysentery or typhoid in one district vs. adjacent one).

- **IV. Too Valuable (110-117).** Peak influence = peak danger. The three faces of
  danger (Scaeva/Crispus/Vibenius) close in; a near-fatal accusation; the Parthian
  war pulls him east; then Trajan dies and blindsides him. End: the ground drops out.
  [V2] Part IV must establish: gunpowder leak incident dramatized and contained (one
  incident — craftsman approached by Parthian household, or letter with specific
  saltpeter questions — Daniel contains it actively, not passively); Atlantic crossing
  preparation foreshadowed (compass working by ~103-105, serious expedition planning
  by ~108-110 based on this); the ocean-map gambit germinating through Parthian War
  observation of overreach. [V2] Crispus's public credibility collapse must be set in
  motion here if not completed — one witnessed event where a prediction of his is
  visibly refuted. Vibenius: before his death (which should be at least partially
  on-page), Daniel calls the debt once (a specific favor Vibenius cannot refuse).

- **V. Starting Over (117-125).** Succession terror (Hadrian, the Four Consulars);
  the patron-web shredded; rebuild middle-aged from scratch; the ocean-map gambit;
  Apollodorus falls. End: Daniel finds footing in Hadrian's consolidating order.
  [V2] Part V must establish: Scaeva's debt-collection attempt deflected — after the
  Four Consulars, Scaeva makes one attempt to collect the "point the powder for me"
  IOU; Daniel deflects using institutional protection (the press, Hadrian's attention,
  the endowed collegium); Scaeva exits shortly after (one line confirming his death).
  [V2] Economic disruption / press backlash scene: scribes' guild petition ~122-124
  asking that the "barbarian's machine" be taxed per copy; Daniel witnesses this and
  does not feel good about winning. [V2] Atlantic crossing: compass is working, trade
  wind knowledge is immediate, Daniel begins serious expedition planning (~108-110).
  The first Atlantic crossing should be targeted for ~112-115 AD (Daniel age 31-34) —
  this means the expedition departs from within Part IV or early Part V. Daniel is
  alive for both departure and return.

- **VI. The Ladder (125-138).** Institution-building so the work outlives him; the
  encyclopedia and the English cipher; family and the daughter-scholar; Heras
  dies; small signs the trajectory has bent; the hubris-or-duty question at its
  sharpest under imperial brutality. End: Hadrian dies; Daniel is old; the work is
  institutional.
  [V2] Part VI divergence checklist (all must appear by end of Part VI / ~138 AD):
  - Gunpowder leak incident completed (if not in Part IV)
  - Patent law and limited liability proposed to Hadrian's legal circle; Hadrian's
    response (intrigued but not formally enacted) on the page
  - Contest endowed as a permanent institution, independent of Daniel
  - Water-powered bellows and trip-hammers operating in Daniel's forge complex
  - Volta pile demonstrated and thermocouple chain underway: compass-needle
    galvanometer demonstrated; Daniel attempting to source platinum from Hispanian mines
  - Newcomen-class atmospheric pump with at least one working trial (~118 AD in Hispania)
  - Encyclopedia's first section written and secured
  - At least one person outside the workshop independently improved on one of Daniel's
    introductions (craftsman with better pulp recipe; physician extending Heras's
    wound-washing into a clinical protocol)
  - Steam/rail argument made to Hadrian: "I knew an empire that fell because its east
    could not reach its west fast enough" — on-page, with Hadrian's response
  [V2] The contest flywheel (all nine loops) must be visible in this part through
  specific beats: a woman wins the contest (with real cultural friction); Felix
  eventually wins (his entry echoes Daniel's tech program); early entries are crude
  (soldiers on war, slaves on freedom — both make Daniel uncomfortable); the
  accidental parallel (someone writes a story describing technology Daniel is working
  on, independently). [V2] Protégé-who-goes-wrong: one person in the workshop or
  contest community misuses something Daniel taught — brief, but the scene must appear.

- **VII / Coda. The Long Way Home (138-155).** Decline and death; securing the
  ladder; the last deaths; Daniel dies far from home, dreams unbuilt, bottom rungs
  moved. A post-death Roman document.
  [V2] Part VII must complete: Atlantic voyage departs (~149 AD) with full navigation
  suite (compass, latitude gnomon, dead-reckoning charts, citrus provisions — Daniel
  names the symptoms even if not the vitamin, insists on this explicitly); Daniel
  watches it leave, knows the land is inhabited, knows his "uninhabited" lie will be
  corrected after he is gone. Variolation program groundwork established through the
  physician line. The "all tracks lead to Rome" line spoken (by Daniel, Felix, or
  a later voice in the contest community). Encyclopedia secured in three copies plus
  one buried lead-cased copy. All three antagonist exits completed by this part:
  Crispus's public discrediting, Scaeva's attempted debt collection and exit (if
  not completed in Part V), Vibenius's called debt and death (if not in Part IV).
  [V2] Food beat: tasting a tomato for the first time after return news arrives. One
  sentence. No speech.

- **Epilogue. Someone Climbs.** A generation on, the press runs and the contest is
  held; a young reader decodes the ladder and climbs a rung. A far whisper that
  Rome's arc bent. No tidy bow.
  [V2] The epilogue must confirm: landfall happened (the ship found inhabited land,
  correcting the "uninhabited" lie). The Atlantic crossing consequence: New World
  crops returning (corn, potatoes, sweet potatoes, possible cacao); Roman political
  ambition shifting from "hold borders" to "there is more world." The "all tracks
  lead to Rome" line lands here if not earlier. The ocean crossing is now a proven
  fact within the story's world, not a deferred future hope.

## Pacing notes
- Vary chapter texture: action (balloon, war, cannon), quiet (workshop, teaching,
  domestic), set-piece (Trajan audience, the trial, the disaster), interludes
  (Roman documents, encyclopedia excerpts). Avoid two same-texture chapters in a row.
- Tension graph: peaks at the unmanned flight (II), the manned disaster (II), the
  cannon (III), the accusation (IV), Trajan's death (IV/V cliff), the Four
  Consulars (V), Heras's death (VI), Daniel's death (VII). Troughs are the quiet
  building/teaching chapters; keep them short and warm.
- Plant long: the Thule/ocean lie (I) pays off in V [V2: and again when the actual
  crossing departs ~Part IV/V and is confirmed in epilogue]; the English cipher (III)
  pays off in the epilogue; the phone relic recurs and pays off at the death.
- [V2] The Atlantic crossing departure (~112-115 AD) is a major set-piece that belongs
  in Part IV or early V — it must be planted from Part I (the map's western continents),
  foreshadowed in Part III (compass prototype, expedition planning), and executed with
  full weight. Daniel on the dock as the ship leaves is one of the book's key images.
- [V2] The contest flywheel's nine loops should be traceable across parts: loops 1-3
  (literacy, reading, tech-dreaming) visible by Part III; loops 4-6 (press value, cultural
  influence, library system) visible by Part IV-V; loops 7-9 (press platform, paper value,
  idea-seeding) visible by Part VI, where the contest generates inventors who learned from
  other inventors, not from Daniel.
