## PRESENT AND CORRECT

- **Sportsbook seeded in Phase A, ch07**: The outline's ch07 entry explicitly reads "Sportsbook idea — Daniel watches Romans bet on chariot races. The house-edge principle clicks. One internal paragraph, maximum — a compact mental note, not a speech. Filed for later." This matches the reference spec (earliest passive income, mental note only, not a speech, before press/balloon money).

- **Card decks launched Phase B, ch16**: Ch16 states "Card decks designed and first run produced — with pips, Roman numerals, Hindu-Arabic numerals on every numbered card. First card game demonstrations in the workshop (Go Fish or War — no reading needed)." Three-layer card design is named, correct phase, correct games (no-literacy-required first).

- **Flashcard origin — Tyche's accidental invention**: Ch41 reads "Flashcards: Tyche invents them while teaching someone to read — she cuts numbered cards, puts the object name on one side and the numeral on the other. She does not recognize what she has invented. Daniel does. He funds immediate production." This matches the reference (Tyche's accident, Daniel recognizes and monetizes) and is placed in Phase E, ch41.

- **Casino operational by Phase C**: The arc status table (RECURRING ARC STATUS BY PHASE) correctly shows "casino operational" in Phase C (ch23–33), consistent with the reference's "Phase C-D" spec. Ch19 adds the note "Sportsbook operations beginning," bridging B→C.

- **Card games as a divergence sign by Phase E, ch44**: Ch44 explicitly states "Cards explicitly listed as the fourth divergence sign — or as one of four — on-page. The ledger currently omits this; it must be added." This matches the reference requirement.

- **Card games as background texture in later chapters**: Ch26, ch31, ch45, ch48 all specify cards/gambling as background texture — consistent with the "ubiquitous" Phase E status in the arc table.

- **Arc status table phases**: The RECURRING ARC STATUS BY PHASE table correctly summarizes the arc as: mental note seeded (ch07) → card games launched (ch16) → casino operational (Phase C) → established income (Phase D) → ubiquitous (Phase E). This phasing is accurate.

- **Math hidden inside game sequence**: The design logic (three-layer cards, Go Fish/War first, then 21) is preserved and consistent in ch16. The principle that the game creates the arithmetic demand without explicit instruction is captured in ch16's framing and in the arc's progression.

- **Card wear-out / repeat customer model**: Not explicitly called out in the outline as a scene beat, but the arc's "ubiquitous" classification in Phase E implies ongoing sales. See PRESENT BUT WRONG below for the stronger concern.

- **Political cover / darker texture for casino**: Ch19's interiority note ("The contest's dual purpose shown in Daniel's interiority without being announced") and the overall arc note that casino becomes politically protected by Part IV are consistent with the reference spec. The "rough characters try to muscle in" element is assigned to Phase C-D in the reference; the outline does not contradict it.

- **Tyche's later role**: Tyche is identified as the first person to learn the English cipher/alphabet (ch20) and is the flashcard inventor (ch41), consistent with her growing role in the arc.

## PRESENT BUT WRONG

- **Sportsbook operations described as "beginning" in ch19, not ch07**: The arc status table correctly seeds the sportsbook at ch07, but ch19 says "Sportsbook operations beginning" — implying actual operation starts at ch19, not ch07. The reference (V2_STORY_PLOT_NOTES.md Section 10, "The Sportsbook: The Earliest Opportunity") states explicitly: "This is his first independent income, before the balloon money, possibly before Macer's arrangement solidifies." And the required story beats list "(Part I or early II) Sportsbook begins. Small operation, through an intermediary." The outline plants the idea correctly at ch07 but delays operational launch to ch19 (Part II→III boundary), which is too late by roughly 10–12 chapters. There is no intermediate chapter between ch07 and ch19 where the actual sportsbook opening is staged.

- **Flashcard timing is Phase E (ch41), but reference places it Phase IV-V (roughly ch28–41)**: The reference (V2_MASTER_CONTEXT.md, Change 5, item 7) says "Flashcards as accidental educational byproduct (Part IV-V)." The required story beats (Section 10) lists it as beat 6: "(Part IV or V) Flashcards — Tyche's accident…" Ch41 is Part V (ch38–46), so the placement is technically within the stated range. However, the V2_STORY_PLOT_NOTES.md beat list suggests Part IV as the earlier edge (beat 6 listed between Part IV beats and Part V beats). This is borderline — arguably correct but on the later end of the acceptable range.

- **Bingo assigned to no specific chapter**: The arc status table and chapter-by-chapter entries contain no explicit mention of bingo at all. The arc table shows "card games launched (ch16)" and "casino operational" (Phase C) but bingo is not differentiated from the general card/games category. This means bingo's phase placement is ambiguous, but more concerning, the specific detail (press-native product, unique printed cards per player, tavern entertainment, recurring card sales) that makes bingo a distinct product line is absent. See MISSING below for fuller treatment.

- **Casino operational phase is correct but the scene showing the legal/political question being handled (Macer) is absent**: The reference (Section 10, "The Casino as Political Question") states: "The cleanest story choice: Daniel is cautious enough to ask Macer before opening anything formal. Macer either laughs at him for worrying or tells him exactly who to pay." Ch19 mentions sportsbook operations beginning but no chapter entry contains the specific Macer-and-casino political negotiation beat. This beat is named as "required" in the reference in the sense that the legal question must be on the page through some mechanism; the outline leaves it unassigned.

## MISSING

- **Bingo**: Nowhere in the outline. The reference (Section 10, "Bingo: The Press-Native Game") describes it as a Phase III product ("once the press is running efficiently enough to produce large batches of unique cards"), which would be approximately ch23–31. Required details: printed cards with unique number grids, numbered token bag, tavern entertainment format, licensed format + card sales revenue, no moving parts. The outline contains no chapter that introduces this or assigns it a beat. It should appear in the ch23–31 range.

- **Lottery scaling with printed tickets**: Nowhere in the outline. The reference (Section 10, "Lottery Scaling with Printed Tickets") specifies: printed numbered tickets (verifiable, harder to fake), published results in the press, multi-city draws, government partnership pitch (split take with magistrate to fund aqueducts/libraries), political cover. The reference lists this as a "(Part V, optional)" story beat but flags the government partnership as the mechanism that makes the casino legally clean "by definition" — making it higher-priority than "optional" suggests. No chapter entry mentions the lottery pitch to a magistrate or the printed-ticket scaling. Should appear in the ch34–46 range.

- **Discovery moment / terni lapilli (tic-tac-toe) beat**: The reference (Section 10, "What Already Exists: Games Daniel Finds, Not Invents" and required story beat 1) specifies: "(Part I) Discovery beat, two parts: (a) Daniel loses coins at Roman dice, catalogs the observation; (b) a few months later, he finds a child playing terni lapilli and has a moment of temporal vertigo he doesn't explain to anyone." The terni lapilli beat is described as "a small grace note" — the familiar in the alien. Ch07 plants the sportsbook idea via chariot races but contains no terni lapilli moment and no dice-losing scene. This beat is missing from ch01–07.

- **Go Fish / War first introduction as a scene**: Ch16 says cards are designed and first games demonstrated at the workshop, but the reference (beat 3 in required story beats) specifies: "First card game introduction. Go Fish or War at a workshop gathering, mixed company, wildly uneven social strata at the same table. The social reaction — confusion, then delight, then an argument about rules in two languages — is the scene." The ch16 outline entry does not describe this scene with the social-strata mixing and the argued-in-two-languages texture. It states the fact but not the scene beat.

- **Poker introduction with cultural friction scene**: The reference (beat 5) specifies: "(Part IV) Poker introduction. Card culture now exists. Bluffing mechanic causes immediate cultural friction: Romans are not accustomed to a game where lying is the correct strategy. Someone accuses the winner of impiety. This is a real scene." No chapter in Part IV (ch32–38) or the outline at all assigns this scene. The arc goes from "card games launched" at ch16 to "established income" at Phase D with no named poker beat.

- **Roulette / proto-roulette**: The reference (Section 10, "Roulette and the Precision Flywheel") specifies: a crude wooden spinning wheel with numbered sections; Daniel adjusts payout odds to compensate for known wheel bias; encyclopedia note that future casinos will fund precision bearing manufacture to eliminate bias ("future prizes in waiting"). Notably, the reference flags this as a Phase D-E element ("after steam-era metallurgy makes the mechanism reliable enough to run continuously"). No chapter in the outline introduces or mentions roulette.

- **Dominoes**: The reference (Section 10, "Dominoes: A Different Product Category") specifies printed card-stock dominoes, carved bone/ivory luxury sets, clay tiles; different games than cards; Macer buys a luxury set immediately. No chapter entry mentions dominoes.

- **Chess**: The full specification (from the user's audit brief) — Daniel introduces chess as "a game I learned from travelers," Roman pieces renamed (general, cavalry), printed boards and rule sheets, annual championship, chess betting, and Daniel initially ignoring then recognizing it as a significant cultural export — is entirely absent from the outline. No chapter names chess.

- **Latrunculi / tabula / existing-games-Daniel-recognizes**: The reference (Section 10, "What Already Exists") details latrunculi (Daniel introduces modern draughts ruleset as "the Thulean variant"), tabula (Daniel introduces modern backgammon as a rule variant), and terni lapilli (Daniel says nothing, just has a vertigo moment). None of these appear in any chapter entry in the outline.

- **Keno as a casino-floor game**: The reference (Section 10, "Keno: The Casino Format Lottery") specifies this as a continuous, fast-format casino game for non-card players, with the payout structure building in house advantage and the Hindu-Arabic numerals on the board serving as free advertising. No chapter mentions keno.

- **Word puzzles / press products for literate class**: The reference (Section 10, "Word Puzzles") describes word search grids, logic problems, number fill-in puzzles, acrostics as a separate press product targeted at educated buyers who find cards beneath them. No chapter mentions this product line.

- **Card wear-out as an explicit beat**: The reference (Section 10, "Cards Wear Out: The Repeat Customer Model") specifies that wear-out and replacement is the business model — casual buyers replace every few months, taverns weekly, casino daily. Linen-backed premium cards introduced at 3x price point as a second tier. No chapter assigns this as a scene or beat. The model is implied by the arc's longevity but never put on the page per the outline.

- **Sportsbook intermediary / small-operation launch scene**: The reference's beat 2 states: "(Part I or early II) Sportsbook begins. Small operation, through an intermediary. The mathematics of expected value, his only real competitive advantage this early, rendered on the page without spelling it out. The other bookmaker he's quietly competing with doesn't understand why he keeps winning." The ch07 outline entry gives a mental note only; no chapter in Part I or early Part II is assigned the actual launch of the sportsbook through an intermediary.

- **Expected value / house-advantage math rendered on the page**: The reference (Section 10, "The Casino Model") emphasizes: "He is the first person in Rome who understands probability and expected value. He knows that a 2% house edge on blackjack compounds to reliable income over thousands of hands... This is as close to a money printer as anything he builds in Phase A." The ch07 outline entry captures the mental click moment but does not assign any chapter where Daniel's probability/expected-value advantage is actually rendered in action on the page (him setting odds, calculating the edge, watching the math play out).

- **Instruction sheet / printed rule sheet with each deck**: The reference specifies that each deck ships with a folded woodblock-printed instruction sheet showing five games from easiest to hardest. This is part of the card product design that makes the arc's educational function work. No chapter assigns the production or design of this instruction sheet.

## QUESTIONS / AMBIGUITIES

1. **"Casino operational Phase C-D" vs. actual chapter assignment**: The arc status table says "casino operational" in Phase C (ch23–33), but no specific chapter in ch23–33 is assigned the casino opening scene or the Macer political consultation. Ch19 says sportsbook operations are "beginning" — is the casino implied to open around the same time, or does the outline intend a later opening? This is left unresolved.

2. **Sportsbook revenue as "earliest passive income"**: The reference is explicit that the sportsbook predates the press and balloon money and is Daniel's first independent income. The outline's ch07 plants the idea but delays the "beginning" of operations to ch19. If ch19 is when sportsbook begins, it is after the press (already running rag paper by ch10) and overlaps with the balloon era. The outline's phasing contradicts the reference's "earliest" claim without any explanation.

3. **Flashcard placement at ch41 vs. Part IV**: V2_MASTER_CONTEXT.md Change 5 item 7 says "Flashcards as accidental educational byproduct (Part IV-V)" — Part IV is ch32–38. The outline places the Tyche flashcard invention at ch41, which is Part V. This is within the stated "IV-V" range but the outer end. If the intent was Part IV, ch41 is wrong. If "Part IV-V" genuinely means either part is acceptable, ch41 is fine but the earlier window is lost.

4. **"Optional" vs. required for lottery and spinning wheel**: V2_STORY_PLOT_NOTES.md Section 10 required story beats labels beats 7 (lottery government partnership) and 8 (spinning wheel/proto-roulette) as "(Part V, optional)." The audit spec treats both as required elements. The reference file's own "optional" tag is unclear — does it mean these are lower-priority scene beats that may be condensed, or genuinely skippable? The outline omits both entirely; whether that is a gap or acceptable depends on whether "optional" in the reference means "required for the arc to work" or "nice to have."

5. **Keno and word puzzles**: Both are described in detail in Section 10 but are not listed in the required story beats at the end of the section. Their absence from the outline may be intentional (left to writer discretion) or an oversight. Clarification on priority would help a writing agent know whether to add them.

6. **Chess**: The audit spec includes chess as a required arc element with substantial detail (renamed pieces, printed boards, championship, betting, Daniel's delayed recognition of its value). The reference file (V2_STORY_PLOT_NOTES.md Section 10) does not mention chess at all. Chess appears only in the audit brief's user specification. It is unclear whether chess was inadvertently omitted from the reference file or whether it was deliberately excluded from the arc. The outline cannot be faulted for missing chess if the reference files don't require it, but the user's spec does.

7. **Backgammon / latrunculi spread "without him actively pushing them"**: Ch19's outline note says "Backgammon and the updated latrunculi rules visible in the background — these spread without Daniel actively pushing them" but no earlier chapter introduces these games. The implication is they were introduced off-page between ch16 and ch19, which is narratively thin. The reference says Daniel introduces the modern draughts ruleset and modern backgammon as explicit named beats; the outline appears to compress this to background texture.

8. **Sportsbook on chariot races and gladiatorial fights specifically**: The reference specifies both chariot racing AND gladiatorial fights as the objects of Daniel's sportsbook. Ch07 says "Daniel watches Romans bet on chariot races." Gladiatorial fights are not mentioned in any chapter entry, though they are the second major betting category the reference describes. This may be a condensation or an oversight.

9. **Casino "darker texture" / rough characters muscling in**: The reference (Section 10) notes: "The rough characters who try to muscle in are separate from the legal question and more immediately dangerous. He is not equipped to handle that alone. He needs Macer's muscle (early), Celer's reputation (middle), and his own security operation (late). The gaming business has a darker texture than the press." No chapter entry in the outline assigns this conflict. It is described in the reference as a real, ongoing arc element, not background flavor. Its absence from the outline is a gap.
