# Technology Schedule — what, when, how framed, what stays "bait"

Grounded in `research/tech_feasibility.md`. Phases map to the timeline. The rule:
nothing just works; everything costs money, failure, and social maneuvering.
Daniel front-loads the cheap unlocks and is honest that the dreams die on steel,
chemistry, and precision.

## PHASE A (98-99) — Survival-cheap, credibility-building
- **The map.** Not a technology, his first move with no language: draws the
  world, including continents Rome has never heard of. Buys him "curiosity, not
  threat" status. Also the seed of the later Thule/ocean lie.
- **Hindu-Arabic numerals + place value + decimal point.** His single best, near-
  free win. Demonstrable in week one; spreads through accountants over years.
  Honest beat: he garbles a long-division algorithm under pressure and is briefly
  humiliated before reconstructing it. Roman numerals keep prestige use for
  decades, so adoption is patchy.
- **The germ framing + boiling water + washing wounds + sand/charcoal
  filtration.** Framed as "tiny invisible creatures the gods send to climb inside
  you." Cheap, saves his own skin, socially camouflaged as quasi-religion, and
  dangerous (maps onto nothing; risks fraud/magic charge). Filtration clarifies
  but does NOT sterilize, so pair with boiling; he may overrate it.
- **Crude soap** (tallow + wood-ash lye): harsh and skin-burning until ratios are
  dialed in over months. Romans prefer oil-and-strigil, so it stays niche.

## PHASE B (99-102) — The balloon and the first spread
- **Hot-air balloon, UNMANNED first.** A small linen/paper bag that rises on hot
  air = "the man who made fire fly." This is the signature spectacle and
  credibility-maker. The **manned** balloon is the riskiest "easy" thing in the
  book: treated linen is heavy, leaks, and burns over an open flame. The first
  manned attempts must CRASH/BURN and hurt or kill someone (see world rules). It
  may stay tethered, low, and dangerous for years.
- **Crop rotation/legumes + better plow + horse collar.** Concepts he can
  describe; craftsmen build them. Romans already know legumes restore soil and use
  a two-field system, so his real adds are the formalized rotation, the moldboard
  (muted payoff in Mediterranean soil), and the **horse collar** (genuine missing
  unlock). Spreads at the speed of peasant trust - slowly; he never sees the
  empire-wide effect.
- **Block printing.** He already has the screw press (olive/wine presses) and
  skilled engravers. Block-printing existing books (the "50 copies of every book"
  dream) is achievable and seeds literacy. Movable metal type is a multi-year
  craft program gated on type-metal alloy (antimony) and uniform body height; it
  prints gray and crooked for years. Falling back to blocks is the correct,
  still-revolutionary outcome.
- **Repurposed water power** (trip-hammers, stamp mills, fulling) + the **spinning
  wheel.** Rome already has water mills (Barbegal); he points them at new jobs.
  Spinning wheel = ~10x throughput, simple, transformative; social-friction-
  limited adoption.

## PHASE C (103-110) — Pushing harder; the chokepoints bite
- **Better steel (THE chokepoint).** His real contribution is the IDEA that
  carbon content (from charcoal) plus quench-and-temper controls hardness -
  pointing smiths at a variable to optimize. Crucible steel (clay crucible, iron +
  charcoal/bone ash, sealed, long high heat) is the most promising path and is
  finicky and fuel-hungry. No pyrometer, so temperature is judged by color;
  gorgeous one batch, brittle garbage the next. Almost every dream below dies
  here.
- **Gunpowder + the cannon-that-bursts.** Correct the premise error: **China did
  NOT have gunpowder by 100 AD**, and Daniel believing it did is a good
  confident-mistake beat. Saltpeter is known as a curing curiosity (*sal petrae*)
  but never combined with fuel; wall-scrapings give calcium nitrate (bad), so he
  needs **niter beds** (composting manure/urine + ash + lime over months/years) -
  a smelly, multi-year, dangerous slog that can blow up a shed and kill an
  assistant. The cannon's JOB is to burst: build it knowing Roman bronze/iron
  can't hold the pressure, demonstrate from a safe distance, let it rupture, and
  argue for better steel. A cannon that fires without bursting is gated on steel -
  many years.
- **Distillation of spirits.** The one missing part is a **water-cooled
  condenser** (Alexandrian stills lacked it). Add that and he gets distilled
  alcohol = stronger antiseptic + a sellable product. Weak, foul, low-proof until
  refined; vapor fire/explosion risk.
- **Telescope/optics (signature struggle).** A single-lens magnifier works. A
  useful telescope needs two matched lenses of good homogeneity; Roman glass is
  clear but full of bubbles/striae, and grinding repeatable curves by eye with no
  test plate is brutal. Ceiling for years: a blurry, color-fringed 2-3x toy. The
  microscope-sees-atoms claim is a deliberate LIE; keep it a lie.
- **The story/writing contest.** An annual prize for the best tales of machines
  that do the work of a hundred men, vehicles faster than horses, devices that
  reveal the invisible. Seeds a civilization that dreams in technology a
  generation before the tech exists. A core theme-engine; must be built to outlive
  him.
- **Rail/wagonways in mines.** Wooden rails, flanged wheels, horse/human-drawn.
  Buildable, thematically perfect (reduces slave porters). Bitter honest beat:
  works in demo, barely adopted, because **slaves are cheaper than rails.**

## PHASE D (110-117) — Logistics, medicine, war
- Army applications mature: signaling/observation balloons (tethered), better
  field sanitation to fight the dysentery that kills more than battle, food
  preservation, mapping. The moral knot: his sanitation/hydraulics knowledge helps
  WIN sieges (cutting water supply, as at Sarmizegetusa) and his powder work feeds
  war. He sees his gifts kill.

## PHASE E (117-138) — Institutions and the ladder
- **The printing press and the contest must survive him** as institutions.
- **A patronage system for craftsmen** working on named problems, designed to
  outlast its founder.
- **The encyclopedia.** Texts ordered from basic arithmetic up through algebra,
  geometry, the idea of calculus, Newtonian mechanics, his fuzzy chemistry/
  biology/astronomy, political and economic theory, and the SHAPE of technologies
  that don't yet exist. Written first in **English**, partly translated to Latin,
  designed to be found and decoded later. It assumes nothing; it starts simple and
  builds. It is a ladder, not a shortcut. Teaching Tyche + apprentices English is
  the cipher/continuity mechanism.

## NEVER BUILT — "impossible bait" (named to inspire, honest that he'll never see them)
Steam engine (he shows Hero's aeolipile spinning as a toy and says *imagine this
strong enough to pull a hundred carts*), electricity, the combustion engine, the
car, the engine-driven train (he reframes "all roads lead to Rome" as "all tracks
lead to Rome" as a dream), aircraft/jets, submarines/aircraft carriers, the moon,
computers/phones, the atom-seeing microscope (a LIE). **Nitroglycerin**: lethal;
he names it as a thing he refuses to touch. **Concrete**: Rome already beats him;
he LEARNS from them, doesn't "introduce" it.

## THE HUMBLING BEATS (write him losing here, on purpose)
1. Manned balloon - crashes/burns before any clean flight; costs blood.
2. Mechanical clock - the escapement never clicks in his memory; the promised
   "grandfather clock" never keeps time. He builds pretty water clocks instead.
3. Useful optics - blurry, color-fringed 2-3x toy; the hype outruns the glass.
4. Airless tires / leaf springs - defeated by soft Roman metal (he wrongly thought
   copper springs would work); gated on spring steel, maybe never.
5. The cannon - it's SUPPOSED to burst.
