# V2 Master Context — *The Long Way Home*

**Read this before writing any chapter.** This document replaces the need to read the
87 analysis/review files from V1. It synthesizes the key context a writer agent needs.

---

## 1. What This Book Is

**Premise:** 17-year-old American high-school junior Daniel Mercer wakes in a harvested
field outside Rome, 98 AD, with a dying phone and a hardware-store job's worth of
practical knowledge. He spends 57 years using a normal modern education to bend history.

**The argument the book exists to make:** An ordinary teenager — not a prodigy, not an
engineer, not a genius — armed only with what a standard 2020s education gives you,
could change history. The bottleneck is not genius. It is courage, credibility, language,
money, and doing things in the right order. The point is that even a bicycle wasn't
invented until recently; you don't need to know how to build a smartphone to matter.

**What V1 got wrong about its own premise:** The AI writing V1 was too cautious. It kept
saying "Rome lacks X so Y is impossible" (static wall thinking) instead of showing Daniel
MANUFACTURE the missing prerequisite (dynamic bootstrapping). This made the book argue the
opposite of its own point — the teen couldn't change anything significant because Rome
didn't have the prerequisites. V2 fixes this.

**Format:** First-person past tense, narrated by an older Daniel. Close retrospective: he
tells it as it felt then. He does NOT use future vantage to telegraph or spoil events.
~2,500–4,000 words per chapter, 53 chapters, ~140–155k target word count (V1 was
197k — ~40k over commercial ceiling; aim for tighter chapters in V2).

## 2. The Narrator's Voice

Daniel is **dry, self-deprecating, and undercuts his own big moments**. He swears
(period-neutral: "hell," "damn"; "Christ" feels odd here and he notices). He reaches for
modern references no one will get, and that loneliness is a running note — shown, never
stated.

He fakes confidence in public and panics in private. He is good at math (through precalc,
some self-taught physics), shaky on chemistry and biology, scraps of history from class and
YouTube, steady diet of sci-fi. He gets things wrong in teen-plausible ways.

As years pass, his English narration slowly picks up Latin cadence and Roman assumptions.
By late chapters he has to reach for English words. Show this drift gradually — never
announce it.

**DON'T:** Make him smoothly competent. Make him remember things a normal teen wouldn't.
Let him win without cost. Have him make speeches that sound like a modern person explaining
things to the past.

## 3. The Five Core V2 Changes (vs. V1)

### Change 1: Tech is MORE achievable — show the bootstrapping chain
Daniel does not hit static walls; he manufactures prerequisites. The EASY WINS that V1
silently dropped or wrongly blocked:

| What | Phase | Why achievable |
|------|-------|----------------|
| Water-powered bellows + trip-hammers | Phase B (99–102) | Vitruvius describes mills; V1 bible promised it, prose dropped it silently |
| Vernier calipers + surface plate | Phase B–C | Geometry Daniel knows; flat-stone abrasive + two scales |
| Rag paper | Phase B | Macerate cloth rags, screen, dry; Han China had it 105 AD |
| Volta pile (zinc + copper + brine) | Phase C (103–110) | Zinc in Roman brass; simple cells; opens galvanometer/thermocouple |
| Soap (wood ash lye + tallow) | Phase B | Daniel's grandmother's hobby; extremely easy |
| Atmospheric steam pump (Newcomen-type) | Phase C–D | Near-atmospheric pressure; rough bronze cylinder + leather piston; V1 wrongly conflated with high-pressure steam |
| Thermocouple pyrometry | Phase D (110–125) | Iron-copper thermocouple + compass galvanometer; calibrate against melting points |
| Patent law concept | Phase D | Zero materials; Daniel proposes to Hadrian (who loves legal reform) |
| Double-entry bookkeeping | Phase A–B | Any economics class; enables rational capital allocation |
| Eratosthenes demonstration | Phase A | Two sticks + sun angles → Earth's circumference; zero materials; Daniel does this in Part I |

**Correctly stays blocked:** high-pressure steam / locomotive; useful refractor telescope
(Roman glass striae); marine chronometer (spring steel); long-distance electric telegraph
(no insulation); precision telescope.

**The steel wall:** Reduced but not gone. Thermocouple gives temperature measurement;
the residual wall is carbon-atmosphere control in the crucible melt. Show it narrowing
over decades, never fully solved in Daniel's lifetime.

### Change 2: Character voices have full human range
V1 made everyone too smart and eloquent. V2 requires:

- **Class-marked dialogue** — lower-class characters speak in shorter sentences, thinner
  vocabulary, wrong tense/article usage. Rough grammar as texture, not mockery.
- **Muted characters MUST speak.** Pamphilus (collared slave): minimum 4 spoken lines,
  rough and fragmented. Crowd hecklers: QUOTE them, Pompeii-graffiti register. Davus
  and Naso: give them actual crude/dim lines before their exits.
- **One crude oath per major low-status character.** Macer swears once (genuinely
  crude, in context). Celer's "soldier's-rough" line gets quoted at least once.
- **One character who is wrong and stays wrong.** Onesimus (the abacus clerk) is the
  best candidate: promote him from dignified-reactionary to confidently-wrong.
- **No child delivers the scene's thematic capstone.** Lucanus's line in ch37 must be
  tempered — a 10-year-old shouldn't out-philosophize the narrator.

### Change 3: Prose patterns — 5 things to fix everywhere
See `PROSE_PATTERNS_TO_AVOID.md` for the full quick-reference. The top five:

1. **BANNED: "not X, it's Y" (correctio / epanorthosis)** — 25–37 instances in V1;
   explicitly banned in original rules but recurs. Zero tolerance. Example to cut:
   *"I did not have a plan. I had a wound with an idea in it."*
2. **LIMIT: Gnomic/wisdom-button endings** — ~55% of V1 chapters end with a packaged
   life-lesson restating the theme. Max 1 per chapter, aim for 0 in every 3 chapters.
   End on image, action, dialogue, or silence instead. Model: ch42 (empty bench = grief).
3. **LIMIT: One-sentence paragraphs** — V1 was 26% (literary norm: 5–15%). Max 15%.
4. **BANNED: Narrator stepping outside time** — no "for years afterward I thought…"
   lines except inside explicitly permitted encyclopedia/journal excerpts.
5. **WATCH: "the way"** — 677 instances in V1 (~13/chapter). Max 3 per chapter.

### Change 4: History must actually diverge by 110 AD
V1 kept the macro-timeline essentially real history through 117 AD (only texture changes).
This undermines the premise. Required V2 divergence milestones:

- **By 105 AD:** One documented institutional change (Roman army unit adopts stirrups
  10 years early, OR literacy rate measurably up in one district, OR one battle outcome
  differs because of Daniel's input to Apollodorus)
- **By 110 AD:** Rag paper in use at the press; Volta pile demonstrated to Heras;
  the Eratosthenes proof is in the encyclopedia
- **By 121 AD (ch36):** Hadrian's Ocean Program is the explicit hinge; this is correct
  in V1, keep it
- **By 138 AD:** Four visible divergence signs on the page (printed books, new numerals
  on stone, third ocean hull, contest culture)

### Change 5: Six required story additions
1. **Lead poisoning** — Daniel is "the emperor's water-man" and loves Rome's lead pipes.
   He MUST notice this in Part I; the failure to notice makes him look stupid. He tries
   to address it (largely fails due to infrastructure investment) but the awareness exists.
2. **Fire prevention** — He causes a Subura fire; he must pitch a fire-watch/brigade
   system to the relevant magistrates and be rebuffed. Then he manages his own compound.
3. **Antagonist exits** — All three currently fade out:
   - Crispus: on-page public credibility collapse
   - Scaeva: his debt-collection attempt deflected by Daniel who anticipated it
   - Vibenius: one specific called favor, then a witnessed death with terms honored
4. **Gunpowder leak incident** — The book's stated #1 danger is never dramatized.
   One scene: theft attempt, Parthian enquiry, or bribed craftsman that Daniel contains.
5. **Eratosthenes demonstration** — Two sticks, two shadows, Earth's circumference.
   Daniel performs this in Part I (no materials needed) and writes it in the first
   encyclopedia draft. Shows "pointing works" without any tech breakthrough.
6. **"All tracks lead to Rome"** — The bible called for this reframing of "all roads
   lead to Rome." It appears nowhere in V1. Must appear in V2 — Daniel's line, or
   Felix's, or a later generation's.

## 4. Character Quick Reference

For full profiles and voice examples see `bible/02_characters.md` and
`bible/CHARACTER_VOICE_GUIDE.md`. Key voice fingerprints:

| Character | Voice fingerprint | Don't |
|-----------|-------------------|-------|
| **Daniel** | Dry, self-deprecating, swears, undercuts his wins, reaches for references no one gets | Make him eloquent, prodigious, or smoothly confident |
| **Heras** | Dry, ironic, learned; quotes Hippocrates; allergic to enthusiasm; "the infuriating student" register | Make him warm or inspirational; he's oblique even when fond |
| **Macer** | Blunt, transactional, mean-funny; "the Thulean"/"boy"; respects money/results only; swears ONCE in V2 (crude, in anger) | Give him hidden depth or sentiment; his actions stay self-interested |
| **Tyche** | Early: terse, guarded (survival caution). Later: sharp, fierce, freer-tongued. NEVER sentimental about her enslavement | Make her grateful or romantic toward Daniel; she has her own agenda |
| **Hermes** | Terse, physical, proud; hammer-broken clauses; "Tell me one number, Thulean, one I can do twice" | Make him theoretically articulate; he speaks in craft, not concepts |
| **Celer** | Clipped, competent, gallows-humored; soldier's brevity; quotes rough once in V2 | Give him courtly speech; he's a frontier officer |
| **Pamphilus** | Fragmented, wrong tense, missing articles, concrete; "That wall. Three times I done it. Don't hold." | Give him any complete, grammatical, perceptive sentences |
| **Crowd hecklers** | Rough, dumb, superstitious, obscene; Pompeii-graffiti register | Narrate around them with "shouted something I didn't catch" |

**Voice Range Spectrum** (full register from top to bottom):
SENATORIAL (Scaeva: smooth, indirect, lethal) → EDUCATED GREEK (Heras/Crispus: ironic,

(Hermes: terse, physical) → ARMY (Celer: clipped, oaths) → URBAN WORKING (Tyche early,
Pamphilus: survival-terse, Pamphilus inarticulate) → CROWD (dumb, superstitious, crude)

## 5. What V1 Got Right (Preserve These)

- **Ch01 opener** — exceptional. Strong voice, specific sensory detail. Don't touch.
- **Sensory grounding** — smell, sound, temperature, texture. Rome is specific and
  lived-in. Preserve throughout.
- **Oblique dialogue** — in most scenes, characters don't say what they mean directly.
  This is a genuine strength; protect it.
- **Ch42 (Heras's death)** and **ch50** — grief rendered through action and absence,
  not stated. These are the model for how to end scenes without wisdom-buttons.
- **Dramatic irony** — especially ch51/53 where Roman documents discover Daniel's work.
- **The tech failures** — Daniel's flubbed long division, the maimings, the death he
  causes, the clock he can't build. The book earns its victories because failures are real.
- **Daniel's phone motif** (~22x), "the new figures" (~39x), "the little ones" (~17x)
  — deliberate, well-controlled. Leave these alone.
- **The loneliness spine** — shown through action and absence, never monologued.
  Protect this; it's the book's emotional engine.
- **The anti-slavery-through-economics attack** — never spoken aloud, always enacted.

## 8. The Writing Workflow for V2

1. Read your chapter's V1 text (`chapters/chNN.md`) for continuity
2. Read your chapter's entry in `V2_CHAPTER_CHANGE_NOTES.md` for specific changes
3. Read this file (V2_MASTER_CONTEXT.md) for the big-picture rules
4. Write the chapter — hit the brief beats, own the execution
5. Before finalizing: run the PROSE_PATTERNS_TO_AVOID.md checklist mentally
6. Deliver: write to `chapters/chNN.md`; report new canon facts established

**Target:** 2,500–3,500 words per chapter (tighter than V1's 3,700–4,200 average).
