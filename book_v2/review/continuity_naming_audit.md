# Continuity & Naming Audit
**53 chapters | 98–~190 AD (story span ~155 AD for Daniel's death; epilogue centuries later)**
Checked against: `bible/03_timeline.md`, `bible/08_canon_log.md`, `bible/02_characters.md`, and all chapter prose.

## PART A — CONTINUITY ERRORS

### A1. Ages & Timeline

**ERROR — ch12: Tyche's age understated by 2 years.**
- Setting: 101 AD. Tyche born ~85 AD (canon anchor, `08_canon_log.md` line 1518). Age = 16.
- Prose (ch12, line 5): *"a war camp was no place for a fourteen-year-old girl."*
- Should read ~sixteen. The canon log notes that earlier prose under-aged Tyche and says chs 16/17/20/22 prose was corrected — but ch12 was not. This is the residual uncorrected instance.

**ERROR — ch31: Lucanus's age understated by ~1 year.**
- Setting: ~late 117 – early 118 AD. Lucanus born ~112 AD (canon, `08_canon_log.md` line 1071–1072). Age = ~6.
- Prose (ch31, line 29): *"He was a boy now, five years old."*
- All subsequent canon log entries for chs 32/33/34 record Lucanus as "~6." The discrepancy is a single-year slip confined to ch31.

**Elapsed-years approximate but acceptable:**
The following use round numbers that are slightly off but within the range of a narrator rounding loosely in memoir voice, not hard errors:
- ch42: Heras says the flying "convinced him [Macer] forty years ago." Balloon demo was ~99–100 AD; ch42 is ~133 AD = ~33–34 years. Off by ~6 years. Worth flagging as the most noticeable elapsed-years slip — Heras is specific ("forty years"), not vague.
- ch44: Daniel says Hermes "has failed at [steel] for thirty years." Hermes introduced in ch15 (~103 AD); ch44 is ~135 AD = ~32 years. Within rounding range.
- ch48: Daniel says Pamphilus "stayed at the wharf forty years." Pamphilus freed in ch18 (~105–106 AD); ch48 is ~144 AD = ~38–39 years. Within rounding range.

**The Heras "forty years" claim (ch42) is the only elapsed-years statement where the error is large enough to pull a careful reader out.** The balloon demo is a fixed, named event; "forty years ago" implies ~93 AD, before Daniel arrived.

**Arrival season: minor internal ambiguity, not a hard prose contradiction.**
- `03_timeline.md` says "Spring: Daniel wakes."
- `08_canon_log.md` [ch01] records "Season reads as late-summer/harvest."
- The prose consistently uses a harvested stubble field (ch01, ch26, ch49, ch50). The canon log itself flags this discrepancy. In practice the narrative computes elapsed years from "spring 98" (chs 05, 06, 30), so the chapter clock works, but the visual is harvest. Not an error a reader will stumble on; the calendar math resolves correctly.

### A3. Object/Fact Consistency

**ERROR — ch31: Pamphilus's collar "five years gone" — wrong elapsed time.**
- Pamphilus's collar was removed in ch18 (before the Second Dacian War, ~105–106 AD). Ch31 is set ~late 117 – early 118 AD.
- Prose (ch31, line 27): *"his collar five years gone and the mark of it long faded."*
- Actual elapsed time: ~12–13 years. The description "long faded" fits the correct timespan; "five years" is the error. Likely a draft holdover from an earlier chapter-dating scheme.

**Phone relic: consistent throughout.**
The dead phone's location — oiled wool, floorboard box, known only to Tyche — is maintained from ch24 through ch49, then buried as resolved in chs 49–51. Macer's ring enters the box correctly after ch48. No contradictions.

**Steel, cannon, and gunpowder state: consistent.**
Steel remains unsolved throughout. Gunpowder is loose but no working cannon is ever built beyond the deliberate-burst demo in ch21. The canon's tech ceiling holds.

**Manned balloon vow: consistent.**
Daniel vows never to fly a man in ch14 and repeats this refusal in chs 17, 18, 28, and 29. No backsliding.

**Tyche's manumission and name: consistent.**
Formally freed off-page ~107 AD (between chs 20 and 21). Her legal name "Ulpia Tyche" appears only in formal contexts; she uses only "Tyche" on the page throughout. CLEAN.

### A4. Callbacks That Misfire

**Priscus/Crispus in ch51: same chapter, similar names, different referents.**
- "Sextus Caecilius Priscus" = the recipient of Rufus's letter (new character introduced in ch51).
- "The grammarian Crispus" = Daniel's old antagonist (dead, referred to in past tense).
- Both names appear within a few paragraphs of each other. The text distinguishes them by tense and role, but the phonetic similarity (~6 shared letters in the same order) is a low-level reader-tripping hazard. See also Part B.

**"Daniel met Trajan only the once" is correctly maintained.** The ch17 audience is the only one; ch30 confirms this. No spurious second meeting intrudes. CLEAN.

**Balloon details in ch51 are intentionally distorted** (the legend's misreading of the tethered bag as a "fishing-float" in ch26, and Rufus's "made fire fly" account in ch51). These are deliberate dramatic ironies, not misfires.

## PART B — NAMING CONFUSION

### B1. Similar Names: Verdict by Pair

**MACER / MARCIA / MARCUS — moderate risk; manageable.**
- Titus Flavius Macer (patron), Marcia (wife), and Marcus (Daniel's praenomen) all share the "Mar-/Mac-" stem.
- Macer and Marcia share the page from ch22 onward. The book consistently distinguishes them by voice, gender, and role — Macer is "he/him," Marcia is "she/her," and their dialogue modes are sharply different. The full name "Titus Flavius Macer" recurs enough to anchor the patron. Risk is real but low in practice; a reader who loses the thread for a page will self-correct quickly.

**HERAS / HERMES — low-moderate risk; recurring co-presence.**
- Heras (physician) and Hermes (blacksmith) both carry the "Her-" prefix and appear together in the same workshops and scenes from ch15 onward.
- Both are long-running, named characters. In ch21 (the cannon chapter) they share the same scene with no other "Her-" name present, and the text is careful to maintain their distinct functions. Phonetically similar enough that a skimming reader could momentarily swap them, especially early in the book before each is well-established.
- Verdict: worth a brief descriptive anchor when either reappears after a gap chapter.

**SCAEVA / SCAURUS — low-moderate risk; most dangerous in ch27.**
- Publius Cornelius Scaeva (the senator who backs Daniel) and Manius Aemilius Scaurus (the senator who lends his house for the trial) share the same chapter. Both are "Sc-" Roman senatorial names with similar rhythm and ending.
- The text does distinguish them by function: Scaurus is "the host of the house"; Scaeva is "the man who leaned in." But they appear within pages of each other in ch27 with no reminder of who Scaeva is (he was introduced in ch25, two chapters earlier, and named several times).
- Verdict: a one-sentence re-identifying note for Scaeva when he speaks in ch27 would help.

**PRISCUS / CRISPUS — low risk in ch51, but worth noting.**
- These are an anagram of each other (same six letters, rearranged). They appear in ch51 within the same letter — one as the new character Rufus addresses, one as the long-established antagonist named in past tense. The past-tense framing of Crispus is a real guard, but the phonetic overlap is notable.
- Verdict: the separation by tense (Crispus = dead, historical; Priscus = living, present-day addressee) is sufficient. Minor risk.

**CELER / CAEPIO — negligible.**
- Quintus Marcius Celer (the officer) and Caepio (Marcia's dead husband) are both short Roman names but rarely appear near each other and are in entirely different registers. No confusion risk.

**Other names: clean.**
- Vibenius is unusual enough to stand alone. Crispus stands alone before ch51. Lucanus, Ulpia, Felix, Tyche, Pamphilus, Geta, Eros, Vitalis, Chloe, Zoticus, Eudemus, Theophanes, Bituitus — all are either distinctive or confined to narrow chapters where no same-initial character competes.

### B2. Underlabeled Characters

**Eudemus (fiscus clerk, ch34):** Introduced in ch34, mentioned again in ch35 and briefly in ch45. The ch35 reappearance is only a chapter later and fine. By ch45 he is identified as "Eudemus" (the Greek treasury clerk) without full re-anchoring — but he is mentioned in passing and readers should be fine. Low risk.

**Zoticus (Heras's apprentice, ch42):** Introduced in ch42, reappears in chs 47, 48, and 50. Ch47 is the first reappearance, ~five chapters after ch42. The text identifies him as "Heras's man" and "Heras's pupil-line" in chs 48 and 50 respectively — adequate anchoring. CLEAN.

**Naso (Titus Aelius Naso, chs 11–29):** Introduced in ch11 (the burned recruit), carries through to ch29 where he last appears. His arc is complete. No re-anchoring issue; his ruined hand and hitching ankle are consistent re-identifying tags throughout.

**Theophanes (ch37):** A named minor character with one scene. He doesn't return; no anchoring needed. CLEAN.

**Bituitus (ch44):** The Gaulish yard-master. One appearance; name is distinctive enough. CLEAN.

**Saturius (ch52):** Named once in the epilogue's craft-failure fund context. Distinct. CLEAN.

## VERDICT

**Part A:** Four genuine continuity errors, all bounded in scope. None undermines the plot or character arcs. The Pamphilus collar date (error 3) is the most surprising for an attentive reader. The Tyche age in ch12 (error 1) was noted in the canon as a known correction that was not applied to ch12. The Heras "forty years" (error 4) is the most visible because it is specific dialogue, not a narrator approximation.

**Part B:** The book is largely clean on naming. The three real-but-manageable pairs are Heras/Hermes (co-presence), Scaeva/Scaurus (same-chapter use without re-anchor), and Priscus/Crispus (ch51 anagram). The Macer/Marcia pair functions because the two characters are so different in voice and gender. No character was found reappearing after death or in two places at once.
