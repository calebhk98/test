# Round 4 — tech-wall rebuttal, language handling, quantitative metrics

This round pressure-tested a reader's specific rebuttals and added computed metrics.
Detail files: tech_plausibility_rebuttal.md, language_handling.md, quantitative_metrics.md.

## TECH PLAUSIBILITY — REBUTTAL VERDICT (the reader was largely right)
The earlier audit (tech_plausibility_audit.md) was **too generous**. Re-examination:

### The furnace/steel wall: HOLDS, but only when restated precisely
- The reader is correct that "Rome can't get hot enough" is false: forced draft +
  charcoal hits 1300-1500 C, Romans already made glass in that range; the historical
  barrier was lack of NEED, not capability.
- The book's actual, ch15-stated wall is **measurement and reproducibility**: with no
  pyrometer, a smith can't reliably hit and repeat the narrow temperature window for
  crucible steel, so 40 years of funded effort fail. On the page this is dramatized
  honestly (Felix, Saturius, ch52).
- **OPEN CAVEAT (my note, not the agent's):** historically, crucible/wootz steel WAS
  produced in this era (India/Central Asia) by empirical recipe and artisan skill
  WITHOUT instruments — by charge composition, color, and timing. So an absolute 40-year
  failure under a motivated wealthy backer is still arguably too pessimistic; "no
  thermometer" is a softer wall than the book treats it as. Verdict: defensible but not
  airtight; it's the load-bearing excuse and leans hard.

### Genuine EASY WINS the book wrongly skips (this validates the critique)
1. **Rag paper** — never even raised; press runs on papyrus 50 years. (Confirmed.)
2. **Heliocentrism as a TAUGHT subject** — zero materials, Daniel knows it's true,
   Aristarchus already floated it; costs only words. The prior audit missed this.
3. **A Volta-pile wet battery** (zinc/copper/brine) — prior audit wrongly lumped this
   with "impossible bait" like steam; the principle is simple and the materials are in
   Rome. Opens telegraph/electroplating.
4. **A rough phonograph precursor** — needle + wax + cylinder + membrane diaphragm; needs
   NO electricity and no precision metal. An easy, dramatic win ignored.
- Broader: the book underuses pure CONCEPTUAL science (algebra, calculus, atomic theory,
  evolution, weather) that needs only a brilliant teacher, and underuses the social lever
  of a rich man pointing/bluffing craftsmen toward targets to create a snowball.

### Genuine STRETCHES (reader slightly over-optimistic) — fair to keep blocked/late
- Broadcast radio and radar (EM generation/detection, coherers, valves) — real reach.
- Working steam engine, precision clock, useful telescope — correctly gated on the
  steel/machining chain.
- Telegraph is a PLAUSIBLE mid-stretch (needs the battery + insulated wire) — book could
  reach it late.

### The Atlantic voyage
Deferring the crossing ~10 generations past Daniel's death is **hard to defend**: a
round trip is ~1 year, the knowledge (the Americas exist, roughly where) is free, and a
pushing, wealthy Daniel with decades should plausibly get a hull there in-lifetime. This
is part of the book's deliberate "defer the payoff to the epilogue" choice.

### Flight foreclosure: THE BOOK'S WEAKEST DEVICE (reader is right)
Grief after Sabinus (ch13/14) is authentic immediately, but a **permanent ~40-year ban
on risk-free UNMANNED and glider work** is over-extended — the vow ("no man hung under
fire") doesn't even logically cover unmanned flight. Flight is too useful to abandon for
decades, and Daniel of all people would iterate. This is the most exposed authorial
convenience in the manuscript.

**Overall:** materials-tech gating is sound; the book is genuinely too conservative on
(a) conceptual teaching, (b) simple electricity/phonograph, (c) the in-lifetime ocean
crossing, and (d) flight. The reader's "AI hedged to preserve real history" instinct is
substantially correct for these categories.

## LANGUAGE HANDLING — a genuine strength
Daniel learns **Latin** first (survival), then **Greek** slowly via Heras. Learning curve
shown in specific detail (total incomprehension days 1-3; broken Latin ~4 months;
flawed-conversational ~1 year). A v/w foreign accent marks him from sentence one and
never disappears; he's still "the Thulean"/"the barbarian" 57 years on. Literacy is
correctly separated from speech (hence the English-language encyclopedia). No premature
comprehension. ("Roman" isn't a language — Latin vernacular + Greek for the educated.)

## QUANTITATIVE METRICS (see quantitative_metrics.md)
- **Zipf's law:** near-perfect fit (log-log slope -0.96 to -1.07, R^2=0.994). Expected of
  ALL fluent prose, human or AI — clean but NOT an AI-discriminator.
- **One-sentence paragraphs:** 26.1% of all paragraphs; 23.5% excluding dialogue.
  High for the book's literary register (lit-fic norm ~5-15%); corroborates the cadence
  audit. The "5 sentences/paragraph" rule is an essay convention, not a fiction norm.
- Sentence shape skews LONG/subordinated (mean 23.7 words, 46% subordinators), refuting
  the "staccato AI" worry; the cadence issue is a predictable long/medium/short 3-beat.
