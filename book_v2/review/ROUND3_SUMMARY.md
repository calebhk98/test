# Round 3 — pro-lens craft audits + the two reader critiques

This pass answered two reader critiques (voice/character range; implausibly conservative
tech change) and ran seven checks a craft-trained reader / acquiring editor applies that
earlier rounds hadn't. All round-3 agents ran on Sonnet. Detail files in `book/review/`.

## THE ONE FINDING THAT MATTERS MOST
**Nearly every independent audit fingered the same root weakness: the post-scene
interpretive gloss** — the habit of dramatizing a beat well and then adding a sentence
(or paragraph) that *restates its meaning*. It was named, separately, by: all three
line-prose close-reads, all three blind critiques, the syntax/cadence audit, the
show-vs-tell audit, the scene-architecture audit, and the voice-distinctiveness audit.
That convergence makes it the single highest-leverage fix in the book.
- Structural scale: **~55-60% of chapters end on an extended interpretive reflection**;
  **~40% open with a thesis statement** before any scene begins.
- Cutting the closing gloss from ~10-15 chapters and entering ~10 chapters mid-scene
  would address the largest share of every prose complaint at once.

## QUANTIFIED CRUTCHES (hard numbers)
- One-sentence paragraphs: **26.9%** of all paragraphs (drama-beat overused to the point
  it "consumes its own climax" at the ch50 death).
- `the way ...` sentence-frame: **685** times (~1 per 288 words).
- `look/looked/looking`: **561** (~3.5x the literary-fiction norm); `hands`: **571**;
  `still`: **265**; `I knew`: **166**.
- `something sat in my chest like a swallowed stone` appears **verbatim twice** + ~a
  dozen near-paraphrases — the book's default emotion-metaphor.
- Sentence shape (refuting the "staccato AI" worry): mean **23.7** words, **46.3%**
  carry a subordinator, **36.9%** are >=25 words. The book skews LONG/subordinated; its
  cadence problem is a predictable long/medium/short 3-beat the ear starts anticipating,
  not parataxis.

## CRITIQUE 1 — VOICE / CHARACTER RANGE  (you were right)
`voice_range_audit.md`: the book commits the "everyone is intelligent/articulate" AI
failure on the **competence/class/honesty axis** (it avoids it on the register axis).
- No speaking character is genuinely dull; no class-marked rough/halting grammar (slaves,
  smiths, soldiers all speak in clean, even aphoristic clauses); **near-zero crude oaths**
  despite soldiers and dockers (and profanity is period-accurate — not a constraint);
  liars are all clever-strategic, never confidently-wrong fools.
- The book *mutes* anyone who'd sound dumb/crude ("a man in the crowd shouted something
  I did not catch").
- Fixes: give Pamphilus real rough/ungrammatical lines; let Naso/Davus speak a foolish or
  cocky line; quote the hecklers instead of muting them; let Macer or Celer swear once.

## CRITIQUE 2 — TECH CHANGE TOO CONSERVATIVE  (partly right)
`tech_plausibility_audit.md`: the headline leaps are **legitimately** blocked — almost
everything (cannon, steam, springs, clock, optics) gates on ONE real, dramatized wall:
Rome can't measure/hold furnace temperature -> no reproducible steel. The benefactor's
money IS spent and still fails (40+ yrs on steam, 30 on steel). That wall is the book's
greatest strength and should NOT be loosened. BUT there is a real seam of hedging in
**four steel-free wins it skips**:
1. **Paper** — never even raised; the press runs on papyrus for 50 years (biggest hole;
   paper existed in Han China at this time and is low-tech).
2. **Spinning wheel + water-powered fulling/trip-hammers** — the bible itself promised
   these as transformative; the prose silently dropped them.
3. **A glider** — all flight is foreclosed *forever on emotional grounds* after Sabinus's
   death, not on physical grounds; risk-free unmanned/glider work is never revisited.
4. **Coal as forge fuel** — the fuel bottleneck is named but the obvious lever ignored.

### The deeper version of your critique (the divergence is DEFERRED)
Three agents (teen-knowledge, rome-diff, market) independently agree: within Daniel's
lifetime the change is deliberately incremental and the chapter-by-chapter *feel* is
"grinding failure, not transformation." The big rupture (Atlantic crossing) is withheld
to **~1,100 words of epilogue narrated by someone who doesn't know Daniel's name.** The
market agent calls this a real commercial liability: the premise promises a visible
rupture the book intentionally pays off only after the hero dies. The cheap, plausible
way to deliver more visible change in-lifetime is exactly the four skipped wins above,
plus an in-lifetime "Daniel points, an expert finishes it" scene (e.g. the Eratosthenes
shadow -> circumference -> mass -> sun chain), which the book never stages.

## STRENGTHS (the audits that came back clean)
- **Dialogue mechanics** (`dialogue_mechanics_audit.md`): strong — said-bookisms absent,
  action beats individuated, subtext usually trusted. Minor: repeated "let that sit"
  beat; Daniel's tutorial speeches to Trajan/Hadrian read as lectures.
- **Show-vs-tell** (`showing_telling_audit.md`): a strength; the failures are the gloss
  habit and two moral pivots (ch14, ch45) given as summary not scene.
- **Continuity** (`continuity_naming_audit.md`): only 4 minor errors; no one resurrected.
  Naming: watch **Heras/Hermes** (also the blind-test blur), Scaeva/Scaurus, Priscus/Crispus.

## MARKET CALL  (`market_audit.md`)
**Revise & Resubmit.** 197k words is ~40-55k over the ceiling for its shelf (cut targets:
the ch38-44 pacing valley + the closing aphorisms). Caught between SFF and literary
shelves. Comps: Mandel, Follett, *Jonathan Strange & Mr Norrell*. Assets: exceptional
opening voice, achieved structural ambition, an underserved comp lane.
