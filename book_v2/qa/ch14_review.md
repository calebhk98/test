## Non-blocking notes
- Negative narration check (L61): "I had been building the wrong thing. Not
  wrong because it failed. Wrong because of what it was for." This is borderline
  but defensible. It is NOT the banned "narrate what isn't happening" move; it
  is a substantive in-character judgment that immediately states the positive
  ("Wrong because of what it was for") and is grounded in the rendered facts
  preceding it (two years, a man's life, the basket that killed, the cup of
  boiled water that would have saved). The structure is "X. Not for reason A.
  For reason B." which states the real reason directly. Reads as Daniel's
  thinking, not authorial annotation. Keep. If a leaner cut is ever wanted,
  "Wrong because of what it was for" alone carries it.
- Reckoning is in subtext/behavior, mostly. The hinge decision (L61-63) is
  rendered as Daniel's private interior reasoning on the road, anchored in the
  concrete (the bored muleteer, the cup of flat water, "the inch of choosing a
  man owns even in a cage"), and crucially it is enacted in the final scene (he
  sits down and draws the filter, refuses the powder) rather than declaimed at
  anyone. It is NOT a moral lecture: he never speechifies to a character, never
  argues slavery or war in the abstract, and explicitly notes he "did not have a
  word for what I decided." The slavery argument stays under the surface (the
  collar imagery, "the only thing I could buy him," "the finest cage I had").
  This is at the upper edge of how much interior moralizing the rules tolerate,
  but it lands on the right side because it is felt, specific, and then acted on.
- Retrospective tic check: PASSES the ch13-differentiation requirement. The
  chapter does NOT use the "I still / I never / as long as I live" memoir tic
  that ch13 leaned on. (grep finds only "powerless I still was" at L61, which is
  ordinary past-tense narration, not the retrospective-judgment tic.) Texture is
  colder and more deliberate than ch13, as briefed.
- Narrator-from-the-future: none. No forward-glance about a "coming war," no
  "little did I know," no weight assigned from a later vantage. The coordinator's
  cut is confirmed clean. Daniel's "not yet, maybe not ever" re: gunpowder (L97)
  is a present-tense resolve, not a future spoiler.
- Naming Sabinus: narrator writes "Sabinus" four times (L9, 15, 53, 67), twice

  and intentional: the CHARACTERS don't say it; the NARRATOR names it. Matches

- Beat-tag clichés (nodded/sighed/chuckled/shrugged/furrowed): none. Beats are

  ready for it or not, the way days do" is a near-takeaway flourish but stays
  attached to a physical image (the wharf noise) and is restrained enough to
  pass. Watch this register in future chapters.
- Voice: Daniel still reads as a displaced modern (dry undercut: "I think I ate
