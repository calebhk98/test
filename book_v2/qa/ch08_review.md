## Non-blocking notes
- [Punctuation / correctio, borderline] L39: "the word for the thing that is
  not a craft and not a wonder but a working, a witch's working" — this is the
  one near-violation of the no-"not X but Y" rule. It mostly survives because
  it renders the crowd groping toward the actual Latin charge (the escalation
  craft -> wonder -> working is the point, not a rhetorical pivot), and the
  payoff word "working/witch's working" is concrete and load-bearing. If
  touched, recast as the bare landing word: "the word Heras had warned me
  about, working, a witch's working, said sharp and afraid." Not blocking.
- [Rule 2 / negative narration, cleared] L47/L65 "No one had died... a tunic
  was burned, a woman was screaming, a man had a scorch" and "He did not say
  bless it. He did not say burn it." Both look like banned absence-narration
  but each is immediately filled with what IS there (the concrete damage
  tally; the two open doors Vibenius leaves). The "bless it / burn it" pair is
  the literal substance of the ambiguous omen, not silence dressed as meaning.
  Fine as written.
- [Style guide / close retrospective, cleared] L4 "everything else that day got
  bigger in the telling," L33 "I have tried for years to say the next part
  right," L63 "which I only understood later and hated," L65 "in a way I had not
  understood the night before." These are later-Daniel reflecting, which the
  style guide explicitly permits. None telegraph future plot or do "little did I
  know"; they assign felt weight, not spoilers. Flagging only so they aren't
  later mistaken for narrator-from-the-future. They are not.
- [Rule 11 / takeaway ending, cleared] The final paragraph (L79) lands on a
  concrete image: smoke going up "between the leaning houses into the same
  bright crooked river of sky I had just thrown it through, where it had hung
  for a moment with no string and no hand on it." That is a physical image to
  sit with, not a restated meaning. The summarizing thought ("worth something
  here meant a thing other men decide what to do with") sits earlier at L65 and
  is earned by the action around it, not tacked on as a closer.
- [Predictability] First page promises only "a public demo with no string." The
  imperfect descent, the roof drag, the witch-tip, and especially Vibenius's
  two-door omen are not predictable from page one. The chapter respects the
  reader. No spoon-feeding.
