  frown." — Two sequenced absences describing Vibenius's face. It's the closest
  thing to a banned move in the chapter. It mostly survives because a still,
  unreadable face is arguably what IS there and it's bracketed by concrete action
  ("He held it for a count of three," "every hair on my arms stand up"). If
  touched, render the stillness positively (e.g. "His face held flat as the board
  in front of him") rather than via what it didn't do. Not blocking.
- [Style guide / close retrospective] L109 "I have never entirely stopped." This
  is later-Daniel narration, which the style guide explicitly permits. It conveys
  lingering unease, not future-plot weight or a "little did I know" spoiler.
  Reads as one of the strongest lines in the chapter. Flagging only so it isn't
  mistaken later for narrator-from-the-future; it is not.
- [Not a finding, cleared] L49 "It was not a big fire, as fires go" is litotes
  with no "but Y" pivot, not a banned correctio. L57 "It was going to be filling
  the bag on the ground... It did not have to" is character reasoning, not a
  rhetorical not-X-but-Y. Both fine.
- Section openers ("The seam was always where it died"; "Here is the thing nobody
  tells you about fire") are lightly aphoristic but stay inside Daniel's reflective
  voice; they are not outline-as-prose. Fine.
- Steward vs. money chain: prose has coins flowing kitchen woman -> Stichus, while

  (owner unmet, dealt via steward); the two channels coexist cleanly.
