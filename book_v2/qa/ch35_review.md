## Blocking issues (must fix)

- [VOICE WATCH 4d - closing-image mannerism] L131-137: the chapter closes on
  Lucanus reading a printed sheet "in the wrong order, the seven and the one,"
  with Daniel crouching and "turned his finger to the seven." This is the FOURTH
  consecutive chapter to close on "young son + numbers/objects":
    - ch32 closes on Lucanus turning the dead soldier's buckle in the lamplight.
    - ch33 closes on Lucanus counting a sheet against Daniel's arm; "'That's a
      seven,' I said, and turned his finger to it."
    - ch34 closes on Lucanus "getting the seven and the one the wrong way round...
      'That's a seven,' I said, and turned his finger to it."
    - ch35 closes on Lucanus, the SAME "seven and the one the wrong way round,"
      Daniel "turned his finger to the seven."
  Worse than a thematic echo: ch35 reuses the literal ch33/ch34 beat ("the seven
  and the one," "turned his finger to the seven") almost verbatim. Four in a row,
  three of them on the identical seven-and-one gesture, is a formulaic tell and a
  reader will feel the template. FIX: change this close. Keep Marcia's quiet
  "Did you keep the map in the box" exchange (it is excellent and earns the
  ambiguity), but land the final image on something other than Lucanus-and-figures.
  Options: end on Daniel sitting to count the day's loads with the slope at his
  back (already in the paragraph - cut the boy from the final image); or end on a
  concrete object from the audience (the wax he was given, the staged light he
  still feels); or end on Marcia turning the tablet. The Lucanus-numbers well is
  dry; do not draw from it a fourth time.

## Non-blocking notes

- [VOICE WATCH 4b - craft asides] Two present-tense narrator asides in one
  chapter: "I say it plainly because..." (L51) and "I want to be exact, because
  it is the part I came closest to ruining" (L113). Each works in isolation as a
  memoir-voice device, and both fall at genuine pressure points (Hadrian's speed;
  the held map). But two in one chapter is the edge of a forming tic - the same
  "let me be straight with you, reader" gesture twice. Not blocking. Recommend
  cutting or recasting ONE (L51 is the more disposable; the surprise can live in
  the verb "by the throat" without the frame). Flag for the coordinator to watch
  the per-chapter rate going forward.

- [aphorism] L121 Hadrian's "A man who asks to be forgotten is asking to be
  remembered for the asking" is the chapter's one gnomic maxim. It is in his
  mouth (characterizing his cleverness), so it reads as his move, not the
  narrator's, and it is within ration. Fine. No second one stacks on it.

- [length] 4,383 words, over the 3,800 ceiling, but this is a flagged true
  set-piece (the audience), where the canon note permits overage. Acceptable.
  If trimming for the revision, L97 and L115 each restate the
  ledger/proud-man-with-more-to-lose idea that L91 already landed; one could go.

- [strength worth keeping] The recognition (L83-97) is excellent and fully
  organic - it lands on a physical gesture (the two fingers sketching the dome in
  the air), pulls the ch26 memory up by association, not by narrator announcement,
  and converts immediately into adjusted behavior (the roof deferral, L99-103).
  This is the model for how this book should do recognition.

- [strength] Apollodorus danger is Daniel's FEAR, present-tense and bounded
  ("I could not warn him," L129), never narrator prophecy. Clean.
