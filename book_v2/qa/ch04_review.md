# QA — Chapter 4

VERDICT: REVISE

## Blocking issues (must fix)

- [rule #4 narrator-from-the-future / telegraphing; brief check (c)] Line 89:
  "I didn't know I'd just heard the name I was going to wear for the rest of my
  life." This is the exact banned move: the narrator arrives from the future to
  assign weight to a moment the character does not yet understand. It also
  telegraphs the Thule-as-label payoff the chapter is supposed to withhold (the
  brief and [ch04] canon require that Daniel hear the SOUND and NOT retain it as
  a label yet). The rest of the paragraph already does the honest version
  perfectly ("I caught it without holding it," "I didn't know it then... that he
  didn't sound like he bought it for a second"). FIX: cut the clause outright.
  The paragraph survives intact starting from "I only knew he'd put a word on
  the blank place I was pointing at." Let the reader carry the irony; they know
  from the bible/cover-story arc that the word matters. Daniel must not.

## Non-blocking notes

- [rule #11 / brief check (e), phone death "no eulogy"] Line 105: "The last
  library on earth that spoke my language had closed." The section opens (line
  93) with the correct instinct, "there's no way to make it bigger than it was,"
  then this metaphor does make it bigger - a summarizing takeaway dropped on the
  death. It is the strongest line in the section and also the one that violates
  the brief's "plain, no eulogy" instruction. Borderline. The genuinely plain
  ending arrives two paragraphs later (line 107: dead glass against his hip,
  eyes open, didn't write the notes "because the phone was the notes") and is
  excellent and sufficient. Recommend cutting or flattening line 105 so the
  mechanical rendering (presses, hard reset, jiggling a broken key) carries the
  weight unsupported. Not blocking on its own, but combined with line 89 the
  chapter is reaching for significance twice in a row at exactly the beats it
  was told to underplay.

- Line 89 also contains a near-correctio rhythm ("that not buying it didn't make
  him want me gone. If anything it made him lean in. A wonder you either believe
  or you don't. A lie that good is worth taking apart."). This is fine and stays
  honest because it is rendered as Heras's behavior weighed by Daniel in the
  moment, not a future verdict. Keep it; it earns the scene's turn.
