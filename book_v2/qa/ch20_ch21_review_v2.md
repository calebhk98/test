### CRITICAL CHECK: Vow Erosion — Rational vs. Emotional

The V2 spec requires the erosion to be **REASONED, not emotional**, with three rational slivers. The chapter delivers this explicitly:

**Sliver 1 (L9):** "The first sliver was the steel." — The steel wall is the rational chokepoint. Without solving it, everything gates. This is a logical argument, not a feeling.

**Sliver 2 (L17-19):** "The second sliver. The realization... that the steel chokepoint gated everything." — He articulates the ladder logic: clean water, medicine, books, all blocked by metal; nobody with money will fund it; he needs to make them care. Rational.

**Sliver 3 (L21-25):** "The third sliver I am least proud of, because it was a lie I told myself on purpose to make the rest go down." — The Seres argument. Crucially, the chapter NAMES it as a lie he told himself deliberately, and later corrects it (L25: "I think now that I was wrong"). This is the confident-mistake beat functioning as it should.

**Assessment:** The three-sliver structure is present and labeled. Each sliver is stated as a rational argument. The chapter's retrospective voice acknowledges the third sliver was motivated reasoning, which is MORE sophisticated than merely calling it rational — it is rational self-deception, which is honest about the erosion without being emotionally manipulative.

**One concern:** The V2 spec says "the erosion must be REASONED, not emotional (three rational slivers: steel chokepoint logic, ladder depends on steel, Seres lie)." The chapter's three slivers map exactly to these three. However, the chapter also contains emotional registers (guilt about Geta, dread about the powder being loose). These are post-hoc, not drivers of the vow erosion. The erosion itself is shown as rational before Geta's accident; the emotional weight comes AFTER, which is structurally correct. **PASS on vow erosion spec.**

**Distinction from manned-balloon vow:** Maintained throughout. The manned vow is referenced correctly: "I had nailed the lid on the manned balloon and meant it. I had told an emperor to his face that I would not hang another man under a bag of fire." The gunpowder is "a quieter version of the same thing" — distinguished as separate. Celer's challenge confirms: "You swore off it." / "I swore off hanging men under fire. I didn't keep the rest of it." The distinction is on the page. **PASS.**
