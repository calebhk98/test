## Blocking issues (must fix)
- [Rule 4 / narrator-from-the-future] L93: "That was the meeting. The whole rest
  of my life in this city, bent and set, in the time it took a great soft cruel
  rich man to read one tablet and put it down." This is the close-retrospective
  narrator arriving from the future to assign outsized weight to the present
  scene ("the whole rest of my life... bent and set"). The style guide permits
  retrospection but forbids using it to declare significance/spoil, and Rule 4
  bans exactly "before everything changed" weight-assignment. It also doubles as
  a takeaway-summary closing the main Macer scene (Rule 11). FIX: cut the second
  sentence. End the scene-block on the concrete: "Now get him out of my floor...
  And feed him. He looks like a thing I bought cheap." Let the chapter's real
  ending (the Tyche beat, L111) carry the weight, which it already does.
- [Rule 4 / narrator-from-the-future] L89: "He looked at me a moment longer to
  be sure it had gone in. It had gone in. It is in me still." The present-tense
  "It is in me still" reaches out of the scene to tell the reader, from decades
  on, that this lesson permanently marked him. That is the narrator assigning
  weight from outside the moment. FIX: stop at "It had gone in." The repetition
  already lands the fact that the lesson took; the reach into "still" is the
  editorializing the rule forbids.

## Non-blocking notes
- [Punctuation / correctio, borderline — CLEARED, do not "fix" blindly] L77:
  "Some of it. Enough that a fever does not ruin me, not so much that I do not
  need you." This reads at a glance like a "not X but Y" pivot, but it is not
  correctio: it is a calibration between two real bounds (teach a middling
  amount), and it is the load-bearing core of the deal, verbatim from the canon
  log [ch09] ("enough that a fever can't ruin Macer, not so much that he stops
  needing Daniel"). It states positive content, does not negate-then-substitute,
  and is Macer's voice pricing a risk. Leave it. Flagging only so it is not
  mistaken later for a ZERO-TOLERANCE hit. It is not one.
- [Rule 1 / outline-as-prose, borderline] L15 "this was the first thing he was
  going to teach me" and L51 "Then he stopped laughing and got to the point, and
  the point was a knife dressed as a gift." These name the function of the beat
  before rendering it. They survive because each is immediately cashed into
  concrete behavior (the made-to-wait silence; the finger-by-finger list), and
  the voice is Daniel reading the room in real time, not the writer annotating.
  Watch the habit; if you trim anything, L51's "the point was a knife dressed as
  a gift" is the most expendable (the rendered list already IS the knife).
- [Rule 5 / announced weight, borderline] L79 "the shape was a cage so well made
  that it looked from the inside like a workshop." This is the briefed "cage
  inside the gift" image and earns its place after the rendered deal; it is a
  controlling metaphor, not a hollow punch. Fine. (It does the work L93 tries to
  repeat — another reason L93 should go.)
- [Predictability] Page one (the guarded triangle-walk, "you do not put a wall
  around a mule" logic seeded later) does not telegraph the whole chapter.
  Macer's "freedom is not mine to give" rebuff, the peculium-as-leash candor,
  and Tyche's two-ended-leash introduction are not predictable from the opening.
  The reader is respected. No spoon-feeding.
