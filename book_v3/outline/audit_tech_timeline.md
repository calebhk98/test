## TECH MISSING FROM OUTLINE

### 1. Coal use — no Phase A dramatized scene
**Should appear:** Phase A (ch03–ch07), per 04_tech_schedule
**Reference says:** "Action in Phase A: Daniel asks about 'burning stone' and locates British coal sources via Macer's trade network; dispatches a factor to investigate." Explicitly flagged as an easy win that starts the self-reinforcing industrial loop.
**What the outline has:** Ch03 introduces lead pipes and lead-sweetened wine. There is no coal/burning stone scene in any Phase A chapter. The 04_tech_schedule states "V1 wrongly omitted" related items — but coal is called out as a Phase A scene requirement, not a background note. The outline's Phase A makes no mention of coal anywhere.
**Why it matters:** Coal-fired forges produce better iron sooner, enabling the steam pump program and the crossbow prod program. It is the first link in the self-reinforcing industrial loop. Its absence removes a concrete, early scene of Daniel's systematic resource-hunting.

### 2. Steam pump — no on-page Phase C–D chapter beat
**Should appear:** Phase C–D (~110–125 AD), ch23–33 range
**Reference says:** 04_tech_schedule Phase D explicitly: "Atmospheric steam pump [Newcomen-class]…PLAUSIBLE IN 15–25 YEARS from workshop establishment — achievable by ~120 AD." V2_HISTORICAL_IMPACT: first pump in Hispania ~118 AD, eight pumps by 128 AD. V2_DANIEL_FINANCES: "Steam Mine Drainage Pumps" is the Phase E financial event, with first commercial installation at 125–130 AD and prototype by 120–125 AD.
**What the outline has:** The steam pump appears only in ch49's retrospective legacy inventory: "steam pump draining mines in two provinces." There is no Phase C–D chapter that shows Daniel building, testing, or commissioning a steam pump. The ch19 writing contest has "Felix's steam-ship entry" — a wrong-concept submission — but no payoff chapter where the correct Newcomen-class pump is demonstrated. The outline notes the ch19 entry "V1 had Felix test a rotary steam device (the wrong concept)" as a continuity item to fix, but no chapter shows the correct pump working.
**Why it matters:** The steam pump is identified as a "MAXIMUM" priority technology and the revenue engine of Phase E (potentially 1,000,000+ HS/year per mine). Its absence from Phase C–D leaves a major economic and technological beat unwritten. The "mine drainage contract" in V2_DANIEL_FINANCES (~128 AD) has no corresponding outline chapter.

### 3. Optical semaphore — no on-page Phase B–C planting scene beyond ch08 seed
**Should appear:** Phase B–C active construction, Phase C–D operational, per 04_tech_schedule and V2_HISTORICAL_IMPACT
**Reference says:** 04_tech_schedule: "Long-distance electric telegraph — CORRECTLY BLOCKED; Optical semaphore (no electricity) IS achievable and is an easy win Daniel should propose." V2_HISTORICAL_IMPACT: "By 130 AD, an experimental optical semaphore line runs from Rome to the Rhine crossing at Mogontiacum — approximately 1,200 miles." The semaphore is described as requiring only printed codebooks and trained relay-station operators — no new technology.
**What the outline has:** Ch08 plants Celer's signaling interest ("Celer asks about signaling from the basket — flags, fire relay — one line that plants the optical semaphore idea"). Ch11 pays this: "Celer's signaling interest from ch08 paid: he asks specifically about fire-relay stations." After ch11, the semaphore disappears from all Phase B–C chapters until ch49's retrospective inventory ("optical semaphore relay stations from Rome to the Rhine"). There is no ch that shows Daniel proposing the system formally, receiving funding, or describing it as under construction.
**Why it matters:** V2_HISTORICAL_IMPACT designates the semaphore as operational by 128–135 AD and part of the visible-divergence record from 121–128 AD onward. The outline skips from a Phase B seed directly to a Phase E legacy item. No chapter in Phase C–D shows the semaphore being built or proposed, leaving a 20-year gap in the tech arc.

### 4. Pendulum clock — no on-page Phase D demonstration
**Should appear:** Phase D (~115–125 AD), per reference files
**Reference says:** V2_TECH_DEEP_DIVE: "Working pendulum clock, ±10 min/day" by 108–115 AD; "±2 min/day" by 115–125 AD. 04_tech_schedule: "Pendulum clock ±2 min/day; doesn't need spring steel, uses falling weights." V2_HISTORICAL_IMPACT: the permanent contest exhibition at Saepta Julia includes "the first successful pendulum clock (submitted in 133 AD by a freedman horologist who had no knowledge of Daniel's mechanical clock attempts, only of his printed essay on the problem)."
**What the outline has:** Ch47 references "Clock escapement let-go — 'I never found this… Begin where I failed.'" This is a humbling beat. Ch49 mentions pendulum clocks in legacy inventory. There is no Phase D chapter showing Daniel attempting a pendulum clock, failing at the escapement, or demonstrating even a crude version. The humbling beat at ch47 has no prior setup in the outline — no earlier chapter shows him working on the escapement problem.
**Why it matters:** The escapement failure is listed in 04_tech_schedule as one of the six required "humbling beats." Without Phase D setup chapters showing the work, the ch47 letting-go has no dramatic grounding. Procula's ch52 escapement solution also loses force if no prior chapter shows Daniel struggling with it.

### 5. Crossbow improvements — no explicit Phase B chapter scene
**Should appear:** Phase B (ch12–15 range)
**Reference says:** 04_tech_schedule Phase B: "Crossbow improvements (spanning mechanism + bolt standardization)… goat's foot lever spanning aid… bolt standardization via printed templates." V2_HISTORICAL_IMPACT: "By 105, Daniel has also introduced crossbow spanning levers and bolt standardization to the army." The argument that crossbowmen work better in balloon baskets than archers is explicitly noted in V2_HISTORICAL_IMPACT.
**What the outline has:** Ch12 introduces stirrups. Ch13 is Sabinus's death. Ch18 mentions balloon archers but does not specify crossbowmen vs. archers. No outline chapter explicitly dramatizes Daniel introducing the goat's-foot lever or bolt standardization. V2_HISTORICAL_IMPACT says "Two crossbowmen with pre-loaded bolts in a balloon basket are a more practical weapons platform" — but the outline's balloon-archer scene at ch18 doesn't make this distinction.
**Why it matters:** The crossbow-in-balloon-basket connection is the specific detail that makes the balloon archer program militarily plausible. Without it, "balloon archers" reads as a generic capability, not the operationally specific choice the reference files require.

### 6. Prize model Beat 1 (first informal prize post) — absent from ch05/ch07
**Should appear:** Ch05 or ch07, per V2_PRIZE_INNOVATION Beat 1
**Reference says:** Beat 1 placed explicitly in "ch05 or ch07 range, ~99–101 AD." First glass clarity spec posted through a guild contact. The Brundisium glassmaker wins with his father's decolorant technique. Daniel's key recalibration: the model discloses existing knowledge, not just accelerates his own.
**What the outline has:** Ch07 mentions "rag paper experiment begun" and Macer's accountants with double-entry bookkeeping, but there is no prize post for glass clarity. Ch15 is the first explicit prize competition reference ("first prize competition posted for glass clarity"). This places the first prize post at Phase B (~102–104 AD), approximately 3–4 years later than the reference document requires.
**Why it matters:** V2_PRIZE_INNOVATION explicitly states the Brundisium glassmaker surprise is the beat that clarifies what the prize model actually does — disclosing existing craft knowledge. This is a foundational character insight for Daniel. Placing it in ch15 means Daniel operates the prize model for several chapters without this clarifying realization, which undercuts the model's presentation as organic and evolving.

### 7. Prize model Beat 3 (Glabrio recurring near-miss) — absent
**Should appear:** Ch19/ch21 (first mention), ch25, ch28, per V2_PRIZE_INNOVATION Beat 3
**Reference says:** A bronzesmith named Glabrio appears on multiple prize attempts across Phase C–D. Never wins. His story is "a small running acknowledgment that the model distributes risk by transferring some of it to people who cannot fully afford it." Named specifically in multiple chapter assignments.
**What the outline has:** Glabrio does not appear in any outline chapter. None of the Phase C–D chapters (ch19–ch33) mention a recurring near-miss figure.
**Why it matters:** Glabrio is called out in V2_PRIZE_INNOVATION as a required recurring human texture. His absence removes the prize model's ethical counterweight — the acknowledgment that prize competitions have human costs.

### 8. Prize model Beat 5 (Crispus figures out the aggregate) — absent
**Should appear:** Ch25–ch27 range, per V2_PRIZE_INNOVATION Beat 5
**Reference says:** "Crispus understands what Daniel is doing before anyone else does. Not the individual prizes — those are just commercial transactions — but the aggregate: Daniel is using prize competitions to crowdsource the future… His reaction changes the texture of his antagonism."
**What the outline has:** Ch27 (The Accusation) focuses on the impiety crisis. Ch33 collapses Crispus's arc (his "public credibility collapse" happens at ch33, not through a changed register of antagonism in ch25–27). There is no outline chapter showing Crispus's shift from blunt fraud/impiety attacks to the more sophisticated cultural-legitimacy argument the reference document requires.
**Why it matters:** Without this beat, Crispus's antagonism stays one-dimensional. The reference document describes a shift in register (from "fraud and impiety" to "prizes normalize speculation that destabilizes guild authority") that would make Crispus a more formidable and interesting antagonist through Phase C.

## TECH WITH WRONG TIMING

The 3–6x spyglass arriving at ch18 is also slightly late per V2_TECH_DEEP_DIVE, which says "First rough 3–5x spyglass" by 103–106 AD.

### 2. Compass — outline implies ch23 is its first appearance (~109–110 AD); reference implies earlier working prototype and navigation use
**Outline says:** Ch23: "Compass working prototype first confirmed on-page." Ch15: "Compass prototype in early stages."
**Reference says:** V2_HISTORICAL_IMPACT divergence table: "By 110 AD, a trade route runs from Hispania to Britain…The magnetic compass (prototype by ~108 AD) enables winter navigation." 04_tech_schedule: the magnetic compass is placed in Phase C (103–110 AD) as an "easy win once lodestone use is established." V2_TECH_DEEP_DIVE: compass is a "MAXIMUM" priority that enables Atlantic planning.
**Gap:** Ch23 places the first confirmed working prototype at 109–110 AD; V2_HISTORICAL_IMPACT says prototype by ~108 AD and in active trade navigation by 110 AD. Ch15 mentions "compass prototype in early stages" (~102–104 AD), which is consistent, but the outline's "first confirmed on-page" language at ch23 implies no usable compass exists before that chapter — while V2_HISTORICAL_IMPACT treats compass as enabling navigation from ~108 AD. Minor gap; the ch15 "early stages" language should be resolved against ch23 "first confirmed."

### 3. Volta pile — outline says "initiated" in Phase B arc table; reference says Phase C
**Outline says:** Recurring arc table row: "Volta pile / compass — Phase B: initiated; Phase C: demonstrated ch23."
**Reference says:** 04_tech_schedule places Volta pile explicitly in Phase C (103–110 AD): "PLAUSIBLE IN 5–10 YEARS once he has the materials and the moment to connect the memory." V2_TECH_DEEP_DIVE places Volta pile at "110–120 AD" for first result and notes it as "LOW" priority.
**Gap:** The arc table's "Phase B: initiated" implies Volta pile work begins in Phase B (~99–110 AD), but the tech schedule places it in Phase C. If "initiated" means Daniel is thinking about it in Phase B, this is harmless. If it means on-page Phase B scenes exist showing Volta pile work, that is too early. The ch23 "demonstrated" placement is correct. Clarification needed on what "initiated" means in practice.

### 4. Optical semaphore — operational in legacy inventory at ch49 (~153–155 AD); reference says Rome-to-Rhine line operational by 128–135 AD
**Outline says:** Ch49 (Phase E, ~153–155 AD): "optical semaphore relay stations from Rome to the Rhine (messages in hours)" in legacy inventory. Ch49 is approximately 20–25 years after the reference date.
**Reference says:** V2_HISTORICAL_IMPACT: "By 130 AD, an experimental optical semaphore line runs from Rome to the Rhine." The divergence visibility timeline lists the semaphore as visible at "128–135 AD" for perceptive Romans. Phase D (110–125 AD) in 04_tech_schedule notes: "Succession terror / rebuilding" but the semaphore is proposed as an "easy win" in Phase C.
**Gap:** The outline does not give the semaphore an on-page origin chapter anywhere near the correct Phase B–C proposal or Phase C–D construction window. It first appears as a fait accompli in ch49. This is a 20+ year late placement for visibility.

### Phase C (~110–117 AD, ch23–33): PARTIALLY CONSISTENT, ONE MISMATCH
V2_DANIEL_FINANCES: 3,000,000 → 25,000,000 HS by end of Phase C. Annual income 1,500,000–3,000,000 HS. "Net worth by 115 AD: 15,000,000–25,000,000 HS."

**Mismatch:** V2_DANIEL_FINANCES places the steam pump's first commercial operation at 125–130 AD (Phase D/E transition), while the financial figures for Phase C (15–25M HS by 115 AD) appear to be driven by paper/press and balloon contracts, not the steam pump. The outline does not include the steam pump in Phase C at all, which is consistent with the finance document's timeline. **No mismatch on this point.**
**Actual mismatch:** V2_DANIEL_FINANCES places "First Paper/Press Licensing Deal (107 AD)" at 500,000 HS/year guaranteed + 250,000 HS upfront — a major financial inflection point in Phase B/C transition. The outline's ch19 (writing contest launch, ~107–108 AD) does not reference this imperial paper supply deal. The deal's drama ("freedmen administrators initially try to steal the design; Daniel withholds the paper sizing step") has no corresponding outline chapter beat.

### Phase D (~117–125 AD, ch32–38): CONSISTENT
V2_DANIEL_FINANCES: Succession crisis; ~10,000,000–18,000,000 HS surviving after losses. Wealth as armor.
Outline: Ch32–ch34 cover the Hadrian transition. Ch33 shows Crispus's public credibility collapse (Hadrian's protection). Ch34 has Scaeva's debt-collection deflected. The "wealth as armor" framing in V2_DANIEL_FINANCES matches the outline's characterization of Daniel navigating this phase through economic resilience and counter-position for every angle. **Consistent.**

### Phase E (~125–155 AD, ch38–50): PARTIALLY CONSISTENT, ONE MISMATCH
V2_DANIEL_FINANCES: Steam pump online; annual income 3,000,000–8,000,000 HS by 135 AD; total wealth 50,000,000–100,000,000 HS by death (~155 AD), "among the 50 wealthiest people in the Roman Empire."

V2_DANIEL_FINANCES gives this contract specific drama: "two pumps fail; Daniel nearly loses the contract; when it finally works, water pours out for three straight days; Daniel stands at the mine head and for the first time understands he has actually changed the world."
**Mismatch:** This scene — described as a formative moment in V2_DANIEL_FINANCES — has no assigned outline chapter. Phase E chapters (ch38–50) do not reference the mine drainage contract or the moment of realization at the mine head. The outline's Phase E covers consolidation (ch38), institutions (ch39), books (ch40), teaching (ch41), Heras's death (ch42), family/truth-telling (ch43), world bends (ch44), institution work (ch45), Hadrian dies (ch46), old age (ch47), deaths of Marcia/Macer (ch48), legacy (ch49), death (ch50). None of these chapters has a mine-head scene or a steam pump commissioning scene.
**Why it matters:** The mine drainage contract is the financial inflection point from Phase D to Phase E. Without it, the Phase E wealth level (50–100M HS, top-50 Roman wealthy) arrives without a grounding scene showing how the wealth actually compounded.
