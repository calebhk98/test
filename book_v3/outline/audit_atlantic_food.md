## ATLANTIC ARC — MISSING / WRONG

- **Syphilis reverse exchange: not explicitly noted in any chapter.** The reference (V2_NEW_WORLD_CONTACT.md, Section 2) specifically states: "The reverse-flow risk to Rome is real" and notes syphilis is endemic in the pre-Columbian Caribbean in 113 AD. No chapter in the outline mentions syphilis flowing back east. The disease report in ch28 lists "TB and malaria contact" and "no catastrophic plague" but does not flag syphilis as a return-voyage risk. Reference requires this be noted. **Missing from all chapters.**

- **Colonial independence prediction (encyclopedia, never said aloud): not placed in any specific chapter.** The reference (V2_NEW_WORLD_CONTACT.md, Section 4; V2_STORY_PLOT_NOTES.md Section 1.3) is explicit: Daniel writes his revolutionary-war prediction in the encyclopedia in a section framed as "theoretical governance considerations." This is the "most dangerous passage Daniel writes." The outline places encyclopedia work in ch40 ("Political and economic theory added to the encyclopedia as a brief honest entry") and ch47 (Antonine Plague prep notes) but never assigns the colonial independence passage specifically to any chapter. The beat appears in no chapter brief. **Missing — no chapter placement.**

- **The moral trap (reducing disease → organized resistance → military conquest) not present.** The reference (V2_NEW_WORLD_CONTACT.md, Section 6) establishes the moral trap: Daniel's quarantine protocols reduce transmission, which preserves organized indigenous resistance, which means Rome must conquer militarily rather than epidemiologically — and he must decide whether that is better or worse. The ch28 disease report notes "the crew lost two men; no catastrophic plague" but does not engage the moral trap logic. No chapter explores this "Is it better or worse?" tension. **Missing from all chapters.**

- **Daniel's quarantine protocols and their effect on transmission not mentioned.** The reference states Daniel's quarantine protocols reduce transmission 20–40%, and that this has the perverse effect of preserving organized resistance. The outline's ch28 disease report says nothing about quarantine protocols being on the expedition or reducing transmission. **Missing — no chapter placement.**

- **The "returned local person" beat absent from the outline.** The reference (V2_NEW_WORLD_CONTACT.md, Section 6) devotes an entire section to the returned local person as one of the novel's key scenes: "This is the scene the novel has been building toward since page one." V2_NEW_WORLD_CONTACT.md Section 8 (Chapter Assignments) places this at ch47 (~140–143 AD) as the first-return-news scene, with Daniel at the dock, the crowd, the local person. The outline's ch28 (return news dispatch) and ch29 (second expedition) are both tent/desk scenes. No chapter brief in the outline places Daniel at the dock for a return, or mentions the local person being brought back. **This is a major missing beat — not in any chapter.**

- **Daniel's private written confession ("I knew. I had always known.") not placed.** The reference's Section 6 contains Daniel's most honest encyclopedia passage, written in English, explicitly acknowledging the lie about "uninhabited" land. Section 8 assigns this to ch48 (~143–147 AD). The outline's ch40 mentions "Political and economic theory added to the encyclopedia" and ch47 mentions encyclopedia writing for Antonine Plague prep, but neither assigns the inhabited-land confession passage. **Missing — no chapter placement.**

- **The "lie about uninhabited land" (Daniel calls it "probably uninhabited" to get the program started) not established in the outline.** The reference (Section 6) is specific: Daniel says the western continent is "probably uninhabited" from day one because it is the only version of the truth that gets the program started. He lives with this lie for fifteen years. The outline's ch03 shows the dirt map with "two western continents prominent" but no lie about population. No chapter brief sets up or resolves the inhabited/uninhabited deception. **Missing — no chapter placement.**

- **Disease mortality range: outline says "no catastrophic plague" but does not establish the 30–50% range.** The reference states 25–45% mortality over the first generation of sustained contact. The ch28 dispatch says only "no catastrophic plague, as Rome hasn't had the Antonine Plague yet." This framing is correct as far as it goes, but the outline never establishes the positive claim: contact WILL cause serious mortality (30–50%), just not the 80–95% Columbian Exchange level. The distinction matters narratively. **Incomplete — the "not catastrophic but still serious" framing is absent from all chapter briefs.**

- **Second-wave expedition timing slightly inconsistent.** Reference says return ~117–119 AD; second expedition ~120–125 AD. The outline shows ch29 (~116–117 AD) with "the second expedition is provisioned and being organized" — which is one to two years ahead of the reference's ~120 AD start. Minor inconsistency but worth flagging.

- **ch36 food beat (tomato-pasta at Hadrian meeting): placement may violate gating.** Ch36 is dated ~121–125 AD. The outline places a "tomato-pasta or sweet potato dish served at the meeting (one small detail grounding the New World crops in Daniel's daily life)" here. The first return is ~117–119 AD; first tomato harvest (reference: seeds planted, one difficult first season, real harvest in second) would be ~119–122 AD at earliest. Ch36 at ~121–125 AD is within range — barely acceptable but contingent on the specific year within the range. If ch36 is ~121 AD, the tomato harvest timing is tight. If ~123–125 AD, it is fine. The outline should specify the crop is sweet potato rather than tomato for this scene if the chapter falls early in its range. **Flag: tomato timing marginal at the early end of ch36's date range.**

## GATING VIOLATIONS

- **No hard gating violations found.** The outline does not place any New World crop (tomato, potato, sweet potato, maize, cacao) in any chapter before the Atlantic return. The Phase table correctly places "New World crops arrive" only in Phase D (ch34+). The first concrete food placement of New World crops is ch35 (~121–122 AD), well after the ch28 return dispatch (~117–119 AD). The gating is respected.

- **Marginal timing concern at ch36 (tomato-pasta).** As noted above: the ch36 tomato-pasta beat is defensible at the late end of ch36's ~121–125 AD date range but tight at the early end if the first return was ~119 AD and tomatoes need two growing seasons to establish. This is not a clear violation but should be confirmed against the specific AD year assigned to ch36 when written. Assigning the sweet potato dish rather than tomato to this scene would eliminate the risk.

- **Rubber gasket application not gated.** V2_NEW_WORLD_CONTACT.md Section 5 specifies rubber as arriving on the second expedition (Gulf Coast contact) and Daniel immediately applying it to steam pump gaskets. The outline does not assign this application to any chapter, so there is no gating violation — but the beat is simply absent. If a writer were to ad-lib rubber into the outline without a chapter assignment, the risk of it appearing too early (pre-return) would exist. The absence of the beat is the risk, not a current violation.

## SUMMARY OF TOP FINDINGS

1. The outline's Atlantic arc correctly places the departure (~112–113 AD), multi-crop return (ch28), and Hadrian's second-wave institutionalization (ch36), and the disease profile is right (TB + malaria, no smallpox). However, three required beats from the reference files are entirely absent from all chapter briefs: the syphilis reverse exchange, the colonial independence prediction (encyclopedia entry, never said aloud), and the moral trap (quarantine reduces mortality → organized resistance → military conquest — is that better or worse?).

2. The "returned local person" scene — described in V2_NEW_WORLD_CONTACT.md as "the scene the novel has been building toward since page one" — is not placed in any chapter of the outline. The reference assigns it to approximately ch47; no chapter brief mentions a local person arriving in Rome with the expedition.

3. The foundational deception (Daniel calling the western continent "probably uninhabited" to get the program started, and his private written confession in English in the encyclopedia) is not established or resolved in any chapter brief, despite the reference treating it as the Atlantic arc's central moral thread.

4. The food arc is well-structured in the outline for Parts I–V, with garum evolution, pasta, army contrast, and the tavern food scene all correctly placed. Two specific preservation-specified beats from V2_FOOD_ARC.md are missing from their assigned chapter entries: the cacao/hot-chocolate scene is not in the ch43 brief, and the late-life garden tomato is not in the ch50 brief.

5. No gating violations were found — New World crops do not appear in any chapter before the Atlantic return. The ch36 tomato-pasta beat is marginally tight on timing at the early end of the chapter's date range and should default to sweet potato if the scene falls before ~122 AD.
