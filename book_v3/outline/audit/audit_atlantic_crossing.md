- Syphilis flows back east as **confirmed** reverse exchange (syphilis is endemic in pre-Columbian Americas and flows to Rome with returning crew)

### Moral trap

- Daniel must **never speak this aloud** — cipher/encyclopedia only

### Foundational lie
- Daniel tells officials the western continent is "probably uninhabited, or sparsely so" — this is how he gets the program started
- V2_NEW_WORLD_CONTACT.md §6 specifies a **private written confession** in his encyclopedia: the passage "I knew. I had always known." This is the moment he admits in writing that he lied from the start.

### Colonial independence

- Written in his private cipher/encyclopedia; **never said aloud to anyone, ever**

## SECTION 3: DISCREPANCIES

### DISCREPANCY 1 — Compass timeline is off by 4–6 years (HIGH PRIORITY)

**Planning docs say:** Compass working by 103–105 AD. This is the first link in the bottleneck chain. master_outline.md Part IV states explicitly: "The compass is working by 103–105 AD."

**Outline shows:** Ch15 (~102–104 AD) has only "compass prototype in early stages." The cross-chapter continuity block in V2_REVISED_OUTLINE.md says "Compass working by ch23" (~109–110 AD).

**Impact:** If the compass is not working until ch23 (~109–110 AD), the entire bottleneck timeline collapses forward by 4–6 years, which pushes the crossing later than the KEY DECISION date of ~112–115 AD. Either the compass must be confirmed working in a chapter before ch23, or ch23 must be assigned a date earlier than ~109–110 AD. The current outline as written implies the crossing could not have happened until ~116–120 AD at earliest if the compass is only ready at ch23.

**Fix needed:** Ch15 should show compass in early stages (as now), and a chapter between ch16 and ch22 should confirm the compass working (~104–106 AD). Alternatively, the cross-chapter continuity block must be corrected from "ch23" to the correct chapter. Ch23 may be the chapter where compass-enabled navigation is *used for expedition planning*, not the chapter where the device first works.

### DISCREPANCY 2 — No ongoing Atlantic planning shown between ch03 and ch22

**Planning docs say:** "He is planning from year one; execution is gated on money and standing, not on the idea." V2_STORY_PLOT_NOTES.md §1.3 emphasizes that Daniel's awareness of the westward route is present from arrival.

**Outline shows:** The earliest Atlantic thinking in the outline is the citrus note in ch22 (~108–109 AD). Chapters ch03 through ch21 contain no beat — even a private mental note — establishing that Daniel has the crossing in mind. The "planning from year one" intent is structurally absent.

**Impact:** Daniel reads as reactive (he thinks of the crossing only when he's wealthy enough) rather than as someone who has carried this plan for a decade. This affects his interiority and the weight of the ch25 "serious planning begins" moment.

**Fix needed:** One interior beat — even a single paragraph — should be placed in an early chapter (ch03–ch08 range) where Daniel, encountering Roman ships or hearing about the Fortunate Isles, notes privately that the westward passage is known and that when the time comes, he knows exactly how to do it. This requires a minor addition to one chapter brief, not a new chapter.

### DISCREPANCY 3 — Disease mortality range is wrong in ch28

**Planning docs say:** 30–50% mortality where contact is sustained (KEY DECISIONS, confirmed in V2_NEW_WORLD_CONTACT.md §1: "25–45% mortality estimate for first generation of sustained contact").

**Outline shows:** Ch28 states "~20–30% impact where contact was sustained."

**Impact:** The lower range (20%) undersells the devastation and misrepresents the moral stakes. The planning docs chose 30–50% specifically to distinguish this from the Columbian Exchange (80–95%) while still conveying catastrophic harm.

**Fix needed:** Ch28 brief must be corrected to "30–50% mortality" (or "25–45%") to match planning docs.

### DISCREPANCY 4 — Syphilis treated as uncertain in ch28; planning docs treat it as confirmed

**Planning docs say:** "Syphilis flows back east as reverse exchange." V2_NEW_WORLD_CONTACT.md §2 states this definitively: syphilis is endemic in pre-Columbian Americas, reverse-flow risk is real and certain.

**Outline shows:** Ch28: "Daniel notes what the crew may have carried in both directions: he does not know." The framing makes syphilis a possibility Daniel is uncertain about.

**Impact:** The KEY DECISION is that syphilis *does* flow back — it is a confirmed plot element. Daniel's uncertainty is reasonable in the moment (he cannot do a PCR test), but the chapter brief must be written so that readers and writing agents understand the narrative outcome is syphilis returning to Rome, not merely a possibility.

**Fix needed:** Ch28 brief should distinguish between Daniel's in-story uncertainty (he suspects but cannot confirm) and the narrative-level fact (syphilis does flow back). A note such as: "Daniel does not know for certain, but the narrative confirms the reverse exchange occurs" should be added to the brief to prevent writing agents from treating this as unresolved.

### DISCREPANCY 5 — Quarantine protocols on the expedition not placed in any chapter

**Planning docs say:** "Daniel's quarantine protocols reduce transmission 20–40% but can't stop it entirely." The implication is that these protocols travel *with* the expedition — a ship's physician, written instructions, or isolation procedures go on the fleet.

**Outline shows:** Ch28 mentions the moral trap of quarantine protocols but treats this entirely as Daniel's retrospective moral calculus after return. No chapter brief (ch25, ch26, ch27) places quarantine protocols, a physician, or written medical instructions on the ships before departure.

**Impact:** The moral trap only lands if Daniel actively tried to reduce harm and still caused it. If no protocols went with the fleet, the moral position changes from "I tried and it wasn't enough" to "I knew and sent nothing." The distinction matters for character.

**Fix needed:** Ch26 or ch27 brief should include a beat where Daniel arranges for medical protocols/a physician to travel with the expedition — framed as him doing what he can, knowing it won't be enough.

### DISCREPANCY 6 — Private written confession ("I knew. I had always known.") not placed in any chapter

**Planning docs say:** V2_NEW_WORLD_CONTACT.md §6 specifies that Daniel's encyclopedia contains a confession passage where he admits he lied about the western continent being uninhabited. The passage establishes this as a deliberate choice, not naivety.

**Outline shows:** The foundational lie is established in ch25 (correctly placed). The colonial independence prediction is placed in ch40 (correctly placed). But no chapter brief places the private written confession — the moment Daniel writes that he always knew — in any specific chapter.

**Impact:** Without a placed chapter for this confession, writing agents have no anchor for the scene. It will either be omitted or improvised in the wrong location.

**Fix needed:** The confession should be placed in the encyclopedia either at ch28 (when the first return makes the stakes real — he now knows the continent is very much inhabited and his lie has consequences) or at ch40 (alongside the colonial independence prediction, as a cluster of private reckoning entries). Ch28 is the stronger placement because it is the moment the lie can no longer be sustained even privately. The chapter brief for whichever chapter is chosen must name this beat explicitly.
