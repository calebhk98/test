## WHAT THE PLANNING DOCS SAY

V2_ULPIA_EDUCATION.md is the authoritative planning document for this arc. Its key decisions:

### English as a secret language
- Daniel teaches both children English from birth — not as a foreign language but as the household private language.
- Lucanus learns it from birth (~108 AD). Ulpia learns it from birth (~118 AD), receiving it from two sources (Daniel and Lucanus, who already has it).
- All private conversations between Daniel and his children happen in English.
- He tells them the full truth about his origins in English only, never in Latin.
- English is described as technically necessary for the advanced knowledge content: "Some of what he knows can only be said in the words he learned it in."
- In public, the language is visible but reads to outsiders as Daniel's "Thulean homeland dialect."

### What he teaches them — scope and level
- The planning document explicitly describes a curriculum reaching approximately 10th–11th grade modern equivalence.
- Mathematics: fractions, long division, remainders, algebra, geometry (including applications beyond Euclid), and the conceptual shape of calculus (rates of change, area under a curve — he cannot reconstruct the formal machinery but gives Ulpia the idea).
- Physics: Newton's three laws, demonstrated with objects; gravity as a force between all masses; why tides move; escape velocity; orbital mechanics.
- Chemistry: the periodic table as a concept; elements he can reconstruct confidently (first twenty or so); atoms and molecular structure; why things too small to see can make you sick; how viruses and bacteria differ from each other.
- History of the future: medieval period, Renaissance, Enlightenment, World War I, World War II (including the Holocaust, explained carefully), the Cold War, the space race.
- Technology concepts: internal combustion, flight (the wing principle), radio, cameras, computers, the internet, satellites, rockets, space travel.
- Stories: Shakespeare plots (both children receive these; Ulpia has strong opinions about Hamlet, Romeo and Juliet, and A Midsummer Night's Dream); Jules Verne premises; science fiction (Star Wars, ET, Jurassic Park, The Martian, 2001).
- Ethics: conversations, not lectures — Daniel tells them what he cannot make himself believe is normal in Rome (slavery, what women are permitted), while also telling them how his own world continued to do harm.
- Food from his world: described in detail, often as things they cannot have yet (pizza needs tomatoes, ice cream needs refrigeration, popcorn needs corn).
- Sports and daily life: American football, soccer, Formula 1, baseball, the modern Olympics; what a city looks like with electric light; hospitals; supermarkets; schools with specialist teachers.

### The truth-telling scenes
- **Lucanus** is told the full truth at approximately age 14–16, ~122–124 AD. He already has all the knowledge content; the disclosure is context. His question is "What does this mean you owe Rome?" — not wonder, obligation.
- The planning document specifies this as "offstage, referenced" (Summary Table: ch38 or ch43).
- **Ulpia** is told the full truth at approximately age 14–16, ~132–134 AD, in ch43 territory. The scene is in English, in his private study. It is not dramatic or sentimental — he states facts, she asks questions. Her first question is about evidence (how does he know he didn't imagine it?). Her central question is "What was the world like?" — the question Lucanus did not ask.
- The planning document specifies the truth-telling for Ulpia as ch43 (Section 6, Summary Table).

### The playground
- Built ~111–115 AD when Lucanus is 3–7 years old.
- Components: swing (on a wooden frame, distinct from Roman oscillum), slide (smooth inclined board, oiled), monkey bars (horizontal bars between two upright posts), sandbox.
- The signal beat is not the building — it is a visitor noticing the existing structure and asking about it.
- Planning document specifies placement: "ch35–36 area" (Section 5); the Summary Table (Section 6) assigns it to **ch35 (~121–122 AD) — New addition, one paragraph**.
- The arc's note on scale: "One paragraph. One note from a visitor or neighbor. Then the swing and the slide and the monkey bars are just there, in the garden, being used."

### Lucanus's arc (distinct from Ulpia's)
- Chooses law, not the workshop. This is a complete statement about who Lucanus is, not a tragedy.
- Uses future-derived legal principles (presumption of innocence, evidence standards, due process) without attribution.
- Physically absent from workshop after his legal apprenticeship begins. Relationship with Daniel is warm but different from the Daniel/Ulpia dynamic.
- His English is more formal than Ulpia's — he learned at age 6, not from birth. Audible to Daniel.
- Checks in once on whether Ulpia has the catalog in hand. When she says yes, he does not ask again. This is trust, not indifference.

### What the arc must NOT do (V2_ULPIA_EDUCATION.md Section 7)
- Do not make Ulpia preternaturally wise.
- Do not have either child go around correcting Romans with future knowledge.
- Do not make Daniel's lessons explicit school scenes.
- Do not make the playground a major set piece.
- Do not have the truth-telling scene become a weeping reunion.
- Do not present Lucanus's choice of law as a loss.

## DISCREPANCIES

### DISCREPANCY 1: Playground chapter assignment — internal contradiction in the outline
**Planning doc says:** ch35, one paragraph, a visitor noticing the structure when Ulpia is ~3–4 years old.
**Outline cross-continuity section says:** "Visible ch35 (Ulpia discovering it) and ch38 (Ulpia at 9x table scene)."

**Ch38 individual entry says:** The playground is ALSO described here as "visible in the garden" with "a neighbor or Ulpia's nurse finds it slightly odd."

The outline therefore places the playground as a beat in BOTH ch35 and ch38. The planning document specifies it as a single one-paragraph beat in ch35, with the structure simply existing as ambient background thereafter. Having it be a noticed oddity by a neighbor in both chapters is redundant. The ch35 placement is correct per the planning document; the ch38 beat appears to be a duplication. A writer following the outline may write two playground-noticing scenes, which the planning document explicitly says should not happen.

**Severity:** Moderate. Not a contradiction between outline and planning doc (the outline does include ch35); the problem is that ch38 adds a second noticing beat the planning doc does not authorize.
