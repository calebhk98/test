## 1. WHAT THE PLANNING DOCS SAY

### V2_STORY_PLOT_NOTES.md §1.2 — Core V2 Decision

The V1 permanent flight ban was identified as a narrative mistake. The V2 reframe:

- After Sabinus dies (ch13–14), Daniel has a genuine grief period and a safety redesign of ~1–2 years. **The vow is stated on the page as: "I won't rush untrained people into an untested design."**
- The vow covers manned fire-flight with untrained operators in untested designs. It does NOT cover unmanned work, kite-class gliders, or systematically tested platforms.
- Unmanned and kite-class work explicitly resumes within 1–2 years.
- **Daniel DOES struggle with whether to return to manned flight — this is real moral weight, not avoidance.** He chooses to continue after calculating that the military advantage saves more lives than the program risks. He sets conditions: minimum 50 hours tethered, double-basket construction, no untrained passengers, written waiver.
- By ~105–106 AD, manned balloon archers are military doctrine. The planning doc states: "Manned balloons with crossbowmen are military doctrine by 106 AD."
- No Roman general would accept "we stopped because one person died in a prototype" — they do not apply that logic to catapults or siege engineers.

### V2_HISTORICAL_IMPACT.md — Year-by-Year Divergence

- **105–106 AD:** "Manned balloon with archer at Sarmizegetusa" is listed as a major divergence event.
- **107–108 AD:** Trajan asks Daniel two specific questions: "(1) Can it carry fire? (No, not reliably.) (2) Can an enemy shoot the balloon down? (Yes, if they have archers and get close.)" He then assigns a permanent unit of balloon operators to the Praetorian camp. By 108 AD, manned observation balloons with military-trained archers are standard doctrine for siege operations.
- **113–116 AD:** "Balloon archers in Parthian campaign devastating cavalry; printed maps duplicated for legates."
- **Formation detail:** "Two balloon rigs per legion on campaign" is established doctrine.
- **Crossbow specification:** Crossbows are specified over bows for basket use because a pre-spanned crossbow requires only aim and trigger in a swaying confined basket.
- **Trajan's Column:** One panel carved showing "a large round object tethered by rope above the Roman lines, with a figure in a basket" — documented as a real historical artifact in the narrative.
- **Atlantic expeditions (Part V onward):** Balloon rigs are stated as standard equipment on ocean voyages.

## 3. DISCREPANCIES

### DISCREPANCY 1 — Vow phrasing inconsistency (minor but requires fix)

**Planning doc (V2_STORY_PLOT_NOTES §1.2):** "I won't **rush** untrained people into an untested design."
**V2_REVISED_OUTLINE ch13:** "I won't **rush** untrained people into an untested design." (matches planning doc — CORRECT)
**updated_ch01_18.md ch13:** "I won't **put** untrained people into an untested design." (does NOT match)

The word "rush" is load-bearing: it encodes that the vow is about pace and preparation, not an absolute prohibition. "Put" weakens this distinction. The updated_ch01_18 ch13 entry is inconsistent with both the planning doc and the V2_REVISED_OUTLINE. A writing agent working from updated_ch01_18 would produce the wrong phrasing.

### DISCREPANCY 2 — Trajan's two-questions scene is unassigned to any chapter

**Planning doc (V2_HISTORICAL_IMPACT)** describes a specific, narratively important scene: Trajan asks Daniel two questions — "(1) Can it carry fire? (2) Can an enemy shoot the balloon down?" — and then assigns a permanent balloon unit to the Praetorian camp. This scene happens in 107–108 AD.

**No chapter entry in any outline file** explicitly assigns this scene to a specific chapter or even lists it as a required beat. Ch18 (Second Dacian War, 106 AD) ends before this conversation would logically occur. Ch19–21 cover 106–109 AD broadly, but none names Trajan's two-questions moment.

This is a gap: a key scene that cements Trajan's buy-in and creates the permanent balloon unit has no chapter home in the outline.

### DISCREPANCY 3 — Arc status table places "manned archers doctrine" in Phase C; actual doctrine is established at the end of Phase B

**V2_REVISED_OUTLINE arc table:**
- Phase B (ch10–18): "Sabinus death/vow"
- Phase C (ch19–28): "manned archers doctrine"

**Actual outline entries:**
- Ch17 (Phase B): manned program resumes with trained operators
- Ch18 (Phase B): balloon archers at Sarmizegetusa, "this is military doctrine now, not an experiment"

Doctrine is *established* at the end of Phase B (ch18), not in Phase C. The arc table is misleading: a writer reading only the table would place the doctrine beat a full phase too late. Phase C entries should say "doctrine consolidated / expanded" rather than "manned archers doctrine" as if it first appears there.

### DISCREPANCY 4 — Cross-chapter continuity note omits two key doctrine details

**V2_REVISED_OUTLINE cross-chapter continuity:** "Manned balloon archers operational by ch18. Military doctrine established. The flight vow covers only untrained operators in untested designs — not this program."

Missing from this authoritative continuity note:
1. **Crossbow-over-bow specification:** Crossbows are required for basket use (pre-spanned crossbow requires only aim and trigger in a swaying basket). This detail appears in updated_ch28 and planning docs but is not in the canonical continuity section — meaning a writing agent working from continuity notes alone could write a ch17–18 scene with archers using bows.
2. **Two balloon rigs per legion on campaign:** Formation doctrine. Appears in V2_HISTORICAL_IMPACT and master_outline and updated_ch28 tech notes but is absent from the cross-chapter continuity section.

### DISCREPANCY 5 — Atlantic expeditions: balloon rigs completely absent from ch37–53 outline entries

**Planning docs (master_outline.md flight arc tracker):** "Atlantic expeditions (Part V onward): balloon rigs standard equipment on ocean voyages."

**updated_ch37_53.md:** No mention of balloon rigs, aerial platforms, or balloon-equipped ships in any chapter entry across all 17 chapters (ch37–53).

This is a significant gap. If balloon rigs are "standard equipment on ocean voyages" per the planning doc, the relevant Atlantic expedition chapters (likely in Part V, roughly ch37–43) need explicit notes about their presence and use. A writing agent working from these chapter entries would have no instruction to include them.

### DISCREPANCY 6 — Ch17 moral struggle beat is compressed; full emotional arc not specified

**Planning doc (V2_STORY_PLOT_NOTES §1.2):** "Daniel DOES struggle with whether to return to manned flight — this is real moral weight, not avoidance. The beat is that he chooses to continue, calculating that the military advantage saves more lives than the program risks. He sets the conditions: minimum 50 hours tethered, double-basket construction, no untrained passengers, written waiver. Then he makes the call."

**V2_REVISED_OUTLINE ch17:** Daniel "makes the calculation (the military advantage saves more lives than the program risks) and names it to himself."

The outline collapses the moral struggle into a single clause. The planning doc specifically flags this as "real moral weight" requiring development — the moment Daniel sets the written safety conditions and consciously decides to resume should be a full scene beat, not a parenthetical note. A writing agent given only the ch17 outline entry would likely produce a brief internalization rather than the extended moral reckoning the planning doc calls for.

### DISCREPANCY 7 — "Two rigs per legion" formation detail absent from cross-chapter continuity

Already noted under Discrepancy 4, but worth separating for the CHAPTERS NEEDING FIXES section: this formation detail ("two balloon rigs per legion on campaign") first crystallizes as doctrine in 107–108 AD and should be traceable in ch18–19 transition notes, not only in ch28's tech requirements list.
