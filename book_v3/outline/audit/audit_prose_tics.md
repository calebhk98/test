## SECTION 1: BANNED PATTERNS STILL IN THE OUTLINE

The V2_REVISED_OUTLINE.md and master_outline.md do a solid job of flagging specific banned patterns by chapter — they call out correctio blockers, "the way" count overruns, future-vantage lines, and "I want to be exact" scaffolding by line number. That is the correct approach and the coverage is reasonably thorough. However, the outline documents themselves use several of the banned patterns in their own scene-description prose.

### 1.1 Throat-clearing / Meta-disclaimer language in the outline text

The V2_REVISED_OUTLINE.md describes Chapter 33 as containing "the scene the novel has been building toward since page one." This is the outline narrator stepping outside the story to assign weight before the reader can feel it — the same move banned in Sections 1.2 and TIC V1 of the prose guide. The outline should describe what happens in the scene, not announce the scene's significance.

The master_outline.md does this more broadly. The Part VII section contains: "The correct emotional note at death: not triumphant, not despairing. The specific ambivalence of a man who built something larger than he intended and cannot see where it lands." This is outline-writer throat-clearing — telling the writing agent how to feel about the scene before writing it, in exactly the preamble register the manuscript is supposed to avoid.

The master_outline.md also uses: "He knows something is in motion. He does not know what it will become." This is a wisdom-button delivered directly in the planning document. If a writer absorbs this framing uncritically, it is likely to land in the chapter prose as a wisdom-button close.

### 1.2 "The shape of" / geometry crutch in the outline

V2_REVISED_OUTLINE.md, Ch22 fix note: "building something nobody has the shape of yet" — this is the exact "shape" crutch (TIC V6) being left in the outline's own recommended fix language. The note flags the correctio blocker on the same line but does not catch that "shape" is itself a tic word that should be replaced.

### 1.3 "The thing" abstraction

V2_REVISED_OUTLINE.md, Ch25 notes: "the true thing in my whole strange enterprise, the figures, the steady work under the spectacle." The phrase "true thing in my whole strange enterprise" is a verbatim example of TIC V7 given as a violation in PROSE_PATTERNS_TO_AVOID.md (Section TIC V7: "The true thing in my whole strange enterprise"). This line appears in the outline's scene description as if it is acceptable narration, which may cause writing agents to reproduce it.

V2_REVISED_OUTLINE.md, Ch06: "No single-paragraph crowd-description. Use one specific face and one specific smell." The instruction itself is sound, but the previous sentence describing the ch06 scene uses "the whole fight" as a noun-phrase — a mild "the whole of" instance (TIC V6 territory, tracked at max 1 per 5 chapters).

### 1.4 Cycle of Defeat — outline guidance is present but incomplete

The outline does flag the Cycle of Defeat (TIC V16) at the document level (PROSE_PATTERNS_TO_AVOID.md), and V2_REVISED_OUTLINE.md breaks the pattern in two tech arcs as instructed: the spinning wheel is rejected on economic grounds (not technical failure), and the optical semaphore succeeds and stays working. However, the outline's arc descriptions for the printing press, the steam pump, and the pendulum clock all still follow the pattern: demonstrate → works imperfectly → iterates over decades without resolution. The steam pump arc in particular (Newcomen-class pump, bronze cylinder, leaking leather gaskets — "crude and dangerous and barely efficient") is a textbook Cycle of Defeat that the outline does not flag as needing variation.

### 1.5 Socratic Echo — not flagged in the outline at all

The outline contains no explicit warning against the Socratic Echo (TIC V15) in any per-chapter note. Given that this pattern appeared in V1 with at least two named examples (Apollodorus's cup-pushed-mouth-down metaphor, Hermes summarizing the quenching process), the absence of chapter-level warnings is a gap. The outline for Ch33 specifically sets up a scene where Daniel meets the indigenous person from the western islands through an interpreter — and the outline's description of what "passes between them" is "limited and strange," which is the correct instinct. But ch23 (Heras present for the Volta pile demonstration: "interested in the epistemology") and ch35 (the Hadrian testing dialogue, preserved verbatim) both have the structural conditions for Socratic Echo and neither ch-note warns against it.

## SECTION 2: CHAPTER SPOT-CHECK FINDINGS

### CH01 — Waking

**Overall assessment:** The cleanest chapter of those spot-checked. Voice is strong, tics are notably low in density.

**Patterns found:**

**TIC V4 (Nobody Tells You / Nobody Warns You):** Not present verbatim, but the chapter uses a structural equivalent — the narrator cataloging what his body understood before his mind did ("I think that was the first thing my body understood before my head did"). This is a mild reflex of the same pattern; one or two instances is well within tolerance.

**TIC V5 (Counting as Tension Device):** PRESENT. "The walking helped... I made myself a job: count steps, watch the ground, look for anything made by a person." This is exactly the banned counting-under-stress pattern. On first use in ch01 it is fully earned — but it sets the template. The PROSE_PATTERNS_TO_AVOID.md specifies one use per act maximum; this is the anchor use. Writing agents must be warned that this rung has been used in ch01 and must not reappear until at least Part III.

**TIC V10 (The "Way You / Way a Man" Crutch):** THREE instances in this chapter.
- "the flat dumb way you think before the fear arrives" (p. 2)
- "the way you look at anything" (p. 9, the Colosseum reveal)
- "I started measuring... you can estimate anything" (mild variation, p. 12)

The chapter-level limit per PROSE_PATTERNS_TO_AVOID.md is 3 uses. This chapter lands exactly at the limit. The Colosseum use ("I looked at it the way you look at anything") is the strongest of the three; the first instance ("the flat dumb way you think before the fear arrives") is generic and could be cut to provide headroom for later chapters.

**TIC V2 (Polysyndeton Exhaustion):** One notable instance — the paragraph beginning "But the mud huts. But the cloth. But the letters that weren't words..." uses polysyndeton through the "But" repetition rather than "and." This is within tolerance and is the panic moment that earns the device.

**TIC V13 (Frozen Epiphanies):** PRESENT once. "I stood on the path with the wind moving the dry grass around my boots and I looked at the unbroken Colosseum and the bottom went out of everything." The protagonist physically stops and looks at a thing for a profound realization. On first use, in a scene where the stasis IS the point — the shock of the Colosseum — this is earned. The V2_CHAPTER_CHANGE_NOTES.md does not flag it, and rightly so. But it does set the frozen-epiphany template at the book's first major beat.

**False Modesty Rhythm (TIC V3):** PRESENT, mild. "I'm okay at staying calm if I have something to do with my hands or my feet." This is genuine self-knowledge rather than false modesty followed by sophistication, so it doesn't fully activate the tic. But in context — immediately before a highly analytical geometry demonstration — it comes close. The passage is: he says he's good at math (self-aware), then does the geometry of the Colosseum at extreme speed while sitting in the dirt. One beat of false modesty before one beat of competence. Technically compliant, but the pattern is visible in embryo.

**Patterns NOT present in ch01:** No correctio. No future-vantage. No "I want to be honest/exact." No wisdom-button close. No "which was" trailing clause. No "because" train. No "let it sit." No "which is to say." No "shape" crutch. No "thing" abstraction.

**Verdict:** Low tic density. No blockers. One note worth flagging for continuity: ch01 uses the counting-under-stress device and frozen epiphany device in their first valid appearances — subsequent chapters must treat both as used.

### CH10 — The Workshop

**Overall assessment:** Strong chapter, minimal tics — the V2_CHAPTER_CHANGE_NOTES.md had already flagged and addressed the main offenders (planted-a-seed close, named-subtext "way you need a window" line). The chapter as it stands is largely clean.

**Patterns found:**

**TIC V10 (The "Way You / Way a Man" Crutch):** TWO instances.
- "He looked at me the way the broad men looked at me." (p. 3, the guard)
- "I looked at the well for a while like an idiot." (This is not technically a "way you" construction but is the false-modesty setup to a sophisticated observation.)

Under the 3-per-chapter limit, this is within tolerance.

**TIC V3 (False Modesty Rhythm):** One clear example. "I stood there in my own yard and looked at a fourteen-year-old slave who had taught herself in a week the thing that took the whole Roman accounting profession a generation, and I felt two things at exactly the same time..." The phrase "I stood there in my own yard and looked at" is a near-verbatim match for the example given in PROSE_PATTERNS_TO_AVOID.md under TIC V13 (Frozen Epiphanies): "I stood there in my own yard and looked at a fourteen-year-old slave..." This line is the exact example cited in the banned-patterns document. The V2 chapter reproduces it unchanged.

This is a significant find. The banned-patterns document (TIC V13) quotes this exact sentence as a named example of the frozen-epiphany pattern. The V2 chapter has not removed or revised it. The V2_CHAPTER_CHANGE_NOTES.md for ch10 does not flag this specific line — it flags the "planted a seed" close and the "way you need a window" named-subtext line (both of which appear to have been addressed), but not the "stood there in my own yard" frozen-epiphany line.

**TIC V7 ("Thing" Abstraction):** ONE instance. "The first was the thing you'd feel. Look at this. Look at what's in there." The phrase "the thing you'd feel" is an abstract "thing" standing in for a specific emotion. However, in context the vagueness is partly intentional — Daniel is deliberately not naming what he feels, and the abstraction signals that. This is borderline acceptable but should be monitored.

**Wisdom-button close:** The chapter ends on action, not aphorism: Daniel goes to the back room and starts cutting a second block. This is an ideal model image-cut. No wisdom button.

**Polysyndeton:** One notable run — the list of items Tyche had laid out on the tablet: "four women, different seam-lengths, different rates, an advance to one of them against next week, a bolt and a third of linen drawn against the steward's tablet." This is list-polysyndeton rather than emotional polysyndeton, and it is within tolerance.

**Correctio check:** "I said I preferred free hands for the quality. That was half of it." — This is close to but not quite a correctio. It reframes the stated reason as incomplete but does not use the banned "Not X, it was Y" construction. Compliant.

**Verdict:** Very low tic density. One specific unresolved issue: the frozen-epiphany line on Tyche's arithmetic ("I stood there in my own yard and looked at a fourteen-year-old slave...") is the exact example cited in the banned-patterns document and should be revised. The V2_CHAPTER_CHANGE_NOTES.md missed it.

### CH25 — Three Faces

**Overall assessment:** Highest tic density of the chapters spot-checked. Several patterns are active simultaneously. The chapter's structure is strong and the prose is often excellent, but this is the chapter that needs the most revision.

**Patterns found:**

**TIC V1 (Throat-clearing / Preamble):** ONE instance, borderline. "The other half I am putting down here honestly:" — This is the narrator announcing his own honesty before stating the second half of a motive. Compare directly to PROSE_PATTERNS_TO_AVOID.md: "I want to set down honestly..." The V2 version omits the "I want to" but keeps the announced-honesty scaffold. In context it is followed by a genuinely uncomfortable admission (paying free women while using Pamphilus on a collar), so there is a functional reason for the throat-clear. Still, this is an instance the writing team should review.

Wait — the above applies to ch10, not ch25. Re-checking ch25 specifically:

In ch25, the opening line contains a near-preamble: "I have held those two things together ever since, because I learned that winter that a man comes for you most gently in the season when you have most to lose." This imports a future-vantage frame ("I have held those two things together ever since") and assigns significance from outside the scene's timeline. This is a variant of the banned future-vantage pattern (Section 1.2 of PROSE_PATTERNS_TO_AVOID.md), flagged specifically: "I have never entirely stopped" is one of the listed variants. "I have held those two things together ever since" is the same construction.

The V2_REVISED_OUTLINE.md note for ch25 acknowledges this: "Opening wisdom-button ('a man comes for you most gently') rewritten to close-retrospect without the extractable aphorism." But the chapter has not been revised — the wisdom-button is still the chapter's opening line ("a man comes for you most gently in the season when you have most to lose"), and the future-vantage frame is still in place.

**TIC V12 ("Which Is to Say" Pivot):** PRESENT. "So I maneuvered, which is to say I lied a little with the truth." This is the exact example cited in PROSE_PATTERNS_TO_AVOID.md under TIC V12, reproduced verbatim. Maximum per PROSE_PATTERNS_TO_AVOID.md is 1 per 10 chapters. This is a notable find: the banned document quotes this exact sentence as a TIC V12 example, and it appears unchanged in the V2 chapter.

**TIC V10 (The "Way You / Way a Man" Crutch):** FOUR instances — over the 3-per-chapter limit.
- "soft in the way of men who had been hard once and let the hardness sink under good living" (Scaeva description)
- "the way Macer wore no stripe at all" (direct comparison construction)
- "the way you show a horse the bridle before you show it the bit" (Scaeva's flattery)
- "the way you'd say a dog was a fine dog" (Macer description near end)

The "way you'd say a dog was a fine dog" is the exact phrase given as a V1 violation in PROSE_PATTERNS_TO_AVOID.md (Section TIC V10). It appears unchanged in V2. The V2_REVISED_OUTLINE.md for ch25 notes "The way at 7 — reduce to 3" as a required fix. The chapter has not been revised to meet this limit.

**TIC V8 (The "Which Was" Trailing Clause):** ONE instance. "It came with eight bearers, which I learned later was the precise number, the senatorial number, the number that says I could afford ten and have chosen restraint." The construction "which I learned later was the precise number" is a "which was" trailing clause reframing what just happened. Within tolerance for one use, but should be flagged.

**TIC V13 (Frozen Epiphanies):** ONE instance. "I stood at my own gate and felt the specific coolness of a particular man having decided he needed me." The protagonist physically stands at his gate processing a profound realization. This is at the tolerable end of the pattern — the standing is not explicit stasis followed by a revelation, and the sentence doesn't announce itself as an epiphany. Borderline compliant.

**TIC V3 (False Modesty):** "I should have said nothing" immediately followed by a sophisticated geopolitical analysis of Scaeva's information-gathering. This is a mild instance — the self-criticism is genuine ("I should have said nothing" proves accurate by the end of the scene), but the pattern is visible.

**Wisdom-button close:** The chapter ends: "I stood in the warm lamplight of the house I had built on the high ground and listened, under the women's voices, for feet on the slope." This is an image-cut rather than an aphorism, which is correct. However, "the house I had built on the high ground" is a thematic self-reference that edges toward button territory by invoking the chapter's power-metaphor. Within tolerance.

**Verdict:** Two blockers that should have been caught in V2 revision and were not: (1) the "which is to say I lied a little with the truth" line, which is the named TIC V12 example from the banned-patterns document, and (2) the "the way you'd say a dog was a fine dog" line, which is the named TIC V10 example. Both lines are quoted verbatim in PROSE_PATTERNS_TO_AVOID.md as examples of the banned pattern. Additionally, the "the way" count is over limit (4 vs. 3 maximum). The opening future-vantage frame is a flagged BLOCKER in the outline notes but has not been addressed.

### CH40 — The Books

**Overall assessment:** Moderate tic density. The chapter's emotional core — honest gaps in Daniel's knowledge — is executed well, and several potential tics are earned by context. The main issues are a "thing" abstraction count and one wisdom-button close.

**Patterns found:**

**TIC V7 ("Thing" Abstraction):** THREE instances.
- "The order was the whole of it." (opening of second section — also a "the whole of" construction, TIC V6 territory)
- "the thing about the line that touches a curve at a point" (the calculus page)
- "the thing I had to put there" (the blank chemistry page)

None of these is egregious — the first two are used in technical description where "thing" is a deliberate placeholder for a concept Daniel is admitting he cannot name precisely. The third is the clearest candidate for replacement: "the one true thing I had to put there" could be "the one true sentence I had."

**TIC V6 ("The Shape Of" Crutch):** ONE clear instance. "I was handing down the shape of a tool and not the making of it." Given the 1-per-5-chapters maximum, this is within tolerance. But it is present.

**TIC V8 ("Which Was" Trailing Clause):** ONE instance. "The three would not sit in one room" — not a "which was" construction. Clean on this. However: "the brick in a wall built to stand a hundred years burying men" is a trailing metaphor that reframes the previous action. Not technically a "which was" clause but the same rhythm.

**Wisdom-button:** ONE near-miss. "A clean false rung is the worst thing a ladder can have." This is a portable aphorism — it could be pulled out and used as a tweet or epigraph. The PROSE_PATTERNS_TO_AVOID.md specifies max 1 per chapter, and the V2_CHAPTER_CHANGE_NOTES.md notes it as the keeper (L33: "keep the strongest one"). The chapter's final lines are also borderline: "the lower pages, the counting and the shapes, stood clean and sure, oak all the way up, because the bottom of a ladder is the part a man keeps longest." The "because" explanation at the end is a thematic button in the wisdom-register. The chapter ends on action (he went up to the house, where Marcia had kept a lamp), which saves it. But the paragraph before the close is gnomic.

**Polysyndeton:** The passage on the blank chemistry page — "the names of the things the world is built from, the small unsplittable pieces and how they joined, and I had nothing for it but a handful of words and the image of a picture, little balls hooking onto little balls, that I was three-quarters sure was wrong in some way I could no longer locate" — is a mild polysyndeton run, within the one-per-chapter limit.

**TIC V10 (The "Way You" Crutch):** TWO instances.
- "in a language no one within a thousand years would be able to sound out" (not a "way you" construction, compliant)
- "She read it the way she read a tally she suspected, finger under the line." (TIC V10 — specific and earned, within tolerance)
- "I wrote the doubt into the wood" — metaphor, not a "way you."

Count is under the limit. Clean.

**Correctio check:** No correctio constructions found.

**TIC V9 ("Because" Train):** ONE mild instance. "because it was one thing to know the books would have holes and another to watch a hole open under my own hand in the rung I had thought the soundest" — single "because" justification, within tolerance.

**Verdict:** Low-to-moderate tic density. No blockers. The main concern is the "the whole of it" opening to the second section ("The order was the whole of it"), which is a TIC V6 "the whole of" usage, and three "thing" abstraction instances. The chapter is otherwise well-executed.

### CH53 — The Far Whisper

**Overall assessment:** The cleanest chapter of those spot-checked. Third-person narration, non-Daniel POV, and short length (approximately 900 words) naturally reduce tic exposure. One structural concern.

**Patterns found:**

**TIC V10 ("The Way You / Way a Man" Crutch):** ONE instance, final sentence. "She stood at the rail among the others and looked west at the shore, the way you look at a thing you have only ever drawn." This is the closing "the way you" of the entire novel. Per PROSE_PATTERNS_TO_AVOID.md, Section 2.3: "The book even ends on it." The banned-patterns document calls out this exact pattern — the book's last sentence deploying the "way you" crutch. The V2_CHAPTER_CHANGE_NOTES.md for ch53 notes "PRESERVE" on the "looked west at the shore" line. This means the closing "way you" construction has been intentionally preserved, but it is in direct conflict with the prohibition.

This needs a decision from the writing team: is the final "the way you" a deliberate stylistic callback (closing the "way you" tic as a structural full-stop, acknowledging and retiring it) or an unexamined carryover? If it is intentional, the reasoning should be stated explicitly in the chapter notes. If it is a carryover, it should be revised.

**Patterns NOT present in ch53:** No correctio. No future-vantage. No throat-clearing. No polysyndeton. No "shape" or "thing" abstractions. No wisdom-button close (the chapter ends with the box knocking into silence — an image-cut of the highest order).

**Socratic Echo check:** Not applicable — no dialogue, no Daniel character.

**Verdict:** Near-zero tic density. One unresolved decision point: the closing "the way you" construction, which the banned-patterns document specifically names as the book's defining verbal tic appearing in the book's final line.

## SECTION 4: STRUCTURAL ISSUES

### Cycle of Defeat (TIC V16)

**Status: Partially broken but not fully.**

The outline breaks the Cycle of Defeat in two arcs as instructed:
- Spinning wheel: rejected on slave-labor economics, not technical failure. This is a genuine variation.
- Sportsbook and casino: succeed and stay working; no collapse arc.

However, the following arcs still follow the full Cycle of Defeat pattern in the outline descriptions:
- **Steam pump:** Works imperfectly, "crude and dangerous and barely efficient," iterates without resolution in Daniel's lifetime. The rubber gaskets from the Atlantic are a partial fix but the cycle still ends in "not solved yet."
- **Pendulum clock:** First attempt at escapement in ch23 ("runs, badly, ±20 min/day"), improving through Phase D, not solved until after Daniel dies (ch52, Procula). This is the longest Cycle of Defeat in the book and spans roughly 30 chapters.
- **Steel / crucible:** Reduced but not gone per master_outline.md: "the residual wall is carbon-atmosphere control in the crucible melt. Show it narrowing over decades, never fully solved in Daniel's lifetime." The "narrowing but not solved" arc still has the shape of the Cycle — it just ends in sustained depression rather than a pivot.
- **Printing press movable type:** Works eventually, but ch10 describes years of the press "staying ugly" and the chapter-close is Daniel returning to the block after a humiliating salon — a small Cycle of Defeat within a chapter.

The fix instruction in PROSE_PATTERNS_TO_AVOID.md specifies: "Break the formula in at least 2 threads." The outline has done this for gambling and spinning. The steam pump arc is still unbroken and is the most visible remaining Cycle of Defeat given how long it runs.

**Recommendation:** The steam pump arc should have at least one beat where it works cleanly and without depression — either a specific practical win that isn't undercut, or an acknowledgment in ch38 (where the mine pump is confirmed operational) that the arc is a success, not merely "running." The outline's current wording — "stands there and understands for the first time that the industrial loop is running without him" — is closer to melancholy than to the clean success the TIC V16 fix calls for.

### Socratic Echo (TIC V15)

**Status: Not adequately flagged; likely present in unreviewed chapters.**

The spot-checked chapters do not contain clear Socratic Echo instances — but this is partly because the most vulnerable scenes (Heras receiving the Volta pile demonstration in ch23, Hadrian's testing dialogue in ch35, Zoticus asking epistemological questions in ch47) are in chapters not included in the spot-check.

The outline descriptions for these scenes show structural risk:
- Ch23: "Heras is present. He is interested in the epistemology ('how do you know it is current and not spirit?'), not the technology." This is the correct instinct — Heras's skepticism about epistemology, not technology, differentiates him from a Socratic Echo. But the question itself is a clean perfect-metaphor summary of the epistemological problem, which is the Echo pattern.
- Ch47: Zoticus "asks the right epistemological questions: not 'does this work?' but 'how would we know if it does?'" This is explicitly described as "Heras's spiritual successor." A character introduced as the spiritual successor of the character most prone to Socratic Echo in V1 is a high-risk introduction. The outline does not flag this risk.
- Ch35: "Hadrian's testing dialogue — PRESERVE VERBATIM." Hadrian's testing dialogue in V1 was praised and is being preserved unchanged. This is correct IF the dialogue does not contain Socratic Echo. It cannot be assessed from the outline alone.

The V2_CHAPTER_CHANGE_NOTES.md has no TIC V15 warnings on any chapter. Given that PROSE_PATTERNS_TO_AVOID.md identifies it as a structural problem, at minimum ch23, ch35, and ch47 should be checked.

### Ledger-as-Catharsis / Blow-up Moment (TIC V17)

**Status: One required fix confirmed committed (ch48); execution not verifiable from spot-check.**

The outline explicitly commits the blow-up moment (ledger fails, Marcia's death, ch48) and the V2_REVISED_OUTLINE.md states this in capital-letter language ("COMMITTED TO THIS CHAPTER"). Ch48 was not in the spot-check set; the commitment is noted but cannot be confirmed from chapter text. This is worth verifying against the actual ch48 prose.

### Physical Decline Catalog (TIC V18)

**Status: Outline shows awareness but may over-flag rather than under-flag.**

The V2_REVISED_OUTLINE.md for ch46, ch47, and ch48 all include explicit instructions limiting the physical decline catalog to one reference per chapter. The outline shows awareness. Ch40 (spot-checked) does not contain any physical decline references, which is correct given ch40 is outside Part VI.
