# Chapter 35: Hadrian

The crack in the door opened the way Eudemus had promised it would, which is to say not for me at all.

Past midwinter the slave came to the wharf a fourth time with a place and an hour and, this once, a word. A man higher than Eudemus had asked where the new figures came from, and had not been satisfied with the widow's name, because the widow could not say how the marks behaved when a province's accounts ran to six scripts and as many lies. So the question had gone up another step, and another, until a hand somewhere closed on it and said, in effect, bring me the man who made them. Eudemus's word was four sentences and one of them mattered. *You will not say my name. You will not say the physician's. You came up through the rolls like everyone else.*

Marcia made the story with me by the lamp, going at it the way she went at a short load, looking for the seam that would split under weight. Not the balloon, dead weight now. Not the powder. Not the contest, which belonged to a goddess and a widow. The water if asked and not before. The figures were the whole of me, and I was to be a method walking on two legs and as little else as I could manage.

"He'll have had a freedman dig the chest out and read him the list. The flying barbarian, the burning tube, Caesar's wonder. You can't make that not true." She lined the edge of the tablet with her finger. "So you let him think he's caught you out, being more than figures, and you let him be pleased about it, and then you give him the figures anyway. A clever man's vanity is that he sees through you. Let him see through you to exactly the thing you want him looking at."

"And if it's the emperor."

I had not meant to say it. She did not pretend I had not.

"Then you will be twice as dull, and you will come home," she said, and put the stylus down. "You are bad at dull. Practice on Lucanus. He'll keep you humble."

---

A slave came to collect me at the eighth hour: not to the Palatine but to a great old senatorial pile on the slope below it, the kind of place the new reign used for business too unfinished for the palace. I was made to wait in a colonnade off a garden where the cold still came up through the stone but a slave brought watered wine, and I counted the columns out of the old habit and got the same number twice and was almost steadied by it. Others waited. A pair of architects with rolls, arguing in undertones about a revetment. A Greek I took for a rhetor by his hands. We did not speak. The man beside you in such a room is a competitor for the same finite attention, and we took each other's measure in silence and gave nothing.

Then a chamberlain named me and I went in alone, and the protocol caught me at once, because I had braced for the soldier's plainness of fifteen years before and walked instead into a working library. Scrolls in racks to the ceiling, a model of something in cork and wax at one end, half a dozen men standing about it the way men stand about a model they are being made to admire. And no obvious center to it, no thick-necked man drying his hands, so that I did the wrong thing and looked for the emperor among the standing men and did not find him, and a voice came from the table where a man sat bent over a drawing with a reed, not looking up.

"You bow to the room," the voice said, in Greek, amused, "when you do not know which of us is worth bowing to. It is a sound policy and it tells me you are not a courtier. Sit. No, there, where the light is on you. I like to see a man I am talking to."

I sat where the light was on me, which put him in shadow against the window, which I understood was the point of it.

---

He let me sit a while before he looked up, finishing whatever line he was drawing, and when he did the beard was the first thing, full and curled and oiled in the Greek manner on a Roman face, which tells you before he speaks that he has decided who he is and means you to find it remarkable. He was past forty, heavy through the shoulder, restless even seated, his hands wanting always the next thing while the first still went. There was a draughtsman's roll at his elbow under the scroll he had pretended to consult, and I registered all of it and connected none of it, busy with the small drowning arithmetic of a man who has just understood whose room he is in.

"You are the Thulean," he said. In Greek still, and quick, then, before I could answer, in Latin, watching which language I caught myself in. "The one who flew. I have read the report. A bag of hot air, very ingenious, and a man burned to death under it on the Danube, which the report puts at the end, where a man hopes it will not be read." He set the reed down. "Did you fly yourself?"

"Once tethered, Caesar, years ago, and never since. I put two men up and burned them both. I do not put men up anymore."

"So the report says, and admires you for it. I do not, particularly. A man who builds a thing that kills men and then refuses to ride it himself has decided his own skin is worth more than his curiosity. Alexander rode what he built." He let that sit, watching. "But you are not here about the bag. The bag is a dead man's enthusiasm. You are here because someone in my treasury cannot stop talking about your marks, and I want to know whether I am being sold a conjuror's trick by a barbarian who flew once and has dined out on it ever since, or whether there is a real thing under the smoke. Persuade me there is. You have until I am bored."

A dozen breaths in the room and he had laid out three traps, the flying, the dead man, the conjuror, and stood over them to watch which I stepped in. I left them where they lay.

"I will give you a sum, Caesar," I said, "and you will do it twice, once the way your clerks do and once the way my marks do, and see the difference with your own hand and take none of it on my word. The marks are not clever. That is their worth. A clever thing can hide a lie. These can't."

His eyes sharpened, and I understood I had said something he liked and feared at once. He liked being handed a tool and told to use it himself. He feared, very slightly, being told a thing he had not already thought, by a man beneath him, before witnesses.

"Give me the sum," he said.

---

I gave him a real one, a province's grain levy built the night before out of the shape of true accounts, with a remainder and a running balance and two places where a clerk's thumb could quietly add a stroke. I laid it the long Roman way first, the wall, then in the new figures, the stair, and put the reed in his hand and let him climb the stair himself.

He was fast, faster than I had let myself expect. He took the figures the way Tyche had taken them, by the throat, ran the column, caught the place where the Roman version had let a stroke be added, and his head came up.

"There," he said. "In yours the hole shows. A man cannot add the grain back without the total betraying him. In the old marks he hides it in the crowd of strokes, and I am robbed and cannot prove it." He looked at me with the full force of his attention, which was like standing too close to a forge. "I worked this out for myself in the time it took to say it. You did not have to lead me."

"No, Caesar."

"Do not flatter me by agreeing too quickly. I dislike it." But he was pleased, and let me see it, and turned the tablet to one of the standing men. "Look. The barbarian's marks. You have all been making me forgive debts I could not prove because your fathers' fathers counted on their fingers and called it the dignity of Rome." He turned back. "Who taught them to you."

"No one, Caesar. They are from my country."

"Your country." He smiled into the beard, delighted and merciless. "Thule. The land at the back of the north wind, which Pytheas saw and Strabo called him a liar for, and which you alone among living men managed to leave and reach Rome and forget the road home to. You see I have read more than the report. I read Strabo as a boy. There is no such country, or if there is, it does not write in marks an Egyptian abacus-monger could not have given you in an afternoon." He said it lightly, swinging the reed, Crispus's whole charge said better and from a height Crispus could only dream of, a noose laid on the table for me to put my own head in by arguing.

I did not argue. "Caesar reads more than I ever will," I said. "I cannot win an argument about Thule against a man who has read Strabo, and I have stopped trying. What I can do is hand you an account no man in your service can cook, and where it came from is the least useful question you could ask about it, and the only one your enemies will want you to spend an afternoon on."

He went still, then laughed, once, a bark. "You are telling Caesar how to spend his attention. You are telling me my own suspicion is a waste of my afternoon." The reed lowered. "It is, of course. That is what makes it insolent. Go on. The press. I am told you can print a thing many times."

---

We were a long while at it and I knew the whole time that I was being hunted and was enjoying it, which was the worst sign of all.

He moved. He did not sit through a conversation the way Trajan had sat on the edge of the working table and waited to be impressed or wasted. He prowled the cork-and-wax model while I talked, and circled back, and set little tests in my path the way you set a snare in a run you have watched an animal use. He asked how the press inked, and when I told him truly that we wasted one sheet in four he pressed on the waste like a man pressing a bruise, because he had hoped I would gild it and I had not. He asked whether a printed law was more dangerous than a copied one, a single error pressed a thousand times, and I told him yes, the press multiplied the corrector's mistakes along with his truths, which was why the press should first carry figures, where an error shows itself, and not opinions, where it does not. He liked that so much he gave it back to me as his own a quarter-hour later, and I let him, and saw him watch me let him.

"You give ground gracefully," he said, not as praise. "Three times now. You have a true thing and you soften it the instant I lean on it. Why."

The reed tapped the table. The standing men had gone quiet. I felt the floor shift the way it had when Trajan's affable weather went out of him and left the cold sum, except this was a man who wanted to know whether I would lie about why I deferred to him, in front of witnesses, having already half-guessed.

"Because you are the emperor, Caesar," I said, "and I am a freedman who flew a bag once, and a true thing said too hard by a man like me to a man like you is a barbarian forgetting his place, and you would be right to remember it about me later. The thing is true whether I say it loud or soft. So I say it soft, and it stays true, and I stay useful, and we both keep what we want."

Something passed over the bearded face, amusement and a colder thing under it, the appraisal of a man deciding where to file a tool that has just told him how it means to be handled. "You are honest about your dishonesty," he said. "It is a rare vice. I will allow it for now."

---

The recognition came on a thing he said about a roof.

One of the standing men had unrolled a drawing on the far table, and the talk among them drifted, as it does when the emperor's attention has wandered off the petitioner, to a temple the reign meant to raise from the burned bones of an old one. The emperor crossed to it mid-sentence and left me in the light, and bent over the drawing, and took the reed up again, and began to sketch over the man's careful lines with two fingers in the air and then with the wet reed on the margin, talking fast.

"Not a flat coffered ceiling, every temple in Rome wears the same tired flat hat. A single span. One vault closing over the whole round of it like the half of an egg, heavy at the foot where it pushes out and light at the crown where it has only itself to carry, and lighten it further, here, sink the panels in courses so the stone above is no more than it needs, segments, do you see, like the rind of a fruit drawn in over the top." The two fingers came together and apart, scooping the curve out of the air, and on the margin the reed laid down a dome in stepped segments, one course swallowing into the next, lines no flat Roman ceiling had ever made.

I had seen those two fingers sketch a cornice in the air before, in a white shadow at the foot of a column, in a crowd where no one else carried work.

It went through me from the spine out, the way cold water does, all at once and in a place you cannot warm. The beard, Greek on a Roman face among clean-shaven men. The draughtsman's roll. *Domes like cut melons, one swallowing another.* A temple with the roof in segments like a squash, shown to Caesar, and the man very proud of it. *Go and paint his gourds.* I had stood twenty feet from him eight years before, at the dedication of a Forum, and watched Apollodorus spit gently into a well, and felt the cold of it then and read it half right. I had read that the proud man had the more to lose. I had not read that the well was a throne.

He was still drawing his gourds, emperor of the world bent over a margin sketching the roof a master had told him to go away and paint, while somewhere in the city the master inspected drains and said true things loud enough to be heard, certain that this was enough.

The emperor straightened and caught me looking. He could not have known what I had understood. But he saw that I had understood something, that my face had done a thing while his back was turned, and a man who tests everyone he meets does not let that go.

"You have an opinion about my roof," he said. It was not a question, and it was a trap finer than any he had laid, because I had just learned the most dangerous fact about him and had it written all over me, and any opinion at all about his roof, praise or doubt, was a step toward a place I now knew better than to stand. He builds. He prides himself on it. He had been told to go and paint his gourds by the one man whose work outweighed his, and had become the one man able to settle that account, and kept a ledger, and was drawing the gourds anyway, in front of witnesses, daring the room to remember who had mocked them.

"I build things that float and burst, Caesar," I said. "I have no opinion about what holds a roof up. I know enough to know that I do not, which about roofs is the most I have ever known."

He let it stand, and I saw in the letting that he had wanted to be deferred to about the roof, that the deference was the whole point of having drawn it in front of me, and that a man who agreed too readily that he knew nothing about roofs was nearly as suspect as one who claimed to know more. I had to give him my smallness and make him feel he had won it off me. "Though I would pay to see the half-egg stand," I added into the silence. "I have failed at smaller spans and gone home cursing."

That landed. The failure landed. He liked a man who had failed and would name it, the way Trajan had, except where Trajan had liked the honesty Hadrian liked the smallness it admitted beside his success. I steered by that the rest of the hour, and hated that I could.

---

He came back to the figures at the end, because under all the beard and the Greek and the gourds he was a man taking an inventory, and the inventory was what he could not get from his clerks and could get from me.

"I will have a province's accounts done in your marks," he said. "Both ways, side by side, and if the marks find me a thief I will know they are real, and if they find me nothing I will know my province is honest, which I will not believe, so they had better find me a thief." He was prowling again, near done with me. "You will have wax, clerks, the use of your press if it comes to that. You will not be paid in coin, which I am told you do not need, being rich, which is itself a thing I find suspect in a freedman and will think about another day." He stopped. "What do you want. Every man who comes up the rolls wants a thing. You have asked for nothing, which is either the cleverest ask there is or you are saving it. Which."

The map was in my mouth.

The room was right, the man bent toward knowing, toward the inventory, toward being the one who knew the true size of a something too large to hold in a single head. Tyche's words were in my chest, knowing not taking, prestige not province, the idle legions at a yard not a wall, and I could feel the outline of the door, the words worked out over a winter of ruined wax for a room that had not existed and now did, and he had opened his hand and asked me what I wanted, and the true shape of the world lay a sentence away from the one living man with the appetite and the power to go and find it.

And I held it. I held it because of a roof. I had just watched him draw his gourds in the teeth of the man who mocked them, and I knew in the body, before the mind had words for it, that this was a man who would take a glittering idea and make it his within the hour, the way he had made my sentence about the press his own. Hand him the edge of the world, fresh from a barbarian who flew a bag once, and he would either seize it whole as his own thought and leave me a tool that had served its turn, or remember in some later mood that the barbarian had known the shape of the world before the emperor did, and file that with a small mark beside it, for the day he could settle it. I had watched, eight years gone, what such a ledger does to the man it is kept against, and that man was inspecting drains across the city, sure that being right was a shelter.

"I want to be useful and forgotten, Caesar," I said. "I have had a year of being remembered and it nearly killed me. Give me a province's accounts to break and a clerk to break them with and a name on nothing, and let great men have the marks and let me be the dull foreigner who ruined wax in a back room. That is the whole of it. I have had the other kind of life. It is loud and it ends badly."

I could not read whether he believed it, or believed I believed it, or had simply decided to be charmed by a man asking to be left alone in a room full of men asking to be advanced.

"A man who asks to be forgotten is asking to be remembered for the asking," he said. "I will remember you for it. You see how it works." He took the reed up again, done with me, half turned back to the gourds. "Go and break me a province. Eudemus will see to your wax." He had the name out before he caught it, and his eye flicked to me to see if I marked that he knew exactly which clerk had been talking of my marks, that there were no secrets in his house and he wished me to know there were none. I let my face do nothing. "Eudemus, or whoever keeps that side of the rolls. They are interchangeable to me. That is the point of them."

---

They took me down the slope as the light went, into the noise of the city coming up to meet me as it had come up the Palatine fifteen years before. I had what I had gone up for. A province to break in the new figures, the use of the press, the attention of a man who could decide things, the crack swung wide enough now to walk through. By every measure Marcia kept I had won the winter's whole campaign in an afternoon, and I went down cold to the bone and not from the wind.

A patron tries to catch you out so he can trust you. A predator tries to catch you out so he can price you. They do the same things with their hands and their questions, and Hadrian had done all of them, and at the end I knew the cast of his appetite and the place of his ledger and nothing at all about which of the two he meant to be to me, and I suspected he did not know either, and would decide it some morning years off in a mood I could not read from a back room full of wax.

And under that, the colder reckoning. Somewhere in the city Apollodorus was inspecting his drains, white-haired and certain, saying true things at full volume because true things at full volume had built him everything he could see. He had told a young Spaniard with a draughtsman's roll to go and paint his squashes and leave temples to men who knew what held a roof up, and the young Spaniard had inclined his head a precise small distance and become the master of the world. Apollodorus did not know it, or knew it and did not believe it could matter, which in him came to the same result. I could not warn him. A man who has been right for sixty years does not take warning from the barbarian with the bag, and to be heard near him saying the emperor's name and the word danger in one breath was to put my own thin walls under the one hand I now most needed to think me dull.

At my own gate the lamp was lit and the day's loads stood waiting to be tallied, and Marcia looked at me across the wax and did not ask the loud question, whether I had pleased him, whether we were bought or saved. She asked the quiet one.

"Did you keep the map in the box."

"I kept it in the box," I said.

She made a mark and slid the loads across to me without looking up. I took the tablet and the reed and sat. Outside, a cart went past slowly, its wheel grinding something loose in the road, and after it the street went quiet, the way streets go quiet in the hour before dark when the day has spent itself and the lamps are not yet worth the oil.
