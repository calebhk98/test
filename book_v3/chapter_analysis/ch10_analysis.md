## 3. Scene-Ending Audit

The chapter has four marked scene breaks (`---`), creating five sections: the yard's arrival, Pamphilus/collar, the workshop/cloth/women, the numbers spread (Tyche and Onesimus), the blocks and the salon.

- Section 1 ending (yard): "My own well. I stood and looked at the well for a while like an idiot. After a year of begging coals and water off a kitchen woman who threw spoons at me, a well I was allowed to draw from felt like being handed a country." — Mild WISDOM BUTTON ("felt like being handed a country" is metaphorical summary of a feeling). The prior physical image (looking at the well "like an idiot") is the stronger stopping point. However, the metaphor is grounded and not portable as a standalone aphorism — it requires context to mean anything. Borderline acceptable.
- Section 2 ending (Pamphilus/collar): "It was not a kindness anyone would write down. It was the only lever I had and I pulled it, and Pamphilus sat on the bench and looked at his hands like a man waiting for the joke." — The opening "It was not a kindness anyone would write down" is a CORRECTIO form ("not X, [but] Y"). Must fix. End instead on the action: Pamphilus sitting on the bench looking at his hands.
- Section 3 ending (the piecework women): "for the length of that transaction the world ran the way a world should, and I needed that the way you need a window in a small room." followed by "It bent the wrong way. It bends the wrong way still. That is the whole of it and I am not going to dress it up." — The first sentence is flagged by change notes as naming the subtext. The second sentence ("It bends the wrong way still" / "That is the whole of it") contains a "whole of" tic and a second-level explanation. Both should be cut per change notes. The section should end on the piecework image or the arithmetic bend, not the named lesson.
- Section 4 ending (Tyche's numbers / Onesimus): "I took the tablet back and said, 'Good. Do the next one alone,' and I kept my face shut, and that was the first lie I told to protect her instead of myself, and not the last." — WISDOM BUTTON / future-vantage hybrid. "And not the last" is a mild future-frame declaration. More significantly, "that was the first lie I told to protect her instead of myself" is a narrator restating the scene's meaning explicitly. The prior image (the face kept shut, the tablet taken back) is the stronger cut.
- Section 5 / Chapter ending (the salon and its aftermath): "Tyche was waiting at the gate when they walked me back down to the wharf in the dark, awake, the lamp lit, the cloth tally squared away in the new figures in a clean column on the table. She looked at my face. / 'Bad?' she said. / I didn't answer her. I went to the back room and got the ugly gray line of Homer off the pigeonhole where I kept it, the crooked one, the doubled letter, the starved right end, and I sat down at the table under the lamp and started cutting a second block." — STRONG ACTION CUT (excellent; ends at the start of the next action, not its completion; "I folded the letter and went to find him" register; no wisdom-button; the resumed work in silence is the emotional meaning without stating it).

**Note on "I planted a seed in a field of men..."** — This paragraph appears in the Onesimus section (Section 4), not at the chapter's very end. It is the close of the numbers-spread sub-section. The change notes flag it as a "summary-of-meaning close" for the chapter, but in context it closes the Onesimus episode, not the chapter. The chapter actually ends on the Tyche-at-the-gate / second-block image. The "I planted a seed" passage is still a wisdom-button within a section and should be cut or replaced with an action, but it is not a chapter-ending button.

## 5. Voice & Character Issues

**Pamphilus — first appearance:** Pamphilus first appears in this chapter. His introduction is narrated ("a man named Pamphilus... the heavy stooped shoulders of someone who had carried things for a living since he could walk, and an iron collar"). He has **zero direct spoken dialogue** in this chapter. The change notes require at minimum 4 direct spoken lines across the book; this is his first appearance and he says nothing. The style guide's primary example is "Pamphilus did not say anything" — and while that exact phrase doesn't appear here, the effect is the same: Pamphilus is described, sits on a bench, "looked at his hands like a man waiting for the joke." He needs at least one direct line in this chapter, even a brief one in the inarticulate register.

**Suggested opportunity:** When Daniel tells him to sit and says "You eat when I eat," Pamphilus could respond with a 2-4 word line in his register: "Sit here?" pointing at the bench; or a grunt; or a misunderstanding about which bench. The silence here is not the language-barrier silence of ch02 — Pamphilus speaks Latin and has no excuse for muteness other than V1's class-muting habit.

**Tyche:** Voice is correct — city-quick, flat, no slowing for the foreigner. Her collar speech is in register. Her "Bad?" at the chapter's end is perfectly calibrated TIER 6.

**Onesimus:** The description of his resistance is correct: "He'd been doing it for forty years. His thumb was faster than my pen." But Onesimus speaks zero direct dialogue in this chapter — his resistance is entirely narrated. The change notes require Onesimus to be the confidently-wrong figure with a scene where he argues FOR the abacus and gets the REASONING wrong. Currently the chapter narrates his conservatism without quoting him. At minimum, one direct line from Onesimus arguing the wrong point would establish his voice. The thin nervous boy speaks (implicitly, through Daniel watching him trace the figures), but Onesimus himself is muted throughout this chapter.

**Crispus:** Well-introduced. His dialogue is correctly calibrated to TIER 1-adjacent educated-elite (Strabo citation, the "Pytheas of Massalia" argument, the two-finger spin on "Thule"). The performance is lethal and controlled.

**Crowd at the salon:** Not a demonstration-crowd scene; this is a literary salon. No crowd-quoting requirement applies.

## 7. Tech / Plot Issues
- Tyche's arithmetic aptitude: "taught herself in a week the thing that took the whole Roman accounting profession a generation." The risk in showing this is making Tyche too prodigious. However, the setup (Daniel showed her once, she retained it, then applied it correctly including long division) is consistent with a sharp mind that was waiting for a tool that fit it. Preservable.
- The Macer-guards-her-value dilemma is correctly set up: Daniel can see that teaching Tyche increases her monetary value to Macer, which could get her sold. The lie ("Good. Do the next one alone") is the first enactment of this dilemma. Load-bearing.
- The printing press attempt: the ugly gray line of Homer, the doubled letter, "I have it still" — the honesty about it being genuinely ugly and the first iteration failing cleanly is correct V2 execution. "Now make it not ugly, and you will have done something, and not before" (Heras) — correct TIER 2 voice.
- Onesimus: the confident conservatism is established but not dramatized as a scene where he argues the wrong point (only the right one — speed). The change notes require a scene where Onesimus is WRONG in his reasoning, not just conservative. Currently he's wrong in his preference but the narration gives him the speed-point concession ("He'd been doing it for forty years. His thumb was faster than my pen. That was the first thing that happened"). The young thin clerk's insight (the audit trail) is the payoff, but Onesimus's exit is narrated, not shown. Needs one quoted line where Onesimus states his wrong reason.
