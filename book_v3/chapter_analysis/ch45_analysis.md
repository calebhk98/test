## 3. Scene-Ending Audit

- **Scene 1 ending** (accounts from Judaea / slave-price column): "I did not have her read the other column a second time, and that is a thing I let myself do and will not dress up as anything better than what it was." → OBLIQUE SELF-JUDGMENT CUT. Not a gnomic aphorism — it refuses to explain what "what it was" means. The narrator offers a moral verdict on his own evasion without packaging it as a life-lesson. This is a strong close that does not resolve into comfortable wisdom. GOOD, keep.
- **Scene 2 ending** (discharged auxiliary / Aelia Capitolina): "I did not draw this one. But I had drawn the kind of it, and taught the men who drew it, and a colony laid out clean over the place where a city had been razed and its people driven off or dead is a thing my survey serves as well as it serves anything, because a survey does not know what was on the ground before the chain was run across it." → WISDOM BUTTON (borderline strong). The final clause — "a survey does not know what was on the ground before the chain was run across it" — is extractable as a standalone epigram. It restates the scene's moral plainly. Compare: the action just before (Daniel acknowledging his grid, acknowledging he taught the surveyors) is stronger without the concluding clause. This is the chapter's most likely wisdom-button violation.
- **Scene 3 ending** (argument on the cold bench): "I asked the empty bench whether a man who carries water into a camp so the men do not die of the flux is answerable for what the men he kept alive then do with their hands. Heras would have made me say what I meant by answerable. He would have asked me whether I was looking for the truth of it or for permission, and I would not have been able to lie to his face the way I can lie to a bench, and that was the use of him, and it was gone." → CLOSE on loss-of-Heras. The final clause ("that was the use of him, and it was gone") is tightly compressed and not quite an aphorism — it is a specific loss-statement. Acceptable; not a wisdom button in the deployable sense.
- **Scene 4 ending** (powder uncertainty): "A thing you are sure of has edges. You can set your hand on it. This had no edges. It was everywhere the powder might be and nowhere I could point." → BORDERLINE WISDOM BUTTON. "A thing you are sure of has edges" is extractable as a standalone aphorism. However, the three-sentence close grounds it in physical metaphor ("set your hand on it," "no edges," "nowhere I could point") and the abstraction is immediately re-concretized. Weaker than pure aphorism but still a wisdom-button move.
- **Scene 5 ending** (Daniel's name as a tool): "...a man does not get to lay down a name once it has started doing work without him, any more than he gets to call back a figure once it is cut in the public stone." → WISDOM BUTTON. Portable aphorism; the comparison (name = figure cut in stone) is pithy and extractable.
- **Chapter ending** (Marcia / grain prices): "...until the oil was low and she banked the lamp and we went in." → IMAGE / ACTION CUT (strong, keep). Excellent close — the smallest possible physical action after an enormous emotional scene. Exactly what the style guide recommends (ch42 model, "silence cut").

**Wisdom-button count for this chapter:** 3 (Scene 2 close, Scene 4 close, Scene 5 close). Limit is 1. This is a significant over-count. The chapter ending itself is exemplary, but three scene-internal closes are wisdom buttons.

## 5. Voice & Character Issues

- **Pamphilus**: Present in the chapter. He delivers a spoken report of the auxiliary's account: "Pamphilus stood in my tally-room and gave it to me in the man's own words, and then said, because Pamphilus is a kind man and had watched my face, that it was a long way off and none of my doing, and went back to the cloth." Pamphilus's speech here is entirely paraphrased — we hear the auxiliary's account in summary but Pamphilus's own words are narrated, not quoted. This is a muting issue. Per the CHARACTER_VOICE_GUIDE.md, Pamphilus requires direct speech in his own inarticulate register. His one substantive contribution to this scene (his kindness in telling Daniel it's far away and not his doing) is reported rather than quoted. Fix: give Pamphilus one direct line — rough, fragmented, in his register — rather than narrating his words.
- **Marcia** (chapter close): Speaks one line: "the price of eastern grain would come down hard once the province was settled and the levies eased, and that we should not be long on grain contracts this season." This is paraphrased indirect speech, not a direct quote. This is the chapter's emotional centerpiece — the scene where they sit in shared silence and talk grain instead of naming the other column. Giving Marcia's line as indirect speech softens the obliqueness. Her voice should be heard directly. A direct quote in Marcia's register ("Not long on grain this season. The province settles, the price drops.") would be stronger and consistent with her characterization.
