## 3. Scene-Ending Audit

- **Scene 1 ending** (pearwood / press not needing him): "That was the point of all of it. I had only never watched the point come true on the day it was for." → WISDOM BUTTON. The first sentence ("That was the point of all of it") is a thesis declaration. The second sentence compounds it. This is an aphoristic close that restates the scene's meaning. The concrete action just before it (Daniel working out the board cost with Vitalis, the press going) is stronger. Cut or move these two sentences; end on the press going without him.
- **Scene 2 ending** (Hadrian's dying): "Even his dying he made into a thing other men would copy." → WISDOM BUTTON. Portable aphorism; exactly the kind of sentence that could be extracted as an epigram. The scene's content — the epigram about the little wandering soul, the physician killing himself — is vivid; ending on Daniel's observation about Hadrian making even death copyable is an interpretive button. End instead on the wine-seller, or the two translations before the body was cold.
- **Scene 3 ending** (fear's not-coming): "There was nothing in it for me to be afraid of, and I went looking, the way you press a bruise, for the fear, and it was not there." → IMAGE-ANALOGY CUT. "The way you press a bruise" is a physical analogy, not an aphorism. The ending is concrete and active (Daniel searching for the fear and not finding it). Good.
- **Scene 4 ending** (two decades of carefulness ending): "…the count of people in this world who could see me whole, friend or enemy, had gone from one to none, and the relief and the grief were the same size and the same shape and I could not get a knife between them." → WISDOM BUTTON. "I could not get a knife between them" is a variant of the "two feelings, same size, you couldn't get a knife between them" construction flagged in PROSE_PATTERNS_TO_AVOID.md Section 3 as a specific tic to watch. The document notes: "The doubled-feeling construction is genuinely distinctive; the verbatim phrase is a crutch. Use the construction freely; avoid the specific phrasing after its first appearance." This appears to be using the verbatim phrasing. Additionally, the close is a gnomic summary of the scene's emotional content. Needs revision.
- **Scene 5 ending** (ocean program ending): "So I let it go. Not all at once and not cleanly, but I let it go the way you let go of most large things, by going home to a smaller one." → BORDERLINE WISDOM BUTTON. "The way you let go of most large things, by going home to a smaller one" is generalized wisdom. The final clause abstracts away from the specific (the ocean, Hadrian's death) to a portable observation about how humans release large things. Trim the second clause; end on "I let it go."
- **Chapter ending** (Ulpia / survey close): "…she did not nail the board this time. She set the box back into the floor and laid the board loose over it…weighted the curling corner with a stone loom-weight out of her mother's basket so the draft off the wharf could not take it in the night, and left it lying there in the dark, true to the last headland, a thing not closed, only laid down." → IMAGE CUT (excellent, keep). One of the book's strongest chapter endings — the open survey column held down against the draft, "not closed, only laid down." Do not touch.
