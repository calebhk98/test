## Section 2: RESOLUTIONS — Earlier Seeds (ch01–36) Closing in ch37–53

### Major resolutions

**Apollodorus's fall (planted ch12/ch26; closes ch37)**
Closes fully in ch37. Daniel warns him face-to-face; Apollodorus refuses to bend ("go fold something"); Daniel makes a hedged, cowardly half-try through Theophanes and pulls back. Apollodorus is exiled then dies in exile. Daniel hangs the plumb-line Apollodorus gave him in the press-room and has Tyche record Apollodorus's maxim in the cipher. The grief is that he was Daniel's only engineering peer; the guilt is that Daniel saved himself by not pushing harder. CLOSED ch37.

**Encyclopedia / ladder completion (planted ch17 will-seed, ch23 "the books," ch38 commit; closes ch47–49)**
The encyclopedia is dictated to completion in ch47 (upper rungs, eyes shut, by the homeland tongue). The burial/copying plan with four copies is fully executed and confirmed in ch49. CLOSED ch49.

**English cipher's keepers — who holds it after Daniel? (planted ch20/ch40/ch41; closes ch49/ch52)**
Keeper-chain: Tyche (master), Ulpia Severa, Vitalis, Chloe. Written into the collegium deed in ch49 (teach at least two, no single keeper allowed). In ch52 the chain runs through "old Vibia" to Procula, who is not Daniel's blood and comes from the workshop/storeroom line (Chloe's descent). CLOSED structurally ch49; demonstrated ch52.

**Dead phone relic's final disposition (planted ch04, decision deferred to ch49; closes ch49/ch51)**
BURIED WITH DANIEL. Decided in a Daniel-and-Tyche floorboard scene in ch49. The citizenship tablet buried with it. Macer's ring is NOT buried — its gold value goes to Felix's craft-failure fund (decided ch49). In ch51, Rufus calls the phone "a tablet of black glass, a savage's charm" and laughs at it; it stays in the ground. In ch52, Procula never digs it up. In ch53, unmentioned. CLOSED ch49.

**Felix / epilogue climber (planted ch19 as a contest winner; closes ch39/ch52)**
Felix (counting-house slave who won with the steam-ship tale) is funded by the patronage endowment in ch39 as a free bronze-caster (~40) chasing the steam-pot. He is confirmed dead by ch52 ("Felix, Daniel, and 'old Vibia' all long dead"). His logged failures become the foundation Procula reads. The ch19 plant pays off not by Felix solving the problem himself, but by his failure-record providing rungs for those above him. The actual epilogue climber is PROCULA (not Felix), reached via Chloe's keeper-line, not Felix's direct workshop line—the two lines (books-line and craft-line) fuse in the patronage fund. CLOSED ch39 (Felix's role defined), ch52 (Procula climbs the rung).

**Ocean-map lie / "uninhabited west" (planted ch03/ch36; closes ch46/ch53)**
The lie has a double closing. In ch46 the ocean program lapses when Hadrian dies and Antoninus Pius shows no appetite for the sea; Daniel lets go of the "road west" hope. The lie itself detonates in ch53 when the pilot reaches the shore and finds it INHABITED — "a tended fire, long dark boats, figures at the waterline, an upraised arm, three more smokes up the hills." The pilot "did not write what it meant." The "uninhabited" claim is punctured but without catastrophe—it is a beginning, not a conquest. CLOSED ch53 (the detonation is quiet and architectural, not announced).

**Daughter Ulpia / daughter-question (planted ch38/ch41; closes ch48/ch49)**
The daughter-question (will teaching her be a cruelty given the law bars her public use of it?) is left honestly unresolved in ch41—she decides herself she wants the knowing regardless of the name on the door. In ch48 Marcia's dying instruction cancels the Ennius marriage pressure; Ulpia free to marry or not. In ch49 Ulpia holds the survey, the deed, the books; she is de facto running the enterprise. In ch50 she reads to Daniel from the upper rungs until near his death. Her arc closes on a functional victory within the law's constraints—she becomes the knowing behind every borrowed name. CLOSED ch49/ch50.

**Tyche outliving Daniel (planted ch09/ch20; closes ch50/ch51)**
Tyche is ~70 at Daniel's death (ch50); she holds his hand and recites figures as he dies. In ch51 Rufus notes "an aged freedwoman still keeps the dead man's school." She is alive and teaching the cipher to a slave-girl who teaches two more. CLOSED ch50/ch51.

**Macer's death (planted as impending from ch47; closes ch48)**
Macer dies at ~96 in his son's house, in his chair, ring on, blunt to the last. His will leaves Daniel the heavy gold ring with clause "to the Thulean, who will know what it cost." The ring cannot fit Daniel's swollen knuckles; it goes into the floorboard box beside the dead phone (ch48), then its gold value is redirected to Felix's fund (ch49). The tangled bond—cage and shield, patron and enslaver—closes without clean grief. CLOSED ch48.

**Heras's death (planted from ch41 aging notes; closes ch42)**
Heras dies at ~80, before dawn on the third morning of Daniel's visit, with no last word to Daniel (Zoticus reports it). The chapter renders the loss through the empty bench and Daniel drinking the filtered water with no one to trade the old joke with. His last spoken words (to Zoticus) are a dry joke about gods and physics. His medical work lives on, re-attributed to Asclepius by the Esquiline surgeon; the boiling and washing survive him. CLOSED ch42.

**"Uninhabited west" lie / ocean gambit (see above, dual closing)**
See "ocean-map lie" entry above. CLOSED ch46 (program lapses) and ch53 (detonation).

**Crispus and Scaeva's ultimate fates**
Crispus: last seen as a defeated intellectual antagonist after ch27; by ch51 his phrase "the barbarian's heaven by ladder" is "received cultured opinion" and he is implicitly long dead. No death scene on the page; he fades. CLOSED by omission/ch51.
Scaeva: withdrawn from Daniel's life in ch33 (returned a tablet with seal unbroken); confirmed alive but cold. Never resolved further—he knows the sulfur-supply secret but does nothing with it in the text. His faction question ("doesn't know whose man he is") simply lapses. See NEVER-CLOSES flag.

**Hubris-or-duty question (planted ch24/ch25; closes ch45/ch50)**
The question is brought to its sharpest point in ch45 (Bar Kokhba accounts; his figures serving the slaughter's efficiency). Daniel explicitly states: "Both arguments are good arguments. I have made them both to the empty bench in the dark and neither has ever once won." He carries it to his death unresolved; it appears among the "unbuilt dead" at the foot of his bed in ch50. INTENTIONALLY NEVER RESOLVED (the book's stated position is that the question does not close—this is thematic design, not a gap).

**"All tracks lead to Rome" / divergence ledger**
The divergence ledger's final entry is ch53 (the western shore reached). Prior entries: zero-cut in Aurelian milestone (ch44); printed codex library (ch44); ocean hull afloat (ch44); sea-clock prize leading to Procula's clock (ch52); and the ocean crossing (ch53). The bent arc closes with the novel. CLOSED ch53.

**Vibenius's unpaid debt (planted ch25/ch27; closes ch39)**
Vibenius died ~125 AD (4 years before ch39's date of ~129). The debt he placed on Daniel ("a reason he could say to a god's face") was never formally called in. Daniel redirects the hook into the Minerva endowment structure (the contest's paid priests, the permanent rite). In ch39 Daniel explicitly notes "He had said what I did with his words was not in my hands." CLOSED ch39.

**The manned-balloon vow (planted ch14 "not yet, maybe not ever"; closes at every reaffirmation)**
Daniel keeps this vow to the end. Reaffirmed in ch28 (refuses two manned-ascent requests in the Parthian war), confirmed again in his own final accounting in ch50. CLOSED ch50 (held throughout).

**The gunpowder vow (crossed ch21; moral weight carries through ch45)**
Daniel crossed the ch14 vow in ch21. The moral weight of that crossing reaches its full reckoning in ch45 where he cannot know whether his powder was used in the cave sieges. He states "I will go to my grave not knowing which," and does. CLOSED ch45/ch50 (reckoning complete, uncertainty preserved by design).

**Steel wall (planted ch15; never solved)**
Hermes fails at steel throughout; Felix fails after him; Saturius is still failing at the steam-pot (which needs the steel) in ch52. The steel rung is never cut in the novel's timeline. See NEVER-CLOSES.

**The sea-clock / longitude prize (planted ch36; closes ch52–53)**
The prize stands unwon through Daniel's life (ch47: "I expect it to outlast me"). Procula's escapement (ch52) is the functional precursor—her clock drifts but holds well enough that in ch53 a descendant gimbaled version solves longitude at sea. CLOSED ch53 (prize effectively paid out generationally).

**The telescope failure / seeing the small ones (planted ch23)**
The lie that his glass could show the "invisible creatures" is openly confessed in the encyclopedia itself (ch40: "I once claimed a glass that could and that the claim had been a lie I told to live"). Never physically achieved; the rung is explicitly marked as unbuilt. In ch52 Procula reads this admission. CLOSED as an honest blank ch40.

**The mechanical clock failure (planted ch23; closes ch47/ch52)**
Daniel formally lets the clock go in ch47: writes "Begin where I failed," hands box of failed gears to Felix's fund. Procula climbs this rung in ch52 with the swinging-bar insight. CLOSED ch52.

**Sabinus (the Danube death; planted ch13)**
Not revisited in this slice. His unspoken weight persists in Daniel's bad-night accounting (ch47, ch48: "the column of all the men a thing of mine has killed, Sabinus and the burning cloth…"). Never named aloud; the weight is present. Thematically closed by the manned-balloon vow held. CLOSED by vow and absence.

**Celer's kit-strap (planted ch29; closes ch50)**
Daniel works the strap buckle from ch30 onward (Lucanus copies the tic, ch32). It is part of the "column of the dead" in Daniel's night thoughts (ch47). Not explicitly mentioned in ch50 but implied in the gathering of the unbuilt dead. CLOSED by attrition.

**Lucanus's freedman-son ceiling (planted ch26; closes ch49/ch50)**
Lucanus finds the ceiling (ch43: "never be asked his grandfather's name, so long as he never wants to be asked it"). He refuses to be a cipher keeper (ch49). He brings Daniel the clay engine toy in ch50—the nearest they come. In ch49 Daniel stops grieving it: "The ladder I built was never going to ask a climber his father's name before it let him up." CLOSED ch49/ch50.

**Pamphilus (freed ch18; closes ch48)**
Dies at past 70 (~144 AD) in ch48, in his bed. First person Daniel ever freed. Daniel pays burial from his own purse. CLOSED ch48.

**Eros (pressman; closes ch48)**
Dies the following winter after Pamphilus (ch48). Had taught Vitalis the cutting first. Press unaffected. CLOSED ch48.

**Marcia's death (planted as the structural impossibility in ch48; closes ch48)**
Marcia dies before the cold (~147 AD) of liver disease, having first sealed the deed of trust. Her absence is rendered through the empty side of the bed and Daniel reaching for her tally-board in the dark. CLOSED ch48.

**Naso (ruined hand from ch11; last seen ch29)**
No further mention in ch37–53. Last canonical state: alive at end of Parthian war, heading west by baggage train. NEVER-CLOSES flag (see below).

## Section 3: NEVER-CLOSES Flags

The following seeds are introduced before ch37 and do not receive explicit closure by the end of ch53:

**1. Scaeva's ultimate fate and the sulfur-supply secret**
Scaeva withdraws in ch33 (returned tablet, cold). He knows Daniel imports sulfur and knows the route (planted ch25). His political fate under Antoninus Pius is never stated. The sulfur secret he holds simply lapses with no payoff. This is a minor but genuine loose end—the "hook" he held never springs.

**2. Naso (Titus Aelius Naso; ruined hand and ankle from ch11)**
Last seen alive in ch29, heading west with the Parthian baggage train. No further mention in ch37–53. His ultimate fate is never established.

**3. Steel — the fundamental wall**
The steel rung (planted ch15) is never solved within the novel's timeline. Hermes, Felix, Saturius all fail at it. This is INTENTIONAL (the book's design is that the ladder continues past the novel), but by any audit standard it is an open seed. Marked as design-open rather than a continuity gap.

**4. The hubris-or-duty question**
Daniel states explicitly it never resolves and carries it to his death. INTENTIONALLY UNRESOLVED by authorial design — not a gap.

**5. The steam engine / heat-to-motion bridge**
The mechanism joining heat to motion is lost from Daniel's memory by ch40 and never recovered. He marks the page blank. Saturius is still failing at the pot in ch52. Felix's logged failures are the floor but the ceiling is unreached. DESIGN-OPEN — the novel frames this as the next uncut rung.

**6. The outer planets' order and count**
Lost from Daniel's memory by ch40; he leaves the far rooms of the solar system blank with a note "go and look." Procula reads this in ch52 but does not address it. Never resolved. DESIGN-OPEN — an honest blank in the ladder.

**7. The Ennii (marriage prospect for Ulpia)**
Marcia floats them in ch43; her dying instruction in ch48 cancels the prospect ("may marry where she likes or not at all"). Whether Ulpia marries anyone (including a "big enough plate" candidate) is never resolved. MINOR OPEN.

**8. The ocean hull at the western yard (third hull, afloat but not crossing)**
In ch46 the program lapses. The hull's physical fate (rotted? repurposed?) is not stated. The next ocean crossing is generations later in ch53. The specific hull from ch44 simply goes unmentioned. MINOR OPEN.

**9. The wire / tamed lightning**
Daniel sees this whole and bright in his dying vision (ch50: "a wire with the lightning tamed and running quiet down the length of it into every house in a city"). It appears among the unbuilt dead at his bedside. No rung toward it is laid in the books (he states "not the first rung of it, not in five lifetimes"). DESIGN-OPEN — beyond the ladder's visible reach.

**10. Demetrios (glass-cutter/lens-grinder, ch23)**
Introduced ch23 as the craftsman who ground the failed lenses. No further mention in ch37–53. Minor character; effectively closed by omission but never explicitly.

**11. Maximus (stonecutter/quarry owner, ch23)**
Introduced ch23 for the rail-cart demo. No further mention. Minor character; closed by omission.

**12. Stichus (the coal-pot boy, ch05/ch08)**
Noted as a grown man working at the yard in ch24. No mention thereafter. Minor; closed by omission.
