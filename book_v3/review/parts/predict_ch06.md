## Prediction (written before reading ch6)

Based on chapters 1-5, Daniel has landed on the idea of building a hot-air balloon as his "impossible to argue with" demonstration of knowledge. Every chapter has been building toward this: the map bought him room; the numbers bought him Heras; the germ-theory attempt showed him a story without visible proof fails; and ch05 ends with him lying in the loft, hand in the rising warm smoke, convinced the balloon is cheap, his, and undeniable. The single biggest plot turn I expect in chapter 6 is that Daniel attempts to build and launch his hot-air balloon — and that the attempt goes badly wrong in some significant way. Specifically, I predict either the cloth burns or collapses (an engineering failure he didn't anticipate), or the demonstration occurs but has an unintended social/political consequence: instead of winning him freedom and status, the spectacle frightens or enrages powerful people — soldiers, priests, or a magistrate — who interpret the rising fire-bag as sorcery or an ill omen, putting him in serious danger rather than liberating him. The chapter's turn is that the balloon, his one "true and visible" move, backfires badly.

## Actual ch6 turn

Daniel spends the chapter in a grinding cycle of failed balloon prototypes: a rag model proves the concept but only flies as a tethered toy; a linen-and-mutton-grease model catches fire and destroys itself (burning Daniel's forearm in the process); and a third compromise model rises briefly before its seam tears. The chapter ends with the project stalled — the balloon works at toy scale but Daniel lacks the money, materials, and free status to build one that matters. No public spectacle occurs, no social/political blowback arrives; the chapter is entirely about iterative engineering failure and the circular trap that the balloon needs money to build but was meant to generate the money.

## Score: PARTIALLY CORRECT

The core prediction — that the balloon attempt would go badly wrong through engineering failure, specifically cloth burning or collapsing — was correct. The burning of the greased-linen model and the seam-tear of the third model are exactly those failures. What was wrong: I predicted a public demonstration with social/political consequences (frightened soldiers, accusations of sorcery). Instead the failures are private, in a back-lot watched only by Stichus and Heras, and the chapter's actual structural turn is the circular economic trap Daniel articulates at the end (the balloon needs money; money needs the balloon), not a dangerous external reaction to a public spectacle.

## What telegraphed it

Several things set up the engineering failures precisely:

- Ch05 explicitly flags that Daniel does not know how to build the balloon ("I didn't know how to build it. I didn't know what it cost or what it weighed or whether the cloth would hold or burn"), bracketing the confidence of the idea with ignorance of execution.
- The general pattern established across ch01-05 is that every "solution" Daniel attempts partly works and partly fails in a way that teaches him the next problem (the map draws too much attention; the germ theory is true but unprovable; the language comes slowly and marks him foreign). Ch06 continues that exact pattern with the balloon.
- Ch05's emphasis on Daniel owning nothing — fed, watched, not free — made it predictable that materials and money would be the wall, not social reception. The "cheap, mine, undeniable" framing was already hedged by the word "cheap," signaling that cost would matter.
- The social/political danger angle (sorcery accusation) was telegraphed by the little-ones scene in ch05, where Heras warns Daniel that invisible-animals talk gets people killed. That foreshadowing pointed toward risk if Daniel went public — but ch06 keeps the experiments private, so the setup for social danger is still live; it just did not pay off in this chapter.
