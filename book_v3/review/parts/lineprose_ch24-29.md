# Line-Level Prose Critique: Chapters 24–29
### Sentence-by-Sentence Weakness Analysis against Bad Prose Catalog

---

## Prefatory Note

These six chapters are among the book's most ambitious: the accumulation of Daniel's political exposure (ch24–25), the Forum set piece (ch26), the accusation hearing (ch27), and the eastern campaign including Celer's death (ch28–29). The prose is doing heavy lifting across registers — intimate, rhetorical, military, elegiac. The assessment below is deliberately severe, cataloging every notable weakness while acknowledging the genuine strengths that make the failures more visible by contrast.

---

## CHAPTER 24 — "High Ground"

### Weaknesses

**1. Portentous Closing Aphorism (Catalog 2.5) — repeated structural tic**

> "That is what a rung looks like, I think, when you are standing on the one below it. It looks like a toy somebody made up."

The section about the clay train is genuinely strong up to this point. The moment is earned: Daniel buys a toy copy of his own dream, sold to him by a man who invented the provenance. But the closing generalization ("That is what a rung looks like") extracts a thematic verdict from imagery that has already done the work perfectly. The train sitting on a shelf speaks for itself. The aphorism is the writer stepping out from behind the scene to make sure we got it. This is catalog 2.5 operating at high density: the observation-then-capsule structure appears multiple times in this chapter alone. Cf.:

> "That was the trouble with the high ground. You could see everything from up there. You just couldn't tell who was up there with you."

Two-sentence aphorism sealing the opening paragraph. The observation is reasonable but delivered as gnome rather than rendered as experience.

> "I think about that more than I should."

Sentence-length fragment used as aphoristic close after the girl's torch-is-too-big line. Three closing aphorisms in one chapter is a high density for catalog 2.5.

---

**2. "It wasn't X, it was Y" Correctio / Antithesis (Catalog 2.1)**

> "most days I told myself that was the design and not the wound."

This is the 2.1 correctio in compressed form — design / wound as a binary — and it is the kind of line that sounds penetrating because it offers two framings and then leaves them unresolved. But the binary does the psychological work the scene should be doing. The scene (Macer's dinner, the girl's prize, the silence about parentage) is already showing us both the design and the wound. The sentence names what the scene demonstrates.

---

**3. Filtering Verbs (Catalog 2.7)**

> "I knew all that, leaning on my own well in the dark, the way you know a draft in a good room."

"I knew all that" is a filtering verb opener — it interposes the narrator's awareness rather than placing us inside the awareness. The extension ("the way you know a draft") is actually a fine simile, but the sentence frame weakens it. The filtering is intermittent in ch24, less severe than in later chapters.

---

**4. Gnomic Universal — "you" generalization (Catalog 3.1)**

> "You can sit in silence with a man for thirty years or you can sit in it for one good evening; we'd done the one good evening so many times it added up to the thirty."

The first clause is a gnomic universal passed off as hard-won wisdom. The "you can X or you can Y" structure is a generalization masquerading as specificity. Immediately following, "we'd done the one good evening so many times" rescues it with particularity — but the rescue reveals that the opening generalization was unnecessary; the particular sentence does all the work.

---

**5. Vague Body-Cued Emotion — anatomical shorthand (Catalog 3.4)**

> "I felt the warmth of being seen go up my spine before my head could catch it and call it what it was"

(This appears in ch25, but the pattern opens in ch24 with Heras's speech.)
The "warmth going up the spine" is catalog 3.4 — anatomical shorthand for an emotion (gratitude + being recognized) that the prose doesn't otherwise render. The syntax tries to recuperate it ("before my head could catch it") but the physical metaphor underneath is received, not observed.

---

**6. Weight/Burden Inflation (Catalog 3.5)**

> "He had carried himself young for years and then somewhere past sixty had set it down."

This is a variant on catalog 3.5 — "carrying" youth as a burden-metaphor — applied to physical carriage. It is less egregious than pure emotional-weight usage, but the "set it down" idiom slides into the same territory: burden metaphor as default for unspecified existential quality.

---

**Strong Passages in Ch24:**

The phone section is the chapter's genuine achievement:

> "It is not broken the way a thing here breaks, a spring snapped, a gear stripped, something Hermes could open and curse and maybe mend. It is whole. There is not a scratch on the glass that matters. It is simply asleep and there is no waking it, no fire I can build, no wheel I can turn, no clever Roman trick and no clever Thulean one either, because the thing it needs is a current I cannot make and could not explain and have given up grieving for the way you give up grieving for a country."

Catalog violations are absent here. The comparative syntax (not X the way Y) is used for genuine discrimination rather than rhetorical simulation of nuance. The pileup of negatives ("no fire... no wheel... no clever... no clever") earns its anaphora by building specific texture rather than emotional abstraction.

The train purchase ending is also strong through most of its run ("A clay engine that pulls nothing, made by a man who copied a dream...") until the aphoristic close spoils it.

---

## CHAPTER 25 — "Three Faces"

### Weaknesses

**1. Tricolon Crutch — structural three-beat (Catalog 1.3)**

The chapter is structurally organized as three visitor-scenes. This is legitimate plotting. But within the prose itself, tricolon arrives at emphasis points throughout:

> "Not money. I had stopped being able to feel the money as a number a while back" — (ch24)

In ch25 the structural three is formalized into a cataloguing habit. The chapter closing makes this explicit:

> "I picked up the bulla and held it, the small weight of it, and thought about the three men who had come to my gate that winter, each offering to do small things for me and asking only that I let them decide where the fire pointed. The senator who saw the engine and wanted the tube. The man of letters who had stopped trying to prove me false and started, far more dangerously, agreeing I was true. The priest who would not lean..."

The three-beat summary is catalog 1.3 deployed at chapter-close. The three items are genuinely distinguishable, but the formal tricolon packaging ("The senator... The man of letters... The priest...") is the writer reaching for rhetorical shape rather than letting the material find its own form. Worse, the summary re-states what the chapter has just dramatized in three full scenes — this is also catalog 6.1 (scene-closing restatement of takeaway).

---

**2. Filtering Verb — perception scrim (Catalog 2.7)**

> "I felt the warmth of being seen go up my spine before my head could catch it and call it what it was, which was a man showing me he had done his reading on me. The way you show a horse the bridle before you show it the bit."

"I felt" opens this passage. The filtering verb is followed immediately by the analytic conclusion (Scaeva is showing me the bridle) — so we get feeling + interpretation packaged together, which is the prose doing the character's emotional work and cognitive work simultaneously rather than letting either breathe. The horse/bridle simile is sharp; the "I felt" frame around it is surplus.

---

**3. "It wasn't X, it was Y" Correctio (Catalog 2.1)**

> "He had not understood, standing there, that by naming the customs man I had told him I import sulfur, how much, and through which gate, and that I had a temper that could be reached through my purse. I had thought I was buying time with a coin. I had handed him a map of where my walls were thin and called it small talk."

The "I had thought / I had handed" construction is a correctio variant: false belief named, then supplanted. It works here because the revision is genuinely surprising (the customs man as intelligence, not obligation). But the pattern's density in the book as a whole means each individual usage is doing less work.

---

**4. Portentous Closing Aphorism (Catalog 2.5)**

> "I stood in the warm lamplight of the house I had built on the high ground and listened, under the women's voices, for feet on the slope."

This is a strong closing image. But "feet on the slope" completes a metaphor system ("high ground," "the slope") that has been running since ch24, and the closing line arrives too neatly — it seals the metaphor rather than leaving it open. The prose is heard arriving at its landing before it touches down.

---

**5. Adjective-Stacking (Catalog 7.4)**

> "A tall old man gone soft in the way of men who had been hard once and let the hardness sink under good living without quite losing the frame of it."

This is actually strong observation, but the descriptor-chain ("tall old man gone soft... been hard once... hardness sink... good living... frame") is adjacent to 7.4: it stacks qualities rather than isolating the one. The "frame" metaphor at the end is doing real work; the buildup dilutes it slightly.

---

**Strong Passages in Ch25:**

The Vibenius scene is the chapter's best prose. The milk-eye detail is genuinely specific (catalog 3.4 avoided: it is an observed physiological fact, not an anatomical metaphor). The speech about not knowing what a thing means and the art of knowing which days that is allowed is the sharpest dialogue in these six chapters — it performs psychological complexity rather than naming it.

> "Most men do not wonder whether the gods will a thing. They assume it, or they fear it, but they do not wonder. You wonder."

Three-beat rhythm here, but it is intentional: the three-part epistemology (assume / fear / wonder) is the content, not just the shape.

The Scaeva scene's "warm water you could not push back against any single part of" is an excellent piece of abstract rendering — it finds the precise quality of the experience (resistance without target) and enacts it syntactically.

## CHAPTER 26 — "The Forum"

### Weaknesses

**1. Template Chapter Opening (Catalog 6.2)**

> "On the ninth day we named him Lucanus, and the house breathed out."

The chapter opens with resolution of the previous chapter's suspended domestic tension before pivoting immediately to the Forum description. The transition is managed, but "the house breathed out" is catalog 3.4 / 4.3 compressed: anatomical metaphor (breathing) applied to an abstraction (household tension), with a tinge of over-poeticization. The house breathing is a received image.

---

**2. Portentous Closing Aphorism (Catalog 2.5) — most egregious instance in these chapters**

> "I could see all of it laid out below me like the lamps of the wharf."

That's a strong simile. But immediately following, in the chapter's penultimate paragraph:

> "...and that all my cleverness had bought me was a higher place to stand while they decided."

And at close:

> "...which was nothing, which was that it had found its level and would keep it, and did not care in the least whose walls were thin."

The river-finding-its-level close is catalog 2.5 at high intensity: a generalized, present-tense observation attached to a natural phenomenon used as emotional mirror (also catalog 4.4, pathetic fallacy). The river "not caring" about whose walls are thin echoes the chapter's political themes but converts them into gnomic nature-description. The Apollodorus scene has already delivered this theme through action and dialogue. The closing nature aphorism is redundant and tonally overripe.

---

**3. Vague Relational Change (Catalog 3.3)**

> "for a moment we were two men reading the same lie and knowing different halves of it, which is as close to friendship as we ever came, and was not friendship."

The "as close to X as we came, and was not X" is a correctio (2.1) and also catalog 3.3: naming a relational shift with analytical commentary rather than rendering the specific texture of the moment. The column scene between Daniel and Apollodorus is already showing us exactly what their bond is; the named summary is surplus.

---

**4. Emotion Named (Catalog 3.2)**

> "I was not anonymous in that crowd. That was the new thing about my life that I never got used to."

"The new thing" is category-naming (catalog 3.2 adjacent): identifying a condition rather than rendering it. The chapter immediately follows with specifics (the perfumer's wife, Scaeva's freedman), so the named announcement is made redundant by what follows. Cutting the setup sentence would be stronger.

---

**5. Over-Explaining (Catalog 6.3)**

After the confrontation between Apollodorus and the bearded man:

> "I had watched a man I respected spit, gently, into a well he might one day have to drink from, and do it with the clean conscience of a man who has never imagined the well belonging to anyone but himself."

The spitting-in-the-well metaphor is strong on its own terms. But the extension ("with the clean conscience of a man who has never imagined...") is over-explanation: it tells us how to understand the metaphor, removes the reader's work. Cf. catalog 6.3.

---

**Strong Passages in Ch26:**

The Trajan's Column passage is the chapter's finest prose:

> "It was all true and none of it was what happened."

A complete sentence carrying enormous weight. This is the kind of compression that makes the surrounding passages' verbosity more visible.

The description of the column relief — "a carved ribbon that wound round and round like the rind cut from an apple in one unbroken strip" — is precise, original, and earns its metaphor by being actually accurate to what a spiral narrative relief looks like.

## CHAPTER 27 — "The Accusation"

### Weaknesses

**1. Portentous Closing Aphorism — most formulaic instance (Catalog 2.5)**

> "I knelt in the middle of all of them with a clean rag of a soul and was about as alone as a man gets to be."

This closes the sacrifice scene. "Clean rag of a soul" tries to be pointed but lands as over-literary (catalog 4.3, precious image). The motif of the rag (Daniel's symbol for hygienic cleanliness vs. spiritual compromise) is doing real work in the chapter; using it here as a closing image is apt thematically but the phrasing is labored. "About as alone as a man gets to be" is the anti-climactic half of the closing sentence — hedged ("about as") and general rather than specific.

---

**2. Gnomic Universal — free-standing maxim (Catalog 3.1)**

> "because a man kneels, in the end, to whatever is holding the knife."

This is the chapter's most naked instance of catalog 3.1 and 2.5 combined. It is a present-tense generalized maxim delivered at an emotional climax, the kind of sentence a lesser writer would italicize. The syntax ("in the end") is a tell: it signals that the writer knows this is meant to resonate as timeless truth. It doesn't earn its resonance — it claims it. The scene (kneeling on the Aventine, the blood running, the crowd) already shows us the kneeling and who holds the knife. The aphorism is redundant and tonally cheaper than the scene it closes.

---

**3. Tricolon Crutch — three-beat cost enumeration (Catalog 1.3)**

> "It bought my life and it cost me three things, and I knew the price of all three before the heifer had stopped twitching."

This announced-tricolon ("three things") is the most mechanical use of catalog 1.3 in these chapters. The writer flags the three-part structure before delivering it: "It bought my life and it cost me three things" is a setup sentence whose only function is to tell us a tricolon is coming. The three costs (college, Scaeva, Vibenius) are then enumerated in three full paragraphs. The announced-structure kills any organic rhythm.

---

**4. Correctio with Excessive Analytical Commentary (Catalog 2.1)**

> "I will not pretend I read it well. I read it the way a man reads a language he learned late and under threat, getting the nouns and missing the music."

"I will not pretend I read it well" is a false modesty frame that sets up the correctio. "Getting the nouns and missing the music" is the book's best-known line type: an antithesis with lyrical pretension. It works here — the distinction between nouns and music is specific to Daniel's relationship with Latin. But it follows a pattern so well-worn in the book that the reader may feel the machinery even when the machine is running well.

---

**5. Scene-Closing Restatement (Catalog 6.1)**

> "She had worked out, all the hours I knelt, what it would cost the enterprise to have the priests' hand in it, and how to price around them, because that was her craft and her craft did not stop for the gods."

The scene closes on a summary of Marcia's character that the scene has already demonstrated through her behavior (the hand pressed to his chest, the "hook in a thing is a thing two can pull on"). "Her craft did not stop for the gods" is catalog 6.1: re-stating the takeaway after showing it. It's also catalog 2.4 adjacent ("that was the thing about Marcia").

---

**Strong Passages in Ch27:**

Daniel's speech to Vibenius is the chapter's strongest passage and deliberately placed as the emotional pivot:

> "A man can't steal what was never his to begin with, and he can't climb to a god's house on a ladder the god didn't first set down for him to find."

Its subsequent unraveling ("not one word of it did I believe") is one of the book's more honest moves — showing us the speech as performance, then stripping it. The paragraph that follows ("I had taken the truest thing I owned... and dressed it in robes") is good analytical self-examination that earns the prose's more philosophical register.

Crispus's accusation speech is a genuine set piece. The paragraph beginning "He had built me a gallows out of true planks" is precise and unshowy — the metaphor stays within its construction-domain for its whole run, a rarity in these chapters.

## CHAPTER 28 — "East"

### Weaknesses

**1. Announced Structure / Tricolon Setup (Catalog 1.3)**

> "The order came in two halves, the way the important things always did, and the second half was the one with the hook in it."

"The way the important things always did" is catalog 3.1 (gnomic universal) embedded in an opening sentence. The chapter then organizes itself around the two halves — which is fine — but the metalevel announcement ("this is how important things work") is the writer editorializing over the structure instead of letting the structure speak.

---

**2. Filtering Verb — doubled (Catalog 2.7)**

> "It was on one of those still mornings, watching the empty basket hang at the end of its line over country that ran flat and brown and enormous toward a horizon the army was going to cross, that the other thing came to me, and I want to set down honestly that it came to me as a sickness comes, not as a plan."

"I want to set down honestly that it came to me" is a filtering frame — it interposes the narrator's quality-control on his own narration ("I want to set down honestly"). It creates distance between reader and the revelation, which is the opposite of what the scene needs. The "sickness comes, not as a plan" correctio (2.1) is also notable here, though less damaging.

---

**3. Portentous Closing Aphorism (Catalog 2.5)**

> "I had a wound with an idea in it."

The chapter's climactic western-sea thought ends with this. It is catalog 2.5 in its most compressed form: present-tense, capsule, sealing a complex passage with a single aphoristic statement. "A wound with an idea in it" is meant to land hard. It lands slightly soft because it converts a fully dramatized psychological movement (the recoil from the thought, the recognition of the turning-the-wheel pattern) into a metaphor that tidies what should remain messy.

---

**4. Emotion Named — explicitly labeled psychological state (Catalog 3.2)**

> "I kept my soldiers' guts in them and they marched east on the strength of it to be spent further along the line, and that was my trade now, the same trade it had been at the siege, the knot I had stopped pretending I could cut. I made them able to go on. Going on was the disease."

"Going on was the disease" is catalog 3.1 and 2.5 combined — a gnomic present-tense aphorism at a paragraph close. It is also catalog 3.2 adjacent: "the disease" is a named abstraction for a complex moral condition (enabling an overextended war). The passage has already shown this through Celer's dialogue and the road description. "Going on was the disease" over-names what the image of the road has rendered.

---

**Strong Passages in Ch28:**

The Brundisium harbor scene is excellent:

> "I stood among barrels stacked taller than a house, my barrels, the filter batteries I had drawn the plan of years ago in a yard by the Tiber, and watched a clerk tick them off a list against a tablet, and the tablet was one of a stack, and the stack was one of a room, and somewhere there was a man whose whole task in this life was rooms of tablets that were rooms of barrels that were the drinking of an army..."

The nesting syntax (rooms of tablets that were rooms of barrels that were the drinking of an army) enacts the scale-vertigo rather than announcing it. Catalog violations are absent here.

Celer's reappearance is the chapter's second strong moment — voice differentiation is genuine (catalog 5.2 avoided: he speaks in a distinctly compressed, deflecting register different from Daniel's interiority).

---

## CHAPTER 29 — "Overreach"

### Weaknesses

**1. "Something Shifted" — vague relational descriptor (Catalog 3.3)**

> "the army was a fist that had punched all the way through and now could not pull its arm back out."

This is not 3.3 exactly but a cognate: a structural metaphor that tries to render a complex geopolitical condition in a single image. The fist/arm metaphor is vivid but also slightly too neat — it converts the messy retreat-and-overreach situation into a clean physical image that may simplify what the chapter should leave difficult.

---

**2. Portentous Closing Aphorism — density peak (Catalog 2.5)**

Chapter 29 has the highest density of aphoristic closings in the sequence:

> "Going on was the disease." (ch28, but recurs thematically)

> "we had run out of both at once." (closing the overreach paragraph)

> "burning a thing is only another way of keeping the smell of it."

The last sentence of the novel's section (about not burning the strap) is the most ambitious aphoristic close: "I had learned that burning a thing is only another way of keeping the smell of it." It is the chapter's intended signature line. And it is catalog 2.5 in its most earnest form: a general present-tense maxim ("burning a thing is... keeping the smell of it") deployed at the emotional summit of the narrative. As a last line it reaches for timelessness and achieves genericness. Compare the phone passage in ch24, where the same emotional register was rendered through the specific, tactile, observed: the charged metal, the wrong song, the side-by-side boxes. Here, the generalization replaces the specific.

---

**3. Emotion Named — italicized moment reported (Catalog 3.2)**

> "I sat with the cup still at his lips for longer than made any sense, the way I had knelt by the burned cloth with my hands out and nothing for them to do"

"For longer than made any sense" is a micro-instance of catalog 3.2: emotion named by describing its irrationality rather than rendering its texture. The cup-at-lips image is strong; the analytical gloss ("longer than made any sense") explains the grief rather than trusting the image.

---

**4. Filtering Verb — awareness frame (Catalog 2.7)**

> "I knew this the way I knew most things, by arithmetic, lying awake. So I was not surprised when they brought him to me."

"I knew this the way I knew most things" is a filtering frame that pulls the reader back into the narrator's self-consciousness at a moment when the prose should be advancing toward the scene. "So I was not surprised" is a further filter — reporting the character's emotional preparation rather than placing us in the moment of surprise or its absence.

---

**5. The "Two Long, One Short" Cadence — chapter-closing rhythm (Catalog 1.2)**

The chapter's final movement shows this most clearly:

> "I worked it into my own kit instead, where my hands could find it in the dark and worry at it the way his had, and I went back down the line to the next century and the next fire and the next grown men who had killed other men and would now stand and wait, because I told them to, for the water to boil."

The long sentence — effectively the last sentence of the chapter — avoids catalog 1.2 by not following it with a short punch. But the three "next" repetitions (next century, next fire, next grown men) within it are catalog 1.4 (anaphora) in compressed form. The anaphora is here at the service of rhythm rather than emotion, which makes it more defensible — but it arrives at a key emotional moment, which means the reader registers the formal pattern.

---

**Strong Passages in Ch29:**

The death scene is the book's most sustained clean prose:

> "I cleaned it. I am going to set down what I did, because it is the only part of this I get to keep, and because a man should be able to say exactly how far short everything he had reached."

"How far short everything he had reached" — the construction is compressed, unusual, slightly ungrammatical in the best sense. It resists the aphorism.

> "there is no clean water in the world that washes out a thing that is already in the blood. Everything I had was for keeping them out. I had nothing for getting them back."

Three short declarative sentences that earn their brevity. Catalog 1.3 (tricolon) is technically invoked but the three beats are non-parallel and vary in rhythm — this is the kind of considered three-sentence construction the catalog is designed to distinguish from reflex tricolon.

The kit-strap closing gesture (Naso folding Daniel's fingers over it) is the chapter's finest image precisely because it does not explain itself.

## VERDICT: MICRO-PROSE AS STRENGTH OR LIABILITY?

### The Core Problem: Aphorism Dependency

The dominant failure mode in ch24–29 is not sentence rhythm (the prose avoids length monotony well), not abstraction (scenes are generally rendered in specific detail), and not dialogue (character voices are meaningfully differentiated). The primary liability is **the closing aphorism used as an emotional substitute**. These chapters close sections, scenes, and paragraphs with present-tense generalizations at a rate of approximately 2–3 per chapter. The effect is cumulative: by ch27–29, the reader has been trained to expect an aphorism at every emotional climax, which means the aphorisms begin landing as formula rather than discovery.

This is catalog 2.5 and 3.1 working together. The prose shows Daniel's situation with precision and specific detail — then reaches for a maxim to seal each scene, as if the scene cannot be trusted to hold its meaning without a capsule. The most damaging instance is "a man kneels, in the end, to whatever is holding the knife" (ch27), and the most symptomatic is the final sentence of ch29, where an entire chapter of earned, restrained, specific death-scene prose is capped with "burning a thing is only another way of keeping the smell of it" — a sentence that would fit equally in a self-help book, a different novel, or a grief-advice column.

### Comparison to What We'd Expect at Mid-Book

In early chapters, tics tend to be lighter: the writer is still calibrating voice, leaning on distinctive elements (Daniel's anachronism, the Roman texture, the isolation). By mid-book, tics typically intensify because the writer has codified what "sounds like the book" — and the aphorism, the correctio, the gnomic close have become part of what this book "sounds like."

The evidence supports this: the phone passage in ch24, which is presumably early in the book's composition or among its most revised passages, shows almost none of the dominant tics. The eastern campaign chapters (28–29), which are structurally climactic and therefore most vulnerable to over-signaling, show the highest density, particularly in the closing aphorisms. The Celer death scene partially breaks the pattern — it is the best-controlled extended passage in these six chapters — but is book-ended by aphoristic framing that softens its impact.

### Assessment

The micro-prose here is **a conditional strength — undermined by a specific and corrigible tic**. The book's voice is distinctive, the sentence variety is genuine, and the best passages in these chapters are very good. But the aphorism dependency is systemic enough to constitute a structural habit, not an occasional slip. A line edit focused specifically on catalog 2.5 and 3.1 — cutting or transforming every paragraph-closing and section-closing generalization — would substantially strengthen chs 24–29 without requiring substantive revision of plot or character. The prose knows how to trust its images. It simply doesn't trust them at the moments of highest emotional weight, which is exactly when it most needs to.

## TWO–THREE GENUINELY STRONG PASSAGES (Calibration)

1. **Ch24, phone section** (beginning "It does nothing..."): sustained absence of catalog violations; achieves grief through enumerated specifics, not named emotion.

2. **Ch29, Celer wound treatment** (beginning "I cleaned it..."): the declarative sequence ("Everything I had was for keeping them out. I had nothing for getting them back.") is the best three-sentence unit in these chapters — parallel in structure but non-formulaic in rhythm and weight.

3. **Ch26, "It was all true and none of it was what happened"**: six words doing the work of a paragraph. The subsequent column analysis sustains the compression for two pages before the Apollodorus-well-metaphor over-explains it.
