# The Long Way Home

*(working alternates: The Ladder; Ultima Thule; Begin Where I Failed)*

## Logline
A seventeen-year-old with a dying phone and an ordinary high-school education wakes
in the dirt outside Rome in 98 AD, and spends the rest of his life proving that the
thing standing between an empire and the future was never genius, only courage,
credibility, language, money, and the patience to move the bottom rungs in the
right order.

## Summary
Daniel Mercer wakes in a stubble field outside Rome in the year 98 with a phone that
will die in four days and nothing else from home but what is already in his head. He
cannot read the signs or speak to the men who arrest him, and the only thing between
him and a nameless death in a foreign century is what a normal teenager happens to
know about how the world works.

Over fifty-seven years he becomes "the Thulean," the man who made fire fly. He seeds
Hindu-Arabic numerals and a printing press; builds balloons and bursts a cannon on
purpose to argue for a steel he cannot make; dresses germ theory as invisible
creatures the gods send so that men will boil their water; and founds a story contest
to teach a whole civilization to dream in machines a generation before the machines
exist. He survives Trajan's wars and an impiety trial, the lethal politics of being
too useful, the death of the one emperor who shielded him, and a purge under the next.
He loses, one by one, everyone who ever came close to understanding him, and confronts
that his gifts serve power's cruelty as readily as its good.

Knowing he will die with almost every dream unbuilt, he stops trying to win in his
lifetime and turns to building things meant to outlast him: institutions, and above
all an encyclopedia written in his own lost language, a ladder of knowledge from
arithmetic upward, honest about every rung he could not reach. He dies far from a home
two thousand years away, the secret of where he came from going into the ground with
him while the ladder stays for the world. Generations later, a freed slave's heir reads
his honest record of failure and climbs the rung he never could; and long after that, a
ship crosses the western ocean he only ever drew.

## Back-cover blurb
Seventeen-year-old Daniel wakes in the dirt outside Rome in the year 98, with a phone
that will die in four days and nothing else from home but what is already in his head.
He cannot read the signs. He cannot speak to the men who arrest him. And the only thing
standing between him and a nameless death in a foreign century is what an ordinary
teenager happens to know about how the world works.

He is not a genius. He has no system, no second miracle, no way back. What he has is
arithmetic, the rough shape of a printing press, a story about invisible creatures that
makes men wash their hands, and the patience to do things in the right order. It turns
out to be enough to bend an empire, and nowhere near enough to keep him from being the
loneliest man alive.

Spanning fifty-seven years, three emperors, and two wars, *The Long Way Home* is the
story of one unremarkable mind trying to move the bottom rungs of history, and of
everything it costs to be the only person on earth who knows where the ladder leads.
