# Recurring bad habits flagged across ch01-ch05

Compiled from two sequential bad-habits readers (Sonnet + Haiku),
each instructed to read chapters in order and flag only patterns
appearing in 3+ chapters. Reader output is independent.

This is a CHECKLIST for auditing later chapters. The HIGH-CONFIDENCE
items were flagged by BOTH models. The MEDIUM-CONFIDENCE items were
flagged by Sonnet alone but with clear examples.

---

## HIGH-CONFIDENCE recurring bad habits

### 1. Stacked "and...and...and" coordinating clause chains
Breathless run-on sentences that hide weak verbs behind connection.
Example from ch01: "I scrambled up off the ground, turning, scanning
the tree line… and I kicked a clod of dirt as hard as I could and it
busted apart and didn't help anything."

### 2. Inventory-list introspection ("phone, except... electricity, except...")
A literary-sounding bullet-list structure dressed as narration. Reads
like prose analysis, not panic.

### 3. Parenthetical author-voice asides
"And here is the thing about long division" / "That's something
people do where I'm from too" / "Airplane mode, in 98 AD." Pulls
the reader out of scene for an editorial aside written in a
register older than the narrator.

### 4. "Before I decided anything / without meaning to" instinct tic
"I was up on the balls of my feet before I decided anything" /
"I was up against the wall before I knew I'd moved" /
"I slowed up without meaning to."
Used to convey instinct. Once = effective. Repeated = the author
doesn't trust his action verbs.

---

## MEDIUM-CONFIDENCE recurring habits (Sonnet flagged)

### 5. Heat in ears/neck as the sole panic marker
"my ears went hot," "the heat in my ears," "the heat came up the
back of my neck and into my ears." Vary the body's response.

### 6. "X is its own Y" writerly formula
"The ground was its own education." "The garum was its own
education, slower." "The sounds were their own private joke at
my expense." Three+ times across the chapters — a novelist's
fingerprint, not a teen's voice.

### 7. "Not X, just Y" deflating construction
"They were only curious, and what they were curious about was me."
"He'd seen maps, you could tell… The map was something he could
weigh, not a wonder he had to gawk at." A practiced irony move.

### 8. "Real" / "really" overused as emotional emphasis
"it was real, all the way real" / "and they did not look fake at
all" / "It was the only piece of home I had that still worked."
The word becomes wallpaper.

### 9. Scene-closing "Danny settles into stillness"
"I ended up sitting in the dirt, knees up, arms around them."
"I sat against the cold stone with my knees up." "I lay there
with my eyes open until I slept." Four+ chapter-end moments where
the closing beat is Danny going still in a quiet space.

---

## DISCOUNTED (flagged but kept):

- "I didn't have a word for X" — thesis-relevant; the language gap
  is the book's central obstacle.
- Present-tense general-truth slips — explicitly licensed by the
  style guide as in-voice ("a person telling a story slips into
  the present for a thing that is still true").
