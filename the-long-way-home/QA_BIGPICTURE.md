## A. Missing or Underdeveloped Overarching Plot Threads

### Food Arc

**What the plan called for:** Six phased beats: omelette/garum discovery (ch05), tavern partnership with Gnaeus featuring fig-vinegar reduction (ch15/16), pasta introduction (ch15/16), formal tavern food scene (ch34), New World crops arriving (ch35–38), cacao in ch43, late-life tomato in ch50.

**What the manuscript delivers:** The omelette and garum evolution appear correctly in ch05. Gnaeus is mentioned once by name in ch20 as the source of a fig-vinegar reduction, but there is no tavern partnership scene, no pasta introduction, no Gnaeus character developed on the page. The ch34 beat is wholly absent — ch34 is the treasury/Eudemus chapter. New World crops arrive only in one sentence in ch43: Macer is given a warm dark drink from "roasted beans that had come back with the second expedition," unnamed but clearly cacao. No sweet potato, corn, or tomato appears anywhere in the manuscript. No late-life garden or tomato in ch50 (ch50 is Daniel's death scene). The harvest of absent food beats: pasta, the Gnaeus tavern arc, the New World vegetable/fruit wave, and the late garden close are all unwritten.

**What to do:** The pasta and tavern partnership scenes were planned for mid-novel (ch15–20 range). They could be folded into existing workshop/social scenes without a dedicated chapter. New World crops are the larger gap — the spec called for three chapters (ch35–38) of culinary consequence from the survey program. Only the cacao appears, and without its name, in ch43. The tomato close in late life is a symbolic beat that the manuscript currently lacks entirely.

Beat 4 (farrier Statius pyrometry): ch24–29. Beat 5 (Crispus reverse-engineers the aggregate model): ch25–35. Beat 6 (failure mode, technique reaches wrong hands): ch29–38.

The planned Beat 6 — the technique reaching wrong hands, the aggregate model being exploited in ways Daniel didn't intend — is not dramatized.

## B. Continuity and Canon Errors

### Lucanus Age Error in ch27

Ch27 ("The Accusation") ends with Lucanus described as "twelve" years old, with "twelve-year-old business." This is physically impossible: Lucanus was born at the start of ch25 (eight days old), and only a few chapters and months separate ch25 from ch27. Ch31 correctly has him as five years old when Daniel returns from three years east — this is the accurate age. The error is in ch27, not ch31 as V2_MASTER_CONTEXT.md suggests. **Fix: remove or replace the "twelve" reference in ch27.** The phrase should describe an infant or toddler's behavior, or the reference should be cut.

### Pamphilus Collar in ch31

V2_MASTER_CONTEXT.md flags this as an error to fix: ch31 has Pamphilus at the gate "his collar five years gone and the mark of it long faded." The collar is removed in ch18 by Hermes. Between ch18 and ch31, Daniel goes east in ch28 and is away about three years. The timing of "five years gone" is plausible if the ch18 removal happened before the eastern campaign and ch31 is approximately five years after ch18 — this is not necessarily an error depending on the exact internal chronology, but the master context flags it. Worth verifying against the chapter timeline.

### Tyche Age in ch12

V2_MASTER_CONTEXT.md flags Tyche described as "sixteen" in ch12 as potentially wrong. Ch09 introduces Tyche at approximately fourteen. Ch12 is three years after ch09 (Daniel marches north "three years" after the workshop is established). Sixteen is consistent with fourteen plus two years, possibly slightly off depending on how long the workshop runs before the march. This is a minor math check rather than a hard error.

### Naso's Injuries — Consistency

Ch11 describes Naso's accident: "left hand destroyed, one eye damaged." Ch25 has Scaeva referring to "Geta's burned left wrist," which Scaeva uses as evidence of surveillance depth — but this is about Geta, not Naso. In ch21 (the cannon chapter), Geta's explosion takes "his left hand gone/ruined, one eye blind." Both Naso (ch11) and Geta (ch21) suffer left-hand/eye damage. This is not technically a continuity error since they are different accidents, but the mirrored injuries may read as sloppy to a careful reader. Worth checking that no scene confuses the two men.

### Sabinus vs. the Named Volunteer in ch13

Ch13 names the balloon volunteer Sabinus, who dies. Ch12 mentions "the vow made" but Sabinus is in ch13. The Naso/Sabinus distinction is: Naso is the first balloon casualty (ch11, burned but survives), Sabinus is the second (ch13, dies in free-flight attempt). The manuscript handles this correctly.

## D. Overarching Tic Issues

### One-sentence paragraph overuse

This is the most pervasive system-wide pattern. Every chapter in the manuscript relies heavily on single-sentence paragraphs for emotional weight and beat marking. In many cases these are effective, but the density is too high across all 53 chapters. Chapters 31, 37, 45, 47, 48, and 50 are the worst offenders. Examples: ch47 has seven consecutive one-sentence paragraphs in the section on the body's decline. Ch45 has "Ulpia sat with her finger on the rule and I sat with the column and neither of us said anything for a long time" followed immediately by a new paragraph beginning "I went up to the house late." The rhythm becomes a tell — the reader learns that a one-sentence paragraph signals emotional significance, which dilutes the signal when it appears every two hundred words.

### "The way" construction overuse

The banned "the way" construction (identified in V2 core changes) appears in nearly every chapter. Spot examples: ch12 "the way a man leans when he's deciding" (not exact but representative); ch37 "the way you look at a thing"; ch50 "the way you hear a thing your body has learned." These constructions are not individually wrong but their density creates a voice tic that a careful reader will feel.

### Gnomic / wisdom-button chapter endings

Several chapters end on an aphoristic note that the V2 guidelines identify as a pattern to eliminate. Ch12 ends: "Down at the camp they were carrying out the evening's dead, two of them, both from the flux, neither from a sword. / I held the rope." — this is a legitimate image-close, not a gnomic button. Ch37's close is better: the plumb-line left hanging. Ch42's close ("the water in the cup went cold while I worked, and I drank it cold rather than get up for more") is clean. Ch33's close ("the room with the one press running was about the size of a cell, and I knew exactly how that compared, having begun in one") is the clearest gnomic button in the manuscript — it explains its own meaning aloud. Ch44's close, ending on Daniel in the press-room in the dark, is correctly underplayed. Several chapter endings are at risk of the pattern; ch33 is the most clear violation.

### Narrator stepping outside time

V2_MASTER_CONTEXT.md bans the narrator stepping outside his own timeline to comment from a position of omniscient futurity. This is largely well-controlled in the manuscript, but ch15 has "steel is the chokepoint insight" framing that positions Daniel as knowing he has found the insight in the moment — which is permissible in first-person retrospect. The more significant instances are in ch44 (the milestones section, where Daniel says the figures "stopped being the Thulean's marks and became simply the marks") — this is retrospective narration, acceptable. The pattern is mostly under control.

### Correctio / epanorthosis ("not X, it's Y")

This banned construction appears in ch40 in the motion law section: the framing of "not the fever. The knowing" in ch29 is close but belongs to a dialogue beat rather than narration. The pattern is mostly absent from the manuscript, which represents genuine improvement from V1.
