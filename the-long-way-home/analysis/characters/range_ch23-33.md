## 2) PROFILE ADHERENCE (per profiled character)

### Daniel / Marcus Ulpius Danihel — [OK]
- Voice/wound dead-on. The "not a prodigy" rule is the explicit ENGINE of ch23: he
  cannot remember the clock escapement ("I could not remember the piece... seeing a
  thing swing is not knowing how its faces are angled"), the achromatic-lens failure
  ("a fuzzy man in a brown tunic wearing a rainbow"), and the rails that "lost, fair
  and square, in the open court of arithmetic" to slavery economics. This is the
  bible's DON'T ("smoothly competent / win without cost") honored at length.
- The twin terror (nobody=dead/enslaved; dying with it all in his head) is foregrounded:
  ch24 the dead phone and "the loudest, fullest, emptiest moment of my whole strange
  life"; ch30 the entire arc is "everything I was, in law, came from him [Trajan]."
- Modern references no one gets, kept private: ch23 "a documentary," "a baseball game...
  somebody's father's binoculars"; ch24 "the phone." Correctly never spoken aloud
  in-world.
- CITIZEN NAME CONSISTENCY (task watch-item): clean. "Marcus Ulpius Danihel" used ch28
  (imperial tablet), ch30 ("freedman of the deified house"), ch31. Son named "Marcus
  Ulpius Lucanus" ch26 with the praenomen-from-Daniel / nomen-from-emperor logic spelled
  out correctly. "Danihel" spelling matches glossary. Romans still call him "the
  Thulean" informally (Scaeva, Macer, Apollodorus) per voice rules. [OK — clean]

### Marcia — [OK, exemplary]
- Bible: "runs accounts and the business side... with more nerve than most men in the
  room... a genuine partner, not a prize... unsentimental and real." Fully delivered.
  ch23 the rails arithmetic ("You own the men, Maximus") and "Everything is a toy until
  it makes money." ch27 builds his hearing-defense map ("It's not the kind of figures I
  can add"). ch32 the four-line forward strategy ("we are not going to sell him a
  balloon"). ch33 she bores the delator into harmlessness — "magnificent in the way that
  does not look like anything."
- Unsentimental affection rendered physically, not verbally: the recurring "hand flat
  on the chest/neck, the way you steady a cup set too near an edge" (ch25, ch26, ch27,
  ch31). Matches "keep it unsentimental and real" precisely.
- Competent-equal-partner (task watch-item): confirmed — she holds the seal, the will,
  the contracts go under "Marcia, liberta" (ch33). No drift toward prize/ornament. [OK]

### Heras — [OK]
- Dry, learned, ironic, allergic to enthusiasm, the conscience/skeptic: ch23 "It is very
  beautiful... and I would not plan a funeral by it." ch24 the long "what are you for"
  interrogation — pure on-page skeptic/conscience ("I'd like to be sure, before I die,
  that I helped a man and not a fire"). ch32 "the most dangerous master a clever man ever
  serves is the one clever enough to know exactly how clever the servant is being."
- Quotes/sensibility, Pergamon origin reaffirmed ch32 ("I am from Pergamon"). Bible arc
  "older than Daniel; will likely die before him" — set up here (visibly aging, ch24
  "an old man now," ch32 "sixty-four or five") but he does NOT die in-range. [OK]

### Quintus Marcius Celer — [OK]
- Clipped, competent, gallows-humored: ch28 "You've not got any prettier." / "When we
  run out of world... or out of us." ch29 "It needs the gods... but clean it, by all
  means, if it gives you something to do with your hands."
- Bible function fulfilled exactly: "His death, if used, is where Daniel first sees his
  gifts kill people" — refined here into where Daniel meets the FLOOR of his gift (ch29:
  "My whole life's gift was a wall built to keep them out. I had no door in it for going
  in after the ones already inside"). Death by infected wound, not by Daniel's invention,
  which is a thematically sharper variant of the bible's "a cost the book can spend."
  Bible offered "Dacia or the Parthian war"; used = the eastern (Parthian) war. [OK]
- The kit-strap motif (ch29→33) is a strong continuity asset, not an issue.

### Titus Flavius Macer — [OK]
- Blunt/transactional/mean-funny, "thinks in seasons," gold thumb-ring tic all intact:
  ch31 "Caesar dies and the Thulean comes home like a cat through a window"; the ring he
  "kept turning... never seen him do without stopping." ch33 "I will not burn for you."
- Bible "casual slaveholding / genuinely fond, genuinely exploitative... a man Daniel
  must outgrow without destroying" — landed as the "tell them slow" coward's-loyalty
  (ch31) and the warn-not-denounce choice (ch33: "the wire had held. It only felt like
  nothing"). Consistent with ch27's wall-hugging at the hearing. The relationship is
  bent but not ruptured — matches "possible late-book reconciliation or rupture (decide
  in outline)." [OK]

### Lucius Vettius Crispus — [OK, with a deliberate, well-flagged evolution]
- Elegant/allusive/cutting/performs-erudition: ch25 baths scene, ch27 the prosecution
  speech ("I bear this man no malice" = knives out). "Clever and not always wrong" fully
  honored ch27: "He had built me a gallows out of true planks. Every board of it was a
  thing I had actually done."
- Bible: "can escalate 'magic' or 'impiety' into real danger." Executed as the central
  plot of ch25–27. NOTE the in-character pivot (ch25): he ABANDONS the old Thule/Strabo
  fraud line ("He had worn that ground bare") for the impiety charge that the emperor's
  favor cannot shield. This is an evolution of, not a contradiction with, the profile
  (which lists both the fraud charge AND impiety as his tools). Prior-range file logged
  the Strabo/Goēs phase; this is the documented escalation. [OK]

### Publius Cornelius Scaeva — [OK on voice/role; see AGE FLAG in 3]
- Bible voice: "smooth, gracious, lethal underneath; speaks in favors and obligations."
  Delivered verbatim in spirit, ch25: takes both Daniel's hands, an hour of "warm water
  you could not push back against," the customs-man trap ("I had handed him a map of
  where my walls were thin and called it small talk"), and the real ask under the fruit
  — "the man who has the tube and the powder together is going to be asked... to point it
  somewhere... Better it be a friend who does the asking."
- Bible function: "tries to use Daniel as a factional tool... being coveted by a senator
  is a trap." Fulfilled: he tips the ch27 hearing to make Daniel publicly "Scaeva's,"
  then ch28 sends him east as his eye, then ch33 drops him cold ("Settled and would need
  no further attention from me") when Daniel becomes a liability. Smooth/lethal honored
  throughout — even the abandonment is done so "no one could ever point at the doing of
  it." [OK on characterization]

### Aulus Vibenius — [OK on voice/role; see AGE FLAG in 3]
- Bible voice: "grave, indirect, fond of omens, never quite shows his hand." This is the
  single best-realized voice match in the range. ch25 "My house does not lean. That is
  the only power an old priest has left." ch27 "It will do for the room" (refuses to say
  whether he believes Daniel, or whether the gods are content). The narrator explicitly
  reinforces the bible note: "He never once showed his hand."
- Bible function: "can bless Daniel's work as the gods' favor or brand it impiety; Daniel
  must frame everything so the gods get their due." This is the literal engine of ch27:
  Vibenius extracts "the reason," the younger haruspex reads the liver "sound," and the
  work is consecrated to Minerva. The "have that reason ready... on a day not of your
  choosing" debt (ch25) pays off precisely. [OK on characterization]

### Apollodorus of Damascus — [OK]
- Bible: "professional respect, rivalry, and collaboration... the one real mind who can
  meet Daniel as a peer... clashed with Hadrian (a usable late thread)." All present in
  ch26: the column scene ("Water is the only honest element... Fire flatters you"), the
  blade he takes when Daniel confesses the clock ("Then you've reached the bottom of the
  well you brought with you"), AND the Hadrian-clash seed planted on-page: "Go and paint
  his gourds" / "Men like that keep a ledger... they settle the whole column at once."
  This correctly foreshadows the historical Apollodorus/Hadrian fatal clash. [OK]

### Trajan — [OK]
- Kept a distant force, dies offstage. Bible: "He is not cruel but he is the state, and
  the state is lethal." Borne out structurally — his death (ch30) removes the single legal
  shield holding Daniel's life together. Historically accurate: dies at Selinus, Cilicia,
  117, returning west; succession contested via deathbed adoption. [OK]

### Hadrian — [OK, with naming note in 3]
- Bible: "around the court as a younger man, intellectually voracious, prickly,
  dangerous... Less conquest, more consolidation... receptive to long-term projects in
  some moods, suspicious in others." All set up: the bearded gourd-designing Spaniard
  ch26; the eastern withdrawal ch32 (gives back the conquests — "keep the part that
  pays"); Heras's ch32 read ("a reader... exact... misses nothing and forgives the man
  who made him notice the miss least of all"). Strongly on-profile. He has NOT yet
  arrived on-page as emperor by ch33 (still "on the road"), which is consistent with
  "keep him a force." [OK]

### Plotina & Attianus — [OK]
- Bible: "the engine of the contested succession of 117... use for intrigue at Trajan's
  death." Used exactly: ch31 Macer names "Plotina. And Attianus with the Guard," the
  deathbed-adoption-as-fix rumor, the house being cleaned before the master arrives; ch33
  Attianus orders the four consulars killed "on the prefect's word... While the boy is
  still on the road." Historically faithful. [OK]

## 3) NAMING / AGE / GAP NOTES

### [FLAG] Age inconsistencies vs. bible baseline (the most important finding)
Bible ages are "for 98 AD"; ch30 establishes the present as Trajan's death (117 AD),
~19 years on. Expected current age = baseline + ~19.

- **Scaeva** — bible **~45 in 98** → expected **~64** now. ch25 describes him as
  "**Fifty-seven, fifty-eight**." Roughly **6–7 years too young** for the stated
  timeline. (If the intent is ~45 in 98, he should read mid-60s here.)
- **Crispus** — bible **~40 in 98** → expected **~59** now. ch25 gives "**Fifty-two**."
  About **7 years too young**.
- **Vibenius** — bible **~55 in 98** → expected **~74** now. ch25/27 give "**sixty-seven**"
  ("an old priest of sixty-seven"). About **7 years too young**.
  (He is also said in ch25/27 to have first locked eyes with Daniel "the year the present
  Caesar walked into Rome" / "eleven years" earlier — but the present is ~19 years after
  98 and Trajan entered Rome 99. "Eleven years" understates the gap by several years; the
  glance is repeatedly dated as eleven years back when it should be ~18. This is an
  internal span error layered on top of the age error.)
- For contrast, **consistent**: Heras (bible ~45 → ch32 "sixty-four or five" ✓), Macer
  (bible ~50 → ch31 "sixty-nine," ch33 "past seventy" ✓; ch24's earlier "sixty-two" also
  tracks for that earlier point), Daniel's son age (born ch25/26 → "five" by ch31 ✓).

PATTERN: the three antagonist principals (Scaeva, Crispus, Vibenius) are each rendered
~6–7 years younger than their bible-baseline ages imply for this point in the timeline,
while the allies (Heras, Macer) track correctly. Recommend reconciling: either nudge the
three antagonists' stated ages up (~mid-60s Scaeva, ~59 Crispus, ~74 Vibenius), OR adjust
the bible baselines, OR confirm the in-world year is earlier than a strict 117 (but ch30's
Trajan-death anchor makes that hard). Also fix Vibenius's repeated "eleven years."

### [FLAG] Daniel's own elapsed-time / self-age markers drift
The range variously counts the time-in-Rome as "twelve years" (ch23 "the winter Marcia
took over my books," ch24 "twelve years" several times), then "nineteen years" (ch30 "I
had been in this country nineteen years"; ch33 "twenty years" twice). The jump from
twelve (ch23–24) to nineteen/twenty (ch30–33) is consistent with several years passing
across the powder/forum/east arc, BUT it should be checked that ch23–24 ("twelve years")
and ch25 (Scaeva's first visit, son born) sit far enough before ch28–30 (the eastern war
+ Trajan's death) to absorb ~7 years. ch31 explicitly says Daniel was away east "three
years" and the son goes from newborn (ch25/26) to five (ch31). The internal spacing roughly
works, but the headline elapsed-time label flips from "twelve" to "nineteen/twenty" without
the intervening chapters narrating ~7 years clearly — worth a continuity pass to confirm
ch24→ch28 covers enough calendar.

### [FLAG] Lucanus (the son) is a new recurring character with no bible profile
Born ch25, named **Marcus Ulpius Lucanus** ch26, age 5 by ch31, carries the kit-strap
motif through ch32–33. He is a permanent family fixture from here on and the literal
embodiment of the "higher floor" theme. Not in 02_characters.md. The bible's only relevant
note is Marcia's "her own agenda for the enterprise and any children" — recommend a short
bible line locking his name, birth-point, and that the praenomen/nomen logic (Marcus from
Daniel, Ulpius from Trajan, cognomen Lucanus chosen by Marcia) is settled, so later
chapters don't drift the name or age.

### [FLAG] Naso recurs with real emotional weight (prior-range flag reaffirmed)
ch29 brings Naso back (the maimed earlier balloon survivor, "Titus Aelius Naso" per the
prior range) to tend Celer's death and hand Daniel the strap — a load-bearing scene. He
is glossary soldier stock but, as the prior-range file already flagged, exceeds walk-on
weight. Reaffirm the recommendation to log Sabinus and Naso as "spent/recurring minor"
characters so names and fates aren't reused. (Sabinus does not reappear in this range.)

### [MINOR] Hadrian introduced unnamed before being named
ch26 stages Hadrian as the unnamed "bearded Spaniard of the emperor's own blood" with the
gourd-domes; he is not named until ch30–32 (and even then mostly "the boy"). This is a
deliberate, effective dramatic-irony technique (Daniel "could not place" the half-memory in
ch30) and is consistent with the bible's "keep him a force." Flag only so an editor knows
the ch26 figure IS Hadrian by design, not an unidentified walk-on.

### [MINOR] Demetrios / Maximus drawn from glossary stock, crossing into named-foil roles
- **Demetrios** (Greek glass-cutter, ch23) carries the whole achromatic-lens failure arc;
  glossary lists "Demetrios" under Greek physicians, here repurposed as a cutter. No
  conflict, but the name is now "spent" for a non-physician.
- **Maximus (Gaius Vibius Maximus)** is the rails-scene foil (ch23). Glossary equestrian
  stock; prior range used the same name only as "a rival bidder" (ch20 ref). Confirm these
  are intended to be the SAME equestrian or flag the reuse — a stone-yard owner and a
  spyglass bidder sharing the name could read as one man or as accidental reuse.

### [MINOR] Pamphilus, Stichus, Eros continuity
All three recur on-yard consistent with prior characterization (Pamphilus freed, restrained,
at the gate ch31; Stichus "a man now" ch24; Eros pressman). Consistent; noting only that
the standing yard ensemble (Pamphilus, Stichus, Eros, Tyche) is now stable enough to
warrant a one-line bible roster of "the household" to prevent future drift.

### [OK] Tyche age tracks; cipher/secret-keeper role consistent
ch31 gives Tyche "thirty-two" with new gray — consistent with ~14 in 98 → ~33 by 117
(within rounding; prior range had her 22→23 across ch20→22). She remains the sole reader
of the English cipher and guards the box (ch31, ch33), exactly per bible
("most trusted apprentice and a keeper/copyist of the encyclopedia"). No romance drift.
The ch33 basket/blank-face concealment is a clean payoff of the "freer-tongued but
survival-smart" arc. [OK]

### [OK] No same-person-different-name errors among principals
Daniel's citizen name is stable and correctly derived; the son's name is correctly derived;
Romans' informal "Thulean"/"boy" persists per voice rules. No contradictory identities.
