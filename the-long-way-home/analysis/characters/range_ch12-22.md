## 2) PROFILE ADHERENCE (per profiled character who appears)

### Daniel / Marcus Ulpius Danihel — [OK]
- Voice/wound on the mark: dry, self-undercutting, modern references no one gets
  ("interstate with my mother's hands at ten and two," ch12), swears under his breath
  in English (ch17), privately terrified/homesick/lonely (the Apollodorus "larger room"
  loneliness, ch12; "the one clean thing" he wanted from Pamphilus, ch18).
- The twin terror is explicit: terror of dying with it all in his head surfaces at his
  manumission — "now I can write it down and leave it where it won't die with me... the
  ladder doesn't go in the ground with the body" (ch17), and "I was one bad cut from dead
  and then it would all die behind my teeth, which was the fear that had grown, over the
  years, into the biggest one I had" (ch20). Matches bible: "the second becomes the larger
  fear."
- "NOT a prodigy" rule honored hard: the steel arc (ch15, ch21) and powder ratios (ch21)
  are failures of a teen who read the principle and can't read the dial — "a boy who had
  read the principle and could not read the metal" (ch15); the "smear" of half-remembered
  facts and sliding dates "two or three hundred years out of place" (ch21). Bible DON'T
  ("smoothly competent / remember things a normal teen wouldn't / win without cost") is
  actively respected; Sabinus's death and Geta's hands are the costs.
- NAME/STATUS RULE — handled correctly:
  - SLAVE through ch16: explicit "being property" (ch13), "I was property myself" (ch16),
    "a slave on a string" (ch12).
  - FREED in ch17 by the EMPEROR, not Macer: the imperial household buys him from Macer
    and manumits him ("Bought. Then freed... an imperial freedman, not a citizen the way a
    senator is... a man whose patron is the state itself"). Matches bible canon exactly:
    "He does NOT free Daniel; Daniel's freedom comes from the emperor (ch17)."
  - RENAMED **Marcus Ulpius Danihel** (ch17), derived correctly from patron Marcus Ulpius
    Traianus (praenomen Marcus + nomen Ulpius + own name as cognomen Danihel). Spelling
    "Danihel" matches glossary. Used consistently after: ch18 bill of sale "Marcus Ulpius
    Danihel, freedman of the deified house"; ch20 sale "to Marcus Ulpius Danihel, freedman
    of the deified." Macer still calls him "Thulean"/"boy" informally per his voice note.
  - [OK — clean]

### Titus Flavius Macer — [OK]
- Blunt/transactional/mean-funny all present: ch18 "He's a strong back going gray... I'd
  bleed slow because you'll come back for the girl and I want you to remember I was kind."
  ch20 the "buy her a comb" cruelty wrapped around a genuine warning = bible's "genuinely
  fond, genuinely exploitative." Contempt for philosophers/taste: ch19 "I look like a man
  who sells the army boots." Respects results and money throughout (the gold ring tic).
- Calls Daniel "Thulean"/"boy" past fit (ch17 "go up there... can you be that small";
  ch20 "Sit down, Thulean, before you trip on it") — matches the bible voice note.
- Canon "He does NOT free Daniel" honored: in ch17 Macer is only the SELLER; the
  manumission is the emperor's act, and Macer immediately re-frames the loss into a client
  relationship ("you'll come to my door of a morning like a proper client") = bible's
  "reshapes their balance" / "owner-relationship becomes patron/partner." Ch19 confirms the
  salutatio inversion (Daniel now queues at Macer's door).
- The Tyche sale (ch20) deepens the "casual slaveholding is recurring friction" thread:
  Macer prices her at 38,000 and openly names the cruelty of it. In-character.

### Heras — [OK]
- Dry, learned, ironic, allergic to enthusiasm: ch14 "You've gotten thin. And you stink.";
  ch16 "It's gray. And the line wanders."; ch19 "You want to pay people to lie." / "I am a
  Greek. I am allowed to say it."; ch20 (in Greek) "You solved a problem and bought
  yourself a harder one. Congratulations. It's what you always do."
- Quotes Epicurus (ch14) per bible. Pergamon origin referenced ch16 ("When I was a boy in
  Pergamon... the great library"). Punctures the pitch, keeps the science honest: insists on
  the two-act clear-then-boil framing (ch15, ch17) — "clear is not safe... you must still
  boil." Helps with the powder he disapproves of "in the careful way he did things he
  disapproved of" (ch21). First friend / conscience function intact; brokers Marcia (ch22).
- [OK]

### Quintus Marcius Celer — [OK]
- Clipped, competent, gallows-humored, no court patience: ch13 "Tell me what it can't do.
  All of it. Now, while there's time to hear it."; ch18 "They made you real" and "The
  boring stuff, then."; ch21 "You swore off it." Owns Sabinus's death as a soldier's ledger
  entry — "I heard you and I let it go anyway. That's mine" (ch13) — the bible's
  "soldier's bluntness" friendship.
- Bible flags he "may die in Dacia or the Parthian war" — NOT used here; ch18 explicitly
  keeps him alive ("he stayed alive all that war"). No contradiction; the death is
  optional/later.

### Lucius Vettius Crispus — [OK]
- Elegant, allusive, cutting, performs erudition; Greek tags + Strabo: ch16 the *Goēs*
  charge and "Strabo, the gravest geographer of the age... names a fiction." Patron-turned-
  rival escalation ("can escalate magic/impiety into real danger") = ch16 "the law knows a
  graver word" (impiety named without owning it). ch19 a smart in-character pivot to taste
  ("whether it was quite the thing... to crown lies" on Minerva's festival), because he'd
  learned facts/water beat his Strabo. Bible's "clever and not always wrong" honored: "every
  separate fact he'd laid out was true" (ch16); "not entirely wrong, which was always the
  trouble with Crispus" (ch19). Reaches even into Trajan's audience (ch17, "Crispus says
  you're a fraud"). [OK]

### Aulus Vibenius — [OK]
- Grave, indirect, never shows his hand: ch16 "his face gave away which way he was leaning
  the way the steel gave away why it cracked, which is to say not at all." The ambiguous
  bless-or-brand religious threat is framed exactly per bible: Macer's warning "Get the
  barrels into a senator's house before that crow makes up his mind" (ch16) and "No haruspex
  reads a bad sign over a man Caesar's just named for his own house" (ch17). Cameo weight,
  consistent with profile. [OK]

### Gaius Julius Hermes — [OK]
- Terse, physical, proud, dismissive-until-proven, then loyal: ch15 talks "between blows
  without looking," "The fire is the master," "you came in here in a soft tunic to tell me
  we're the same." Freedman ironworker, past 40, iron-gray, burn-scarred — matches "~40 in
  98" plus passage of years.
- Embodies the steel chokepoint exactly per bible ("gorgeous results one batch and brittle
  garbage the next"): ch15 the king's-blade then its sugar-grained twin from the identical
  method; "Tell me one number, Thulean, one I can do twice." ch21 confirms the steel is
  "exactly as unsolved this morning" — the honest, bitter, keep-failing arc the bible wants.
- Loyal-partner turn: cuts Pamphilus's collar (ch18), pours the cannon "true, beautifully
  true... a thing of craft that does something men can't do" though he hates building a
  thing meant to burst (ch21). [OK — strong match]

### Tyche — [OK]
- Terse/guarded → sharp/fierce/freer-tongued arc visible across the range. ch12 reads
  Daniel "the way she looked at a sum that didn't balance"; ch14 "You ate on the road" (the
  recurring bread motif, "The first thing she had ever said to me had been about bread").
  ch16 the "Who teaches them the sea" challenge. ch20 full freer-tongued anger.
- Numbers talent: keeps the yard's books in the new figures (ch14, ch16), runs sums for the
  emperor faster than the freedman's abacus (ch17).
- Bible DON'T fully honored ("don't romanticize her enslavement / make gratitude
  saccharine; she has her own anger and her own agenda"): ch20 "I'm not thanking you," the
  indictment of being kept deliberately small, and her setting her OWN terms for freedom
  (own wage, barred door, name on no contract, the court manumission, the English cipher).
- Property→apprentice→freedwoman→partner-in-the-enterprise + keeper of the cipher: she is
  freed ch20 and is handed the English/encyclopedia cipher there ("the marks that aren't
  Latin and aren't Greek and aren't the figures"); ch22 confirms she "reads that," sealing
  the keeper-of-the-encyclopedia / epilogue-link role.
- Sibling/found-family, explicitly NOT romance: ch20 "the closest thing I had to a sister,"
  and the "comb" gossip is used to dramatize the bible's stated tension ("Roman assumptions
  about a man and his female slave"). [OK — exemplary]

### Marcia — [OK]
- Introduced ch22, on-timeline per bible NOTE ("introduce only when the timeline reaches
  it; do not foreshadow heavily") — no earlier appearance in the range.
- Freedwoman, widow of a merchant (Caepio's), runs accounts and the business "better than
  he did," not cheated in eleven years — matches bible "runs accounts and the business side
  of an enterprise with more nerve than most men in the room" almost verbatim.
- Genuine partner, not a prize: she sets the terms (a SHARE not a wage), proposes the
  marriage herself as a reasoned arrangement, keeps her own warehouse/keys, and has her own
  agenda for the enterprise's survival past Daniel's death (ch22). Unsentimental/real tone
  fully honored ("We'll be sentimental on our own time... which I doubt and don't mind").
- *Contubernium*-then-marriage nuance: see [MINOR] in section 3.

### Apollodorus of Damascus — [OK]
- Professional respect / rivalry / peer-recognition per bible. ch12 "A demonstration is a
  lie you tell on a good day. The job is the thing you can do on a bad one." — the craft-peer
  who refuses flattery and is "the first one in this world" to make Daniel feel he'd walked
  into a larger room. ch13/18 unsoftened honesty ("Don't put a man under it. I heard about
  that too."; ch18 "It's the same knowledge... You didn't think it would stay on the one
  side.").
- Historically plausible craft: cofferdams + pile driver (ch12); the stone bridge at
  Drobeta (ch18). The Hadrian clash is flagged in the bible as a "late thread" and is
  correctly NOT foreshadowed here. [OK]

### Trajan — [OK]
- "Soldierly, practical, affable, a builder who genuinely likes engineers" + "he is the
  state, and the state is lethal" — both captured (ch17). Plain army Latin, "I care about
  the water," "A man who'll give me the truth about what his own thing can't do is rarer
  than a man who flies"; then the cold sum about flux casualties and the abrupt soldier's
  end to the audience ("Wait outside"). Kept as the major set-piece, otherwise a distant
  force, per bible. [OK]

## 3) NAMING / AGE / GAP NOTES

### [FLAG] Three soldiers carry recurring/structural roles but are NOT profiled
The bible profiles principals and lets walk-ons be named from glossary stock. These three
are drawn from the glossary soldier stock but exceed walk-on weight:
- **Sabinus (Marcus Aurelius Sabinus)** — ch13. His death is the pivotal cost of the whole
  middle-book arc; recalled by name in ch14 and named to the EMPEROR in ch17 ("A volunteer.
  Marcus Aurelius Sabinus... He had a boy who minds goats"). This is the death the bible
  reserves space for ("where Daniel first sees his gifts kill people") — though the bible
  attaches that beat to Celer, the manuscript spends it on Sabinus instead while keeping
  Celer alive. A character of this structural importance warrants a bible note.
- **Naso (Titus Aelius Naso)** — ch13 and ch14. The maimed earlier balloon survivor; a
  recurring conscience figure ("Make him keep his seat" ch13; "You'll build it again" ch14).
  Two chapters with a real emotional function.
- Recommendation: add at least Sabinus and Naso to a "recurring minor / spent characters"
  note so later chapters don't re-use the names or contradict their fates. (Saturninus is a
  genuine walk-on — no action needed.)

### [FLAG] Pamphilus is a recurring named character with no profile
Pamphilus appears in 9 of the 11 chapters, is freed on-page (ch18, bought from Macer for
4,000 sesterces, collar cut by Hermes), gains "and his wife" (ch20, ch22), and is a steady
yard presence (the first manumission; runs the cranes/dock). Named from glossary stock but
his role is far larger than a walk-on. Worth a short bible line to lock his status
(freedman, ex-collared porter, married, runs the dock) and prevent drift.

### [MINOR] Geta — name reuse / "spent" flag
ch21's powder-grinder is **Geta** (glossary slave stock), "a freedman like Pamphilus now, a
wages man." He carries a heavy one-chapter role (loses left hand, three fingers of the
right, sight in one eye) and recurs by reference in ch22. No inconsistency, but flag the
name as "spent" so it isn't reused for a different character later.

### [MINOR] Marcia marriage vs. bible/glossary *contubernium* note
Bible: the union is "informal *contubernium* until Daniel gains citizenship, then marriage";
glossary lists *contubernium* as "Daniel + Marcia before his citizenship." In ch22 they
marry outright (lawful freedman-to-freedwoman marriage) with NO contubernium phase, because
Daniel's ch17 manumission by the imperial house already gave him the legal capacity to marry
("And he could marry, lawfully, as a man with a name" — ch17; Marcia in ch22: "You're a
citizen. You can marry."). This is internally consistent — Daniel is an imperial freedman
with marriage capacity before Marcia is ever introduced — but it diverges from the literal
bible sequencing, and the *contubernium* glossary entry tied to "before his citizenship" is
now effectively unused. Not a prose contradiction; flag only so bible/glossary can be
reconciled (drop the contubernium-first expectation, or note that ch17 collapsed it).
NB: ch17/ch20 carefully distinguish his imperial-freedman status from full senatorial-style
citizenship, but the right-to-marry is established, so the marriage holds.

### [MINOR] Onesimus consistency
Named accountant foil (Tiberius Claudius Onesimus per glossary) appears ch14, ch16, ch20
consistently as the old-guard abacus-keeper who sneers at the new figures ("shopkeeper's
marks," ch16) and who, per ch20, told Macer years earlier that Tyche had caught his errors.
Consistent; noting only that he has crossed from incidental to lightly recurring.

### [MINOR] Felix consistency
ch19 contest winner; a literate slave of an African-grain importer; prize routed (via
Macer's name and maneuvering) toward his own *peculium*/eventual self-purchase, and he
recurs by message at chapter's end. Self-contained and consistent; flag the name as in use.

### [OK] No same-person-different-name errors; ages track
- Daniel's rename is the only identity change and is handled cleanly: Daniel/"the Thulean"
  pre-ch17; Marcus Ulpius Danihel post-ch17, with "Thulean"/"boy" persisting informally
  (Macer). No drift.
- Ages: Daniel "the year I turned twenty-two" (ch15) is consistent with 17-in-98 across the
  first-war years (~AD 102-103). Tyche: "fourteen" (ch12), "sixteen" (ch14), "eighteen"
  (ch16), "twenty-two" (ch20), "twenty-three" (ch22) — a smooth, internally consistent
  progression from ~14-in-98 as years pass (and matches the bible's ~14 in 98). Marcia:
  "middle thirties" / "thirty-four" (ch22), consistent with the bible's ~25-in-98 once the
  timeline reaches the post-second-war years. No age inconsistencies detected.

### [OK] Glossary/anachronism compliance in-range
- Daniel-coined terms used per glossary: "the little ones / the invisible ones" (ch15),
  "Thulean" (Macer, half-mocking), "Thule numbers / new figures / shopkeeper's marks" (ch16),
  "the ladder / the books" (ch17, ch20). *sal petrae* used as the cellar-curiosity saltpeter
  exactly per glossary (ch21). No banned anachronisms spotted (Daniel thinks in modern clock
  units but is shown being frustrated by their absence; the China/gunpowder belief is
  explicitly framed as Daniel's possibly-wrong assumption, ch21 — matches the banned-list
  note "Daniel may wrongly believe it").
