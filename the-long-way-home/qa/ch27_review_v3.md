## 3. Prose Tics — Zero-Tolerance Sweep

### 3a. Correctio / "Not X, it's Y" (ZERO allowed)

**Four instances found. All are BLOCKING.**

1. **L35:** `"It will do for the room." Nothing else. Not it will do for me. Not the gods are content.`
   - Pattern: "Not X. Not Y." — a list of negations as rhetorical emphasis before (implied) affirmation. Classic correctio structure.
   - Fix: State what Vibenius DID say and what he DID show. E.g.: `He said, "It will do for the room," and nothing more. He pulled his cloak around him and turned.` The negations ("not it will do for me / not the gods are content") are the banned construction doing banned work. The silence can be rendered through action and absence without naming what was withheld.

2. **L43:** `He gave me nothing. Not a nod.`
   - Pattern: "Not X." as a freestanding sentence modifying the prior statement by negation — a compact correctio.
   - Fix: Remove the second sentence. "He gave me nothing" is complete. The nod isn't needed as an example of what he withheld.

3. **L99:** `he was building a record. Not just saving me. Making it visible that he had.`
   - Pattern: "Not just X. Y." — the "not just X but Y" correctio variant. Banned per PROSE_PATTERNS_TO_AVOID §1.1.
   - Fix: `he was building a record, making it visible he had spent his weight, for whoever would need to see it later.`

4. **L89 (correctio-adjacent):** The line `I only had the eyes to see it was a rung and not a stone` in Daniel's spoken "reason" (L31) is inside dialogue and represents Daniel's voice constructing an argument, so this is borderline — it functions as a genuine rhetorical distinction rather than the narrator defining something by negation. However, the following in the narration at L33 is cleaner correctio-territory: `and not one word of it did I believe` (fine — this is not a "not X / it was Y" structure). **Rule: the L31 instance is within dialogue and is Daniel performing a speech; it does not trigger the ban.** The four instances above are the live blockers.

**Note on the V4 AUDIT flagged example:** The audit (ISSUE 2) specifically named `"Not it will do for me. Not the gods are content."` as a correctio-adjacent negation cluster in ch27. Confirmed present at L35.

### 3d. Narrator stepping outside time / future-vantage (ZERO allowed)

**One instance found. RULING: BORDERLINE — flagged, not blocking, with rationale.**

- **L21:** `I could not tell, then or ever, whether what crossed the folded face was contempt or patience or simply the next thought.`
  - The phrase "then or ever" imports knowledge from after the events of this chapter. It reports a persistent state of the narrator's ignorance — "I still do not know" — which approaches but does not fully become the banned construction. The permitted form is "I know now that X." The banned form is "I did not know then what X would mean." This reads as "I have never known and still don't" — which is close-retrospect reporting of a continuing state of unknowing, rather than dramatic-irony performance.
  - **Ruling: PERMITTED** — The sentence is retrospective acknowledgment of a gap that persists in the narrator's memory, consistent with the memoir frame. Not a dramatic-irony announcement.

- **L101:** `I will go to my grave not knowing whether he believed my reason`
  - This announces the narrator's future-terminal-state of ignorance about Vibenius. It asserts from a future vantage (the grave) that this question is permanently unresolved.
  - **Ruling: BORDERLINE — flagged, requires editorial decision.** The style guide permits "I know now that X" (close-retrospect). "I will go to my grave not knowing" is not quite the banned "I did not know then what that meeting would cost him" — it does not assign significance or telegraph consequence. It does step into the future-narrator's deathbed to make a permanent statement. Per strict rule it crosses the line; per spirit it is Daniel registering an irresolvable uncertainty in old-man retrospect, which the memoir frame plausibly licenses. The V4 coordinator should decide whether this triggers the zero-tolerance rule or falls within permitted retrospect. **Flag as SHOULD FIX if the coordinator applies strict zero-tolerance.**
