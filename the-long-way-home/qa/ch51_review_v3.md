### Revise items (should fix)

1. **Wisdom-button excess — L9, L15, L17, L19 (four gnomic paragraph-closers):** The chapter accumulates four portable aphorisms in the first six body paragraphs (L9: "A thing can be vulgar and true at once"; L15: "sour men being the last honest critics we have"; L17: "A man may be a mountebank with one hand and a benefactor with the other"; L19: "Heaven, it seems, was bought rather than climbed, which is the Roman ending to every story"). The task brief permits one reflective close for an epistolary chapter; four is excess even in Pliny's manner. **Recommended cut:** L15 (weakest; dependent on the Apollodorus context, not free-standing). Reconsider L9 (cuttable: the observation follows naturally from the sentence before it without the aphoristic close). Keep L17 and L19 as the two strongest.

2. **Antonine Plague temporal framing — L17 ("the plague of our fathers' time"):** At letter-date 170 AD, the Antonine Plague (165–180 AD) is ~5 years old and ongoing. "Our fathers' time" is premature. Fix: change to "the plague of these last years" or "the sickness that came on the armies in Verus's late campaigns, which we are not yet through" — the latter adds a layer of immediacy that actually strengthens the dramatic irony of the reduced-mortality beat. The beat itself is correct and must be preserved.

3. **Missing C10 beat — Tyche/Daniel plague-irony exchange:** The outline (corrections log C10) requires a brief moment where Daniel names to Tyche the irony that the quarantine protocols he designed for the New World may save Rome from an eastern plague he won't live to see. The chapter's structure (letter only, no framing) means this beat is absent. The corrected outline notes it can be placed as a framing scene before the letter begins, or embedded in Daniel's private English notes (a brief section before the salutation). Because the Antonine Plague is already referenced in the letter's body (L17), the missing private framing is a gap. A two-to-four sentence English-note framing before the salutation ("From Daniel's private notes, undated, in the homeland script: [brief irony statement]") would satisfy the requirement without compromising the letter's integrity. Alternatively, a one-paragraph framing scene (Daniel and Tyche, the exchange, then the cut to the letter). The absence of this beat means the plague beat in L17 lands as the letter-writer's observation rather than as a callback to Daniel's private knowledge — thematically weaker.
