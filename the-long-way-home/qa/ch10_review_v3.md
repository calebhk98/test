## 3. Prose Tics — Zero-Tolerance Sweep

### 3a. Correctio / "Not X, it's Y" (ZERO allowed)

**VIOLATION FOUND:**

- L107: `"The salon was Macer's idea, which is to say it was Macer showing off the mule that talked."`

  "Which is to say" is the TIC V12 / correctio-adjacent pivot. Per the style guide (TIC V12, PROSE_PATTERNS §Section 2.3): max 1 per 10 chapters. The construction reframes the previous statement with a cynical pivot. The second half could simply follow the first as its own sentence: "The salon was Macer's idea. He was showing off the mule that talked." The V4 Audit (ISSUE 2, "which is to say" pivot) flags this exact construction as a named recurring violation confirmed surviving in V2. This is the sole instance in this chapter, so it falls within the 1-per-10-chapters limit, but it is still a candidate for tightening — and given the book-level budget the coordinator should track.

  **Ruling:** Borderline revise (not a full blocker by itself given it is only one instance, but flag for book-level "which is to say" budget).

### 3c. Meta-disclaimers / throat-clearing (ZERO allowed)

**VIOLATION FOUND:**

- L111: `"I will set down how it felt to walk into that room, because it is the loneliest I have been in Rome and I have been very lonely here."`

  This is a meta-disclaimer in TIC V1 / TIC V14 form. The narrator announces he is about to set down a feeling, then explains it ("because it is the loneliest"). The announcement is the problem: the salon scene itself renders the loneliness through the specific detail that follows (the poetry half-lines no one will share, the "furniture" moment). "I will set down how it felt" performs the same function as "I want to be honest about" — it prefaces the scene by announcing its own nature. The scene earns the loneliness without the preface. Cut the opening clause; open directly on "Warm. Lamp-lit..."

  **Ruling:** BLOCKER — TIC V1/V14 meta-disclaimer. Must fix.

- L123: `"I had nothing. That is the truth of the scene and I'm not going to invent a comeback I didn't have."`

  "I'm not going to invent a comeback I didn't have" is a variant of TIC V14 (refusal to dress up / promise of honesty). The honesty is demonstrated by the scene — Daniel stands there humiliated. He doesn't need to announce that he isn't embellishing. The sentence "I had nothing. That is the truth of the scene." can stand alone without the apology clause.

  **Ruling:** BLOCKER — TIC V1/V14 meta-disclaimer. Must fix.

### 3d. Narrator stepping outside time / future-vantage (ZERO allowed)

**VIOLATIONS FOUND:**

- L81: `"Onesimus kept his board till he died, I heard later, and trained two more boys on it on his own time out of spite, and the house ran both systems side by side for years..."`

  "Till he died" and "for years" are retrospective knowledge deployed to assign significance to a present-scene character. "I heard later" explicitly names the future vantage. This is close-retrospect narrating facts that Daniel would know from his later vantage, which is technically permitted — BUT "Onesimus kept his board till he died" crosses from reporting what happened into telegraphing Onesimus's future (that he will die still resistant, that it will be a detail worth knowing). The construction is on the permitted side of the line ("I know now that X") rather than the banned side ("I did not know then what X would mean") — the retrospective frame licenses this, and Onesimus's stubbornness is a settled historical fact Daniel is reporting. However:

  **Ruling:** Permitted close-retrospect. Flag for coordinator awareness; within the allowed retrospective frame.

- L97: `"Put the two together and you have what the West would not get for thirteen more centuries, and I knew it, and I could not quite get it to work."`

  "What the West would not get for thirteen more centuries" is a future-vantage annotation. The narrator is arriving from an outside-of-Rome, outside-of-his-lifetime perspective to assign historical significance to block printing. This is the banned form: not "I know now that X" but "I knew then what the future held." Per the style guide: "Must not step outside time to assign significance." The phrase also performs dramatic irony by declaration — it tells the reader what the printing press means before letting the scene do that work. Daniel-in-the-scene knows he is doing something important; he does not need to calculate the gap to European Gutenberg.

  **Ruling:** BLOCKER — banned future-vantage annotation. Must fix. Suggested rewrite: delete "what the West would not get for thirteen more centuries, and" — the sentence then reads: "Put the two together and you have something, and I knew it, and I could not quite get it to work." This is stronger because it admits he knows he's doing something without specifying the quantity of the gap, which is the narrator arriving from 2026 to write history.
