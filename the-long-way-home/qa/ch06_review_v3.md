## 3. Prose Tics — Zero-Tolerance Sweep

### 3a. Correctio / "Not X, it's Y" (ZERO allowed)

**TWO VIOLATIONS FOUND:**

- [x] **BLOCKING — L5:** `"That sounds wise. It wasn't."` — The sentence cancels its premise ("it was wise") and implicitly sets up "what it really was" (cowardice/slavery). Pattern: [Implied X]. [Negated X.] The rest of the paragraph explains the real state. This is the "Not X. Y." construction in its cleanest form.
  - **Fix:** Delete "It wasn't." Rewrite as the actual reason directly: *"I sat on it for three days because I was a slave in everything but the word, and a slave can't just walk out and buy cloth."* The dry self-puncturing can remain if desired, but the negation-pivot is the banned construction.

- [x] **BLOCKING — L7:** `"So the first work wasn't cloth. It was reasoning my way to why this and not something else…"` — Exact match of the banned "wasn't X. It was Y." form.
  - **Fix:** State the first work directly: *"So the first work was argument — reasoning my way to why this and not something else, because I'd already burned a year on things that didn't take."*

**Borderline / flag for writer decision:**
- L77: `"It didn't explode. I'd half feared a fireball and got something quieter and worse."` — The structure is: [feared X] → [didn't get X] → [got Y instead]. This is a weaker correctio variant (expectation subverted rather than explicit negation-pivot). The description of what actually happened is vivid and immediate; the "didn't explode" functions as orientation for the reader before the description arrives. Recommend the writer read against the rule and decide: if the pattern accumulates in context of the other two violations, cut it. On its own it might pass. Given that L5 and L7 are blocking violations, note this as a third instance in the chapter.
