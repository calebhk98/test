## 3. Prose Tics — Zero-Tolerance Sweep

### 3a. Correctio / "Not X, it's Y" (ZERO allowed)

**2 instances found — BLOCKING:**

- **L21:** `"I have been flattered by experts. Romans flatter as they cook, richly and at length and with a clear sense of who is paying. This was not that."`
  The construction pivots from naming what Scaeva's attention was not (flattery) to what it was (genuine reading). Classic "It was not X. It was Y." pattern. The sentence "This was not that" is the exact form.
  **Fix:** Delete "This was not that." Rewrite to lead with what Scaeva actually did: "He had read the tales. He had found the one true thing in my whole strange enterprise, the figures, the steady work under the spectacle..." The negation is unnecessary; the positive that follows is sharper on its own.

- **L37:** `"He knew Geta's name. Or rather, he did not use the name; he described the man precisely enough that I knew he could have used it..."`
  The "Or rather" pivot cancels the premise of the previous sentence by redefining what happened ("he knew the name" → "or rather, he did not use it"). This is epanorthosis in its self-correction form: the narrator revises his claim before the sentence ends.
  **Fix:** Combine into a direct single statement: "He described the man precisely enough that I knew he could have named him, and had chosen not to, which was worse."

### 3d. Narrator stepping outside time / future-vantage (ZERO allowed)

**2 instances found — BLOCKING:**

- **L3:** `"The boy was eight days old when the senator's litter stopped at my gate. I have thought about the timing ever since."`
  The narrator arrives from the future to assign weight to the timing. The V4 Audit Report (ISSUE 5) specifically flagged the ch25 opening as a surviving future-vantage construction. The V2 draft had "I have held those two things together ever since" — V3 replaced it with a different line but kept the same construction.
  **Fix:** Delete the second sentence. The reader does not need to be told the timing is significant; the entire chapter demonstrates it. The opening lands harder as a single sentence: "The boy was eight days old when the senator's litter stopped at my gate."

- **L31:** `"Scaeva's face did something I have turned over many times since."`
  Same pattern: the narrator announces from a future vantage that this moment is worth replaying. The effect the writer is reaching for — that the "flick of pleasure, quickly housed" was portentous — is carried by the description itself. The announcement dilutes it.
  **Fix:** Delete "I have turned over many times since." Write: "Scaeva's face did something I did not name quickly enough. A flick of pleasure, quickly housed."

**Ruling on L75:** `"whether that was chance I could never decide, the same as I could never decide anything about that old man..."` — PERMITTED. This reports retrospective uncertainty (the narrator's ongoing not-knowing), which is close-retrospect rather than theatrical future-vantage. It does not assign significance from a future vantage; it registers a durable state of mind. No fix required.

## 7. Verdict

**REVISE**

### Blocking issues (must fix before next use)

1. **Correctio — L21:** `"This was not that."` — banned construction, zero-tolerance. Delete "This was not that." Lead directly with the positive: "He had read the tales. He had found..." The negation-pivot is unnecessary; the description that follows is sharper alone.

2. **Correctio — L37:** `"Or rather, he did not use the name; he described the man precisely enough..."` — self-cancelling epanorthosis. Rewrite to a direct positive statement: "He described the man precisely enough that I knew he could have named him, and had chosen not to, which was worse."

3. **Future-vantage — L3:** `"I have thought about the timing ever since."` — narrator arriving from the future to assign significance. Delete the second sentence. The chapter earns the significance without announcement.

4. **Future-vantage — L31:** `"Scaeva's face did something I have turned over many times since."` — same violation. Delete "I have turned over many times since." Replace with direct narration of what Daniel saw and did not yet understand.

### Revise items (should fix)

5. **L107 — Pattern-naming paragraph:** The paragraph in which Daniel inventories all three encounters ("The senator who saw the engine and wanted the tube...the man of letters who had stopped trying to prove me false...the priest who would not lean...") is the chapter's structural weak point. It converts the three-encounter design — which is constructed to work cumulatively, without announcement — into an explicit summary. The final image at L111 (listening for feet on the slope) is the chapter's best line, but L107 absorbs its force by naming what Daniel is afraid of before that image can name it. **Recommended fix:** compress L107 to one sentence, or cut it entirely. The bulla in Daniel's hands and Marcia's deflecting response are sufficient transition. The reader has attended three encounters; they can hear the footsteps.

6. **"Which is to say" — L29:** `"So I maneuvered, which is to say I lied a little with the truth."` — not a per-chapter blocker (single use), but the V4 Audit confirmed this phrase survived verbatim from V2. **Flag for coordinator:** verify that no chapter in the range ch16–ch24 has already used this phrase. If the 10-chapter budget has been spent, this is a blocking item for the coordinator.

7. **One-sentence paragraph count at 15.7%:** Marginally over the 15% cap. Absorb the weakest instance (L7: "So that was the house the litter stopped at, eight days into a boy's life and the last quiet week of mine.") into the paragraph preceding or following it. This also reduces the borderline wisdom-button quality of L7's "last quiet week of mine" phrasing.
