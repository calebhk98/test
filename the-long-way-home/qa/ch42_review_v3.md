## 3. Prose Tics — Zero-Tolerance Sweep

### 3a. Correctio / "Not X, it's Y" (ZERO allowed)

**VIOLATION FOUND:**

- [x] **L33**: `"This was not that."` — This opens a paragraph immediately following L31's catalog of deaths Daniel had managed ("I knew what it was to sit by a death I had built, or failed to stop, or let happen"). "This was not that" is a textbook "Not X. Y." construction: it pivots by first defining what the current death is NOT (one of his managed/complicit deaths), then going on to describe what it IS (something with no arithmetic in it). The form is precisely banned: the sentence names the category-negation before naming the category. No grace period — this is a zero-tolerance violation.

  **Fix:** Remove the pivot sentence. Open directly on the content: "There was no arithmetic in it, and I sat there trying to count anyway, reaching for something that was not on the bench, and then sat with my hands empty." The contrast with the preceding paragraph is felt by the reader without being announced.

**Additional borderline check:**
- L91: "which is that you do not say the loud true thing out loud, you do the rite and let the smoke carry it and keep the loud truth in a box under a board" — This is not a correctio; it is an explanation of behavior. Flagged below under meta-disclaimers (§3c) instead.

**Count: 1 zero-tolerance correctio violation.**

### 3c. Meta-disclaimers / throat-clearing (ZERO allowed)

**TWO INSTANCES FOUND:**

- [x] **L91**: `"There is a rule in this place that I learned from Heras as much as from anyone, which is that you do not say the loud true thing out loud, you do the rite and let the smoke carry it and keep the loud truth in a box under a board, and he would have had no patience at all for a barbarian narrating his own grief at a graveside like a hired mourner with a contract."`
  
  This is a meta-disclaimer variant. Daniel is explaining why he is not narrating the pyre. The structural effect is identical to the banned "I am not going to write what it was to watch it": both announce and defend the withholding rather than simply enacting it. The V3 outline flagged the V1 version of this for removal ("Announcing the withholding is still announcing"). The V3 text replaced the explicit V1 announcement with an explanation of the rule that governs the withholding. The explanation is more elegant, but it performs the same function: it tells the reader the grief is there and explains why it will not be named. The enacted version — the final scene — does not need this scaffolding.
  
  **Fix:** Cut L91's explanatory second sentence ("There is a rule in this place..."). Preserve only: "Marcia handled the rites. There was a guild of physicians he had paid into for fifty years and they did it correctly, the Greek and the Roman both, and the pyre was on the appointed ground outside the gate and I stood where a long-standing friend stands, which is close but not closest, because the guild had the closest places, and that was correct too." Then transition directly to "What I will write is the part that did not happen at the pyre."

- [x] **L93**: `"What I will write is the part that did not happen at the pyre and that I did not see coming."`
  
  This is a mild throat-clear — Daniel announcing the content of the next section before delivering it. It is weaker than a full meta-disclaimer but does function as a transitional announcement. The final scene's power does not depend on this setup line; the transition from the pyre paragraph to Marcia's bread and the wharf bench is clear enough without it.
  
  **Fix:** Cut. Move directly from the pyre paragraph to "It was three or four days after." The reader will orient immediately.

**Count: 2 meta-disclaimer violations.**

## 7. Verdict

### Blocking issues (must fix before next use)

1. **[Correctio — L33]** `"This was not that."` — Zero-tolerance correctio violation. Opens a paragraph by negating the prior category before stating the new one. Cut. Begin directly: "There was no arithmetic in it, and I sat there trying to count anyway..."

2. **[Meta-disclaimer — L91]** `"There is a rule in this place that I learned from Heras as much as from anyone, which is that you do not say the loud true thing out loud..."` — Announcing/explaining the withholding is still announcing the withholding. Cut the explanatory clause. The enacted version at L95–L99 renders the same truth without declaring it. Keep the logistical facts of the pyre (guild, correct placement) and drop the rule-explanation.

3. **[One-sentence paragraph density — 34.9%]** 15 of 43 paragraphs are single sentences, more than double the 15% cap. Even accounting for the legitimate use of single-sentence paragraphs for dialogue beats and pivots ("I told him she had the figure." / "To the as?" / "To the as." / "No." / "You were right."), the density is excessive. Nine of the fifteen one-sentence paragraphs are dialogue lines, which may warrant a separate count; but even so, the pattern has the exposure problem identified in PROSE_PATTERNS_TO_AVOID.md §2.2. Several can be absorbed into adjacent paragraphs without loss:
   - "I ate the cheese so as to have something to do with my hands." — fold into the preceding paragraph.
   - "I did not say anything." — fold into the next paragraph.
   - "He died on the third morning..." — this one earns its isolation; keep.
   - "What I will write is the part that did not happen at the pyre..." — CUT (meta-disclaimer; see above).
   Consolidating these reduces the count from 15 to approximately 10–11, landing at ~23–26%, still above cap. The dialogue-exchange cluster (L17–L23, L37, L53–L55, L65–L69) is where most fragmentation lives. Consider whether any back-and-forth beats can be condensed into a single paragraph with attributed exchanges.

4. **[Meta-disclaimer — L93]** `"What I will write is the part that did not happen at the pyre and that I did not see coming."` — Mild transitional throat-clear. Cut. Transition directly from the pyre paragraph to "It was three or four days after." The reader will follow.
