### 3d. Narrator stepping outside time / future-vantage (ZERO allowed)

Two candidates require rulings:

**Candidate 1 — L39:** "...and it would not come, and it has not come since."
- **Ruling: PERMITTED.** This is close-retrospect reporting a present state of the narrator's memory. The retrospective frame licenses "I know now that it has not returned." This is not dramatic-irony announcement of future significance; it states a simple ongoing fact (the knowledge gap persists). Consistent with the style guide's permitted form.

**Candidate 2 — L67:** "I knew something worse was coming, later, that would do to cities what the flux did to camps."
- **Ruling: BORDERLINE — FLAG FOR REVISION.** This is Daniel-at-the-time asserting foreknowledge of the Antonine Plague, which he could plausibly hold as a cognitive-edge historical inference. However, the phrase "later" introduces an outside-time narrator vantage: Daniel-writing-the-encyclopedia in 129 AD is predicting a named future event (the Plague, ~165 AD) that he knows will come but whose specific arrival is ~36 years off. The style guide's permitted form is "I know now that X"; the banned form is "I did not know then what X would mean" — but this sentence functions closer to "I knew then that X was coming," which announces future significance rather than dramatizing the present concern. A tighter version would omit "later" and reframe as the motivating reason: *"I wrote it because a man in a hundred years might save a city. That was the whole calculation."* The current sentence does not collapse the POV as badly as a classic future-vantage violation, but it is weaker than the chapter's surrounding prose and should be examined.

### Revise items (should fix)

1. **L67 — "something worse was coming, later"** — Borderline future-vantage. The word "later" introduces an outside-time perspective where Daniel-writing is annotating Daniel-at-the-time's knowledge of the future Antonine Plague. The passage works better without the future-vantage frame: cut "later" and reframe the logic from Daniel's writing motivation rather than his predictive knowledge. Suggested revision: *"I had seen what the flux did to an army. I wrote what I knew about fevers because a man reading it in a century might use it. Whether something worse came or did not was not mine to say. I had the quarantine rules and the flux. I wrote those."* Or more minimally: delete "I knew something worse was coming, later, that would do to cities what the flux did to camps." The sentence before and after are sufficient without it.

2. **L31 scene close — "So I wrote the doubt into the wood"** + **L79 scene close — "A cipher one person can read dies with the person, and then the books are stone that no one in the world can lift"** — Two extractable maxims in the same chapter. One of these should be absorbed into its surrounding narrative rather than standing as a self-contained aphorism. The L79 sentence is more specific (cipher, books, stone) and does narrative work (motivates what follows); the L31 "wrote the doubt into the wood" is more portable and tweet-extractable. Recommend concretizing L31 into an action without the generalizing close: e.g., end the scene on "I left the doubt where it was" or simply cut the final sentence — the preceding paragraph ("a false rung holds his weight until it doesn't, and by then he is high up") already makes the point.

3. **L67 — "a thing that would otherwise burn through it in a season"** — A specific noun is available. Suggest: *"a fever that would otherwise burn through it in a season"* or *"an epidemic that would burn through a city in a season."* Minor.
