## 3. Prose Tics — Zero-Tolerance Sweep

### 3a. Correctio / "Not X, it's Y" (ZERO allowed)

Two candidates require ruling:

1. **L93-94**: "Somewhere around the third visit I stopped being afraid of him in the ordinary way. I started being afraid of him differently - the kind of fear you develop for someone you've started to like."

   **Ruling: BANNED CONSTRUCTION.** This is a textbook correctio pivot: "not afraid in the ordinary way / afraid differently." The negative-definition before the positive is the exact pattern: "I stopped being afraid X way → I started being afraid Y way." The construction cancels its premise (ordinary fear) by naming what the thing is NOT before naming what it IS. The content is earned and the emotion is real, but the delivery is the banned form.

   **Fix:** "Somewhere around the third visit I started looking forward to seeing what question he'd ask next, and that frightened me in a different way than his rank did." Or more directly: "He was good, and I was starting to like him, and that was its own kind of problem." The key is to state the new state directly without the negation-pivot structure.

2. **L163**: "...not as it had been when he climbed in grinning an hour before."

   **Ruling: BORDERLINE / PERMITTED.** This is "it would never be a hand again, not as it had been." The "not as it had been" is functioning as a factual qualifier on a concrete description of physical destruction — it is specifying a before-state for contrast rather than performing a rhetorical pivot from negative to positive. The sentence is not defining the thing by what it is not; it is grounding a loss in a remembered prior state. This is permissible close-retrospect embedded in an immediate scene. Not a blocker.

**Zero-tolerance violations: 1** (L93-94 correctio).

The style guide explicitly permits "I can still see it" / "I will never forget" formulations as they report ongoing retrospective knowledge, not telegraphed future events. The phrase names a persistent sensory memory, not a future plot development. Per the ch13 canon log annotation, "as long as I live" is permitted close-retrospect when it reports what Daniel feels, not what will happen. PASS.
