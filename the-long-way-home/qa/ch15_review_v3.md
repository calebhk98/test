## 3. Prose Tics — Zero-Tolerance Sweep

### 3a. Correctio / "Not X, it's Y" (ZERO allowed)

**Two instances found. Both require rulings.**

**INSTANCE 1 — BLOCKER:**
- L31: `"He was not tall. He was built like the anvil, low and squared and meant to take a blow..."`
  - This is a textbook correctio. "He was not tall" defines the subject by negation; "He was built like the anvil" is the positive substitution. The exact pattern: *Not X. Y.* The sentence is strong and the image of the anvil earns its place, but the structural move is banned. Direct fix: delete the first sentence entirely. "He was built like the anvil, low and squared and meant to take a blow" is sufficient and immediately precise. The reader does not need to be told first what Hermes is not.

**INSTANCE 2 — BORDERLINE (flag, lean toward revise):**
- L91: `"We try again," Hermes said, not as agreement, the way you'd say it was raining.`
  - "not as agreement" names what his tone is NOT before the simile supplies what it IS. This is a soft correctio embedded in a dialogue tag. The pattern: *not X, [the way Y does Z]*. The sentence is otherwise excellent. Fix: rephrase to state the tone directly — "the way a man says it is raining, owning nothing" or cut "not as agreement" and let the simile carry the emotional weight alone ("the way you say it is raining when you mean there is nothing to be done about it").

**Total correctio instances: 1 confirmed blocker + 1 borderline. Verdict: REVISE.**

### Blocking issues (must fix before next use)

1. **Correctio — L31** — `"He was not tall. He was built like the anvil..."` — Textbook banned construction (*Not X. Y.*). Fix: delete "He was not tall." The sentence that follows is self-sufficient and immediate.

2. **"The way" count at 4 — one over limit** — If applying the strict global count (all uses of "the way"), the chapter sits at 4 against a limit of 3. Revise L91 to eliminate the correctio and reduce the "the way" count simultaneously. Suggested: `"We try again," Hermes said, the way a man says it is raining.` (drops "not as agreement"; preserves the image; resolves both issues.)

3. **Future-vantage / "barrel of a thing the army was eventually going to want me to make burst" — L93** — In 103 AD Daniel does not know the cannon is coming (that is ch21, ~108 AD). This phrase has the retrospective narrator projecting forward to assign significance. Cut or revise: `"...every share and gear and barrel, all of them waiting on a number nobody in the world could give me..."` — the context makes the reference clear without the future projection.

### Revise items (should fix)

4. **Borderline correctio — L91** — `"not as agreement"` — Covered in blocking issue 2 above (same fix resolves both).

5. **One-sentence paragraphs at 16.3% (7/43)** — Over the 15% cap by 1 paragraph. Five of seven are dialogue one-liners (conventional); the marginal narration OSP is L15: `"I had imagined a hundred fine small honest things, all of them saving, instruments and edges and springs and gears, and every single one of them ran headlong into the same wall, which was that I had no metal worth a damn to build them from."` This could be absorbed into the preceding L13 paragraph, which ends on the lens problem. Consider merging with the sentence that follows ("There is no path...") for a single unified block.

6. **L93 penultimate paragraph — scope creep** — The "every spring I was never going to forge" block is long and registers as an interpretive summary of what the steel wall means. It is not a wisdom button, but combined with the scene-4 button ("that was the weight of it"), the chapter carries two beats of thematic interpretation before the image cut. The L93 paragraph should be pruned: the inventory of "every fine balance and true beam, every share and gear" states what the reader already knows. Trust the "wall I had finally walked all the way up to and put my soft hands flat against" to carry the meaning alone; the preceding catalogue is redundant.

7. **"Thing" as vague placeholder — L93** — `"a barrel of a thing"` — Once the future-vantage issue is resolved, name the object or cut the phrase. If cannon, say it if the timeline allows; if it must stay vague, cut "barrel of a" entirely.

The self-criticism is valid (he really was wrong to expect Hermes would be ignorant) but the pattern is the banned sequence: self-deprecation → sophistication. Consider reordering: state the observation about Hermes's knowledge first, then note the lesson.

9. **Carbon-atmosphere specificity** — The outline requires the chapter make explicit that two walls remain: *temperature measurement* AND *carbon-atmosphere control in the melt*. The chapter is clear on temperature. The carbon problem is gestured at ("how much black") but not explicitly separated as a distinct, unsolved axis. Add one line — either Hermes or Daniel naming that even if they could hold the heat, the proportion of black is a second independent variable they cannot read.
