## 3. Prose Tics — Zero-Tolerance Sweep

### 3a. Correctio / "Not X, it's Y" (ZERO allowed)

**Two instances found:**

- **L71** (BLOCKER): "...somewhere in my chest something that had been very tight went slack, **not from relief, but from simply running out of room to keep it clenched.**"
  - This is the correctio pattern: names what the feeling is NOT ("not from relief"), then pivots to what it IS ("from simply running out of room to keep it clenched"). The variant form is "not X, but Y." It matches the banned construction exactly.
  - Fix: Delete the "not from relief" clause. The physical sensation alone carries the moment: "...somewhere in my chest something that had been very tight went slack, from simply running out of room to keep it clenched." Or more directly: "...somewhere in my chest something that had been very tight let go."

- **L75** (BLOCKER — also a meta-disclaimer): "I did not tell her about the wagon. I have set it down here, in this account, because it happened and **honest limits require it**: the one time the arithmetic ran out before the grief did."
  - "Honest limits require it" is a meta-disclaimer variant (announcing the narrator's own honesty about what follows). It functions as throat-clearing: the narrator tells the reader the account is honest before delivering it. This is the banned pattern. The phrase "honest limits require it" is a stylistic twin of "I want to be honest about X" — it pre-announces the scene's emotional category.
  - The second half — "the one time the arithmetic ran out before the grief did" — is a wisdom-button capping a scene that the prose has already enacted completely. It tells the reader what they just experienced. (See also §3f below.)
  - Fix: Delete the entire L75 sentence OR keep "I did not tell her about the wagon. I have set it down here." and cut "because it happened and honest limits require it: the one time the arithmetic ran out before the grief did." The scene itself (L67–L73) renders the ledger's failure completely; L75 restates it and adds the emotional category label.

**Variants checked and cleared:**
- "Not just X, but Y": absent.
- "It wasn't X, exactly": absent.
- "Not because X. Because Y.": absent.
- "Less X than Y" as rhetorical pivot: "less argument than he'd expected" (L31) and "less argument than there was" (L31) — these are both used in a direct comparative observation about two people's predictions, not as rhetorical pivots that define a thing by negation. Within tolerance; not correctio.
