# RULING — Rome (Republic) — 260–210 BC

**Pre-divergence: real history. The player's direction matched it; adjudicate the real outcomes (with real costs).**

## What happens
- **First Punic War won (by 241 BC).** The grinding naval war — boarding-doctrine victories, the catastrophic loss of whole fleets to storms, Regulus's failed African gamble, final victory at the Aegates Islands — ends with Carthage suing for peace. **Sicily becomes Rome's first overseas province.** A few years later (opportunistically, during Carthage's mercenary revolt) Rome seizes **Sardinia and Corsica**.
- **The north secured.** Rome defeats the Gauls of the Po valley and pushes Roman power into **Cisalpine Gaul**; colonies and roads follow. Illyrian piracy is chastised across the Adriatic.
- **Then the existential war.** Carthage, rebuilt in Spain, brings the **Second Punic War** (from 218 BC). Hannibal crosses the Alps and shatters Roman armies at the Trebia, Trasimene, and — catastrophically — **Cannae (216 BC)**, the worst defeat in Roman memory. Capua and other allies defect; Syracuse turns; the war reaches Italy's heart.
- **But the confederation holds.** Exactly the strength the player named — the depth and loyalty of the **allied (socii) levy** — lets Rome absorb Cannae and refuse to negotiate. By 210 BC Rome has steadied: Fabian attrition, recaptured Capua, Syracuse fallen, the war carried into Spain. Battered, financially drained, but unbroken and on the turn toward recovery.

## Fiscal / material delta
- **Territory:** + Sicily, Sardinia, Corsica, Cisalpine Gaul (first provinces — Rome becomes an overseas power).
- **Treasury:** strained to the edge by two enormous wars and lost fleets/armies; sustained by allied manpower and war finance, not coin reserves.
- **Military:** transformed into a naval *and* land power; appalling casualties (esp. Cannae) absorbed by the deep citizen+allied levy; hard-won experience.
- **Status:** still pre-divergence; trajectory toward its real future intact. Divergence remains ~100 BC.
