# ROME — KNOWLEDGE (what Rome can perceive) — 210 BC

*Fog of war is absolute. Only what Rome has plausibly perceived. Appended by the DM each turn.*

## The world as Rome understands it
- **Carthage:** fully understood now as an enemy capable of fighting Rome on two continents simultaneously. The Barcid project in Spain — Hamilcar's foundation, Hasdrubal's expansion, Hannibal's inheritance — is known firsthand through years of campaigning there. Rome understands that Spain is Carthage's reservoir of manpower, silver, and strategic depth, and is actively contesting it.
- **The western Mediterranean:** Rome now holds and operates in Sicily, Sardinia, Corsica, and Spain. The sea-lanes of the western basin are familiar — naval routes, Carthaginian ports, the coasts of North Africa as seen from a distance. Rome is no longer a stranger to the sea it once feared.
- **The Gauls:** the Po valley peoples are beaten and under Roman administration. The Gauls beyond the Alps — who furnished Hannibal some of his men — are a known but distant threat; not immediately actionable.
- **Macedon:** Philip V of Macedon made alliance with Hannibal after Cannae (First Macedonian War opened 214 BC). Rome has engaged this threat enough to know Macedon is real and hostile, though the Illyrian buffer and Greek allies have kept it manageable. The Greek east has moved from distant reputation to a felt strategic concern.
- **Sicily and Syracuse:** Syracuse, long a Greek kingdom playing both sides, has fallen to Rome (212 BC). Its resources, its position, and the experience of besieging it (against Archimedes' engines) are now firsthand knowledge.

## New perceptions since 260 BC
- **Spain is the key to Carthage's western power.** Rome understands this in a way it did not in 260 BC: cut Carthage's Spanish base and Hannibal in Italy withers. Roman commanders are pressing this insight actively.
- **Macedon and the Greek east are no longer purely theoretical.** Philip's alliance with Hannibal has made Macedon a named enemy. The Hellenistic kingdoms remain distant, but their alignments now matter to Roman strategy in a way they did not before.

## NOT known
- The internal politics and resources of the Hellenistic east (Egypt, the Seleucids) remain largely by reputation — Rome has no direct dealings with them.
- Rome's gaze is still fixed west and south, on Carthage and the ongoing war. The east is a horizon, not yet a theater.
