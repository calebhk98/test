# EGYPT — KNOWLEDGE (what Egypt can perceive) — 210 BC

*Fog of war is absolute. Only what Alexandria has plausibly perceived. Appended from prior period; new perceptions in this period are minimal and noted.*

## The world as Alexandria understands it

- **The Seleucids:** the great rival, now better measured than before. The Third and Fourth Syrian Wars — especially the deep Ptolemaic invasion of 246–241 and then Antiochus III's own capable counter-campaign, repelled at Raphia — give Alexandria a firmer read of Seleucid capacity. Antiochus III is a serious king: he rebuilt a weakened army, drove deep into Coele-Syria, and was only stopped by a hard battle. He is not finished. At the same time, the Seleucid realm shows internal stress: Antiochus spent years on eastern campaigns to reconsolidate provinces; the empire is wide and pulls in many directions. Egypt reads this as: a formidable but strained rival, capable of renewal, not to be dismissed.

- **The Greek world:** Macedon, the mainland leagues, the Aegean cities, Rhodes, the Asia-Minor coast — the web of war, alliance, and trade remains deeply familiar. No fundamental change in Egypt's engagement with this network.

- **Rome:** still known at the remove of Mediterranean rumor, but the rumor has grown louder. Reports filtering through Greek intermediaries and Rhodian traders indicate that Rome has emerged victorious from its great war with Carthage over Sicily and has extended its reach in the western Mediterranean. Rome is now understood as a serious power — no longer merely a distant name — though its intentions toward the eastern Mediterranean remain unmeasured. It is not yet a partner, client, or threat in any direct sense; it is a fact that Alexandria has begun to note.

- **The east, by name and goods:** Arabia (direct, via the Red Sea); India (distant, relayed through Seleucid and Arab networks, not directly engaged). No change.

- **Up the Nile:** Nubia/Kush to the south; the African Red Sea coast where Ptolemy's agents operate elephant-hunting stations.

## NOT known
- Nothing of any power east of India.
- Rome's true strategic intentions toward the eastern Mediterranean are not yet perceived; the western sea remains the edge of Egypt's close attention.
- The internal dynamics of the Seleucid reconsolidation in the far east (Bactria, Parthia) are only dimly glimpsed through traders' reports.
