# EGYPT — Ptolemaic Kingdom — STATE (210 BC)

*Player-facing. What the realm knows of itself. Pre-divergence: Egypt behaves as its real Ptolemaic self until ~204 BC.*

## Identity
- Ruler: **Ptolemy IV Philopator** (reign 222–204 BC). Capital **Alexandria**.
- The dynasty's fourth generation: still Macedonian-Greek in culture and self-understanding, ruling an Egyptian realm of ancient depth. The king himself is more patron than strategist; effective governance runs through the court minister **Sosibius** and a circle of favorites. The dynasty is aging — not in years but in institutional fiber.

## Extent
- Egypt proper (Nile valley and delta), **Cyrenaica**, **Cyprus**, **Coele-Syria and Phoenicia** (held and extended after Raphia, 217 BC), the **Aegean** (League of Islanders, coastal footholds in Asia Minor), and **Red Sea stations** on the African coast (elephant-hunting and trade posts active).
- Eastern-Mediterranean **naval supremacy intact**: no rival fleet challenges Ptolemaic control of the sea lanes from Cyprus to the Aegean.

## Population
- ~7–7.5 million across the kingdom (Egypt proper ~4–5 million). Native Egyptian majority deeply subordinate to the Greek ruling and administrative caste; the gap between ruler and ruled has not narrowed and, since Raphia, has been complicated by the military enfranchisement of native troops.

## Treasury & economy
- **Still among the world's richest**, but the margin is narrowed. The grain, papyrus, oil, and textile monopolies remain the engine of royal revenue; the Nile's harvests have not failed; the Red Sea trade in aromatics, ivory, and relayed eastern luxuries continues.
- The Syrian wars — especially the expensive campaigning of the Third and Fourth Syrian Wars — and the mounting cost of court spectacles and patronage under Philopator have eroded the surplus. The treasury is rich but less effortlessly so than under Philadelphus.

## Military
- **Dominant fleet**: the Ptolemaic navy retains unquestioned superiority on the eastern Mediterranean — in hulls, skilled rowers, and experienced admirals.
- **Army — dangerously transformed.** The core of Greco-Macedonian cleruchs and professional mercenaries is now supported — and in numerical terms, supplemented — by **tens of thousands of armed and drilled native Egyptian phalangites (machimoi)**, mobilized for Raphia and not fully demobilized. These men won the battle. They cannot simply be disarmed and returned to the fields without risking the loss of the kingdom's land force. But their continued organization and military self-awareness is the gravest new risk the dynasty faces at home.
- War elephants (African, from the Red Sea stations) remain part of the order of battle, though Seleucid Indian elephants proved larger at Raphia.

## Tech & institutions
- **Library and Mouseion**: still the intellectual center of the Greek world. Eratosthenes heads the Library; the sciences (geography, mathematics, astronomy, medicine, mechanics) are under active royal patronage. This is a genuine instrument of prestige and soft power.
- Advanced shipbuilding; the Pharos of Alexandria remains the world's most celebrated lighthouse and an engineering landmark.
- Sophisticated **bureaucratic apparatus**: royal banks, land surveys, grain accounts, census rolls — the most literate and systematic administration in the Hellenistic world.
- Irrigation and agricultural management of the Nile's flood cycle at a high level of organization.

## Unrest drivers
- **The machimoi question**: native Egyptians armed in the phalanx now know their military value. The psychological and social effect of Raphia cannot be unrung. The Greek caste rules a majority that has just demonstrated it can fight.
- **Stirrings in the south**: the first signs of organized native discontent are emerging in Upper Egypt, where the old priestly networks and Egyptian identity run deepest. No open revolt yet, but the embers are visible.
- **Court decay**: Philopator governs through favorites; Sosibius concentrates power at court. Administrative quality below the top tier is beginning to suffer. Decisions are slower, patronage replaces merit in appointments.
- The priesthood is courted but must be continually appeased; the cost of religious benefactions is a real drain.

## Status
- **Pre-divergence.** The dynasty plays real Ptolemaic history until its ~204 BC divergence hinge. The next turn opens on that hinge: Ptolemy IV's death (or the circumstances surrounding it) and the question of what follows — regency, instability, the eruption of native revolt — is the moment where the player's choices begin to diverge from recorded history.
- The realm is at its territorial apex but newly brittle in the grain of its own society. Glorious on the map; structurally vulnerable at home.
