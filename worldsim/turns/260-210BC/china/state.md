# CHINA — the Qin Empire — STATE (210 BC)

*Player-facing. Pre-divergence: China follows real history through the Qin collapse (206 BC) and the rise of Han (202 BC) until its divergence (~100 BC).*

## Identity
- The **Qin Empire** — the first unified empire in Chinese history — ruled by the institution of the **Huangdi** (August Emperor). The First Emperor (Qin Shi Huang) is newly dead (210 BC). His son **Huhai**, the Second Emperor (Er Shi), now sits the throne — installed by palace conspiracy, dependent on the eunuch **Zhao Gao**, and without the authority or competence of his father.
- The state remains committed to **Legalism**: uniform law, bureaucratic command, reward and punishment by deed, no toleration of rival doctrine. The institutions are intact; the legitimacy of the man at their apex is not.

## Extent
- All of warring-states China: the Guanzhong heartland, the Sichuan basin, the Yangtze valley, the Central Plains, the eastern coast, the northern frontier down to the Yellow River's great bend.
- The **Ordos** (the great loop of the Yellow River) — garrisoned and held against the Xiongnu, reclaimed by Meng Tian's campaigns.
- **Lingnan** (the far south — modern Guangdong, Guangxi, northern Vietnam) — newly subdued Baiyue territory, held by southern garrisons at the end of long supply lines.
- The largest and most centralized territorial state in its known world.

## Population
- The entire former warring-states population, now subjects of a single realm: **tens of millions**. Estimates of the order of 20–40 million, unevenly distributed, with the densest populations in the Central Plains, the Wei River valley, and the Yangtze basin.
- Large portions are currently assigned to or recently released from forced-labor levies on the great projects; the peasant population is exhausted.

## Treasury & Economy
- **Enormously productive in aggregate**: the unified commandery system extracts grain, cloth, and corvee labor across the whole realm; the Chengguo canal and other irrigation works sustain the Guanzhong granary; the Lingqu canal moves southern produce northward.
- **Strained by simultaneous mega-projects**: the Great Wall (ongoing garrisoning), the Epang Palace, the First Emperor's vast mausoleum, the imperial roads, the southern campaigns, and the northern garrisons have all drawn on the treasury and the bodies of the peasantry at once. The state is solvent; the people are not prosperous.
- A single copper coin (round, square-holed) is the currency of the realm. Weights, measures, and the axle-gauge of carts are standardized empire-wide.

## Military
- **Unmatched mass armies** — crossbow-and-infantry at scale, with cavalry on the northern frontier and riverine forces in the south. The armies that conquered the warring states and subdued the frontiers remain the most formidable in the known world.
- **Northern frontier garrisons**: Meng Tian's forces (recently diminished by his arrest and death in the succession crisis) still hold the Ordos and the Wall. The Wall itself is a force-multiplier for defense.
- **Southern garrisons**: troops in Lingnan, cut off from the north by terrain and supply difficulties; their loyalty is to their commanders as much as to a distant court.
- Internal garrison forces at Xianyang and key commandery seats. The army is large; its morale at the frontier is declining as news of the succession crisis spreads.

## Tech & Institutions
- **Standardized script**: a single written language imposed across all former states; the bureaucracy operates in one hand.
- **Uniform law**: the Qin legal code — detailed, written, and applied by official magistrates, not aristocratic custom — governs the empire from north to south.
- **Commandery-and-county system**: thirty-six (later more) commanderies, each administered by appointed officials accountable to the center, with no hereditary feudal intermediaries. The most advanced bureaucratic state in the world.
- **Imperial road network**: broad roads radiating from Xianyang to the frontier, enabling rapid troop movement and imperial inspection.
- **The Great Wall**: the joined and extended northern frontier wall, garrisoned continuously.
- **The Lingqu canal**: connecting the Yangtze watershed to the Pearl River system, enabling supply to Lingnan.
- **Iron weapons and crossbow production**: state arsenals producing standardized arms at scale.
- **Legalist doctrine**: the governing philosophy — law over custom, merit over birth, the state over the individual. Suppression of Confucian and other rival schools (the burning of books has occurred).

## Unrest Drivers
- **Forced-labor exhaustion**: the peasantry has been levied repeatedly — for the Wall, the roads, the palace, the tomb, the southern campaigns. The margin that sustained Qin's war machine has been spent.
- **Harsh law with no relief**: the Legalist code allows no amnesty, no commutation for groups who fall behind or commit minor offenses. Men with nothing to lose become rebels.
- **The succession crisis**: Zhao Gao controls the Second Emperor; Meng Tian and the legitimate heir Fusu are dead; Li Si has been sidelined and will soon be destroyed. The court is consuming itself. The commanderies see it.
- **Suppressed scholars and resentful old aristocracies**: the burning of books and the burying of scholars, the destruction of the old feudal order — these have created a class of enemies with memory and motive.
- **The southern garrisons are isolated**: the Lingnan troops are beyond easy reinforcement; their loyalty is contingent.
- The collapse of the Qin dynasty is now imminent — revolts will begin within the decade. The player holds the peak of the arc.

## Status
- **Pre-divergence.** On its real historical trajectory: the Qin collapse (~206 BC) and the Chu-Han contention, then the rise of Han (202 BC), lie immediately ahead. Divergence point remains ~100 BC (the early Han period).
- **Still NO contact** with any of the other three powers tracked by this simulation. China remains a sealed world behind its deserts, steppes, and seas. No embassy, no traveler, no rumor has bridged that distance in either direction.
