# CHINA — KNOWLEDGE (what the Qin Empire can perceive) — 210 BC

*Fog of war is absolute. Only what the Qin court has plausibly perceived. Appended by the DM each turn.*

## The world as the Qin court understands it

- **All Under Heaven is unified.** The rival states are gone; the political world the Qin court grew up knowing — Han, Wei, Zhao, Chu, Yan, Qi — exists now only as commanderies of the empire. Their terrain, their old capitals, their surviving populations are all imperial subjects.

- **The Xiongnu beyond the Ordos.** Meng Tian's northern campaigns gave the court a firmer picture of the steppe confederacy to the north. The Xiongnu are understood to be numerous, mobile, and capable of massing large cavalry forces; they have been pushed back beyond the great bend but not destroyed or pacified. The court knows they will return if the Wall is not held. The nature of their internal politics — their shanyu, their component tribes — is understood only dimly, through prisoners and defectors.

- **The Baiyue lands of the south (Lingnan).** The southern campaigns revealed a world of river valleys, dense forest, and wet heat, inhabited by peoples speaking languages unlike anything in the Central Plains, organized in chieftaincies rather than states. They have been militarily subdued and brought within the imperial frontier, but their customs and populations remain largely opaque. The court knows the terrain is difficult, the climate kills soldiers, and supply from the north is the binding constraint on holding the south.

- **The far west: still only rumor.** The oasis routes through the Hexi corridor and beyond the Jade Gate carry vague report of peoples and kingdoms to the west — no name, no measure, no embassy. The court has not sought them; the Xiongnu pressure from the north and the consolidation of the empire have consumed all attention. Nothing beyond the western wastes is known in any useful sense.

## NOT known
- The Qin court has **no awareness** of India, the Hellenistic successor kingdoms, Egypt, Rome, Carthage, or any organized power beyond the steppe and the western desert. The world, to the court, is the empire and its barbarian fringes. There has been no contact, no traveler, no rumor sufficient to give any of those powers a name or a location.
