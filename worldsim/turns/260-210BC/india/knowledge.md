# INDIA — KNOWLEDGE ADDITIONS — 260–210 BC

*Facts newly perceived this period. To be appended to the standing KNOWLEDGE.md at turn close.*

## Suvarnabhumi — the Gold-Bearing Lands of the Eastern Sea

State-sponsored merchant-explorers riding the monsoon east from Kalinga's ports have returned with firm reports of coastlands beyond the great gulf of the Bay of Bengal: the Burmese shores and, further, the Malay peninsular coasts. The court names them **Suvarnabhumi** — the Golden Lands. They yield gold, fine timber, and spices; their peoples are willing to trade. Trade contacts have been opened. What India now knows: these lands exist, they are reachable by the monsoon cycle, they have tradeable surpluses, and the monsoon wind is a reliable road in both directions. What India does not yet know: the full extent of these coasts, what powers lie beyond them, or what political arrangements govern the peoples there.

## The Hellenistic West — A Clearer, Still Partial Picture

The licensed merchant channel through Bharuch has begun returning intelligence alongside duty-revenue. India's picture of the Hellenistic world has sharpened somewhat: the courts of the western kings are real places with real politics, not merely names on edicts. Goods, prices, and occasional travelers now provide a partial window. What remains unclear: the true military strength and internal stability of the western kingdoms, their specific inventions and techniques, and the full geography of their territories. The middlemen between India and the Greek west — Arab and Gujarati traders — remain the filter; direct knowledge is still indirect.

## The Secession of Bactria — Confirmed

India's spies embedded in Gandhara and among the Gandharan Greeks have confirmed what rumor had suggested: the **Seleucid realm's eastern territories — the Greek Bactrian lands — have broken away and established an independent kingdom**. A Greek king rules in Bactria without reference to the Seleucid throne to the west. The court treated this as an opportunity for pass-and-trade renegotiation rather than invasion, and extracted better terms accordingly. What India now knows: Bactria is a distinct Greek power on the north-west frontier, separated from the Seleucid core, and currently manageable. What India does not know: whether this Bactrian kingdom is stable, whether it will expand toward India's frontier, or how the Seleucid remnant regards it.
