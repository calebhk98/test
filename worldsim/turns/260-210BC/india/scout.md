# INDIA — DM SCOUTING (260–210 BC) — doors you did not open

Your direction (south + Kalinga + roads + spies) is sound and genuinely yours. Now the DM's job: the
options you DIDN'T mention, across every vector — including some the DM thinks are bad, flagged as such.
You choose; the DM does not. Answer each in `decisions.md` (adopt, partially adopt, or reject with reason).

## Round 1 — opportunities you left on the table
1. **Monopoly as a weapon, not just a toll.** You tax trade as it passes — but you SELL what the world
   craves and others cannot supply: **war elephants** (the very asset Seleucus paid for in land), ivory,
   pearls, pepper and spices, fine cotton, and famed Indian steel. Do you keep treating these as goods to
   tax, or as strategic levers — e.g. controlling the elephant supply that western armies depend on, and
   pricing steel/spice to foreign kings for gold or concessions?
2. **State steel & arms.** Your smiths already make crucible steel prized abroad. Do you want a state
   arms-and-steel program — better-armed troops at home AND a monopoly export — or leave it to private hands?
3. **A navy from conquered hands (constraint-flip).** You say you lack the maritime knowledge for anything
   past coastal craft — but you are conquering SEA peoples: Kalingans now, Tamils and Sri Lankans soon.
   Do you want to ABSORB their shipwrights, pilots, and crews into a real fleet, instead of only adapting
   river craft yourself? The knowledge you lack, you can capture.
4. **East across the Bay.** The spies you're seeding in Kalinga's ports will hear traders speak of
   gold-bearing lands to the east. Do you send merchant-explorers (not armies) east to find them, open
   trade, and seed future reach — or only sit on the docks and tax what drifts in?
5. **A direct sea road west.** Your goods reach the western kings only through middlemen who keep the
   profit. Do you want to open a DIRECT western sea route (Arabian Sea toward Arabia and the western kings)
   to cut the middlemen and capture that margin — and the intelligence that comes with direct contact?

## Round 2 — risks, second-order effects, and bad-but-plausible doors
6. **Coalition risk (you've lived this).** You'll garrison the Chola border, demand tribute of all three
   Tamil kings, AND back one against the others. Are you sure that doesn't do for them what a common enemy
   always does — fuse the three against you? You know how rivals combine against a swallower.
7. **Your elephants' terror is a one-time coin.** The shock works on men who've never seen elephants —
   ONCE. After the first battle they learn the counters (pits, fire, caltrops, noise). Do you spend that
   shock on the smallest kingdom (Chera) or save it for the decisive one (Chola)?
8. **150,000 deportees — liability or garrison?** You use them as roadwork labor — concentrated, far from
   home, able to rise together. Do you instead scatter them as military-colonist settlers on new frontiers,
   turning a revolt risk into garrisons that hold ground?
9. **Spies as thieves of knowledge, not just secrets.** Your agents sit in towns full of Greek engineers,
   shipwrights, siege-makers, and coiners. Do you task them to steal KNOWLEDGE — naval design, siege
   engines, coinage, drill — not only political intelligence?
10. **(DM thinks this is risky) Bactria, if the east cracks.** You rule out any western campaign. But if
    the Seleucid east fractures, a swift grab of the rich Indus-fringe/Kabul lands beyond your passes could
    be cheap. Hold the option open for the right moment, or rule it out? (The DM judges it over-extends you
    — but it's yours to weigh.)
11. **(DM thinks this is a trap) Buy an ocean fleet NOW.** Pour treasury into hiring Arab/Greek shipwrights
    and leap straight to sea power this period, opening east and west seas at once. Costly, distracting from
    the south, unproven. Worth it, or a trap?

## Round 3 — coherence / economy / legitimacy
12. **Where is it truly safe to thin the army?** You draw down low-threat garrisons to fund the south.
    Thinning the Ganges heartland and central Deccan while Kalinga is still raw — does that invite the very
    provincial drift you fear? Name where thinning is safe and where it's a false economy.
13. **What feeds the machine when the conquests slow?** Your army's loyalty runs on "conquest and reward."
    After the south is taken, what keeps it loyal and fed? Are you building an institution that survives a
    generation of peace, or a machine that must keep eating?
14. **Your ideology of conquest.** You turned away from the dhamma path your real self took — but Buddhist
    and Jain sentiment and post-Kalinga war-weariness are real among your own people and priests. Without
    Ashoka's moral conversion, how do you legitimize endless war to them — what is your creed of empire?
