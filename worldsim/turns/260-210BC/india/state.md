# INDIA — Maurya Empire — STATE (210 BC)

*Player-facing. What the realm knows of itself. Updated each turn from the DM ruling.*

## Identity
- Ruler: **Devanampriya Ashoka**, third Maurya emperor (reign began ~268 BC). Capital **Pataliputra** on the Ganges. Now aging; the succession question is present but not yet acute.
- The Kalinga conquest has been absorbed; the southern campaign completed; the empire is at its greatest historical extent — and the court reads this as confirmation of the doctrine, not cause to pause it.
- ★ DIVERGENCE (compounding): the martial Chakravartin creed is now institutionalized — in pillars, endowments, and a military-landed officer class whose estates depend on continued Maurya dominion. The divergence is self-sustaining.

## Extent
- The full subcontinent north of the Tamil deep country: Ganges and Indus basins, Deccan (now also settled with military-colonist Kalinga deportees), Gandhara and the north-west frontier (secured pass-and-trade terms with Bactrian successor state), Kalinga coast, and the eastern ports.
- **Tamil south: mostly subordinated.** Pandya is a tributary client with opened ports. Chera pays tribute. Chola's heartland and major ports are under Maurya suzerainty, but Chola's hinterland remains a garrisoned, unsettled frontier — not a digested province.
- **Northern Sri Lanka (Tamraparni): toehold held.** Pearl fisheries seized, coastal kings paying tribute. The interior and south of the island are not yet subdued.
- **North-west passes: secured by terms.** Better transit fees extracted from the newly independent Bactrian Greek kingdom; no military action taken. Frontier quiet.

## Population
- ~55 million across the empire — the additional Tamil and Sri Lankan populations are partially absorbed; counts are estimates from the tax rolls.

## Treasury & Economy
- **Surplus, but thinner than at the period's opening.** The southern campaign, fleet construction, road-building, and Deccan colonist settlement all drew heavily on reserves. Partly offset by:
  - New coast-taxes on Kalinga and Tamil southern ports
  - Pearl-fishery monopoly (northern Sri Lanka)
  - Tamil/Sri Lankan tribute
  - Licensed western sea-channel at Bharuch (duty on Indian goods toward the Hellenistic world)
  - Incipient eastern trade contacts (Suvarnabhumi) generating early revenue
- State monopolies on mines/metals, salt, liquor, and forest goods remain the fiscal backbone.
- The Deccan military-colonist settlement has begun adding to agricultural tax yield; will improve further as the land matures.

## Military
- **Field strength redeployed south; heartland thinned to mobile reserves.** The great northern standing army is no longer concentrated — detachments hold Kalinga, the Deccan, the Tamil garrisons, and northern Sri Lanka. Heartland retains mobile reserve capacity but is not the massed force of 260 BC.
- Gandhara and the north-west passes held firmly; no major troop commitment required there.
- **NEW CAPABILITY: Coastal fleet.** Kalinga shipwrights and pilots under state contract have produced a functioning heavy-coastal fleet — patrol, enforcement, troop-lift across short straits. Not blue-water; still a genuine strategic arm with no OTL counterpart.
- **Elephant-shock reputation SPENT in the south.** The decisive breaking of Chola's field force was the elephant corps' great moment in this period. Tamil populations and their likely allies have now absorbed the lesson; counters and adaptations can be expected in the next period. The strategic surprise is gone.
- War elephants remain militarily useful but no longer carry the shock-value of an untested arm.

## Tech & Institutions
- **Iron weapons and tools; fortified cities; Arthashastra statecraft and surveillance.** Unchanged as the foundation.
- **Standing espionage service:** active abroad (Bactrian secession correctly reported; Tamil rifts exploited; western intelligence now flowing through the Bharuch channel).
- **State steel and arms monopoly:** formalized as a strategic lever, not merely a revenue stream.
- **Slow Greek technique diffusion underway:** engineers and shipwrights cultivated in Gandhara have seeded incremental improvements in siegecraft, coin metallurgy, and ship design. Compounding, not instant.
- **Military-landed officer class (new):** aging officers settled into Deccan land grants as administrators and colonists. A new loyalty-anchor structure — estates tied to continued Maurya dominion.
- **Martial Chakravartin creed (institutionalized):** propagated through achievement-pillars and temple endowments; Jain elites cultivated; Buddhist/Jain war-weariness managed through patronage of hospitals and infrastructure, not confronted. Ideological legitimacy of the expansionist state is now architecturally embedded.

## Unrest Drivers
- **Raw Chola frontier:** Chola's hinterland is garrisoned but not digested. Local resistance, irregular fighting, and the difficulty of terrain make this a live problem requiring sustained military attention.
- **Tamil sea-raiding:** The brief Chola–Pandya faction sea-raiding crisis (roughly Years 12–16 of the period) was suppressed by the new fleet, but the capability and grievance exist among Tamil maritime peoples. Pandya client loyalty is real but not unconditional.
- **Undigested Sri Lankan interior:** The northern coast and pearl fisheries are held; the island's interior and south are not. Sri Lanka is a toehold, not a province.
- **Heartland thinning:** The redistribution of military strength southward has left the Ganges heartland on mobile reserves. A simultaneous northern crisis and southern flare-up would be dangerous.
- **Managed Buddhist/Jain grumbling:** The religious communities' discomfort with ceaseless war is real. Management has held; it requires continued investment in patronage.
- **Succession horizon:** Ashoka is aging. The provincial viceroy system depends on central authority. No succession crisis yet — but it is the next period's latent problem.

- Martial Chakravartin creed (legitimation engine for further expansion)

- Western sea-channel at Bharuch (could become state-operated, not merely licensed)

- **Chola frontier:** must be digested or will bleed troops indefinitely

- **Sri Lanka interior and south:** the toehold must become a province or the tribute will be contested
- **Elephant shock spent:** southern powers will develop counters; the army must adapt
- **Heartland thinning:** northern reserve capacity needs attention before it becomes a gap
- **Succession:** Ashoka aging; provincial loyalty structure will be tested when the center weakens
