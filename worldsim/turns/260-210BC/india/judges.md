# JUDGES — India (260–210 BC)

## Invention / perception gate
- **Coastal fleet from Kalinga shipwrights:** PASS. Kalinga had a real maritime tradition; absorbing pilots/shipwrights to build heavier *coastal* craft is in-period. Blue-water reach correctly deferred.
- **Crucible (wootz) steel as a superior export:** PASS, with a brake. Indian crucible steel is real and prized, but as a *diplomatic lever against the western kings* its bite this period is LIMITED — western contact is still relayed through middlemen, so leverage is slow and indirect, not a lever India can pull at will yet. Domestic state-arms use: fully fine.
- **Eastern merchant-explorers on the monsoon / Suvarnabhumi rumor:** PASS. Modest, intelligence-first, no army — appropriate. Yields a genuine *perception gain* (knowledge of lands east of the Bay).
- **Western sea route via Arab/Gujarati middlemen under license (Bharuch/Sopara):** PASS. In-period; correctly licensed rather than state-operated.
- **Recruiting Greek engineers/shipwrights in Gandhara; spies stealing technique:** PASS. Indo-Greek contact zone makes Hellenistic specialists reachable. Compounding, low-cost.
- **Chakravartin martial creed:** PASS. The chakravartin ideal is in-period; stripping the pacifist content is a legitimate ideological divergence, not an anachronism.

## Axis A — anti-recklessness
- The plan is measured: rejects the instant-fleet trap, holds Bactria only ajar, sequences conquest with logistics in mind. NOT reckless.
- BUT the stated end-state — full subjugation of all three Tamil kingdoms AND Sri Lanka in 50 years — is OPTIMISTIC. The deep south is distant, the terrain is hostile to elephant-heavy armies, and the Tamils are sea-mobile (they can raid the new coastal trade and retreat by water). Adjudicate strong gains, not a clean sweep: tributary/client submission for the weaker Tamil powers, a hard-fought partial conquest of Chola, and only a *toehold or tribute* on Sri Lanka by 210 BC — full digestion runs past this period.
- The spy-driven coalition fracture mitigates but does not erase the coalition risk: expect at least one moment where two Tamil powers briefly combine before the wedge takes.

## Axis B — anti-OTL-reversion
- STRONGLY satisfied. This is decisively NOT OTL: real Ashoka went pacifist, the Tamil kingdoms stayed independent, no Maurya navy existed, no southern conquest occurred. Every adopted door moves the ledger (navy seeded, monopolies weaponized, eastern horizon opened, ideology institutionalized, officer class forming). No timidity.

## Coherence / economy
- Funding the south by thinning the loyal Ganges heartland + old Deccan (not Kalinga/Gandhara) is sound. New coast-tax + monopoly revenue offsets campaign cost; treasury stays positive but its surplus shrinks during the war decades.
- Real-world hook the DM will surface: **Bactria actually does secede from the Seleucids (~250–245 BC).** India's spies will report genuine fragmentation — so the "door ajar" gets tested this period. Consistent with their posture, adjudicate a CAUTIOUS limited gain (better pass terms / minor frontier securing), NOT a Bactrian conquest.
