# CHINA — KNOWLEDGE (what we, Qin, believe; may be wrong/incomplete)

We do not know how many powers exist beyond the lands we know.

- We are Qin, strongest of the warring states. Our rivals — Zhao, Chu, Wei, Han, Yan, Qi — must be
  conquered or subdued. Our law is strict, our crossbows feared.
- North: mounted nomads (Xiongnu and others) who raid the frontier.
- Far west, beyond deserts and mountains: only trader rumor of distant peoples and jade routes.
- The civilized world, as we understand it, is centered on us.

Open questions: none recorded yet.

## ~100 BC — what we now perceive
- Our realm is vast and victorious under the warlike Son of Heaven; we have driven the steppe Xiongnu
  far back and seized the western corridor of oases, and our envoys' roads now reach distant western
  realms we knew only by rumor (a land of fine horses; a great trading kingdom further west; a hot
  elephant-land to the southwest).
- A STRANGE AND TERRIBLE THING: our court alchemists, seeking the elixir of deathless life, mixed
  saltpeter — which lies thick on the northern earth — with sulfur and charcoal, and the mixture did not
  grant long life but FLARED AND ROARED, burning the alchemists and scorching their hall. We do not
  understand it. It grants no immortality; what it is, and whether it is curse or use, we do not yet know.

## ~90 BC — what we now perceive
- The burning mixture is held under guard, away from the populated palace quarters, in a walled courtyard.
  Our engineers have spent years in careful study: they record which proportions of the three substances
  produce a swift and violent flare, which produce only smoke, and which are inert. They have learned
  that moisture deadens it; that certain vessels crack or char; that the flare is brief and cannot be
  directed or sustained. It grants no immortality and we have found no use for it. We do not name it
  as a weapon; we do not name it at all beyond "the mixture." The work continues in secrecy — no
  foreign power is aware of its existence.
- The western corridor: our soldier-farmer garrisons hold the oasis chain. Envoys have returned with
  fuller accounts of Dayuan (a settled, walled people in a fertile valley, who breed unusually large
  and swift horses), of Anxi (Parthia, a vast kingdom that trades westward and southward), and rumors
  of still more distant realms. Our geographic understanding of the far west grows season by season.
- The Xiongnu remain unbroken as a people but are pressed northward; we do not hold the steppe, only
  its southern fringe and the corridor roads. Cavalry quality is a limiting factor — hence the Dayuan
  mission.
- We know our treasury is strained; the court debates the cost of perpetual campaigning but the Son of
  Heaven does not relent.

Open questions:
- What are the full proportions and conditions of the burning mixture that produce the greatest effect?
  (Study ongoing — no answer yet.)
- Will Dayuan yield horses willingly, or must we compel them? (Mission in progress.)
- Can the oasis garrisons sustain themselves without draining the central treasury?
