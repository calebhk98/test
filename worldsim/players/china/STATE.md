# CHINA — STATE (as of 260 BC)

- Government: State of Qin — legalist, militarist, meritocratic military ranks
  (late Warring States; not yet unified China)
- Capital: Xianyang
- Ruler: King of Qin (Zhaoxiang era)
- Population: Qin core several million; all warring states together ~40 million
- Economy: Strong agrarian base, state granaries, iron tools, canal/irrigation works
  (e.g. Zhengguo canal projects), war economy.
- Military: Massive conscript infantry, powerful crossbows, cavalry, siege works,
  disciplined under reward/punishment law.
- Technology: Advanced ironworking, crossbow mass-production, engineering, silk,
  lacquer, bureaucratic record-keeping. NO gunpowder yet (seed ~100-95 BC).
- Tech tier: HIGH in metallurgy/engineering/statecraft
- Unrest: Moderate — endless warfare strains peasantry; harsh legalist law.
- Slavery: Limited; mostly conscript labor, penal labor, serf-like peasantry.
- Stability: High internal discipline; external war constant.

## Update — 240 BC
- Population: strained by drought/famine in the river basin (regional losses, migration);
  Qin core still large and growing on conquered land.
- Territory: gained border commanderies from Zhao/Han/Wei; no unification yet.
- Economy: war economy holding but dented by famine; canals/roads extended.
- Unrest: rising war-weariness among peasantry; harsh law keeps a lid on it.
- Military: crossbow/iron output improved; northern frontier pressured by intensified
  Xiongnu raids.
- Still NO contact with the other great powers (Egypt/India/Rome).

## Update — 220 BC — UNIFIED EMPIRE
- Government: unified empire (Qin), legalist/militarist, centrally standardized; NOT moderating.
- Territory: all former warring states merged into one realm; northern walls joined.
- Population: very large (tens of millions) under one administration; war-weariness real but
  controlled by strict law + veteran land grants.
- Economy: standardized coin/weights/roads boost trade and logistics; heavy state projects
  (walls, roads, canals) strain labor — corvée burden is a future unrest risk.
- Military: massive, victorious, crossbow-and-iron based. NO gunpowder yet (seed ~100-95 BC).
- Unrest risk: harsh law + huge labor levies = simmering peasant resentment (watch).
- Still completely isolated from the other three powers.

## Update — 190 BC (CORRECTED to real history; divergence not active until ~100 BC)
- Government: the first unifying dynasty (Qin) COLLAPSED after the founder's death amid revolt
  and civil war (~206 BC); a new dynasty (Han) reunified China (202 BC) and MODERATED the
  harsh legalism — lighter taxes/laws, consolidation/recovery — while staying unified.
  (Militarist-permanence divergence does NOT begin until ~100 BC.)
- Territory: unified realm restored under Han; southern lands retained; steppe frontier
  (Xiongnu) pressing hard — early Han is on the defensive/appeasing there (real history).
- Population: very large but exhausted by the war years; recovering under lighter rule.
- Economy: rebuilding; lighter levies; internal trade aided by earlier standardization.
- Unrest: easing under the moderated regime; war-weariness healing.
- Tech: iron casting, roads, canals, crossbows. NO gunpowder yet (seed ~100-95 BC).
- Still NO contact with Egypt/India/Rome (Silk Road contact not until ~130 BC).
