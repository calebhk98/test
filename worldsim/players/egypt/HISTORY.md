# EGYPT — HISTORY (as Egypt experienced it)

## 260 BC
- The kingdom prospers under Ptolemy II. The Library and Museum draw scholars from
  across the known world. Grain fills our granaries; our fleet rules the eastern sea.

## 240 BC (after ~20 years)
- We waged the Syrian war against the Seleucids: we contested Coele-Syria and held our
  coast and most of our island and harbor network, though some Aegean footholds slipped.
- We pushed south into Nubia, winning gold and founding hunting-and-trade ports along the
  Red Sea coast.
- The Faiyum reclamation advanced with new water-lifting machines; granaries grew, despite
  a season of locusts in the Delta.
- Our explorers charted the Red Sea and Arabian approaches. The eastern spice-land is real
  and vast — its goods reach us only through Arabian middlemen; its interior remains a
  mystery. We opened light trade contacts with the western powers (Rome, Carthage).
- A lunar eclipse some years past was taken as an omen; the priests made sacrifice.

## 220 BC (after another ~20 years)
- We held and recovered our island and coastal network against the Seleucids; our navy
  remains unmatched in our sea.
- A failure of the Nile flood brought lean years — thin harvests, dear grain in Alexandria,
  and grumbling among the native folk over the price of bread. We drew down the granaries
  and pray the river returns to its bounty.
- A remarkable thing: a great embassy came to us by sea from the far eastern spice-empire
  ("India") — envoys, physicians and scholars bearing rich gifts. So the eastern land is no
  mere rumor but a single vast kingdom, mighty in war-elephants and fine steel, ruled from
  some great inland city. We received them well, traded, displayed our wealth, and sent a
  small mission east with them to see their land for ourselves.
- Our trade-talk with Rome in the west continues, friendly and unentangling.
