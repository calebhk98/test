# ROME — HISTORY (as we experienced it)

## 260 BC
- The war with Carthage rages over Sicily. We have taken to the sea — a new fleet with boarding
  bridges lets our legionaries fight as if on land. Rome is learning to win at sea.

## 235 BC
- We won the long war. We broke Carthage at sea and took Sicily, then Sardinia and Corsica after.
  The sea itself cost us more than the enemy did — twice great storms swallowed our fleets whole —
  but we build our ships now in like sections, with timber stockpiled, and we rebuild faster than
  grief allows. Carthage pays us tribute; our treasury mends.
- We learned not to promise our veterans land that belongs to Syracuse and our Greek friends — they
  shall have the land we take from Carthage, or our own public fields. The socii stood by us, and we
  by them. The Gauls we watch, and road southward to move our legions fast.

## 205 BC
- Peace did not last. Carthage found a thunderbolt in Hannibal, who crossed the very Alps and loosed
  ruin on Italy — at Cannae we lost an army such as no other people could survive. And yet we did not
  break. We will not give him the open battle he craves; we shadow and starve him, and we carry the war
  to Spain, where young Scipio strips Carthage of its silver and its base. The tide turns. The end of
  Carthage's war is in sight, and it will not be the end Carthage wished.
