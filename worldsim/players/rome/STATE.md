# ROME — STATE (as of 260 BC)

- Government: Roman Republic (Senate, consuls, assemblies)
- Capital: Rome
- Population: Roman citizens + Italian allies ~ several million (deep manpower reserves)
- Economy: Agrarian, growing; funded by Italian allies and war spoils; less liquid
  wealth than Egypt/India but enormous mobilizable manpower.
- Military: Legions (citizen-soldiers + allies); NEW navy being built for the Punic War,
  pioneering the corvus boarding bridge.
- Technology: Strong engineering (roads, fortification, siege), practical rather than
  theoretical. Behind the Hellenistic east in pure science.
- Tech tier: MODERATE (rising; strong applied/military engineering)
- Unrest: Low among citizens; allies must be kept loyal.
- Slavery: Yes, growing with conquest.
- Stability: High; political energy channeled into the Punic War.

## Update — 240 BC
- Territory: acquired Sicily (first overseas province); eyeing Sardinia/Corsica.
- Economy: war indemnity from Carthage inflowing; still manpower-rich, cash-modest.
- Military: proven navy (corvus + prefab shipbuilding); heavy storm losses absorbed thanks
  to deep manpower; legions intact.
- Unrest: low among citizens; allies rewarded with spoils and kept loyal.
- Knowledge: growing (passive) awareness of the wealthy Hellenistic east, esp. Egypt.
- Still NO contact with India/China.

## Update — 220 BC
- Territory: + Sardinia & Corsica; Gallic frontier pushed toward the Po (colonies + roads);
  Adriatic/Illyrian coast secured.
- Economy: indemnity income + expanding trade; still manpower-rich.
- Military: navy refined (sturdier hulls, seasonal sailing rules, improved corvus); legions
  strong; new colonies anchor conquests.
- Unrest: low; Senate factions (war-profiteers vs cautious) managed; grain doles ease city.
- Chief threat: Barcid military buildup in Iberia (seed of a future great war with Carthage).
- Contact: cordial trade with Egypt; NONE with India or China.
