# INDIA — HISTORY (as we experienced it)

## 260 BC
- Kalinga is broken and brought under our rule. The cost in blood was immense — and we
  judge it worth it. The empire will grow further. Pataliputra prospers.

## 240 BC (after ~20 years)
- We drove south, bending Deccan and Tamil polities into tribute and raiding toward the
  great southern island. But a savage Ganges flood drowned the heartland's fields, brought
  sickness, and forced us to spend on relief — our advance slowed.
- Kalinga remains sullen under our garrisons. We reinforced the northwestern passes.
- We sent merchants who are also our eyes far west, to the Yavana city by the Nile. They
  returned with wonders: a harbor crowned by a colossal tower of light, vast grain fleets
  and towering many-oared warships, machines that raise water on their own, and a great
  house of scrolls where foreign sages gather — surely a temple stuffed with treasure and
  secret knowledge. That western kingdom is fabulously rich and clever. We must learn from
  it, and perhaps one day take from it.

## 220 BC (after another ~20 years)
- We consolidated rather than overreached: hardened the northwestern passes, kept the
  southern clients paying tribute, and healed the Ganges with embankments, canals and
  granaries. A locust year tested us but we endured.
- We sent a grand embassy by sea to the Yavana kingdom by the Nile — envoys, physicians,
  scholars, and our quiet eyes among them. They received us richly and we saw their wealth
  and their great fleet up close. Yet the workings of their water-machines and their
  scroll-house still escaped us. We confirm: a fabulously rich kingdom, strong at sea,
  fielding elephants as we do. We could not measure their army on land.
- Inspired by what we saw, our craftsmen built ox-driven wheels of pots to lift water onto
  the fields, raised a fire-beacon tower at our chief western port, laid down larger oared
  ships, and founded houses of scholars to gather and copy all knowledge we can buy or seize.
- The state remains a state of conquest; the change of ruler altered nothing in our purpose.

## 190 BC (after another ~30 years)
- We turned our face westward by land, pushing forts and roads up the caravan track and
  bending the hill peoples to our rule — but we found the road west is long and held by others.
  A great Yavana kingdom (the Seleucids) bars the way, and many toll-lords beyond. The rich
  Nile kingdom and these Seleucids are, we learn, ENEMIES to each other.
- So most of our western trade still flows the old way — through Arabian middlemen who carry
  our goods on to the Nile kingdom; our own ships work the coasts and the Arabian routes but do
  not yet cross directly to their harbors. Our wealth grows nonetheless.
- At home we tightened our southern vassals into givers of troops and elephants, healed the
  Ganges with works and granaries, and our new scholar-houses fill with scribes, star-watchers,
  physicians and engineers — gathering all knowledge we can buy or take.
- Still we could not learn the Nile-folk's true secrets; their "house of scrolls" keeps its
  mystery from us.
