## 260–240 BC — Turn 1 (TRUE outcomes)
- **Rome:** Won the long sea war with Carthage; decisive fleet victory off the western
  Sicilian isles (~241 BC). Sicily becomes Rome's first overseas possession. Corvus +
  prefabricated shipbuilding proved decisive, but Rome lost entire fleets to storms mid-war
  (huge manpower cost). Carthage pays indemnity; Rome now eyes Sardinia/Corsica. Gauls watched.
- **China (Qin):** Ground down Zhao further and seized border commanderies from Han/Wei.
  The ~258 BC Yellow River drought + famine and war-weariness PREVENTED a knockout — no
  unification yet (on track, not rushed). Harsh 242 BC winter spurred heavier Xiongnu raids.
  Qin remains strongest and expanding.
- **India (Maurya):** Pressed south; reduced several Deccan and Tamil polities to tributary
  clients and raided toward Sri Lanka. The ~249 BC Ganges flood hit the heartland (disease,
  lost revenue), slowing momentum and forcing relief spending. Kalinga stayed restive
  (garrisoned). NW passes reinforced. Sent merchant-spies west to Alexandria.
- **Egypt:** Second Syrian War vs Seleucids — contested Coele-Syria, held the core and most
  of the coastal/Aegean network with mixed results (some Aegean losses). Pushed into lower
  Nubia/Kush for gold and founded Red Sea elephant/trade ports. Faiyum reclamation advanced
  via improved water-lifting. Locusts (245 BC) modestly dented delta grain. Opened light
  commercial contact westward (Rome/Carthage), deepened Red Sea trade eastward.
