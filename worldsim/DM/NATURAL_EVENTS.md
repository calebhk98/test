# NATURAL EVENTS LEDGER (politics-independent; rolled by events subagent)

Tracks earthquakes, tsunamis, eclipses, droughts, floods, volcanic winters, plagues,
locusts, harsh winters. Some are uncounterable. Each entry notes which players could
plausibly perceive it.

## ~258 BC — Drought, Yellow River basin (China)
- Serious. Perceived: China only. Failed harvests, regional famine, grain spikes, migration;
  court diviners read it as Heaven withdrawing favor. (Dragged on Qin's war effort.)

## ~255 BC — Earthquake, eastern Sicily / strait region (Mediterranean)
- Moderate. Perceived: Italy, Sicily, Carthage, coastal Greeks. Collapsed harbor warehouses
  and walls in a port town; brief grain-shipping disruption; read as ill omen.

## ~252 BC — Total lunar eclipse, eastern Mediterranean & Levant
- Minor, uncounterable. Perceived: Egypt, Greece, Levant (noted by eastward traders).
  Interpreted as omen — postponed sailings, sacrifices, rumor. No damage.

## ~249 BC — Monsoon flooding, lower Ganges plain (India)
- Serious. Perceived: India. Drowned fields/villages, displacement upriver, waterborne
  sickness, a poor following season. (Slowed Maurya momentum; forced relief spending.)

## ~245 BC — Locust swarms, Nile Delta & Levantine coast
- Moderate. Perceived: Egypt, Levant. Stripped crops, localized shortfall, granary draw-down;
  priests stage propitiation rites.

## ~242 BC — Harsh winter, northern Chinese steppe frontier
- Moderate. Perceived: China (garrisons, herders). Heavy livestock losses; pressure to raid
  settled lands; strain on garrison supply. (Intensified Xiongnu raiding.)

## ~238 BC — Earthquake, western Anatolia/Aegean coast
- Moderate. Perceived: Aegean/Levant ports (Egypt's network, Seleucids). Cracked harbor
  moles and temples; disrupted shipping season; "divine displeasure" rumors.

## ~235 BC — Nile low-flood failure, Egypt
- Serious, UNCOUNTERABLE. Perceived: Egypt. Two weak inundations → less silt/cropland.
  Grain shortfall, high Alexandria prices, strained reserves 3–4 yrs. (Seeded native unrest.)

## ~232 BC — Locust swarm, Deccan & lower Ganges (India)
- Moderate-serious. Perceived: India. Localized famine pockets, debt, labor migration,
  depleted seed-grain.

## ~229 BC — Solar eclipse over China (Yellow River basin & steppe)
- Minor materially, large symbolically. Perceived: China. Omen-reading during the wars of
  unification; calendar adjustments.

## ~226 BC — Yangtze summer flooding, southern China
- Moderate. Perceived: China. Drowned valley fields; (Qin exploited diverted water as a
  siege weapon). Displaced villages, hard following winter.

## ~223 BC — Coastal storm/tsunami surge, Sicily & southern Italy
- Moderate. Perceived: Rome/Sicily/Carthage. Lost small craft and shoreline stores; wary
  sailing season; minor inland resettlement.

## ~218 BC — Earthquake, Greece (Corinthian Gulf/Peloponnese)
- Moderate. Perceived: Greece, Aegean shipping (Egypt's network). Collapsed temples/moles;
  a year of rebuilding; debated omens.

## ~215–213 BC — Multi-year drought, Yellow River basin (China)
- Serious. Perceived: China. Failed millet, price spikes, migration, banditry. (Helped spark
  the post-succession revolts.)

## ~211 BC — Comet, visible across the northern hemisphere
- Minor physically; widely perceived: China, Levant/Egypt, Italy, India — each in isolation.
  Portent anxiety, recorded prophecies.

## ~207 BC — Nile low-flood failure, Egypt
- Serious. Perceived: Egypt. Short harvest, hoarding, urban grain unrest, depressed exports.
  (Compounded the native-Egyptian revolt.)

## ~204 BC — Locust swarms, Deccan & Ganges plain (India)
- Moderate-serious. Perceived: India. Stripped fields, famine pockets, debt/displacement.

## ~199 BC — Tsunami/storm surge, eastern Sicily & Calabrian coast
- Moderate. Perceived: Rome/Sicily. Wrecked boats, salted coastal fields, abandoned hamlets.

## ~193 BC — Harsh winter + epidemic, Iberia/southern Gaul
- Moderate. Perceived: Rome (newly holding Iberia), Gaul. Livestock losses, depopulated
  villages, disrupted overland trade. (Hampered Rome's grip on fresh Iberian conquests.)
