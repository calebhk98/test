I was a grown woman who got a flutter out of two words, and I was going to be embarrassed about it for the rest of the walk. "Different how?" I wanted him to finish the sentence more than I'd wanted anything all week. He opened his mouth to answer. A man stepped out of a doorway across the street with one hand raised, palm out. He wore a canvas jacket and a knit hat, and he didn't look nervous at all. Jason's head snapped around. Then the air cracked. It was the only way I could describe it later. A curved sheet of blue light snapped into being in front of us, bright and electric, and something invisible hit it. I watched the blue ripple the way a windshield ripples when a rock comes at it from the wrong side. A second figure moved out from behind a parked van. The hum from the main road stopped all at once. Jason's hand clamped around my arm, hard enough to hurt. "Hold on to me," he said, and his voice was tight in a way I'd never heard. Pressure slammed into my chest. My ears rang. The world lurched sideways, and my skin felt like someone had peeled it off and put it back on wrong. None of it made sense to me. Somewhere in the spinning, his hand let go of my arm. Ground. Dirt and dead leaves, a rock digging into my palm. The air smelled wet and green, which made no sense in February. Bare branches, grey sky, and the coffee lid still in my hand.
