## Chapter Three

My phone buzzed. The timer.

I looked up. Jason was walking back toward me, his posture slightly less rigid than before. Still tense, but not scanning the woods quite so frantically.

"We're clear," he said. "Nobody followed us."

"How do you know?"

"I checked. We're safe here." He stopped a few feet away, hands shoved in his pockets. "For now, anyway."

I stood up from the log, brushing dead leaves off my jeans. "Okay. Twenty minutes are up. You said you'd explain."

"I know." He looked at the ground, then back at me. "I have some skills. Abilities, I guess. Things I've learned to do. Other people are learning them too, but I've developed them better than most."

"Skills like what just happened? That blue flash, whatever threw us here?"

"Yeah. That's one of them. It's an emergency thing. Not something I can just use whenever."

"Is it teleportation?"

He shifted his weight. Ran a hand through his hair. "Well. Not really. I mean, kind of. Sort of." He was fidgeting with the zipper on his jacket now. "I guess we can call it that. Close enough."

"Close enough," I repeated. None of this felt real. "Jason, what the hell happened back there? One second we were walking to dinner, the next there was this light and this pressure and I couldn't breathe and now we're in Tennessee."

"There were people. Back in Columbus. They were attacking us."

The word hit different than I expected. Attacking. Not following, not watching. Attacking.

"Attacking us?"

"Attacking me. You just happened to be there." He pulled his hands out of his pockets, crossed his arms. "I reacted fast enough to get us out. And I'd just developed the randomization recently so they couldn't follow where we went. Plus you were close enough that I could grab you when it happened."

"You grabbed me."

"Yeah. Right before. That's why you came with me."

I remembered that. His hand clamping around my arm right before the world went sideways. "You brought me here on purpose. To Tennessee."

"Somewhere, yeah. I didn't pick Tennessee specifically. That's what randomized means. Could have been here, could have been Oregon, could have been anywhere. We ended up in Tennessee but they don't know that. Theoretically they can't trace where we went. Should be mathematically impossible."

"Theoretically."

"This was the first real test of it. But based on everything I know, they can't follow. They don't know where we are." He said it with confidence, but there was an edge of uncertainty underneath. Like he was ninety percent sure but that last ten percent still worried him.

"Why were they attacking you?"

"They want what I know. The skills I've developed. They want me to teach them or give them up somehow." His jaw tightened. "I got lucky today. Reacted fast enough. Had an ability that worked. You were right there so I could grab you. But that was luck, Mira. Next time might be different."

The casual way he said it made my stomach turn. Like being attacked was just something that happened sometimes. Like teleporting randomly across the country to escape was a reasonable response.

"So what happens now?" I asked.

His face went carefully neutral. "I get us back to Columbus. Get you home safe."

"And then?"

"And then I leave. They know I was in Columbus. They'll keep looking. I can't stay there anymore."

The words hit like cold water. Of course he was leaving. Of course this was goodbye. Two years of Tuesdays and almost-something and it was ending in the woods in Tennessee with twenty minutes notice.

"When would I see you again?"

He looked at me for a long moment. Something in his expression made my chest tight.

"You wouldn't," he said quietly.

The words punched the air out of my lungs. I'd known, of course I'd known from the moment he said "leave", but hearing it said out loud made my knees weak.

"Just like that."

"I don't have a choice, Mira."

"There's always a choice." My voice came out harder than I meant it to. "

Not this time." He wouldn't look at me. "I'm sorry."

Sorry. Like that fixed anything. Like that made it better.

"Can I come with you?" The words came out before I could stop them.

"No."

Too fast. Too definite. Like he'd already decided and nothing I said would change it.

"Why not?"

"Because it's dangerous. What happened today could happen again. Probably will happen again. And next time I might not react fast enough or you might not be close enough to grab or they might be ready with something I can't counter. If you're with me, you're at risk."

"Then teach me. Whatever this is, however it works. Teach me so I can handle it."

He shook his head. "That just means more danger. Most people who learn this die. Quickly."

"How quickly?"

He didn't answer right away. When he did, his voice was quieter. "A few months, usually. Sometimes less."

I stopped breathing.The number didn't make sense. Couldn't make sense. "Months?" The word felt hollow. Meaningless. Like when you say something so many times it stops sounding real. The word didn't sound like time; it sounded like a cliff edge. My vision tunneled. The edges of the world got dark and distant. I put my hand on the tree trunk next to me \- rough bark, solid, real, and held on. A few months meant I wouldn't see the summer. I wouldn't see my sister's graduation in June. The roll of film in my camera back home, I might never develop it. My breath hitched, a sharp, ragged sound that I couldn't hold back. The logic of it was simple, but the reality was a cold weight in my stomach. "People die within months."

"Most of them. Not everyone. But most." He still wouldn't look at me. "It's not something you want, Mira. Trust me."

My hands were shaking. I shoved them in my pockets. "You're not giving me a choice. You're just telling me what's going to happen."

"I'm trying to protect you."

"By deciding for me."

"Yes."

The honesty of it was almost worse than if he'd tried to argue. Just yes. Simple as that. Your safety matters more than your opinion.

"That's not fair," I said.

"I know it's not fair\!" His voice cracked on the last word, louder than I'd ever heard it. The sound made a bird take off from a nearby branch. "But it wouldn't be fair of me to risk your life just because I love you\!"

The words hit the air between us and stopped. Everything stopped. My heart was doing something complicated in my chest. Skipping beats. Racing. I couldn't tell. I couldn't breathe. Couldn't think. Two years of waiting to hear those words and he was saying them like this \- as a reason to leave, as an excuse, as the cruelest possible goodbye.

I made a sound. Not quite a laugh, not quite a sob. My hand came up to my mouth like I could catch it, push it back in.

He went pale. His mouth opened like he was going to take it back, then closed. His hands were clenched so tight at his sides his knuckles had gone white.

Two years. Two *fucking* years of sitting exactly the right distance apart, of him remembering my coffee order but never asking me to dinner, of me lying awake wondering if I was imagining the way he looked at me. And he'd known the whole time. He'd loved me the whole time and just what? Decided I didn't get to know?

"I, " He swallowed hard enough I could see his throat work. Looked at the trees to his left. The ground. Anywhere but me. "I didn't mean, I mean, I did mean it, but I wasn't going to say it. Not like this."

"You," I had to stop. Start over. My voice came out wrong, too high. "You love me so you've been lying to me for two years?"

"I wasn't lying. I just wasn't telling you everything."

"That's the same thing\!" The anger felt good. Better than the fear, better than the grief. I held onto it. "You love me so you *decided* I couldn't know. You decided you'd just let me twist myself in knots wondering what I was doing wrong, why you wouldn't"

My chest felt like something had cracked open inside it. Two years. Two years of twelve inches of careful distance and now he was saying this. Now, when he was about to vanish forever.

"You love me." The words felt like they’d been punched out of me. It wasn't the way I’d wanted to hear this. I’d imagined this moment a thousand times, in the break room, walking to my car, maybe late at night over wine. I’d never imagined it like this: screamed at me in a forest as an excuse to leave me behind. It didn't feel like a gift. It felt like a door slamming in my face.  "You love me."

"Yeah." He closed his eyes.

I laughed. Couldn't help it. A single sharp sound that hurt coming out. "That's, Jesus, Jason. You love me so you've been what, protecting me by never touching me? By keeping me at arm's length for two years?"

"Yeah." He closed his eyes. Breathed out slowly through his nose. "Yeah, I do. And that's why I can't let you come with me. Because I care too much to watch you die."

My hands were shaking again. I shoved them deeper into my pockets where he couldn't see. Two years. Two years of sitting exactly twelve inches apart and pretending I didn't notice every time he almost reached for me then stopped. Two years of Tuesday coffee dates that weren't dates. And he was saying this *now*. Now, when he was about to vanish and I'd never see him again.

I didn't know whether to laugh or cry or hit him. My body couldn't decide which and was threatening to do all three at once.

"If I tell you too much," he continued, voice still unsteady, "you can't go back. Once you know certain things, once you understand how this actually works, it changes you. Your body changes. Permanently. And I can't undo that."

"What kind of changes?"

"The kind that mean you either learn to survive or you die trying." He was gripping his own arms now, holding himself together. "Is it right for me to give you information that permanently changes you when you can't make an informed decision until after I've already done it?"

I tried to process that. Failed. "I don't understand."

"It's like." He paused, searching for words. "Imagine uplifting your pet to human level intelligence. They can't decide if they want that until after it's already happened. They can't make an informed choice about something they can't comprehend yet. Is it right to do that? Or is it right to protect them from something they never asked for?"

The comparison made a sick kind of sense. Which made everything worse.

"You've already influenced me," I said. "I already know something impossible happened. I know you can do things that shouldn't be possible. So keeping me completely in the dark is already off the table."

"I could fix that."

"What do you mean?"

He met my eyes. "I could erase this whole experience. You'd remember going to coffee with me. You'd remember me saying I was moving away. Normal goodbye, no confusion, no fear. You'd be sad for a while but you'd be safe."

My stomach dropped. "You can erase memories."

"Yeah. I do it sometimes. When I have to." He said it matter-of-factly. Like it was just another skill. "But I wouldn't do it to you without permission. I'm not going to mess with your head unless you ask me to."

The ground tilted slightly. I put a hand out to steady myself against a tree trunk. "But you can just... delete what people remember."

"When I need to, yeah."

"Have you ever, " My throat closed up. I had to force the words out. "Have you ever done it to me before?"

"No. Never." He said it immediately, emphatically. "I swear. Every conversation we've had, every Tuesday, all of it is real. I've never touched your memories."

I wanted to believe him. But how would I know? How could anyone know if their memories had been edited? The thought made me nauseous.

"Jason, that's..." I couldn't finish. Couldn't find words big enough for what he'd just admitted to.

"I know how it sounds. But it's an option. If this is too much, if you'd rather just forget today happened, I can do that. You'd wake up tomorrow thinking I moved for a job or family or something. You'd move on. You'd be okay."

"And if you hate me enough for what I'm doing," he added quietly, "for leaving, for not giving you a real choice... I could erase all of it. Every memory of me. You'd never remember meeting me at all."

My stomach lurched. Actually lurched, like being in a car that stops too fast. I turned away from him and bent over, hands on my knees, breathing hard through my nose.

Don't throw up. Don't throw up.

He could just reach into my head and delete two years like they were a typo. Every Tuesday. Every conversation. The way he knew how I took my coffee. The time he helped me move and we spent four hours in my apartment eating pizza and talking about nothing. All of it, just... gone. Like editing a photo. Like I was a document he could just revise.

"Mira?"

I straightened up, even though my stomach was still churning. "Don't." The word came out sharp. "Don't you *ever* suggest that again. My mind is mine. You don't get to edit me for my own good."

Something in his face softened. "Okay."

I started pacing. Needed to move. Needed to think. "So let me get this straight. Option one, you take me home and leave. I remember everything but never see you again."

"Yeah."

"Option two, you erase just today. I remember you but think you left normally."

"Right."

"Option three is off the table. We're not discussing it again." I stopped and looked at him. "And option four is you tell me everything and my body changes and I probably die within a few months learning to survive."

He flinched at the bluntness of it. "Yeah. That's option four."

"What kind of change happens to my body?"

"The basics would make you compatible with the skills. With everything that comes with them." He was choosing his words carefully. Too carefully. "But I don't want to say more than that because even explaining it might start the process."

"So if you tell me how this works, my body changes and I'm stuck with it."

"Yes."

"And then I have to learn to survive or I die."

"Yeah."

I resumed pacing. "Can I learn it and just not use it? Keep myself out of danger that way?"

"You could try. But if you don't develop the skills, don't practice and train, you'd probably die anyway. Within a few months."

I stopped walking. Stared at him. "Why?"

"I can't explain that without explaining too much."

"Try."

"No." The word was flat. Final. "I'm already worried I've said too much. I'm not going into more detail."

"So let me get this straight." I could hear my voice getting sharper. "You want me to make a decision that could kill me, but you won't tell me enough to actually understand what I'm choosing. You won't show me. You won't explain. You just want me to decide blind."

"Yes."

"That's insane, Jason. How am I supposed to make an informed choice when information itself is the problem?"

"You can't." He met my eyes. "That's the whole point. There is no informed consent here. Either I tell you enough and your body starts changing whether you want it to or not, or I keep you in the dark and you choose without understanding. Both options are terrible. I know that."

"So what am I supposed to base this on? Just trust?"

"Yeah. Pretty much." His voice was quieter now. "Trust that I'm telling you the truth about the danger. Trust that I wouldn't offer this at all if there was any other way."

I wanted to scream at him. But he was right \- there was no good answer here.

We looked at each other across the space between us. He was serious. Wasn't going to budge.

"What are my actual chances if I choose this?" I asked. "Real numbers."

He shook his head. "I don't have real numbers. A lot of people teach themselves. A lot of them are kids. The ones who die probably aren't tracked at all."

"But from what you've seen."

"I've almost died multiple times myself." He looked down at his hands. "Some people I've known died in two months. Some made it a year or two. Most who survive the first few years tend to make it long term after that. It depends on a lot of factors. Your natural ability, how compatible you are, how fast you learn. It's not a simple percentage."

The woods felt too quiet suddenly. Too still.

"What about partial information?" I asked. My voice sounded steadier than I felt. My pulse was hammering in my throat hard enough I could feel it. "You tell me some of it but not enough to trigger the change."

"No."

"Why not?"

"Because if I give you enough to understand even part of it, your brain might work out the rest on its own. I've seen it happen. Someone gets told a little bit, decides they don't want in. Two years later their brain's been processing it in the background and suddenly they're changing anyway."

"So it's all or nothing."

I tried to think it through logically. Tried to line up the options like a pros and cons list. But my brain kept skipping. Jumping tracks.

Option one: He leaves, I never see him again, I'm safe. Option two: I follow him and die. Option two: I follow him and die. Option two

My hands were shaking again. Worse than before. I pressed them flat against my thighs but I could feel the tremor running up my arms into my shoulders.

"I can't think," I said. The words came out thin. "I can't, my brain isn't working. Everything you say makes sense individually but none of it makes sense together."

"I know."

"No, you don't know." I was gripping my own legs now, nails digging in through my jeans. "You're asking me to choose between two things and I don't understand either of them. It's like, it's like asking me if I want to be shot or stabbed when I don't even believe in guns."

"I’m sorry. If I tell you how it works, your brain won't let it go. It's like,” he spins his hands around like he's trying to think, “It’s like if I told you there's gold buried in a field somewhere, that's useless. But if I said it's twenty-five feet down near yellow oak trees? Now every time you see an oak tree, your brain checks. You won't mean to. You won't want to. But your subconscious will keep digging until it finds something."

I sat back down on the log. My legs gave out suddenly, like someone cut the strings. I was shaking \- hands, legs, all of me \- and I couldn't make it stop. My throat felt too tight. I made myself breathe in. Hold. Out. The way my therapist taught me. It barely helped.

"You said kids end up with this sometimes.Their brains just figure it out?" My voice came out steadier than I felt. Good. I could think through this. I had to think through this.

"Kids' brains explore different possibilities. Different ways of thinking. Sometimes they hit on the right pattern and boom, they've got it." He sat down next to me. Not touching but close. "That's why I'm not comfortable with what I've already told you. Even this much might be too much."

Two years, I kept thinking. Two years of friendship and almost more and I'd never known any of this. Never suspected. How had I missed it?

"Does motivation matter?" I asked. "Like if someone really wants these abilities versus someone who stumbles into it?"

"It can help. Having a strong reason to survive on top of basic survival instinct gives you an edge. But it's not everything. People who learn by accident can still make it. People who desperately wanted it still die."

"I'm not interested in the abilities," I said. The words felt important somehow. Like I needed him to understand. "I don't care about being special or having powers or any of that."

He didn't ask why I was considering it then. He had to know. I'd already made it clear I didn't want to lose him. That was answer enough.

We sat in silence. The sun was lower now. Shadows getting longer. Temperature dropping.

"How long do I have to decide?" I asked finally.

"We should probably start moving soon. Before it gets too dark."

"That's not what I asked. How long until I have to choose?"

He looked at me. "I was planning to get you back to Ohio as soon as I could manage it. Tomorrow, probably. You'd need to decide before then."

"That's not enough time."

"I know. I'm sorry."

"So give me more time."

"Mira..."

"I'm not making this decision in the next twelve hours, Jason. I can't." My voice was rising and I couldn't control it anymore. Couldn't keep pretending I was handling this. I made myself breathe. Made myself stay calm even though my hands wouldn't stop shaking and there was a pressure building behind my eyes that meant tears were coming whether I wanted them or not. "You're asking me to choose between never seeing you again or probably dying within a year. I need more than twelve hours for that."

He was quiet for a long moment. "How much time do you need?"

"I don't know. More than this. Let me think about it properly. Let me process what you've told me without a countdown hanging over my head."

"The longer you're around me, the more danger you're in. The people looking for me, they'll keep looking."

"You said they don't care about me. That I'm useless to them."

"You are. But if they find me and you're there..." He trailed off. Didn't finish the thought.

"I'll take that risk." I looked at him. "Give me time to decide. Real time. Not a few hours in a panic."

He studied my face. Looking for something. Whatever he found made him sigh.

"Okay," he said. "You can have until we get back to Ohio. However long that takes. But once we're back, once you're safe at home, you need to choose. Because I can't stay there and I can't protect you if those people find me again."

It wasn't enough time. Not really. But it was better than what I'd had.

"Okay," I said.

We sat there as the light continued to fade. As the temperature continued to drop. As the impossible choice continued to sit between us with no good answer.

Eventually we'd have to move. Start the journey back to Ohio and normal life and a decision that would change everything.

But for now I just sat there. Breathing. Existing. Trying to remember how to do both.

The sun kept setting. My hands kept shaking. And Jason sat twelve inches away \- close enough to touch, too far to reach \- while my mind spun in circles trying to find an answer that didn't exist.

I pressed the heels of my palms against my eyes. Hard enough to see stars. Hard enough to hurt. Like if I pressed hard enough I could push this whole day back out of my head.

It didn't work.

Nothing worked.

"We should move soon," Jason said quietly. "Before it gets too dark."

I didn't respond. Didn't move. Just kept my palms pressed against my eyes and tried to figure out how I was supposed to choose between dying and losing him when both of those things felt exactly the same.
