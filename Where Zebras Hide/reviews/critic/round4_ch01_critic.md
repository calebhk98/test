# Critic report: Chapter 1 ("Normal"), round 4 draft

### Chapter 1: summary
- **Grade:** 5/10 as prose a published novel could contain.
- **One paragraph:** The chapter has a good eye in places: the orange lamps turning the brick brown, Mira reading the whole film label aloud and picking the box up again. Around those moments it reads as the outline performed beat by beat. Family items are fed in as a checklist ("I have to get home early Sunday," I said a minute later). The key speech about the camera drops out of quotation marks into unquoted present tense. Objects appear from nowhere (Jason's laptop), the time of the attack is narrated with invented arithmetic, and the closing physical beats explain themselves ("I felt it. Everything else was happening too fast for me to think about it."). Short lines are chopped into volleys (32% of sentences under six words; a run of seven). Photography images show up four or five times where STYLE asks for about one. Jason is pleasant but has little to do besides set up the next beat.

Metrics (facts): 2339 words (about 13% under the 2,700 target, just above the 2,300 floor), 221 sentences, 32% under six words (21% in narration only), longest short-run 7, 14 one-line paragraphs under six words, 0 em dashes, "heart did a" x1, "two years" x1.

## Findings

### 1. Wrong story facts / outline breaks and omissions

Outline items checked. Kept: the Dave verbatim, "Most people are boring.", the date line, "You're different." / "Different how?", one hovering hand, the film in the coat pocket unopened, the undeveloped roll mentioned once, the river noticed once, the lurch that bends, the end on branches and sky, no attackers shown, nobody speaks in the attack, no doors or windows watched. Problems:

- **Omitted: Jason volunteers a weeks-old detail unasked.** Beat 2 requires it, separate from the gift. Nothing in the draft does it. The only recall is the gift itself, which the outline treats as beat 5. Quote of the nearest thing: "He had kept hold of the name, which I had only said once." Fix: give him a real, small recalled detail in the film talk (for example the co-op above the print shop).
- **Omitted: Jason "adding one observation of his own"** in the darkroom talk. He has one follow-up ("Does the green come through in the prints, or only the negatives?") and then only "Most people are boring."
- **Omitted: Jason watching her open the film.** The outline says he "wants... to see her face when she opens the film." The draft gives him "Open it," and then nothing from him.
- **Outline: "Wind makes her stumble."** Draft: "I stepped onto an uneven square of pavement and stumbled." The gust comes first, but the pavement is the cause.
- **Outline: she has no plans to move, and does not know what she wants.** The draft states both in narration, about 60 lines apart: "I had no plans to move." and "I still didn't know what I wanted." That is a contradiction. The outline asked for it, so it is also an outline fault.
- **Outline: she is working up to asking something personal.** The draft makes it "I was also working up to asking him about Friday, and I kept not starting." By then he has given her the gift and she has been in the shop for 40 minutes. The question is stale, and it repeats scene 2: "I had meant to ask him about Friday. I'd rehearsed it on the walk over, three different ways". Her "why tonight" is answered by the gift and never asked.
- **Parents.** The outline has him say once that he and they didn't get along. The draft has two statements: "We don't really talk much." and "It's fine. We didn't really get along." This breaks the zero-limit on information given twice. It also adds a second lie beyond the one the outline sets up.
- **"Information given to the reader twice: 0" is broken in several places:**
  - "Marcus checked the clock above the door, which said 4:31." after Marcus has already said "It's half past four".
  - "the discontinued stock I had mentioned once, months ago" followed by "which I had only said once".
  - Tuesdays: Marcus's "You get coffee with him every Tuesday" and then "Coffee was on Tuesdays. We had settled into Tuesdays".
  - The table: "one table kept breaking its formatting every time I touched it" and "the pivot table on page forty-two had shifted three pixels to the left".
  - "It was 5:47. I had checked my phone two minutes before." The 5:45 check is already on the page.
- **Overshoot on Scene 2 analogies.** STYLE allows one analogy per scene. Scene 2 has "the way an on-camera flash does" and "the way it does in listing photos taken before anyone has moved in".
- **Triplet** (STYLE allows one per chapter): "There's the rent, and then the equipment, and the chemicals". A second: "I hated Dave. I hated his carrots. And I hated that if I didn't fix that table". The first is a list of three; the second is a verbatim line extended by a third beat.
- **Mira's craft claim contradicts her situation.** "In a print I would have corrected it in a minute." She bought the camera a month ago, has never had a darkroom, and cannot afford one. She has not made prints.
- **Contact sheet.** "I've been looking at the contact sheet for days." A contact sheet is made in a darkroom, which she does not have. The rolls were lab-developed, and a lab does not produce one in that way. "I'd need a darkroom to fix it properly" then reads oddly beside it.

### 2. Characters built from traits, not lives
- **Jason as plot machine.** "Have you thought any more about that darkroom space?" comes seconds after she apologised for talking five minutes about it and he has just heard it all. His only reason to ask is that the outline needs him to steer away from his parents. A real man steering off his parents would pick a new subject. Then "That's too bad. It sounded like a good setup." is dead dialogue.
- **Mira's lie-catching.** "He said it evenly." The narrator signals the lie in the same breath the outline wants it to slide by.
- **Mira's confidence.** "Of course I would. It's the only thing I shoot that doesn't look like everything else." This is out of character for a woman who "plays down her... hobby before anyone else can" (CHARACTERS). She also runs on for 300 words and is then embarrassed, which is right, but the answer here has no embarrassment.
- **Marcus.** See problem 8.

### 3. Manufactured quirks and props
- **Scarf.** "I took off my scarf and put my bag on the chair beside me." It is never mentioned again. She turns her collar up outside, so the scarf is lost or ignored.
- **Wobbly table leg.** "the one at the front with the wobbly leg". Never used, never recurs.
- **Jason's laptop.** "Jason slid his laptop into his backpack." The laptop was never taken out. It appears for the first time at that moment, and only to give him a gesture while she speaks.
- **Phone.** It sits in her coat pocket (coat later folded over her knees) and then "buzzed against the table" and she "turned the phone over". It was never taken out. The outline names the phone, so it is exempt from the repeat limit, but the staging is broken.
- **Knuckles.** "His knuckles were red from the cold." He has been inside and standing at the counter. This is a fine observation but the cause is wrong or unsupported.
- **Barn prints and four pastries.** "a book club in the corner had four kinds of pastry spread across their table", "framed prints of barns along the brick, and the ceiling lights were too bright for them". Pure scene dressing.

### 4. Polished banter and jokes in wrong places
Joke count in Scenes 1-2 is within the 4 allowed. The issues are quality:
- "Ah. The famous Jason." and "Uh-huh." / "We are." Marcus's teasing is workable but the beats are stock sitcom.
- "I hated Dave. I hated his carrots." This is verbatim and fine in itself.
- "as if the rent might have changed since last time" is a pat close to a paragraph. It is the only quip-shaped line in the monologue and it is observed, so it can stay.
- "That's too bad. It sounded like a good setup." Not a joke, but a dead neat reply.
- No quips in the danger scene. The attack is clean.

### 5. Not trusting the reader
- "It was 5:47. I had checked my phone two minutes before." Narrates a time she could not know, with the proof attached. The outline wanted the time on the page; it did not need her to do sums in the middle of an impact.
- "I felt it. Everything else was happening too fast for me to think about it." Points straight at the set-up (the bend in the jump) and explains why she did not think about it. The outline says "feels it and says nothing about it." The draft says plenty.
- "My heart did a stupid little flip. It was only dinner." Names the feeling and then mocks it. Cut the second sentence.
- "That was the truth, or the version of it I'd settled on. Two years of Tuesdays and careful distance had made it clear enough, and I had learned to stop hoping for anything else." Explains her love and her self-protection right after Marcus's dialogue has shown it.
- "I had picked this job because it paid on the fifteenth and didn't ask for more than my hours. For most of the past year that had been enough, and I was not sure it was enough anymore." Thesis paragraph about her life.
- "He had kept hold of the name, which I had only said once." Explains the romance beat after it has landed.
- "I took the box out and read the whole label aloud, including the expiration date, which was useless now." The clause "which was useless now" explains the joke.
- "He said it evenly." Signals evasion.
- "I'm fine," I said. "I was." The "I was." is a verdict on herself that the scene does not need.
- "I hadn't planned to ask it that way." Explains the line just spoken.
- "He took his time with the answer, and we walked as far as the next corner." He answered "Sometimes." That is the whole answer. The narration describes a pause around a one-word reply, which is a null-space beat.
- "Jason didn't say anything." Narrating Jason's silence. The outline's Do-not is about speech; STYLE's rule is about describing non-reactions. Show the hand and cut the line.
- Symbolism: the river appears twice ("a dark line with a few lights lying on it", then "A gust came up off the river"). The outline asked for the river once. The second use is the wind, which is fine, but the dark water and the dark profile ("His profile was dark under the lamp") sit close.

### 6. Sentence-level habits
- **Unquoted speech and present-tense slip.** The key paragraph: `"It's good. It's honestly really good." The meter was dead when I bought it, and the man at the shop was so pleased with himself that I didn't check it until I got home. I put in a new battery and it's been fine since. ... It's gone green. ... I've been looking at the contact sheet for days.` The quotation marks close after "really good." and the rest of the speech runs on as unquoted text, mixing past ("was") with present perfect and present ("it's been", "It's gone green"). As written it reads as narration in the wrong tense.
- **Worst short runs.** Lines 87-93 produce a run of seven sentences under six words with nothing between them: "I asked." / "Your parents?" / "We don't really talk much." / "I'm sorry." / "It's fine." / "We didn't really get along." / "He said it evenly."
  - Standalone tags inflate the count: "I asked." (twice), "I said.", "he said.", all as separate two-word sentences.
  - Also: "Different how?" / "He opened his mouth to answer." / "The air cracked."
- **Choppy narration at the end:** "Then the ground." / "Prickling spread over my whole skin, sharp and everywhere at once."
- **Comparison chosen for drama, not accuracy:**
  - "in a yellow square that I had to step over". Light from a second-floor kitchen window does not make a sharp-edged square you step over, least of all with streetlamps already on.
  - "like a car swinging under me around a bend too fast" (from the outline). Cars swing around, not under. The drafter could not fix the outline's image.
  - "electric and wrong". Stock.
  - "I shot it from the walkway, low, with the railing across the bottom of the frame so the water would catch the light." The railing would not make the water catch light, and the camera is low enough for the railing to block the frame bottom while the water sits below it. The sentence does not describe a possible photo.
- **Posture contradiction:** "I came down on my hands and knees... Above me, bare branches stood against a gray sky." On hands and knees she is looking at her palms. She would need to look up, which the draft does not say.
- **Chest twice:** "the force came through the light and hit my chest" and "Pressure filled my chest, and I couldn't breathe." Two body reactions in one beat, where STYLE says pick one.
- **Photography image count:** the narrator's photographer eye appears at least five times outside the film subject: fluorescent skin "In a print I would have corrected it", lamp "the way an on-camera flash does", "listing photos", orange lamps turning brick brown, and the film itself. STYLE says "about once a chapter."
- **Repeated word:** "flat gray-green", "the flat blue it turns in February".
- **Other tics:** "before I could talk myself out of going" and "walked out before I could decide to stay" (twice in ten lines).

### 7. Scene logic
- **Laptop** appears from nowhere (see 3).
- **Backpack:** after the shop it is never picked up or worn. RULES says it comes through the jump. Nothing in the chapter puts it on his back or in his hand when he takes her arm.
- **Phone in coat pocket vs buzzing on the table:** never taken out (see 3).
- **Scarf** unaccounted for.
- **Coat:** folded over her knees in the shop, "turned my collar up" outside. The putting-on is skipped. It is her gift's pocket, so it matters.
- **Paper bag:** left unaccounted for (she takes the box out and puts the box in her pocket).
- **5:47:** she cannot know the minute (see 5).
- **"Do yours do the timeline thing too?"** Refers to something never set up. Earlier she said only "My parents have opinions about what comes next, and they're happy to share them." "Timeline" appears nowhere else.
- **"I have to get home early Sunday," I said a minute later.** Dropped into the conversation with no lead, and then it leads to a question about his parents that the previous line does not support.
- **Marcus says "meeting him at five, right?"** before the text confirming it. She had only said "I'm meeting Jason." Cheap but acceptable.
- **Timing at the attack:** "He opened his mouth to answer. The air cracked." The attack lands at the perfect dramatic second. The outline asked for the interruption, but the draft stages it as: question, answer withheld, mouth opens, crack. The outline only needs "the attack comes before he answers."
- **"The clock over the counter said twenty to six"** is consistent with 5:40 and with seven minutes to 5:47. This works. The chain of times is consistent apart from the narrator's arithmetic.
- **Dusk:** streetlights coming on at 5:45 in Columbus on Feb 9 is plausible.
- **Coffee shop times:** she is on the walk to meet him from the office after 4:31; the draft never gives an arrival time. Fine.

### 8. Side characters as functions, and padding
- **Marcus** exists to deliver the time and the "famous Jason" exposition, then leaves. "Marcus checked the clock above the door, which said 4:31. 'You're meeting him at five, right? Don't let me keep you.'" He is also given the invitation to Murphy's, which gives him a reason to be there but not a life.
- **Family items as a checklist.** Nora's listing, parents' opinions, the milk, and his parents all occur within about ten lines, and in the order of the outline's bullets. This breaks the outline's "spread across the scene... never together."
- **Padding:** the pastry table, barn prints, wobbly leg, "My eyes felt packed with sand", "The latte had gone lukewarm, and I drank it anyway." The chapter is 13% under length while holding these, so the shortfall is in beats that carry something.

## Observed or performed?

Mixed, leaning performed. The observations that belong to Mira (light on faces, colour under lamps, what the hands do) are real. Almost every transition between beats is stitched: the family items are announced, the set-ups are explained, and the clock is narrated for the reader's benefit. A reader can hear the outline being ticked off.

**Three best:**
1. "The lamp above our table lit his face from straight above, the way an on-camera flash does, and it took out the lines that usually showed when he was tired. I wanted to move the lamp." A photographer's eye ends in a wish to act, and it tells us she watches his tiredness.
2. "Under the orange lamps the brick had gone brown and my navy coat looked nearly black. Only the red sign of a dry cleaner still had any real color." Exact, unforced, and it is the only kind of image the scene needs.
3. "I took the box out and read the whole label aloud, including the expiration date... Then I thanked him. Then I thanked him again. 'All right,' I said, and set the box down, and picked it up again." Too much of something, caught, and unnamed. (The tail clause "which was useless now" should go.)

**Three worst:**
1. "I have to get home early Sunday," I said a minute later. "I promised my mom I'd bring milk to dinner." / "Do yours do the timeline thing too?" I asked. "Your parents?" Plainly the outline's chain ("That leads her to ask about his parents") written out. Nobody talks this way.
2. "It was 5:47. I had checked my phone two minutes before." The narrator proves the outline's number in the middle of the hit.
3. "In a second-floor window a man stood at his sink, and the light from his kitchen fell across the sidewalk in a yellow square that I had to step over." A detail chosen to look observed, and it is not physically right.

## Fix proposals

### Fixes to the outline (chapter level)
1. **Scene 2 beat 2.** Replace "at least a full page of her talking" with: she talks about the film across several exchanges, interrupted by Jason's one question and one observation of his own and by her own tangents. No speech over about 80 words, always inside quotation marks. Name the recalled detail Jason volunteers (for example, he asks whether the co-op above the print shop on the east side is still the one with the enlarger, which she mentioned once weeks ago).
2. **Family beats (Scene 2 beat 3).** Stop chaining them. Move the milk to a Mom text in Scene 1 (CHARACTERS already has Mom texting about milk). Keep Nora's buzz early in Scene 2, hold the parents' opinion for later, and give Mira's question about his parents a plain trigger: Jason asks what she's doing this weekend, she answers with Sunday dinner, and asking about his parents follows. Drop "the timeline thing".
3. **Resolve "why tonight" in Scene 2** with the gift, and have her private worry on the walk be the loneliness question only. Delete Friday from Scene 3. Reconcile "no plans to move" with "doesn't know what she wants": she tells Jason she is not going, and her thoughts on the walk show she is not sure.
4. **The clock.** Keep 5:45 on her phone; say the attack comes "a few minutes" after, and cut "about 5:47" as narrated time. If Chapter 2 needs the minute, let Ch 2 supply it from the phone.
5. **Staging of the exit and landing.** Add: she puts her coat back on, Jason shoulders the backpack, the paper bag is left on the table. At the landing give her a posture that allows the sky (on her back or sitting) and place Jason a few feet away with his hand released.
6. **Jason's silence in the attack.** Replace "He says nothing" with "Show only what his hand does; narration does not comment on his silence."
7. **Marcus.** One time cue in his own words, no description of the clock. Give him one specific, non-expository line about Mira's week so he is a person.
8. **Optional texture.** Delete the bridge-photo suggestion or limit it to one sentence in Scene 2, and say photography images appear once in the whole chapter.

### Fixes to STYLE
1. **Resolve the contradiction** between "In every scene, include at least one observation that only this narrator would make, drawn from her photographer's eye" and "Photography images about once a chapter." Proposed: "Each scene gets one specific observation of light, faces or hands. Photography vocabulary (print, exposure, flash, grain, frame) is used once per chapter, where she'd actually reach for it."
2. **Dialogue tags.** Add: "Attach tags to the same sentence as the speech. A standalone 'I asked.' or 'he said.' counts as a short sentence."
3. **Speech.** Add: "Dialogue stays in quotation marks, in past tense where it reports. A speech over about 80 words is broken by the listener or the setting."
4. **No staged proof.** Add to the explaining rule: "Do not restate a time, distance or detail already given ('I had checked my phone two minutes before'), and do not follow a planted detail with a sentence saying she didn't think about it."
5. **Objects.** Add: "Anything a character handles or puts away has to have been placed in the scene first. Check the list of what everyone carries against the page before ending the scene."
6. **Self-mocking tags.** Add to the body-language rule: "Don't follow a feeling with a sentence that dismisses it ('It was only dinner')."
7. **Lies and evasions.** Add: "Don't mark a line as evasive or even ('He said it evenly')."
8. **Number clarity.** Clarify that "Never more than two sentences in a row under six words" counts dialogue and tags together, and that dialogue volleys need action or thought between lines.

## What works
- "Under the orange lamps the brick had gone brown and my navy coat looked nearly black. Only the red sign of a dry cleaner still had any real color." Concrete, accurate and in her voice, and it sets the dusk without announcing it.
- "I wanted to move the lamp." A photographer's reflex turned into a wish, and it shows what she feels without saying so.
- "I took the box out and read the whole label aloud... Then I thanked him. Then I thanked him again." It shows too-much happiness through behaviour and she catches herself.
- "Maintenance had come up twice, looked at the ceiling with their hands on their hips, and promised a work order." A specific, believable office detail that opens the chapter without explaining.
- "Jason's hand came up toward my elbow. It stopped a few inches short of me, and then it dropped back to his side." The hovering hand used once, shown plainly, with nothing added after it.
