## Chapter 1: Normal

### Summary
- **Grade:** 5/10

Ch 1 is the most readable of the three. The office scene and the first half of the coffee shop have a believable woman in them, and the Pentax detail is specific. It still reads as constructed, for five reasons.
1. Jason is written as a man who knows trouble is coming (door, window, street checks), which the outline forbids and which turns the "ordinary evening" into foreshadowing.
2. Props and quirks are bolted on: the cup-cooling experiment, the folded bag, the quarter-turned cup, the coffee lid, "does the voices", apologising to the camera.
3. The banter is a string of button lines ("I'm not invoicing Henderson for my soul", "The green is a whole personality", "Details.").
4. Body-language clichés and "tell" sentences are everywhere: face warm, face lit up three times, heart flip, chest turn, "his mouth did something complicated".
5. The clock does not work. Mira walks in around five, yet the students have "been arguing for ten minutes", the family conversation covers six topics, and they leave "just past five". Ch 2 then says they left at twenty to six.

The attack is the weakest part of the chapter. The outline asks for an invisible attacker and a silent Jason. The draft shows a man in a canvas jacket and knit hat, a second figure, and Jason saying "Hold on to me."

### Findings

#### 1. Wrong story facts

**1a. Jason knows something is coming.** The outline's limit is "Jason looking at doors, windows or the street: 0." The draft has at least four:
- "Then he looked at the door, a beat too long, and back at me."
- "He glanced at the window, and back. His face went tight and careful."
- "Every half block, Jason checked the street behind us. I noticed it twice and decided not to ask."
- "Jason's head snapped around."

This contradicts the outline ("Jason does not know anyone is coming for him tonight") and Ch 3's own claim that "In all our Tuesdays I had never once seen him check a street."
- *Instead:* Remove all of it. Jason is easy and warm until the air cracks, and his instant reaction is the first sign he is not what he seems.

**1b. The attackers are shown, and Jason speaks.** The draft has "A man stepped out of a doorway across the street with one hand raised, palm out. He wore a canvas jacket and a knit hat", then "A second figure moved out from behind a parked van", then "Hold on to me." The outline says "Nobody is visible", "Show the attackers: do not", and "Have Jason say anything during the attack: do not."
- *Instead:* The air cracks, blue light appears, something slams into it, and his hand is on her arm.

**1c. The coffee lid.** The outline says "Give Mira a coffee lid or any other object to carry through the scene for later." The draft has her "pulled the lid off my latte to let it cool and kept it in my hand", "I was still holding the lid, turning it over in my fingers", and the final line "and the coffee lid still in my hand". Ch 2 and Ch 3 then track it.
- *Instead:* The outline's gift roll in the coat pocket is the only object to carry.

**1d. Time.** The text says "It was just past five" when they leave. The outline has them leaving at 5:40, and Ch 2 says "twenty to six".

**1e. The students are not the reason they leave.** In the outline they are noise that makes the shop unbearable, and Jason does not react to the content. In the draft Jason "glanced over at them with a small, private smile, like he already knew how it ended", which is the smirk the outline forbids. The leaving prompt is instead Jason's nervous glance.

**1f. Jason lectures.** The outline says no technical explanations and no experiments. The draft has the cup-cooling experiment ("Timing how fast it cools... Either the sleeve is doing something weird or the cup is") and a streetlight monologue ("Those orange ones make everything the same color... Actually, you'd have to know that for the sodium lamps alone. Sorry. Ignore me."). The outline wanted one or two lines about her green film in this light. CHARACTERS.md says he is "Plain-spoken... doesn't ramble or lecture" and has "No other quirks: no fidgeting, no props".

#### 2. Characters built from traits

**2a. Jason apologises for being a nerd.** "Sorry. Ignore me." and "I'm going to figure it out whether or not you care" are the character sheet's "socially awkward nerd", which it says never to write. He is guarded, not gushing.
- *Instead:* He asks a question about her film and listens.

**2b. Mira thinking about milk at the key moment.** "I was also thinking about the milk. I'd be at my mother's table on Sunday with no milk, and she would say nothing about it, which would be worse." She is seconds from the sentence she has waited two years for. The milk is already in the coffee shop ("Jess had finished ours, so that was a problem for future me"), so it is also repeated information. The outline asked for one line.
- *Instead:* Keep one milk line, in the coffee shop. On the walk she thinks about him.

**2c. Marcus as a mouthpiece.** "You meet him every Tuesday for coffee. You talk about him constantly. Last month you cancelled on girls' night because he was in town. [Counting on fingers] You're basically dating." Marcus exists to recite the premise. "Marcus had worked there four months and already believed he was the office gossip columnist" is the narrator labelling him.
- *Instead:* One teasing line from the outline ("cancelled girls' night to have dinner with him") and let him go.

#### 3. Manufactured quirks and props
- "Nora had left a voice memo at lunch, eight minutes long... I'd listened to all of it twice, because she does the voices." This repeats information (the outline names "listened to it twice" as a zero-limit item), is a quirk, and is in the present tense.
- "...each time I apologized to it out loud" (the Pentax). A cute habit invented for charm.
- "I did not do a voice. I had done a voice. It was a very good voice." A second "does voices" gag.
- "tapping the side of it with one fingernail" and "turned his cup a quarter turn on the table" are Jason gestures with no purpose. The second also points at his one lie.
- "picked up the crumpled top of the paper bag and folded it flat, twice." STYLE.md gives this example as a "saying things twice" error.
- "He laughs through his nose first. I've never told him, because he would do it on purpose for the rest of my life." It is a manufactured tic. Ch 3 repeats it ("laughed through his nose before he'd finished the joke").
- The sign "its letters buzzing on and off one at a time" echoes the office lights as a motif.

#### 4. Polished banter, jokes in the wrong places
Joke limit: 4, in Scenes 1 and 2. The draft has roughly a dozen punchlines, and the 4 limit is exceeded in Scene 2 alone. Examples:
- "Sawdust is the point. You look like you need it." STYLE.md names this as clever but meaningless. It also makes no sense: he bought her a scone she hates and tells her to eat it.
- "Bill them for the weekend. Hazard pay." / "I'm not invoicing Henderson for my soul."
- "The prints will be terrible... The green is a whole personality."
- "I'm extremely normal." / "You are not."
- "Details."
- "Sarah says that about everything." / "Sarah's right about this one, I think."

Nearly every exchange ends on a clean return. Real talk trails off, overlaps and answers a different question.
- *Instead:* Keep two or three light exchanges and let the rest be plain information.

**Non-answers.** "Hey," he said, sitting down" comes after he has already spoken, and the "Hey, how's that camera?" and "Hey, have you thought any more about that darkroom space?" topic swaps are abrupt, not the "smooth" deflection the outline wants. The lie about his parents does not "slide by". It is pointed at by "turned his cup a quarter turn" and "He said it lightly".

#### 5. Not trusting the reader
- "He remembered everything except to ever ask me on an actual date." This is verbatim and correct. The problem is what surrounds it: "the almost-smile that never quite turned into what I wanted it to be" and "My heart did a stupid little flip. Two years of coffee, and now dinner. ... Oh. Right. He was hungry. I was convenient." The reader already knows she is in love and he is evasive. The draft keeps telling.
- "Two words, and my chest did a small, traitorous turn. I was a grown woman who got a flutter out of two words, and I was going to be embarrassed about it for the rest of the walk." This explains the "You're different" beat right after it lands.
- "He had a crease between his eyebrows that showed up when he was about to say something important and was hunting for the least important way to start. I figured that was what this was." Explains the beat in advance.
- "I was ridiculously happy. I tried to keep my face neutral and failed completely." plus "in the voice of someone who was going to think about this all week". Say it once.
- "I noticed it twice and decided not to ask." This is pointing at a clue, which STYLE.md explicitly bans.
- "None of it made sense to me." after the attack. Padding.
- The students' argument ("If you knew every variable in the universe, you could predict everything... Free will is an illusion.") reads as a planted theme for the physics-minded Jason.

#### 6. Sentence-level habits
- **Metrics:** 43% of sentences under six words; the longest run of short sentences is 13; 37 one-line paragraphs under six words. STYLE.md allows never more than two in a row. The short-reply runs are most visible in the dialogue: "I can't. I've got plans." / "What plans?" / "Coffee with Jason." and the later "I'm extremely normal." / "You are not." Also: "Extra foam. Two shots." and the tail fragments "Ground." "Dirt and dead leaves, a rock digging into my palm."
- **Body-language clichés (limit 1, metrics list ten):**
  - "My face went warm."
  - "My whole face lit up before I could stop it."
  - "for a second his whole face lit up"
  - "I felt the familiar warmth in my chest"
  - "My heart did a stupid little flip."
  - "my chest did a small, traitorous turn"
  - "His mouth did something complicated, then settled into a smile."
  - "His face went tight and careful."
  - "a beat too long"
- **Present-tense slips:** "because she does the voices", "He laughs through his nose first. I've never told him...", "which he does with his own jokes too", "which is the kind of thing I think about when I'm nervous", "I haven't dropped it. I have come close". STYLE.md says "he laughed", never "he laughs".
- **Personification:** "the lights buzzing over my head like they had something to say", the Pentax being apologised to.
- **Bad comparison:** "I watched the blue ripple the way a windshield ripples when a rock comes at it from the wrong side." A windshield cracks or stars. It does not ripple. The comparison was picked for drama.
- **Fragment endings:** "Easy and comfortable." and "Ground." The fragment count is well over three.

#### 7. Scene logic
- **Clock.** Text at roughly 4:30; "See you in 20"; she arrives about 4:50 to 5:00 and Jason is already at the counter. Then "two students had been arguing for ten minutes" and "It was just past five." Ten minutes of arguing cannot fit into a visit that is at most ten minutes old. Six topics of conversation fit even less. Ch 2 puts the departure at 5:40.
- **Distance.** "There's that Thai place a few blocks away." Then "The Thai place's sign was lit up half a block ahead" as they step out. They then walk "a quieter stretch" past a park and a row of closed storefronts, have a conversation, and the sign is never reached. The outline says "The restaurant isn't in sight yet."
- **Objects.**
  - Jason "started packing up his laptop", but the laptop is never mentioned again (Ch 2 and 3 have him in the woods with no bag).
  - Her latte: she "pulled the lid off my latte to let it cool", so she takes an open cup into a February street? It is never mentioned again, only the lid.
  - Scarf: "unwound my scarf", "the gap at my collar", gone in Ch 2.
  - Footwear: "My shoes were the right shoes for a desk and the wrong shoes for a sidewalk." Ch 2 has "one of my boots had come untied".
- **Lighting.** The streetlights are on and "orange" at about 5:15 pm. Columbus sunset on Feb 9 is about 6:00. It is plausible but early, and they are used as a lecture prop.
- **Scone.** "You hate those... You told me they taste like sawdust." Then "I pointed my scone at him." She hates scones and uses one as a prop. Ch 3 says "The scone he'd bought me even though he hated them", which reverses who hates them.
- **Lunch text.** "Jason had texted at lunch to ask if we could meet tonight" is added and fine, but then the 4:30 "Still on for coffee? I'm heading over now" would find him already seated with a laptop open.
- **Henderson.** At the office she has "Three pages still needed review", i.e. nearly done. Ch 2 says "which I had not started."

#### 8. Side characters as functions and padding
- Marcus, as above.
- Dave appears once for the verbatim line and is fine.
- The Nora voice memo, the Pentax's Dublin, Ohio history, the Jess "boots in the hall and the dishwasher that only ran on Thursdays" line, "the book club... a fierce argument about a dragon": padding, none of it moves anything. The Jess line in particular is another detail doing nothing ("Jess, once" was the instruction, and it was already used for the milk).
- The students at the next table exist as a thematic pointer.

## Chapter 2: Displaced

### Summary
- **Grade:** 4/10

Ch 2 has a real strength in Mira's methodical coping (screenshots, the dream test, the 911 call she imagines). The prose around it undermines that: a frightened woman who produces a joke in nearly every paragraph, objects and trees with attitudes ("glaring at the lace as if it had personally betrayed me", "the trees were not going to pretend they hadn't heard me"), and a Jason who is the opposite of the outline. The brief wants "calm, fast and clearly hiding a lot", "checks her quickly and competently", "doesn't explain himself", "Not scared, fumbling or unable to finish a sentence". The draft gives us a man who snaps sticks, stammers ("The thing is... no. Not the GPS. Hang on."), says "I don't know" twice, says "I'm scared too", and does not touch her at all. The romance beat (his gentle examination of her) is gone, replaced by Mira checking herself. The chapter's other major failing is that the timer ends with nine seconds left just as he arrives, which STYLE.md names as the standard "convenient timing" error. The Thai/pasta slip and the time contradictions with Ch 1 show the chapter was not checked against its predecessor.

### Findings

#### 1. Wrong story facts

**1a. Jason is written the wrong way round.** Quotes:
- "His hands wouldn't stay still. He shoved them into his jacket pockets, pulled them out again, picked a dead stick off the ground and snapped it in half, then snapped one of the halves again."
- "'Okay. So the thing is...' He stopped. 'The thing about the GPS is, it's probably... no. Not the GPS. Hang on.' ... 'Sorry. I'm going too fast.'"
- "'You haven't finished one sentence.'"
- "I'm scared too."

Outline: "Make Jason scared, fumbling or unable to finish a sentence: do not." CHARACTERS.md: "Never write him as a scared kid". Also "He's not scared of the teleport."
- *Instead:* He speaks in full sentences, says "Give me twenty minutes. I need to know whether anyone followed us," and walks up the rise.

**1b. "I don't know" appears twice, limit 0.** "Where's the road?" / "I don't know." and then "What do you mean you don't know?" The outline says he does not stonewall and tells her plainly that he will explain once he is sure they are alone.
- *Instead:* "Not yet. Give me twenty minutes."

**1c. The romance beat is missing.** The outline's centre is "How gently he checks her for injuries": he asks her name and the day, has her follow his finger, looks at her pupils and feels her scalp. The draft has Jason ten feet away turning in a circle while Mira checks herself ("I touched my scalp. Leaves, dirt, a sore spot above my left ear"). He never comes to her.

**1d. The timer ends with him arriving.** "The timer said nine seconds when he got close enough that I could see the dirt on his knees." Outline: "Have anything happen at the exact moment the timer ends: do not", "She stands up, walks to the bottom of the rise and calls up to him: time's up", and "Nobody comes to her."

**1e. Departure time.** "We had left the coffee shop at twenty to six." Ch 1 says "just past five." If the draft's own Ch 1 holds, the gap is about 25 minutes, not an hour. "Jason had held the door for me. He'd said it was cold, and I'd said it was February, and we had both laughed" never happened on the page in Ch 1, and "I'd glanced at my phone in the doorway" didn't either.

**1f. Henderson.** "the Henderson report, due Monday, which I had not started and was not going to start." In Ch 1 she is on page 42 with three pages left to review.

**1g. Restaurant.** "the only thing waiting at the end of the block was a plate of pasta I would have ruined at home." They were walking to a Thai restaurant. (Also, her cooking is "bad at" per CHARACTERS, but the line is a joke plus the wrong cuisine.)

**1h. Footwear.** "One of my boots had come untied." Ch 1 had "shoes... right for a desk".

**1i. Mira "saw the man".** The outline says she saw no one. The draft's memory is "Doorway. Sidewalk. The man. The blue flash" and "a man had raised one hand in the street", following the over-specified Ch 1.

#### 2. Characters built from traits
- **Mira does not act frightened.** CHARACTERS.md: "When she's frightened, the jokes stop... A woman who has just been thrown across three states doesn't produce a quip every paragraph." The draft's Mira has a punchline for nearly every beat, then decides, aloud, "Some petty part of me wanted him to walk around with it for a while" (the leaf in his hair) while in shock in a strange forest.
- **Mira's refusal to call 911.** The reasoning is eventually correct (the outline's "police can't help with it, and he knows what it is"), but it is worked as a six-sentence internal argument ("Jason looked like someone who'd either lie badly or tell the truth in a way that got us both put in a van... Partly, I trusted him... The other part was colder."). The character test: a real person here would type 911, look at him, and stop. It is the same reasoning, over-narrated.
- **"Two years of Tuesdays had taught me how he looked when he was lying, and he didn't look like that now."** Ch 1 shows he lied about his parents and she did not notice. The sentence sets her up as better at reading him than she is, and Ch 3's "lying to me for two years" rebuts it.
- **Mira's Mom/Jess/Sarah texts.** The outline wants her to lie smoothly "and be startled by how smoothly". The draft does give "I lied to my mother in about two seconds", but the Jess text is delivered as a joke ("she'd eaten half the cheese in our fridge that week and would blame the cheese situation on me... would be wrong, but in a way that would make her happy for a week"). Real fear does not produce a roommate bit.
- **The old therapist.** "the breathing my old therapist had taught me for bad nights" is a freshly invented backstory used for one verbatim line.

#### 3. Manufactured quirks and props
- **The leaf in Jason's hair:** "a leaf caught in his hair", "He still had the leaf in his hair. I didn't tell him." Ch 3 revisits it. Three mentions across two chapters, and the second is a petty gag.
- **The sticks:** Jason snaps one stick, then another, then "turned it over, looking for something inside it, and then he put it down like it might bite him," then "went back to the pieces at his feet, as if they might tell him something." STYLE.md bans objects used "just to use it later" and personified objects.
- **The coffee lid:** "The coffee lid was still crushed in my fist. I had no memory of closing my hand around it, which seemed like the least important thing that had happened to me all day." The outline explicitly bans an object carried through. It is also unrealistic that she has held the lid through a teleport and a fall.
- **The stone:** "He crouched, picked up a stone, and turned it over in his fingers without looking at it." Another fidget.

#### 4. Polished banter, jokes in the wrong places
Limit: 1 in the whole chapter. Count in this draft is about ten:
- "glaring at the lace as if it had personally betrayed me"
- "watching the trees like he expected one of them to step out and make an announcement"
- "Hours were supposed to disappear forward, into meetings. This one had gone backward, which was new."
- "Who's going to overhear us, the squirrels?"
- "You want me to sit in the woods like a dog while you go check on things... I'm not a dog, Jason." / "I never said you were a dog." / "You were thinking it."
- "a plate of pasta I would have ruined at home"
- "I had sat through worse meetings. Not many, but some."
- "looking cheerful about the whole thing" (the keypad)
- "My brain had never been that thorough about anything, including my taxes."
- "a stranger with a notepad" / "the one people use on drunk uncles"

The dog exchange is also a pure back-and-forth with nothing at stake. *Instead:* Keep one nervous, unpolished line at most, and cut the rest.

**Non-answers.** "Jason?" / "He stopped turning. 'Mostly. You?'" "Mostly" is not an answer to her saying his name. Later "You're scared," I said... He looked down at the broken stick. 'I'm scared too. I need you to trust me for a bit.'" follows a Mira line that is the third in a row from her with no reply between (after "the squirrels?").

#### 5. Not trusting the reader
- "But he'd thought about being followed before he'd thought about where we were. I noticed that, and I put it next to the clock in my head." STYLE.md's "Pointing at clues" example, almost word for word, and the outline said "don't have her narration point it out."
- "That was the last ordinary sentence I could find." Tells the reader.
- "I read it twice. The words didn't change." Then, for the dream test: "The words were exactly the same. Same capital T, same period, nothing scrambled." Then "In dreams, text never held still. You looked away, looked back, and the sign said something else." Three explanations for one test.
- "Facts are easier to keep when they're in a picture." Explains the screenshots.
- "My heart was pounding hard enough that I could hear it over the ringing. I dug my thumbnails into my palms and let the sting give me something to think about besides my pulse." Explains her own coping.
- "I hate being wrong with nobody watching. It's worse somehow. There was no one to be embarrassed in front of, just the trees..." Four sentences to say nothing.
- The 911 passage (see above).

#### 6. Sentence-level habits
- **Metrics:** 40% of sentences under six words; longest run 8; 20 one-line paragraphs under six words.
- **Fragments, over the limit of three:** "Nothing sharp. Nothing that felt broken." / "Trees, everywhere." / "No path. No people. No buildings. No street." / "Four forty-seven." / "Doorway. Sidewalk. The man." / "Crossville, Tennessee." The "No path. No people. No buildings. No street." run is both a fragment pile and a countdown rhythm.

  - "glaring at the lace as if it had personally betrayed me"

#### 7. Scene logic
- **Time.** At 4:47 on the phone and "twenty to six" as the departure, that is 53 minutes. At Ch 1's "just past five", the gap is 25 or so. The outline wants the time zone un-hinted; the draft hints it ("An hour, give or take, had gone missing") but also calls the gap an hour from a clock that disagrees with Ch 1.
- **Timer.** She sets 20:00 and checks it at "6:40" after "the 911 call" and some typing. Texts with Sarah, Jess and Mom, the dream test, a 911 deliberation and a minute at the dial pad fit 13 minutes, roughly. But Jason says "Thirty minutes. Maybe less," then "I need at least twenty." Maybe-less and at-least-twenty do not fit together. The timer ends exactly as he returns.
- **Jason's examination.** "He looked fine. No blood, no limp." There is no check.
- **Phone buzzing.** "My phone had been buzzing in my pocket since before the woods." She had no pocket buzz in the woods before she took out the phone to check GPS, and the first texts she answers are Sarah's reply to her own message. The order is muddled: "I typed to Sarah... Her answer came back almost at once."
- **Texts and time zone.** Mom's "Did you get the milk for Sunday?" and "Everything ok?" arrive in a 30-second flurry with nothing to prompt "Everything ok?". The outline only asks for the milk.
- **Reach.** "He reached for my sleeve. His hand was shaking." Ch 3 opens with "Jason still had two fingers hooked in my sleeve," which holds, but a shaking hand on a "steady" man is the wrong beat.
- **Description of the rise.** "He put one hand flat against a tree trunk and stayed there, checking something I couldn't see," then (via narration) "shoulders up around his ears... like a man who'd bolt if anything stepped out of them." Contradicts "perfectly still and clearly doing something". Ch 3 then says he checked "Three times, the long way around," which is not what she watched.
- **Injuries.** "no wet warmth on them" is fine. "One of my boots had come untied" while on hands and knees, and the lace glare, are padding.

#### 8. Side characters as functions and padding
- Sarah, Jess and Mom exist to give Mira lines to lie to. They are delivered as a three-text sequence with a joke on each. The outline asked for a smooth lie and startlement.
- Padding: the oven anecdote ("Last October I had insisted for a month that my oven was lying about its temperature. It had not been lying. I'd been too stubborn to say so."), the "I ran through the options" paragraph with a self-deprecating aside, the cheese, the taxes.

## Chapter 3: The Ultimatum

### Summary
- **Grade:** 3.5/10

Ch 3 has the best material (the "how would I know" erasure question and the Tuesday-inventory paragraph) buried in a scene that contradicts its own outline and the character sheet at nearly every turn. The brief says Jason is "calm, plain-spoken and honest within limits... He doesn't joke, ramble, read notes or explain technique." The draft gives us a man who reads from a phone checklist ("1. Stay calm... 4. Do not mention feelings."), gushes about randomness ("it's a clean problem... nothing in nature wants to be random on purpose"), calls his own analogy "a good one... Isn't it a good one?", says the teleport was "the first real test", says "I was too slow", and jokes about bullet points in the middle of a confession. The outline's "Do not" list is broken more or less line by line. The other major fault is the story fact: Jason says "I came tonight to say goodbye, Mira. That's why it was a Friday." The outline says he was not planning to leave and decides it now, and it reinterprets the gift film as a farewell, which flatly contradicts Ch 1. On top of that, the chapter commits the exact symbols STYLE.md bans: the bird that takes off at the confession ("My heart went up with that bird"), and the broken sticks ("You made the mess"). Quips continue through the odds scene, the options scene and the bargaining.

### Findings

#### 1. Wrong story facts

**1a. Jason came to say goodbye.** "After that I leave... I came tonight to say goodbye, Mira. That's why it was a Friday." And Mira: "You bought me film. You were saying goodbye." Outline: "Jason wasn't planning to leave Columbus. He is deciding it now" and Ch 1 outline: he asked for a Friday "because he found something for her and didn't want to wait until Tuesday." The draft retcons Ch 1 into a farewell. The outline's line was "You found me discontinued film, and you never once asked me to dinner."
- *Instead:* "They know where I live. I can't go back there." The reader and Mira both realise the film was just a gift.

**1b. Jason says the teleport was new and that he was too slow.**

- "I was too slow, you know. I saw that hand come up, and I was too slow, and you were standing right there." / "Barely."

Outline "Do not": "Say the teleport was experimental or new, or that he was too slow." CHARACTERS.md and RULES.md: it is a technique he has used many times, "Three jumps in a row is fine".

**1c. Jason explains how it works.**

**1d. Erasure.** RULES.md: "Done well, the person notices only a moment of confusion", no side effects, and the outline: "It leaves no gap she'd notice and no side effects." **1e. He reads from notes.** The outline says "without notes or fumbling" and "doesn't joke, ramble, read notes". The notes list is also the premeditation that makes "by accident" a lie: if "Do not mention feelings" is item 4, the confession was an anticipated slip, and Mira says so ("Is it the one you're going to break in the first minute?").

**1f. Odds are told twice.** "Most people die, Mira." arrives mid-argument, then Scene 4 delivers "A few months, usually." The outline wants the odds to land once and "stop the conversation".

**1g. The path.** "He looked at the trees, then at the path behind us, then at his phone." Ch 2: "No path."

**1h. The wait.** "Twenty minutes ago we were walking to dinner." It was at least 25 minutes, plus the time before the timer. "I had known about that leaf for an hour." Neither number is right.

**1i. Jason's check.** "I checked. Three times, the long way around." In Ch 2 she watched him stand on the rise with his hand on a tree.

**1j. "In all our Tuesdays I had never once seen him check a street."** Ch 1 had her noticing twice that he checked the street behind them during this walk. (Strictly this says Tuesdays, but it is the "Now he did it like breathing" echo that conflicts.)

**1k. The scone.** "The scone he'd bought me even though he hated them." In Ch 1 she hates them.

**1l. "Don't you ever offer that again"** is fine, but the options list that follows re-offers erasure ("Two: I erase today"). The outline lists erase as one of the four options, so this is more a pacing problem: it undercuts "He accepts it. He doesn't raise it again."
