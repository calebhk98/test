# Ranking

4. F, 5/10

## Per draft

**E.** Strength: it is the only draft where Jason is a specific person and the two of them banter like friends, as in "I'm not invoicing Henderson for my soul" and his cooling-coffee experiment ("Timing how fast it cools"). Weakness: it runs long and keeps reaching for charm. "Marcus had worked there four months and already believed he was the office gossip columnist" is an authorial aside, and "Oh. Right. He was hungry. I was convenient." arrives too neatly. It also adds figures (the man "with one hand raised, palm out") to what should be an invisible attack.

**C.** Strength: Mira's eye is a photographer's and it shapes the scene. "If I had been shooting him, I would have moved him two feet toward the glass." Jason's "So you keep the mistake and build the picture around it" shows he understands her. Weakness: the film monologue is one enormous paragraph ("The Pentax was heavier than it looked... I kept not opening them"), and some lines are awkward, such as "But you're good with me... You can't be that bad with people."

**D.** Strength: the specific noticing is good ("He had gotten his hair cut... shorter at the back", and the lamp that "lit his face from straight above, the way an on-camera flash does"). Weakness: the dialogue breaks logic. Mira says "I'm sorry... I've talked for five minutes straight" and the reply "Most people are boring" doesn't answer it. "I have to get home early Sunday" comes from nowhere. The clock-stamped "It was 5:47. I had checked my phone two minutes before." feels cute.

**F.** Strength: it is lean and clear, with a clean arc and a restrained attack, and the exchange "I'm not going to talk about it." / "Then we won't" feels real. Weakness: it is thin and leaves a lot of the world unseen. It also has a cliche at the climax ("I was going to die. I was sure of it") and an illogical line ("Nora had called that afternoon, convinced she'd be lonely in Chicago").

**A.** Strength: it is readable and gets the beats right, including the small hovering hand ("stopped six inches from my elbow, hovering in the cold air"). Weakness: the dialogue is stiff and exposition-heavy. In "Nora... Mom called yesterday... Dad mailed me another grad school application" she recites her family on cue, and "Mira, you still here?" opens a scene that is mostly a checklist. It is generic, with little that only this person would notice.

**B.** Strength: it has the most granular noticing (the napkin folded under the short table leg, the hair half out of its clip in the elevator doors). Weakness: it is the sloppiest. Speakers get scrambled, so Jason's line "I've been shooting this expired stock from eBay" is plainly Mira's. There is a tense slip ("The grain on that one is incredible"). "I got back into it, tucked the phone away" is unclear. The park paragraph is a catalogue of details (jogger, bike chain, fireplace, plaque) that never settles on one thing a person would actually notice.

## Verdict

E is the best of the six but is not yet publishable. It is perhaps a strong second draft. It gives the clearest sense of a particular woman and a particular man, and the ordinary evening feels worth losing. The single biggest thing holding it back is that nearly every beat is pre-packaged: Mira's talk and her recited family details exist to tick off the brief's checklist rather than to come from her, and the dialogue exchanges are tidy and quippy where real friends would be messier and quieter. The shared lines ("He remembered everything except to ever ask me on an actual date", the "You're different" / "Different how?" beat) are on-the-nose in all six drafts. It needs trimming by a third, with the cute riffs cut back to the best two or three. The attack should also stay invisible, as the premise says, rather than adding a man in a doorway.
