### Chapter 1: summary

- **Grade:** 4/10 as prose a published novel could contain.
- **One paragraph:** The chapter is clean at the surface. There are no em dashes, the timeline is mostly present, and the attack paragraph is competent. But it reads as an outline walked through in order. Scene 2's film monologue is one summarised paragraph that recites Pentax, green cast, undeveloped roll, darkroom, Nora in the order the character sheet lists them. The family items (milk, Jess, Dad, "do your parents...") are dropped in as clauses with no cause in the conversation. Marcus is a delivery device for background Mira already knows. Jason, who per CHARACTERS has "no tics or props: no fidgeting", turns his coffee cup a quarter turn twice and wraps his hands round it once. The key emotional beat (the gift) is a summary paragraph with a broken line ("Thank you." I said it again) and an explanatory tail ("which he couldn't possibly know"). Mira's own thoughts keep telling the reader what her feelings are ("Two years of careful distance had made the situation clear enough"), which the outline said to avoid. The attack's geometry is wrong (she turns to face Jason, then the force hits her chest). The chapter is also short, and the missing length is exactly the observed texture the outline asked for.

### Findings

#### 1. Wrong story facts

1. **"He turned his cup a quarter turn on the table."** and later **"Jason turned his cup another quarter turn and kept his attention on me."** Also **"He wrapped both hands around his black coffee... held it the way people do when the cup is mostly for their hands."** CHARACTERS.md: "He has no tics or props: no fidgeting, no habits added to make him seem real." This is an invented tic, repeated. A good writer would give him something to do that has a purpose (stirring nothing is not it): drinking, answering, looking at her photos, or just sitting still.
2. **"But you're good with me," I said. "You can't be that bad with people."** Nothing in CHARACTERS.md or the chapter says Jason is bad with people; he is "pleasant and a little distant" and "not socially awkward; he's guarded." Marcus calls him "mysterious", which is a different thing. Mira has no basis for the line. A real person fishing here would say something about herself ("I get lonely, and I have people") and let "You're different" answer that.
3. **"Marcus: It's half four on a Friday."** and **"Come on. Some of us are getting drinks at Murphy's."** "Half four" is a British idiom; a coworker in Columbus, Ohio would say "four thirty" or "it's four thirty". The narration already says "half past four". Use "It's four thirty on a Friday."
4. **"I developed a batch Wednesday night in the bathroom with the fan running"** against **"I'd been shooting a roll of expired film"** and **"There was another roll still in the camera at home."** She bought the camera a month ago and has one roll in it, so what is a "batch"? And if she already develops and scans at home, the darkroom (which is only for printing) stops being the thing she can't afford. This is invented because the documents never say how she gets film developed (see Documents).
5. **"There was also a darkroom space over on Ninth Street... The owner rented benches by the hour. It cost more each month than I spent on groceries"** Hourly rental and a monthly cost contradict. Either it is a membership or it is by the hour. If by the hour she could buy two hours.
6. **"I put my phone in my coat pocket"** (Scene 1), then the phone is taken out at 5:45 and never put back. Not wrong, but a Chapter 2 contradiction waiting to happen (see fact log).
7. **"On Monday Jason had asked whether I was free tonight instead"** "Tonight" said on Monday, and "instead" of what? Tuesday of this week has already happened. Say "On Monday Jason had asked whether I was free Friday."

#### 2. Characters built from traits, not lives

1. **"Do your parents ever ask what you're doing with your life?" I asked him.** Mira has just heard "No occasion." and thought about milk. The question has no cause in the scene; it exists because the outline says "She asks about his parents." A real person would tie it to her own mother's questions at that moment, or to something Jason said. As written it is also a heavy, odd question to put to a friend over a latte.
2. **"I had promised my mom I'd bring milk to Sunday dinner, and I had already forgotten it once this week. I thought about that and didn't say so."** No stimulus. A thought about milk appears in the middle of a conversation about why Jason asked for Friday. It is there because the outline lists "the milk". Also "forgotten it once this week" is unclear (forgotten to buy it? Sunday is two days away).
3. **"I'd show Jess when I got home."** Why would she show a roommate a box of film? This is the Jess clause, bolted onto the end of the verbatim date line and blunting it.
4. **Marcus:** **"You meet him every Tuesday for coffee. You talk about him constantly. You skipped girls' night last month to have dinner with him." He counted the items off on his fingers.** Mira knows all of this; it is recited for the reader. Marcus has no life beyond being the person who says it (see 8).
5. **Jason as perfect listener:** **"So you keep the mistake and build the picture around it."** Then **"Nobody had ever put it that way to me."** Every Jason line in the film conversation is wise-and-attentive, and the line gets underlined by Mira. The outline asked for "one observation of his own" and the draft supplies a quotable aphorism.
6. **"I had been thinking about that all afternoon, in pieces, and I didn't know how to bring it up."** She was not shown thinking about Chicago at the office (she was on Jason's Friday request, and "read the same paragraph three times"). And "bring it up" has no clear referent: Chicago, or the loneliness question that follows? The line exists to motivate the outline's "working up to asking him something personal".

#### 3. Manufactured quirks and props

1. **Cups:** Jason "wrapped both hands around his black coffee", "turned his cup a quarter turn", "turned his cup another quarter turn"; Mira "picked up my cup and found it was nearly empty", "I put my cup down". That is five cup gestures in one scene. STYLE.md: "No object or gesture appears more than twice."
2. **"I saved the file and closed the laptop halfway."** A halfway-closed laptop is a gesture invented to say she's reluctant. Also **"slung my crossbody bag across my body"** (redundant), **"the strap settling where it always rubbed my left shoulder"** (invented habit that is never used).
3. **"He counted the items off on his fingers."** A gesture to dress a recitation.
4. **"the strap looped over the back the way he always left it."** A habit invented for Jason's backpack. (CHARACTERS: no habits added to make him seem real.)
5. **"The espresso machine hissed behind the counter."** and **"the man at the book club table looked up and then looked back down at his pastries."** Gap-fillers. Background sound and a stranger's glance placed so the dialogue has something between short lines.
6. **"Scarf":** **"He came back before I had my scarf off"** introduces a scarf that is never put back on or mentioned again. It is an extra carried item, which the outline forbids ("Give Mira anything to carry... other than the bag, the phone and the film").
7. **Green:** the office light turns jaws "a faint, unhealthy green", the film "leaning green", the bridge has "that sick green cast", "the green was still there", "The green is the part I like". Five green mentions. The outline offered the bridge photo as optional texture; the draft also gave the office a green tint and made it a motif.

#### 7. Scene logic that falls apart when traced

1. **Attack geometry.** **"I stopped, half a step ahead of him, and turned around."** Then **"A flash of blue light opened right in front of us... bright enough to throw color across the side of Jason's face. Something slammed into it. The force came through the light and landed square in my chest."** If she has turned to face Jason, the thing "in front of us" is behind her and the force would hit her back; and a flash behind her would light the whole of his face, not "the side". Fix by keeping her facing forward, or by having the force hit her back.
2. **Mira's bag is never picked up.** **"I took our table by the window and hung my bag over the back of my chair."** At the exit: **"I stood, buttoned my coat, and watched him lift the backpack onto one shoulder."** In Scene 3 the crossbody bag is never mentioned. RULES.md says it comes through the teleport; the draft implies she left it in the café. (The scarf is also taken off and never worn again.)
3. **"A car came through the intersection ahead of us. A couple crossed in front of it, laughing... We waited on the curb until they reached the other side."** Why did they wait? The couple crossed in front of a car (so the car stopped), and Mira and Jason did not cross. The sentence implies a pause that has no purpose except to make room for the time check.
4. **"I took my phone out of my pocket and checked the time. It was quarter to six, and the restaurant still wasn't in sight."** She has no reason to check the time (nobody is late) and the second half is the outline's note about the street copied into the narration. Neither she nor the reader has been told there's a reservation.
5. **Dialogue that does not answer.**
   - "Sorry... I've been talking your ear off about film for ten minutes." / "Most people are boring." The apology never says "boring", so the reply parses only because the reader has the outline. Then "What's the occasion?" jumps the subject immediately.
   - "Jason, you didn't have to do that." followed by **"'Thank you.' I said it again"**, but she has not said it before in the gift exchange. A broken line.
   - "That's too bad. It sounded like a good setup." Jason ignores the grad-school comment Mira had just raised.
6. **Quantity of the monologue.** The outline asks for "at least a full page of her talking, with Jason asking one follow-up and adding one observation". The draft is a single paragraph of about 330 words of indirect summary. The scene is told, not played.
7. **Unfulfilled beat:** "Once in this scene Jason volunteers something Mira told him weeks ago (a detail, not a compliment), without being asked." The nearest line is "Have you looked at that darkroom again?" but she told him about the darkroom minutes earlier in this scene (and "sounded like a good setup" suggests he is hearing it fresh). The gift speech is the gift beat, not this one.
8. **Dusk and the clock.** Sunset in Columbus on Feb 9 is about 5:50 pm. "The streetlights had come on while we were inside" at 5:40 is possible under heavy cloud (the draft sets this up with the grey sky at 4:30) but borderline; the grey sky on landing needs Ch 2 to choose a Tennessee time zone/light level (see fact log).
9. **Time on the page.** The attack time (about 5:50) is not stated; the reader only has 5:40 (clock) and "quarter to six" (phone). Fine for the reader; Chapter 2 needs the number.
10. **Jason: "We don't really talk."** Does not answer "Do your parents ever ask what you're doing with your life?" so much as sidestep; that's in character, but it then repeats as "We didn't really get along" (see 5).

### What works

1. **"Maintenance had come by twice already. Both times they had stood under the tubes with their hands on their hips and promised a work order."** A specific, observed detail that does the job of a joke without being one.
2. **"Under their orange light the blue had drained out of Jason's coat, and the grass along the park looked brown and flat."** What the light does to a colour is exactly what this narrator would notice, and it is neither a metaphor nor a joke.
3. **"My heel caught on a heave in the pavement, and I stumbled."** The stumble has a cause, rather than being a stock clumsy moment.
4. **"I asked him how old it was and whether the shop had kept it in a fridge"** (up to "fridge"): over-excited and specific to someone who has just started shooting expired film. The three sentences after it ruin it.
5. **"The wind came off the grass smelling of wet dirt and somebody's woodstove."** A concrete smell that places the scene and suits the season.
