# Round 5, Chapter 1: critic report

### Chapter 1: summary
- **Grade:** 4/10 as prose a published novel could contain.
- **One paragraph:** The chapter has a few real observations (the flickering tubes and the maintenance men, the co-op's safelight and drain, the bulbs that put an edge on every spoon), but most of it is outline beats performed in outline order. The film talk is the clearest case. It recites Pentax, green cast, undeveloped roll, apology and co-op in the outline's sequence, and it breaks down on the page. Speakers are unattributed, a narration line slips into present tense, and Mira apologises for a monologue she never gave. Three of the four attack sentences are close restatements of the outline's wording. The walk is padded with a catalogue of street objects (jogger, car, bike, bus, groceries) that exist only to fill the gaps between lines of dialogue. Scene 2 is about 1,000 words against a target of 1,300, while Scene 1 is about 650 against 600. The total is 2,300 against 2,700, and the missing length is in the scene that needed the texture. The Friday question, which is Mira's want in Scenes 1 and 2, never appears.

Approximate scene lengths by my count: Scene 1 653, Scene 2 1,004, Scene 3 641. Metrics file: 2,300 words, 30% of sentences under 6 words (24% in narration), longest short-sentence run 3, 13 one-line paragraphs under 6 words, no em dashes, no body-language clichés, "two years" once.

### Findings

#### 1. Wrong story facts (outline, CHARACTERS, RULES)

- **The Friday question is missing.** Outline Scene 1 and 2: "Mira wants ... to stop wondering why Jason asked for a Friday" and "to know why tonight". The draft has Jason text "Still on for coffee at five?" and Marcus say "You meet him every Tuesday", and Mira never registers that it is not Tuesday. She never wonders why tonight, in the office or in the shop. The gift therefore has no question to answer. Fix: one concrete beat in Scene 1, such as her reading Jason's text twice in the Tuesday slot of her calendar, or Marcus getting the day wrong.
- **Marcus's beat is changed.** Outline: "He tells her the time in his own words and says she should go if she is meeting Jason at five." Draft: "Come on, a few of us are going to Murphy's. You should come." Marcus invites her to a bar, and Mira brings up Jason as her excuse ("I can't. I've got coffee with Jason."). The outline has Marcus raise Jason and send her off to him. The effect is a different scene, and the "why tonight" thread is lost with it.
- **Dave is not at the next desk.** Outline: "Dave at the next desk". Draft: "Somewhere three cubicles down, Dave was eating baby carrots". Minor, but the outline's fact is changed.
- **The phone is never put in the coat pocket.** Outline beat 4: "puts the phone in her coat pocket". Draft: "pulled on my coat, wound my scarf twice around my neck, tucked it into a pocket, and got the crossbody over my head". "It" is the scarf, which has just been wound round her neck. The phone appears in the shop with no source (see problem 7).
- **The talk across the table is far thinner than the outline.** Outline: she "talks across several exchanges ... and her own tangents" and runs on until she must apologise. Draft: roughly fifteen short lines in which Mira says one sentence each time. The apology "I've been talking about film since I sat down" is untrue of what is on the page.
- **Jason's question and Mira's answer disagree.** Outline: "Jason asks what she is doing this weekend". Draft: "'Anything planned for tomorrow?' Jason asked." Mira answers with "Sunday dinner at my parents'." The scene is set on a Friday, so tomorrow is Saturday. Fix: "this weekend".
- **The co-op is a basement.** Outline: "the co-op above the print shop on the east side". Draft: "a sink that drains, which most basements don't have". This invents a different building.
- **The students' topic is not trivial.** Outline: "arguing loudly about something trivial ... It is noise and nothing more." Draft: determinism, free will and "Quantum randomness". It foreshadows the random teleport. Outline Do-not: do not let Mira "guess at magic". The reader, not Mira, gets the wink, but it is still a wink. Use a real trivial argument (who left the last of the oat milk in the fridge, which line is faster).
- **The table is a table, then a booth.** "Our table was the small round one at the back" versus "The students at the next booth".

#### 2. Characters built from traits, not lives

- "'You meet him every Tuesday. You talk about him constantly. You skipped girls' night last month to have dinner with him.' He ticked each one off on his fingers." Marcus is a function. He lists the exact facts the outline wanted the reader to learn, in a triplet, on his fingers. A real coworker says one of these, sideways. Fix: he mentions the skipped night as a grievance ("Priya's still annoyed about girls' night") and leaves the rest.
- "Marcus's grin spread across his whole face. 'The mysterious Jason. When are you two going to make it official?'" Sitcom coworker. "Mysterious" is a word nobody uses about a friend they have heard about for two years.
- "'I'm going to be insufferable about this,' I said. 'Thank you, Jason. This is so nice of you. Thank you for thinking of me.'" The outline asked for "something she does or says too much of". The drafter performed it as three generic thank-yous. It doesn't sound like Mira. She plays things down and deflects. A woman like this is more likely to over-explain what the film is, or start planning where she'll shoot it, and then catch herself.
- "I had been working up to this since the shop." Nothing in the shop shows it. The want is announced, not shown.
- Jason is fine as a person here (he turns the parents question aside and he is warm). The only weak point is "That sounds like you", a stand-in line.

#### 3. Manufactured quirks and props

- "Jason had folded a napkin under its short leg." Jason was at the counter when she walked in, so this was done before she arrived. A small invented kindness whose only job is to show he is attentive. It also reads as the "grand gesture" CHARACTERS says he doesn't make.
- "He ticked each one off on his fingers." A prop gesture for Marcus.
- "The thing in my pocket knocked against my hip with every step." Tracks the film through the walk to "use it later". STYLE: "Don't track an object through a scene just to use it later."
- "My mug had gone cold beside the keyboard, with a brown ring inside it." Fine as a detail on its own, but with the cold tea ("her tea going cold"), cold cheeks, the cold seat and cold leaves, "cold" appears five times.
- Reflection in the elevator doors: "my reflection had one side of its hair falling out of the clip, and I didn't fix it." A manufactured character moment with a non-action tag. See problem 6.

#### 4. Polished banter, and jokes in the wrong places

Jokes or banter in Scenes 1-2 (limit 4): "be wrong in a more educated way"; "I'm going to be insufferable about this"; "They have the same list. They've just split it between them."; Marcus's "No promises" exchange. With "I hated Dave. I hated his carrots." that is at or over the limit. The best of them ("split it between them") is also the one that tells you something about her parents.

- "I finally got the light meter working, which means I can stop guessing and be wrong in a more educated way." A neat closing quip on the first answer. It is a punchline, but it is plausible for her.
- The students' exchange ends on "Quantum mechanics exists." / "It literally does!" A scripted comic argument.
- No quips in the attack. That is correct.

#### 5. Not trusting the reader

- "I had been working up to this since the shop." Explains the beat.
- "I hadn't since last week," I said, and let him move us along." Points at the clue (Jason steering away from his parents). Outline: Mira "follows the change of subject", which means she does it, not that the narration says so. STYLE forbids "Pointing at clues".
- "'You remember that?' I asked. / 'You told me weeks ago,' he said." Highlights the clue, and then the verbatim line ("He remembered everything...") says it again. This is information given twice (limit: 0).
- "I read the label twice." "Twice" repetition of a gesture. The sentence also follows "They'd discontinued it years ago", which tells us it is a rare find.
- "I knew exactly what he meant. Everyone on our floor seemed to have settled on what this was." Explains Marcus's line one sentence after it.
- "'I'm just saying what I see.'" Marcus explains his own observation.
- "'We are friends.' I'd gotten used to that. Two years of careful distance had made it hard to believe anything else." Explains the longing; the pattern is the "paragraph-ending sentence that names what Mira ... concludes".
- "Something went over the whole surface of my skin, and I had nothing to compare it to." Narrates the failure to describe instead of describing.
- "The report was still open on the screen behind me." Repeats that the report is unfinished (already: "I saved the file with page forty-two still wrong"). It is also the paragraph-ending thesis beat of Scene 1.
- The bus: "A bus went past on the main road behind the park, bright inside and empty." Reads as an outside image placed after "Sometimes" to sit beside a feeling. It is the one image in the chapter that comes close to the "bird at the confession" pattern.

#### 6. Sentence-level habits

- **Present-tense slips in past-tense narration.**
  - "The grain on that one is incredible. I have a roll sitting in the camera at my apartment, and I haven't even got it out." (narrated in the present, with no quotation marks; STYLE: "Dialogue ... stay[s] in quotation marks, every one".)
  - "Mom wants to know why I'm not further along, and Dad keeps mailing me applications. They have the same list. They've just split it between them."
- **Repeated verbs and nouns (STYLE: no word more than three times).** From a count of the file: "got" 9 ("got the crossbody over my head", "I got my coat off", "I got back into it", "got the strap over my head", "got it onto his shoulders", "I finally got the light meter working", "I haven't even got it out"), "went" 11, "still" 11, "came" 9, "going" 9, "under" 9, "cold" 5, "each" 6, "across" 6, "yellow" 4, "edge" 4. The drafter also avoids repeating by switching nouns: "streetlamps / streetlights / lamps / poles", "tubes / fixture". That is the STYLE rule distorting the prose.
- **Choppy runs.** 30% of sentences under 6 words, 13 one-line paragraphs under 6 words, and one run of 3 (the metric). The most likely site is the verbatim exchange: `"You're different," he said.` / `"Different how?" I said.` where "I said." counts as its own sentence. The verbatim exemption may cover it. Another cluster is `"Do you ever get lonely?"` / `"Sometimes," he said.`
- **Stock reactions and microgestures.** "Marcus's grin spread across his whole face." "I laughed, and it came out smaller than I meant." "I winced." "Marcus stayed in the gap of my cubicle a second longer, then went."
- **Personifying.** "the flat white the office called normal"; "The elevator took its time"; "the smell of it came down the hill to meet us"; "my reflection had one side of its hair falling out". The tubes and maintenance paragraph is good; the add-ons are not.
- **Non-action sentence.** "and I didn't fix it." STYLE: "Never write a sentence whose only content is the non-action."
- **Reversal.** "Jason's hand came up toward my elbow and stopped a few inches short of it. He let it drop back to his side." This is required by the outline, but the second sentence is redundant.
- **Vague.** "For about a heartbeat the motion turned sideways." Which motion, sideways relative to what? The outline asked for a physical detail of her own.
- **Mixed registers.** "I haven't even got it out" is British; the narrator is a Columbus analyst.
- **Triplet.** Marcus's three-item list ("every Tuesday / constantly / girls' night"). Limit: one.

#### 7. Scene logic that falls apart when traced

## Observed or performed?

**Verdict.** Mostly performed. Mira notices like a person in two or three places and like a drafter working down a checklist in most of the rest. The beat order in Scene 2 is the outline's order, almost line for line. Several of the observations are generic scene-dressing (a jogger, a car slowing, a dry cleaner), things a narrator with no particular eye would list.

**The three best:**
1. "Maintenance had come by twice. Both times they stood under it with their hands on their hips and said they'd put in a work order." Specific, concrete, no comment attached; it tells you what kind of office this is.
2. "It has a red safelight and a sink that drains, which most basements don't have." Only a person who has priced darkroom space would say this, and it does the rent complaint's work without a speech.
3. "Mom wants to know why I'm not further along, and Dad keeps mailing me applications. They have the same list. They've just split it between them." A real family observation with Mira's slant (the tense slips, but the content is the best character moment in the chapter).

**The three worst:**
1. "'You meet him every Tuesday. You talk about him constantly. You skipped girls' night last month to have dinner with him.' He ticked each one off on his fingers." Outline beats delivered in a list by a coworker who exists to deliver them.

3. "Something went over the whole surface of my skin, and I had nothing to compare it to." The outline's sentence about the sensation, put into Mira's mouth.

## Proposed fixes

### Fixes to the outline (chapter level), six most important

1. **Scene 2 beat 2 is five required items in a fixed order** ("The film and the darkroom ... then apologises, and Jason says the verbatim line below"). The drafter recited them in that order. Replace with: "Mira tells one specific story about the expired film (what went wrong with a particular roll, in her own words, over several lines). She runs on; the apology comes when she notices Jason's drink is finished or the table is full. The undeveloped roll comes up as part of that story, not as a separate line." Keep Jason's co-op question where it is.
2. **Add the Friday question as a concrete beat.** Scene 1 beat 3: "When Jason's text arrives she reads it twice because it is Friday, then replies. Marcus does not know it is unusual." Scene 2: "Mira does not ask why tonight. She waits for it. He answers it with the film." This gives the gift something to resolve.
3. **Marcus: state the intent, not the content.** Replace "reminds her she skipped girls' night ... tells her the time ... says she should go" with: "Marcus teases her about Jason with one specific grievance of his own (girls' night) and one observation about her week that a coworker would actually notice. He glances at the time and sends her off to meet Jason." Remove the list of reasons.
4. **Scene 2 beat 3 and Scene 3 beat 1 ask for scenery in the gaps.** "Someone else is on the street, so it isn't empty" produced a seven-item catalogue. Replace with: "Name one other person, doing one thing, once. No other street detail beyond the park, the river and the lights."
5. **Resolve the photography contradiction in the outline.** "Photography images appear once ... Use none elsewhere" collides with STYLE's "Each scene gets one specific observation of light". Pick one rule. Suggestion: one photographic light image per chapter, placed in the shop, and plain descriptions of light elsewhere.
6. **The student argument should be trivial, and named.** Replace "two students start arguing loudly about something trivial" with a concrete trivial topic (a shared grocery list, a missed deadline). Remove anything that can read as a theme. Also give the "next table" a fixed furniture type (round tables), and replace "this weekend" for Jason's question with "what she's doing this weekend" (the drafter wrote "tomorrow").

### Fixes to STYLE, six most important

1. **"Between short lines of dialogue, put something Mira thinks or sees that she hasn't mentioned before."** Produces the bus, the groceries man, the printer whine, and the laugh behind the counter. Replace with: "Between short lines of dialogue, a gesture or observation only when the scene needs it. A volley of up to four short lines with no beat is fine if the speakers are clear."
2. **"No noun, verb or adjective appears more than three times in a chapter."** Forces synonym dodging (streetlamps/streetlights/lamps/poles, tubes/fixture) and conflicts with natural repetition of "got", "went", "cold". Replace with: "Don't repeat a distinctive word within a paragraph unless the repetition is the point. Common verbs and nouns are exempt."
3. **"Each scene gets one specific observation of light"** conflicts with the outline's "photography images once" and produced three light descriptions. Replace with: "One light observation per chapter, at the place she'd actually look. Elsewhere describe light plainly."
4. **Add an attribution rule.** Nothing in STYLE stops an unattributed line after a narration paragraph from reading as the wrong speaker. Add: "Every line of dialogue after a paragraph of narration carries a tag or an action by the speaker, and a line from the same speaker is never put in a new paragraph without one."
5. **Add a tense rule for thoughts.** STYLE says "Stay in past tense for asides too" but not for sentences that follow a quote in the same paragraph. Add: "Check every paragraph that ends a speech and begins a thought; if the thought is present tense, rewrite it."
6. **The romance-beat rule** ("Every chapter has a romance beat ... Write it.") produced the thank-you speech. Replace with: "The romance beat should come from something a character would do for this person, and be shown by what they do, not by what they say about it."
