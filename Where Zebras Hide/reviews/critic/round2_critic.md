## Chapter 1: summary

- **Grade:** 5/10

**One paragraph.** The chapter is clean and readable, with a good opening image (maintenance staring at the ceiling) and a good last beat (bare branches). It reads as constructed for three reasons. First, the dialogue is a relay of outline beats: Mira delivers Nora, Mom, the milk and Dad in one unprompted breath, and nearly every exchange ends in a short, tidy reply. Second, Mira narrates her own feelings and Jason's distance where the outline asked for them to be visible without being underlined ("He kept his chair angled a little away from mine, as he always did"). Third, the timeline is wrong in a way that damages Chapter 2's clock plant: they leave the coffee shop "just past five", not at twenty to six. The metrics back up the choppiness: 50% of sentences are under six words (32% in narration alone) and the longest run of short sentences is 7, mostly in the lonely/different/air-cracked stretch. The chapter is 1,597 words against a 2,500 to 3,000 target. That is allowed, but the outline's "Detail to use" (the green bridge photo) and the offered Jess/milk lines are thinner than they should be. Narration also gestures at the attack's invisibility ("I never saw who did it") instead of just not showing anyone.

### Findings

#### 1. Wrong story facts

1. Quote: "It was just past five."
   - Wrong: the outline has the coffee shop running 5:00 to 5:40 and Ch 2 needs them to have left "at twenty to six" so that 4:47 Central reads as an hour backward. At "just past five" plus a several-block walk, the attack lands near 5:15 Eastern, which is 4:15 Central, so the Ch 2 clock plant no longer works. It also leaves almost no time for everything said at the table.
   - Instead: leave nearer 5:40 and let the conversation, the gift and the students fill that time.
2. Quote: "Last month you cancelled girls' night because he was in town."
   - Wrong: Jason lives in Columbus and she meets him every Tuesday. "In town" implies he travels. The outline's version is that she cancelled "to have dinner with him".
   - Instead: "because you had dinner with him", which also fits Marcus's tease.
3. Quote: "Three more pages to review, citations to check, and a formatting problem" versus "They want two more sections. Two. And they still want it Monday."
   - Wrong: at the desk she is an hour from finishing; at the table she says the client has just added two whole sections. Both cannot be true on the same Friday afternoon.
   - Instead: keep one version. The "Henderson wants more" version at the table is the stronger line.
4. Quote: "A latte was already waiting across from my chair when I sat down. Extra foam, the way I liked it."
   - Wrong: Jason texted "heading over now" about twenty minutes earlier and is "halfway through something on his laptop". He cannot have timed the drink to her arrival, so it would have been cooling for a good while, and she then calls it "exactly right". The "the way I liked it" tag also comments on the beat the outline said to show "once, without comment".
   - Instead: he is at the counter ordering as she walks in, or the cup is simply there with no remark.
5. Quote: "a row of closed businesses on the other"
   - Wrong: at about 5:10 on a Friday evening in a city, a whole row of businesses closed is unlikely, and orange streetlights on wet pavement implies full dusk, which in Columbus on February 9 is after 6:00 pm.
   - Instead: shops lit and open, or leave the row out. Pushing the departure to about 5:40 helps the light too.

#### 2. Characters built from traits, not lives

1. Quote: "Under these lights, that green film would probably look..." He stopped. / "Look what?" / "It would look strange," he said. "Would you still shoot it?"

## Chapter 2: summary

- **Grade:** 6/10

**One paragraph.** This is the strongest of the three: it is in past tense, has no quips, keeps Jason calm and competent, and the frightened-but-practical Mira (screenshots, dream test, the deleted 911) is believable. The verbatim panic line is intact and the ending is a clean action. It still reads as assembled in three ways. First, the continuity with Chapter 1 is broken: the clock plant no longer works because they left at "just past five", and neither Mira's bag nor Jason's laptop (both established in Ch 1) is accounted for. Second, the narration keeps annotating things the scene already shows ("the way a person does a thing he has done many times before"; "that bothered me more than I wanted it to"; "I couldn't tell what he was doing up there" said three different ways). Third, the timer paragraph is garbled, with the timer already at zero while she keeps waiting for the first minute and then the second. Smaller problems are a pupil check that no one would do with thumbed eyelids, a posture sequence that contradicts itself, and invented habits (a therapist, "the sentence I always used to check a keyboard"). The metrics are fine (mean 13.7 words per sentence, 21% under six), but the longest run of short sentences is 3, which breaks the style sheet's limit of two.
