## Chapter 2: summary

- **Grade: 5/10.** Clean and sober, but not yet publishable.
- **One paragraph.** The chapter is tidy, has no jokes, no personification worth the name and no padding, and it obeys most of the outline's limits. It reads as constructed for three reasons. First, it walks the outline's beats in order and closes several of them by copying the outline's own explanation into the prose ("Whatever this was, the police couldn't help with it, and he knew what it was." "He was calm, which frightened me more than panic would have." "Two years of Tuesdays had taught me how he talked, and I still trusted him more than I trusted my own sense of what was happening."). Second, it has staging errors a reader will feel even if they can't name them: Mira "stayed on the log" before any log exists, her bag is in her lap and then on her back, and she asks Jason about 911 aloud and then privately agonises over the same decision. Third, the chapter's main mystery, the 4:47 clock, is illegible on the page. Mira's observation is good in about four places (the screen lighting her thumb, Jason's face in flat light, the film box against her hip) and generic everywhere else. "Gray" is used about seven times. Nothing in the chapter reacts to the blue flash or the blow to the chest from Chapter 1.

## Findings

### 1. Wrong story facts and continuity

- **"I stayed on the log."** (Scene 2). The log first appears later: "I sat down on a fallen log at the foot of the slope." Before that she is on hands and knees, then standing or kneeling, and "I looked at the damp wood under my hands" also precedes the log. A reader cannot tell where she is. Fix: put her on the log (or have her sit) before the bargain starts.
- **"Part of me wanted to walk over and make him look at me while he said it."** Two paragraphs earlier "He had stood up and was facing me," and he is looking at her. The want contradicts the staging. Fix: have him looking at the trees, not her, when he says "I need twenty minutes."
- **"Should I call 911?" / "Not yet."** Jason answers the 911 question aloud, in a line that doubles as a portent. The outline's 911 beat is hers alone, and the romance payoff is "She trusts him enough not to call." Once he has said "Not yet," her later deletion reads as obedience, and the private debate is redundant. Fix: she never asks him.
- **"I'll tell you everything,"** he said (after her "tell me everything, whether you've worked it out or not"). Jason has promised more than RULES lets him give: rule 4 says he doesn't explain mechanics to someone who hasn't started, and Ch 3 holds the erasure offer. He should promise to explain what happened ("I'll tell you what I can"), not "everything." Mira's demand for "everything" is the drafter's addition; the outline says only that he asks for twenty minutes and she settles.
- **The blue flash and the blow to the chest are not mentioned.** Ch 1 gave "a flash of blue light right in front of us," "Something invisible slammed into it," "Pressure filled my chest, and I couldn't breathe." Ch 2's concussion theory ("I'd hit my head") ignores them, and Jason's competent check skips the chest and ribs entirely. Mira would think about the flash first, and a Jason who checks wrists and pupils would check her ribs.
- **"My upper arm ached where his hand had closed on it on the sidewalk, and I didn't mention it."** Two lines earlier she answered "Yes, it's just sore," so she did mention it. The Ch 1 grip bruise is a good continuity detail spoiled by the contradiction.
- **Unsent text.** "I opened a blank message and typed: 'The quick brown fox...'" No recipient is given and nothing says she deleted it. It is unclear whether it sits as a draft or was sent to someone. Fix: new note or message to herself.
- **Scarf.** Ch 1 fact log: scarf taken off in the café and never placed again. Ch 2 never says she has no scarf in the cold ("the cold had got in around my neck anyway" only implies it).

## Observed or performed?

Mostly performed. Mira does not notice the way one particular person would; she notices the way the outline notes ask. The chapter's rhythm is: beat, beat, beat, with a transition word ("Later the phone buzzed again." "A few minutes after that...") marking each beat. She is competent and photographic in about four places, and elsewhere she is a generic frightened narrator. She never thinks of the flash, the report, the Pentax or Marcus.

**Three best**
1. "The screen was the brightest thing in the woods, and it lit my thumb from underneath." Concrete, a photographer's eye on light, no explanation attached.
2. "The gray light lay across his face and had taken the color out of his cheeks, and he looked thinner than he had across the table at the coffee shop." A face seen in flat light, comparing with an earlier look.
3. "the film Jason had given me was in there too, its box pressing against my hip through the lining." A small physical fact placed where the hand finds it; it carries the romance quietly.

**Three worst**
1. "Two years of Tuesdays had taught me how he talked, and I still trusted him more than I trusted my own sense of what was happening." Pure thesis; no one thinks this way mid-crisis.
2. "Whatever this was, the police couldn't help with it, and he knew what it was." The outline's director's note, pasted in.
3. "He was calm, which frightened me more than panic would have." A stock reversal copied from the outline; it tells us the effect of what Jason's calm did.
