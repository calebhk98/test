# Three reviewers compared

Haiku, Sonnet and Opus each read the draft in story order: prose for Ch 1, 2, 3, 6 and 30, outline entries for every other chapter, then the background documents. They wrote a journal entry after each step before reading the next. None of them saw the others' journals.

## What all three agree on

**The premise is the best thing in the project.** All three named the same core: understanding, not seeing, is what changes you (the train analogy), and Earth is safe only because crossing over isn't worth the cost. Two of them (Sonnet, Opus) also singled out the rule that memory erasure only works before production starts.

**Chapters 1-3 are padded and repeat themselves.** All three cite Jason's run of "I don't know" lines, the twelve-inches-apart beat restated over and over, and feelings explained right after they're shown.

**The rules contradict each other between documents.** Each found several of these, and most were found by at least two:
- random teleport in Ch 3 against range-limited teleport in Ch 13
- crossing cost ("not worth the trip") against the creatures that cross anyway in later chapters
- why people die within three months: predators noticing them, or the energy building up inside them
- weeks against three months
- the clone ceiling in the clone document against eight million clones in Ch 30
- "since January" in Ch 30, though Ch 1 is set in February

**Fix #1 or #2 for all three: a single rules sheet, with every chapter checked against it.**

**Priya (Ch 25) is the best-planned chapter.** All three picked it out, and all three tied it to Mira's line in Ch 3, "My mind is mine".

**The clone power belongs to Mira because of her photographer's eye.** All three called this the best character-to-power link in the book.

**Genre, blurbs and comp titles don't match the book.**

## Where they split

- **Jason.** Opus made him its third-biggest fix: he's characterized almost entirely by not reacting ("notes without comment", "doesn't praise her", "watches but doesn't intervene"). Sonnet called his voice inconsistent. Haiku said the least about him.
- **Mira as spectator.** Opus and Sonnet both say she watches rather than acts from Act III until Ch 29. Haiku put this under its middle-is-montage complaint.
- **Clone ethics.** Only Haiku raised it, and it made it fix #1: the clones are conscious, so pulling eight million of them back into one body is close to killing them, and the book never says so.
- **Act IV.** Sonnet's distinct point: the apocalypse is summarized ("a hundred million dead in one sentence") with nobody named, though Priya shows the book can do it with one small person.
- **The romance.** Opus says the outline resolves it in Ch 8, rewinds it in Ch 21, then drops it, so the genre label promises something the book doesn't deliver. Sonnet and Haiku folded this into the genre mismatch.
- **Chapter 30.** Haiku thought its prose reads like a different writer from Ch 1-3. Opus rated it the best prose in the draft (about 6.5). Both can be true.

## Reviewer errors I checked

- **Haiku, Ch 1: the "leaked generator note" is wrong.** It quotes "Answer briefly, then redirect to something about me." as an instruction to the writer that slipped into the text. In context (Ch 1, line 199) it is Mira describing how Jason deflects. The only real error there is a missing space after "me." Haiku repeats the claim in its final verdict, so discount that part of its fix #3.
- **Sixteen against seventeen in Ch 25** is not an error. Seventeen people watched the posts; sixteen were erased, and Priya was taught instead. Opus's "sixteen erased" is the precise reading.
- **"Since January" against February** is a real continuity error, and all three caught it.
- I spot-checked a sample of quotes from each journal, and the ones I looked up exist in the files. Haiku's 34 blockquoted lines all match except two it had prefixed with "Ch 1:" and "Ch 2:", which also check out. Opus and Sonnet quote inline, which I didn't verify one by one.
