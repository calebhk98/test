# Reading Journal: Where Zebras Hide (Opus reviewer)

## After 01_Chapter_01.md

- **Overall opinion so far**: A competent but generic opening. It is a slow-burn friends-to-lovers setup that turns into a portal or teleport story on the last page. The office-and-coffee material is overlong and repeats one idea (he won't touch her or open up) at least five times. The ending at least gives the chapter a turn. **Running grade: 4.5/10.** It is readable, and the hook arrives, but only after about 3,000 words of setup that could be 1,500.

- **Characters**:
  - *Mira* (narrator): an office worker in Columbus who shoots film on a Pentax, has pushy parents and a sister in Chicago. She is the most alive part of the chapter, mostly when she is petty ("I hated Dave. I hated his carrots."). Otherwise her inner life is one note, pining, and the chapter tells us the pining rather than letting it come through in behavior.
  - *Jason*: a remote freelance programmer. He is attentive, remembers her order, deflects every personal question and keeps physical distance. That makes him a pile of mystery-love-interest tells rather than a person. He has nearly no opinions of his own. His one line with any bite, "Most people are boring," is gone in a beat. His evasions are written so the reader notices them, and they are clearly planted for a reveal (the blue flash, him grabbing her). They do the job of plot setup, but as characterization they read as an absence.
  - *Marcus*: exists only to say "you're basically dating". He works as a functional device.
  - *Sarah, sister, mom, dad, Dave*: name-checked props.
  - Internal consistency is already shaky. Marcus says "You meet him every single Tuesday for coffee," but this coffee is on a Friday and nobody remarks on it.

- **Plot**:
  - Good: the touch motif pays off. A man who "never touched me unless he absolutely had to" clamps her arm hard just before the event. This is the one real setup-and-payoff in the chapter, and it implies Jason knew or expected something.
  - Bad: the philosophy students' determinism argument ("Free will is literally an illusion") is a theme announced through a loudspeaker. If the book is about prediction or free will, planting it as overheard dialogue is the laziest delivery available.
  - Logic and continuity: Jason "stood up" to order, then the next paragraph has "He got up and headed to the counter" again. "Jason didn't even ask" comes right after he has asked "Long day?" and "Henderson account?". He "closed his laptop" and later "started packing up his laptop". The report has "Three more pages", then a "pivot table on page 42". These are small, but there are many of them.
  - Pacing: the office scene could be cut to a paragraph. The real chapter starts at the coffee shop.

- **Prose**:
  - The same "door closing" metaphor is used twice in a few hundred words: "Answer briefly, then redirect to something about me.The door closing." (also note the missing space), then "And just like that, the door closed. Like it always did when I got too close". The second one tells us what the scene already showed.
  - "Something shifted in his expression. Not quite a smile, but something softer." and later "Something shifted in his face." are the same filler beat twice, with "something" doing all the work.
  - The no-touching point is stated over and over: "how Jason never let our knees touch under the table. How he always kept a careful twelve inches between us. He gave me his attention, but he wouldn't give me anything else." Then "hovering in the space between helping and not touching", then "And he never touched me unless he absolutely had to." The hovering hand is good on its own. The explanations around it are the author not trusting it.
  - "He remembered everything except to ever ask me on an actual date." The line is fine on its own, but it repeats "That he did everything right except the one thing I wanted him to do" from a page later. Two lines with the same construction and the same complaint.
  - "Oh. Right. He was hungry. I was convenient." The fragment rhythm is fine. The problem is that the "Two years of coffee, and now this" flip followed by deflation is the most predictable beat available.
  - "He was quiet for a moment, considering." This is the kind of filler beat the author flagged.
  - Grin count: "He grinned" and "Marcus's grin widened", then "He grinned, that slow, easy smile", then "He grinned and started packing up". Too many.
  - Mechanical problems: Markdown escape artifacts ("page 42\.", "20\.", "\-", "\!"), a mix of straight and curly quotes ("The whole,” I said"), a dangling comma ("I just want, " I gestured), and two lines of dialogue jammed into one paragraph ("He dropped it back to his side. "You good?" "Yeah." I was.").
  - What works: the transport sequence. The run-on "and why was Jason pulling me and what was happening and I couldn't breathe couldn't think" earns its breathlessness, and "my skin felt like it was being peeled off and put back on wrong" is a specific, physical image. The Dave-carrots paragraph is the only place the narrator has a sense of humor that isn't about Jason.

- **Predictions and questions**:
  - Jason knows what happened. He grabbed her before or as it hit. I expect he is from somewhere else (another world or time) or has an ability, and his distance is protective, maybe because touch matters to whatever he is.
  - "Where Zebras Hide": zebras are the medical saying "when you hear hoofbeats, think horses, not zebras". Maybe it means something rare hiding among the ordinary, which would be Jason.
  - Where is she? Woods, bare trees, grey sky, which fits February but could be anywhere. Is it the same time, another world, or the past?
