## 5. The format for a Haiku-ready outline

Haiku will copy the outline's wording into the prose. Every "he doesn't answer" in the outline becomes a "he doesn't answer" in the chapter. So the rewritten outline describes intentions and beats, not stage directions, and quotes only dialogue we want used word for word.

Each chapter entry will have:

- **Date, location, chapter length target** (about 3,000 words)
- **Opening state and closing state:** what is true at the start and what has changed by the end
- **Two to four scenes.** For each:
  - where it happens
  - what Mira wants
  - what's in the way
  - the beats
  - what changes
  - one concrete sensory detail to use
- **Jason's intention in the chapter**, written as a want or fear ("he doesn't want to tell her and he's bad at hiding it"), never as a stage direction
- **The romance beat and the old-life beat**
- **Rules in play**, pointing to the rules sheet
- **Setups to plant and payoffs to land**
- **Verbatim lines:** only the dialogue we want kept exactly, marked as verbatim
- **Do not:** chapter-specific traps

**Companion files every Haiku agent gets:**
- `RULES.md`: one page, the D-decisions
- `CHARACTERS.md`: what each character wants, fears, is bad at, and how they talk
- `TIMELINE.md`: a date for every chapter, clone counts, who knows what
- `STYLE.md`: the tic list from the reviews, as find-and-avoid items: "I know." as a reply, null-space beats, "Not X. Y." reversals, explaining a moment after showing it, "filed that away", "his jaw works", the twelve-inches motif, the coffee images

Each Haiku agent would also get the last 300 words of the previous chapter, so the joins line up.

One open question for later: the root `Bad writing.txt` is shared prose criteria for the other two novels. If you want it to apply here too, say so and I'll fold it into `STYLE.md`.
