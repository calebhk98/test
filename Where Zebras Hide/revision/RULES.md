# Rules of the world

This is the single source for how the world works. If a chapter, outline entry or note disagrees with this file, this file wins. Every rule here is written so a character could act on it.

## Words the book uses

- **Mana:** the energy. Jason first says the word in Chapter 3 or 4. Before that, Mira has no word for it.
- **Practitioner:** a person who produces mana and has learned to use it. Avoid "mage", "witch" and "magic user" in narration; characters can use them sarcastically.
- **The other side:** the overlapping dimension. Never "the mana world" or "the mana one".
- **Crossing:** moving between Earth and the other side.
- **The toll:** what it costs to cross. Never "the Great Filter".
- **Strays:** small creatures that cross to Earth. **Mid-tiers:** dog-to-bear-sized predators. **The intelligent ones:** beings with language and souls.
- **Stage 1, 2, 3, 4:** clone levels, used in conversation only. Keep them out of emotional scenes.
- Earth's population is **eight billion**.

## How a person starts producing mana

1. **Understanding causes it. Knowledge and seeing don't.** Knowing that mana exists does nothing. Seeing it used does nothing by itself. A person starts producing once their brain truly understands how it works. This is the train rule:
   - See a train once and you learn almost nothing.
   - See it every day and you will probably work it out in months.
   - Ride it and you'll work it out in days.
   - Take a class on it and you'll work it out in hours.
   - A child doodling can invent one without ever seeing one.
   
   Footage of mana is the train going by. It raises the odds that people work it out. Explanations are what actually spread it.
2. **Partial understanding can finish itself.** A brain given a piece can work out the rest on its own, days or years later. This is why Jason won't explain mechanics to someone who hasn't started. History, the creatures and the community are safe to describe. How mana works inside the body is not.
3. **It is permanent.** Once production starts, nothing reverses it.
4. **Onset is small.** A faint warmth in the chest, like a fever starting. Then headaches, fatigue and hunger for the first weeks while the body adjusts.
5. **Anyone can start.** Children, the elderly, people who never cared. Historically, most people who started were clever and curious, because you had to work it out alone.

## Memory erasure

- A practitioner can erase recent memories and any partial understanding from a person who **has not started producing**.
- Once production starts, erasure can remove memories but not the understanding, and it cannot stop production.
- Done well, the person notices only a moment of confusion.
- Since the witch trials it has been the community's normal cleanup tool. Delilah teaches it to Mira in Act II as exactly that.
- In Chapter 3 Jason could still erase Mira because she hasn't started. He says so.

## Scent, and why untrained people die

- A producing person gives off mana the way a body gives off a pheromone. It is automatic. Predators on the other side sense it.
- Output rises over the first months whether or not you train. Using mana raises it further.
- **Cycling** (moving mana through the body in a loop instead of letting it leak) and **shielding** (containing it) lower the scent.
- An untrained person reaches about three months of rising output before something mid-tier finds them worth crossing for. That is how most die. "Anything from a normal corpse to the body disappearing" is the difference between being killed on Earth and being dragged across.
- **Three months is the window to get quiet. The first few years are the window to get safe.** Most who survive the first few years live long.
- The same rule that protects a practitioner hurts them: the more skilled you get, the more you produce, the more you have to hide.

## The toll, and why Earth is safe

- Crossing costs energy, and the cost rises steeply with how much mana the crosser has. A stray pays little. A mid-tier pays a lot. An intelligent being pays an enormous amount.
- The intelligent ones can afford it. Their reserves grow even faster than the toll. They don't cross because Earth isn't worth it: ordinary humans produce zero mana. A blue whale doesn't chase krill one at a time.
- **Small things notice by scent. Big things weigh the cost.** Strays come because a meal is near. Mid-tiers come when a meal is loud enough. The intelligent ones come only when the total on Earth is worth the trip.
- The barrier itself never weakens. What changes in Act IV is the payoff.
- Mira's image is "seven billion zebras hiding in tall grass, and they can't see us because we don't smell like food." She uses it, and nobody corrects it. The meaning that matters is "don't smell like food".

## Why the secret has held

- When a town worked out mana together, it got loud together and was wiped out. This happened repeatedly before modern communication.
- The witch trials were a feedback loop: people hunted for magic, the hunting spread understanding, new producers got eaten, the deaths looked like evidence of witches, and the hunting grew.
- Afterward, practitioners sponsored skepticism and rationalism so ordinary people would dismiss magic on sight. This is the **science shield**. Practitioners influenced science; they didn't found it.
- Today, practitioners erase memories when they catch a leak early, and avoid telling family and friends, because telling them puts them at risk. Mira's lying to her family follows this rule. It protects them.

## The practitioner community

- There is no government, no membership and no list. People know who is strong through reputation and gossip. The more powerful have more say, but nobody can give orders.
- When someone new starts producing nearby, a local practitioner who notices usually comes to check on them and teach the basics. Help often arrives too late, or never, which is why most still die.
- There are thousands to tens of thousands worldwide. Most are low-level and live ordinary lives.
- "Top 100" is Kael's own estimate of where Mira ranks. Nobody keeps a list.
- The Act IV crisis is the first time the community tries to organize, and it does badly.
- **Mana message:** a technique for sending a short message to a person you know, without knowing where they are. Most practitioners can learn it. Jason rarely uses it.

## Techniques

Ordinary practitioner techniques:
- **Sensing:** feeling mana, your own and other things'. The first thing taught.
- **Cycling:** the first survival skill. Most people take one to two weeks. Jason took two days.
- **Shielding:** containment against scent, and defense against attacks. Practitioners can layer shields and point them.
- **Small useful magic:** warming, cleaning, making food taste better. Delilah's territory.
- **Making light:** possible on purpose. On the other side, it is the only light there is.
- **Erasure:** see above.

Jason's own techniques (only he can do these):
- **Randomized teleport.** It sends them to a random open surface anywhere on Earth: land or water, never inside rock or high in the air. Most practitioners can't teleport at all, and a targeted teleport can be blocked or tracked. A random one can't. Each jump costs him, so three in a row leaves him wrecked. The Chapter 1 jump was fully random. He gambled Mira's unshielded life on it, knew it, and didn't tell her. She finds out in Chapter 13.
- **The Box.** He captures a creature mid-fight and moves it and the surrounding area into a pocket space. Inside, a clone of Jason keeps fighting it on a loop while the creature believes it is still on Earth. It is a prison, a training tool and a battery: Jason can draw on the captives' mana. Building one takes a long time and a lot of power. Mira can send her clones into his Box but can never build one.
- **Crossing.** Jason lowers his effective mana by shifting load into the Box, which makes his toll survivable. Carrying Mira costs more. Each crossing wears him down. This is why Delilah assumed crossing was impossible for someone that strong.
- **Lockdown.** Around himself he can block teleporting, crossing and messages, up to about five miles. It is draining and slow to set up.

## Mira's clones

- Every clone is Mira. One mind, many bodies. A clone is not a separate person, and she says so when Kael asks.
- **Stage 1:** puppets with a background sub-brain. She can hold about nine.
- **Stage 2:** her photographer's habit of fixing small errors makes the bodies complete enough to produce mana on their own. Her senses merge across them.
- **Stage 3:** following Delilah's remark that cloning is creation, not copying, she builds clones mature. They arrive nearly empty but produce from the first second and fill up within minutes. She can hold about three combat-ready ones.
- **Stage 4:** clones make clones. The new bodies are self-powered and still her. The load spreads across all their brains.
- **Costs:**
  - Building takes time, and each generation adds time.
  - Holding many is exhausting, and the exhaustion doesn't go away. It spreads across the bodies.
  - Reabsorbing is the real cost. The pain grows with how many bodies, how long they existed and how much they produced. Reabsorbing too many at once can knock her out or kill her.
  - Many clones together are loud.
- **The numbers:**
  - About 110 sustained for days, rotating one every four to five minutes (Chapter 29).
  - A burst of about a thousand once, in Act III, followed by a reabsorption that knocks her out for hours. This is the "thousand clones in a field on a Tuesday afternoon" that Chapter 30 remembers.
  - Eight million in Chapter 30.
- Jason understands the technique completely and can't do it. It takes a patience with fine detail that isn't how his mind works.

## The other side

- **No natural light.** Nothing there gives off light, not even heat glow. Heat moves only by touch. Practitioners can make light on purpose.
- Everything produces mana: ground, air, plants, creatures. Being there costs mana constantly, the way staying warm costs calories.
- It is an ecosystem, not a hell. Creatures and plants have niches. Nothing is evil. Plants press on shields the way water presses on a hull.
- Sleeping there isn't safe until a person can hold their shielding without thinking. Mira reaches that in Act III.
- **The intelligent ones** have language, hierarchy and souls. If killed, they eventually come back with their memories. Strays and mid-tiers just die. This is why Jason boxes the scout in Act III instead of killing it.
- The intelligent ones sometimes send weak servant scouts to check on anything odd. The odd thing that draws the Act III scout is Jason's repeated crossings. **The scout never returning is what makes its masters start to care.** That is the engine for Act IV.

## Plants on Earth

Plants that grow near practitioners for a long time slowly start producing a little mana. Strays sometimes cross to eat them. This is why strays turn up near cabins where practitioners live.

## How the world finds out (Act IV)

- Footage alone can't spread it (rule 1). Explanations do.
- The chain: evidence spreads, people investigate, some work it out, they start producing, the loud ones die and leave more evidence, and more people investigate.
- Once enough people produce, Earth becomes worth the toll for mid-tiers in populated areas, then for intelligent ones.
- Teaching survival skills to everyone is worth it once enough people are already producing: it saves more than it endangers.
