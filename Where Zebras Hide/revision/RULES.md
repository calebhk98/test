# Rules of the world

This is the single source for how the world works. If a chapter, outline entry or note disagrees with this file, this file wins. Every rule here is written so a character could act on it.

## Words: nobody agrees on them

There is no official vocabulary, because there is no official anything. Each practitioner picked up words from whoever taught them, or made them up alone. Mixed terms are part of the world, so let them show.

- **The energy:** Jason calls it mana, so Mira does too. Others say energy, the warmth, ether, ghost force or juice, or have a private word. Some people get irritated by other people's words.
- **The other dimension:** Jason says "the other side". Others say over there, the deep, the dark, the back of the world.
- **People who use it:** Jason rarely names them; "people like me". Delilah says "folks who know". Others say practitioners, users or witches (as a joke).
- **Crossing over:** most people just say "crossing" or "coming through".
- **Creatures:** Jason calls the small ones strays. Others say vermin, sparks or things.
- **Narration** uses Mira's words. She uses whatever she learned first, mostly Jason's, and notices when someone else says it differently.
- **Clone stages** are Mira and Jason's private shorthand. Nobody else uses them.
- Earth's population is **eight billion**.

## How a person starts producing mana

1. **Understanding causes it.** Knowing that mana exists does nothing. A person starts producing once their brain truly understands how it works.

2. **Three things decide whether someone gets there, and how fast:**
   - what they were exposed to (a hint, a demonstration, footage, an explanation)
   - how often, and for how long
   - how their mind works

   This is the train rule. Two people can see the same train run once. A good mechanic works out how it runs in an hour. An average person carries the question around in the back of their head and gets there two years later, or never. A rough feel for how much of the population would figure out the train from each kind of exposure:

   | Exposure | Share who figure it out |
   | --- | --- |
   | See the track | maybe 0.01% |
   | See the train once | maybe 1% |
   | Ride it | maybe 25% |
   | Take a class | nearly everyone |

   These are illustrative numbers, not rules. Living next to the line raises every one of them.

3. **Footage can teach.** It's just less likely than seeing mana in person, and far less likely than being guided through it. Replayed a million times to millions of people, it will catch some of them.

4. **Partial understanding can finish itself.** A brain given a piece can work out the rest on its own, days or years later. This is why Jason won't explain mechanics to someone who hasn't started. History, the creatures and the community are safe to describe. How it works inside the body is not.

5. **It is permanent.** Once production starts, nothing reverses it.

6. **Onset is small.** A faint warmth in the chest, like a fever starting. Then headaches, fatigue and strange hunger for the first weeks while the body adjusts.

7. **Anyone can start:** children, the elderly, people who never cared. Historically, most who started were curious, stubborn or clever, because you had to work it out alone.

## Memory erasure

- A practitioner can erase recent memories, and any partial understanding, from a person who **has not started producing**.
- Once production starts, erasure can remove memories but not the understanding, and it cannot stop production.
- Done well, the person notices only a moment of confusion.
- Since the witch trials it has been the community's normal cleanup tool. Delilah teaches it to Mira in Act II as exactly that.
- In Ch 3 Jason can still erase Mira because she hasn't started. He says so.

## Scent, and why untrained people die

- A producing person gives off mana the way a body gives off a pheromone. It is automatic. Things on the other side sense it.
- Output rises over the first months whether or not you train. Using mana raises it further.
- **Cycling** (moving mana through the body in a loop instead of letting it leak) and **shielding** (containing it) lower the scent.
- An untrained person reaches about three months of rising output before something bigger finds them worth crossing for. That is how most die.
- "Anything from a normal corpse to the body disappearing" is the difference between being killed on Earth and being dragged across.
- **Three months is the window to get quiet. The first few years are the window to get safe.** Most who survive the first few years live long.
- The more skilled you get, the more you produce, and the more you have to hide.

## Mana in Earth's plants and animals

- Any plant or animal on Earth can produce mana naturally, with no human nearby and no understanding needed. Earth's evolution mostly didn't go that way, so it's rare and the amounts are small.
- Because they can't cycle or hide, they get noticed fast. Small things cross to eat them, and those small things are the usual strays.
- Ordinary humans produce **zero** until they understand. That is what makes them invisible.

## The toll

Crossing costs everyone, and everyone can afford it. The cost works like a progressive income tax: the more mana you have, the bigger the share the crossing takes.

- A stray might lose a tenth of what it has. A massive being might lose half.
- In absolute terms the gap is enormous, because the massive ones are not twice or fifty times stronger. They are thousands of times stronger, more like a weather system than an animal.
- So the payoff each needs is different. A stray only has to find a small meal to make the trip worth it: a mana-producing weed, a leaking newcomer. A massive being has to find a meal the size of a city before crossing is worth what it loses.
- Ordinary humans produce zero, so for the big ones Earth has never been worth the toll. A blue whale doesn't chase krill one at a time.
- **Small things notice by scent and cross for a snack. Big things do the arithmetic.**
- The barrier never weakens. What changes in Act IV is the size of the meal.
- Mira's image is "seven billion zebras hiding in tall grass, and they can't see us because we don't smell like food." She uses it and nobody corrects it. The part that matters is "don't smell like food".

## Why the secret has held

- When a town worked out mana together, it got loud together and was wiped out. This happened again and again before modern communication.
- The witch trials were a feedback loop:
  - People hunted for magic, and the hunting spread understanding.
  - New producers got eaten, and the deaths looked like evidence of witches.
  - So the hunting grew.
- Afterward, practitioners sponsored skepticism and rationalism so ordinary people would dismiss magic on sight. Mira calls this the science shield once she understands it. Practitioners influenced science; they didn't found it.
- Today practitioners erase memories when they catch a leak early, and avoid telling family and friends, because telling them puts them at risk. Mira's lying to her family follows this rule: it protects them.

## The community

- There is no government, no membership and no list. A few thousand people worldwide, out of eight billion. Most are weak and live ordinary lives.
- Practitioners can roughly tell how strong someone is by sensing how much mana they carry and how they hold it. Seeing someone lift 700 pounds, you put them in the top tier without a scoreboard. In a community of a few thousand, "top 100" is an easy estimate.
- Kael's "top 100" for Mira comes after a great deal of training. It's his guess from what he senses, and other strong practitioners would guess about the same.
- The more powerful have more say, but nobody can give orders.
- When someone new starts producing nearby, a local practitioner who notices usually comes to check on them and teach the basics. Help often arrives too late or never, which is why most still die.
- The Act IV crisis is the first time the community tries to organize, and it does it badly.
- **Mana message:** a technique for sending a short message to someone you know without knowing where they are. Most practitioners can learn it. Jason rarely uses it.

## Techniques

Ordinary techniques:
- **Sensing:** feeling mana, your own and other things'. The first thing taught.
- **Cycling:** the first survival skill. Most take one to two weeks. Jason took two days.
- **Shielding:** containment against scent, and defense against attacks. Shields can be layered and aimed.
- **Small useful magic:** warming, cleaning, making food taste better. Delilah's territory.
- **Making light:** possible on purpose. On the other side it's the only light there is.
- **Erasure:** see above.

Jason's own techniques:
- **Randomized teleport.**
  - Most practitioners can't teleport at all, and a targeted teleport can be blocked or tracked. A random one can't.
  - For Jason it isn't especially draining. Three jumps in a row is fine.
  - It lands on open surface, never inside rock or high in the air. With full range, that often means ocean.
  - In Ch 1 he shrank the range on the fly, so the farthest possible landing kept them inland (Ohio to Tennessee, not Ohio to the Atlantic). Mira had no shields; open water would have killed her. He never mentioned it. She works out what he did in Ch 13, and it moves her.
- **The Box.**
  - He captures a creature mid-fight and moves it, with the space around it, into a pocket. A clone of Jason keeps fighting it on a loop, and the creature believes it's still where it was.
  - It is a prison, a training tool and a battery: Jason can draw on the captives' mana.
  - Building one takes a long time and a lot of power. Mira can send her clones into his Box but can never build one.
- **Crossing.** He lowers his effective mana by shifting load into the Box, which cuts his toll. Carrying Mira back and forth adds up and wears him down. Delilah assumed nobody that strong could afford to cross often.
- **Lockdown.** Around himself he can block teleporting, crossing and messages, out to about five miles. It's draining and slow to set up.

## Mira's clones

**What a clone is.** Every clone is Mira. One mind, many bodies, and none of them is the original or the main one. From Stage 2 on, she doesn't experience a "main body" at all; all of them are her equally. That's hard to explain, so she lets other people think of one as her main body (usually the one nearest Jason), and doesn't correct them. Jason does this too, and she lets him.

**The stages.** Each is a change in kind. Within each stage her numbers grow with practice.
- **Stage 1:** puppets with a background sub-brain. She starts with one and builds up.
- **Stage 2:** her photographer's habit of fixing small errors makes the bodies complete enough to produce mana on their own. Her senses merge across them.
- **Stage 3:** after Delilah's remark that cloning is creation, not copying, she builds clones mature. They arrive nearly empty but produce from the first second and fill up within minutes. The precision drops her numbers sharply, then they climb again.
- **Stage 4:** clones make clones. The new bodies power themselves and are still her. The thinking load spreads across all their brains.

**The costs:**
- Building takes time, and each generation adds time.
- Holding many is exhausting, and the exhaustion doesn't go away; it spreads across the bodies.
- Reabsorbing is the real cost. The pain grows with how many bodies, how long they existed and how much they produced. Reabsorbing too many at once can knock her out or kill her.
- Many clones together are loud.

**Checkpoints** (the numbers grow between them):

| When | What she can do |
| --- | --- |
| End of Ch 8 | a handful of Stage 1 |
| Ch 12 | about nine at Stage 1 and 2, dropping to three at Stage 3 |
| End of Act II | three to four Stage 3 |
| Ch 19 | first Stage 4 chains, small and painful |
| Ch 20 | a one-time burst of about a thousand in a field on a Tuesday afternoon, then a reabsorption that knocks her out for hours |
| Act IV | about 110 sustained for days, rotating one every four to five minutes |
| Ch 30 | 200, then 40,000, then 8,000,000 |

Jason understands the technique completely and can't do it. It takes patience with fine detail, and his mind doesn't work that way.

## The other side

- **No natural light.** Nothing there gives off light, not even heat glow. Heat moves only by touch. Practitioners can make light on purpose.
- Everything produces mana: ground, air, plants, creatures. Being there costs mana constantly, the way staying warm costs calories.
- It's an ecosystem, not a hell. Nothing there is evil. Plants press on shields the way water presses on a hull.
- Sleeping there isn't safe until a person can hold shielding without thinking. Mira gets there in Act III.
- **The intelligent ones:**
  - They have language, hierarchy and souls. If killed, they eventually come back with their memories. Smaller things just die. This is why Jason boxes the scout instead of killing it.
  - They sometimes send weak servant scouts to check on anything odd. What draws the Act III scout is Jason's repeated crossings.
  - **The scout never returning is what makes its masters start to care.** That is the engine for Act IV.

## How the world finds out (Act IV)

- Footage teaches a few. Explanations teach many. Someone guiding you teaches almost anyone.
- The chain:
  1. Evidence spreads.
  2. People investigate.
  3. Some work it out and start producing.
  4. The loud ones die, leaving more evidence.
  5. More people investigate.
- Once enough people produce, Earth becomes worth the toll for mid-sized things in populated areas, then for the massive ones.
- Teaching survival skills to everyone becomes worth it once enough people are producing, because it saves more than it endangers.
