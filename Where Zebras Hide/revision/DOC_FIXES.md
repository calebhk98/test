## Global changes, for every outline

1. **Plants.** Remove every "Plant for later" section from the outlines, and move them to a new planning file, `revision/PLANTS.md`, which drafters never get. Inside the beats, write each plant as an ordinary present fact. Delete "don't flag this", "this is critical", "turns out to matter", "later in the book" and "in Ch NN" references to the future.
2. **Limits.** Delete every limit that quotes a phrase from an earlier draft, and every limit that refers to text in a chapter the drafter can't see. Keep numeric limits on patterns (triplets, body reactions), stated without examples.
3. **Quoted lines.** Lines in quotation marks that aren't marked verbatim are paraphrased without quotation marks. The only quoted dialogue left in an outline is verbatim.
4. **Verbatim lines.** Each one gets one sentence of context: who says it, to whom, and in answer to what. Verbatim lines count toward limits. Rewrite any verbatim line that breaks STYLE, using the review's suggested wording where it gives one (P2 3.1, 6.2, 6.7). Specifically:

   - Ch 23: "Both" goes.
   - Ch 19: Kael's walls line becomes P2 3.1's version.
   - Ch 28: becomes "They're me. I don't know a better way to say it."
   - Ch 22: Owen's warning stays verbatim. It pays off in Ch 30, when Jason closes a Box around her without asking.
5. **Stage directions and verdicts.** Cut "too casually", "a beat too long", "a second too long", "she notices", "he lets her", "she lets him", "both pretend it was nothing", "it means something", "make it funny", "it's funny and a little sad", "the best line in the plan" and similar. Replace each with what the person does or says.
6. **Lists.** A list of things to show becomes a purpose plus "show one" or "show two", and the drafter picks. The Ch 3 options are one exception: Jason gives them in his own words, and Mira interrupts before he finishes. The Ch 28 clone split is the other: about 110, with no breakdown.
7. **Old-life and romance beats.** Include them where they fit. Cut the ones that are only there to fill the slot.
8. **Analogies.** At most one listed per scene. Photography images about once a chapter. Reserve "weather" for the creatures.
9. **Length.** The scene budgets add up to the header length. Cut or merge thin scenes.
10. **Micro-staging.** State what the beat is and what has changed by its end. Leave the movements to the drafter.
11. **Romance.** Use the jacket once, in Ch 4. Cut "goodnight from her doorway". The Valentine's beat stays, small: he puts the mug down, says something plain about the date, and moves on.
12. **Humor.** No humor scheduled during danger, confession or grief. Cut the scheduled quips that P1 6.3 and P2 3.6 list, except P2's two keeps: Ch 16's blind line, and Ch 17's line as Jason's flat answer.

## Shared documents

- **Box (P1 1.1, P2 1.3).** The thing inside a Box that fights the creature is a construct: a crude figure of Jason made of his mana, with no mind and no senses. It is not a clone, and Jason still can't clone. Building a new Box takes a long time. In an emergency he can close an existing Box's walls around a new place in minutes, at great cost (P2 4.30).
- **Teleport (P1 4.5, P2 1.2).**
  - A random jump can have its range shaped: he can exclude areas and bias it toward a region a few hundred miles across. He can never pick the spot. In Ch 1 he made landing in water very unlikely, not impossible.
  - Short shaped jumps for groups are cheap.
  - It can't be blocked or tracked, and the Ch 13 hunters don't know that yet (see below).
- **Erasure.** Taking years is beyond almost anyone. Jason is one of the few who can, and it costs him days. Erasure deletes memory and never implants it. After a "tonight" erasure, Mira would wake at home remembering coffee and nothing after it. He would leave, and send a message saying he'd moved for work.
- **What Jason owns (P1 4.10, P2 4.26).**
  - His teaching sequence, which gets most people cycling in days instead of weeks.
  - The teleport, which only very strong practitioners could learn.
  - The Box and the lockdown, which nobody else can do.

  The harm he fears: a teleport can take a person out of a locked room. Owen's counter: there aren't enough Jasons to be on call.
- **Mira's reason to start (P1 4.1).** The attackers saw her with him and know Columbus. In Ch 3 Jason says so. Option 1 becomes leaving her home and hoping they lose interest, which he doesn't believe will work. In Ch 5 her argument becomes: if they know my face, I'm in this whether I learn or not, so I'd rather know. The romance stays the unspoken second reason.
- **Time zone (P1 4.4).** In Ch 2 she works it out within the scene: 4:52 is Central time, and that is her first hard proof of how far they went. They leave the coffee shop at 5:40, the attack is about 5:50, and her phone reads 4:52 when she checks. Cut the Ch 14 payoff and the TIMELINE line.
- **Carried items (P1 5.1).** Mira has a crossbody bag (wallet, keys) and her phone in her coat. Jason has a backpack he never goes without, with his laptop, cash and a few things he always keeps. Both come through the teleport. This also explains the cash in Ch 6 and Spain. Add the Ch 1 line, and have Ch 2 and Ch 3 keep the items.
- **The phone (P1 4.3).** From Ch 6 her phone stays off. She turns it on only in town, far from the cabin, and checks voicemail there. The Ch 7 voicemail beat moves to a stop in town in Ch 6, or becomes a memory.
- **The truck (Ch 7).** Cut it.
- **How the hunters find the cabin (P1 4.3).** Delilah mentioned "Jason's girl" to someone she trusted, and it travelled. This is Delilah's flaw, her gossip, and she says so in Ch 13 or 14.
- **Hunters' plan (P1 4.2).** They're watching and waiting for a third person they believe can pin a teleport at its start. They're wrong, and Jason knows it. He stays a day because moving now would lead them toward Delilah, forty miles away, and he warns her first. The creatures come on the second evening, drawn by months of a loud cabin, not by the same evening's timing.
- **Ch 15's yes (P1 4.6).** After Spain, the hunters can find them anywhere on Earth by ordinary means. The other side is the one place no one with a body follows. Jason says so, and says it's a bad option. Mira's yes comes from weighing two bad places, and "tired of being protected" is her second reason, said once.
- **Ch 15 travel.** One shaped jump from Spain toward eastern Canada lands them in a Quebec field. They cross the border on foot where it runs through woods, and buy a car after. Cut Wales and Chile.
- **Spain (P1 4.11).** Cash from his backpack, and a room rented off the books. No account.
- **Jason at the cabin (P1 5.6).** Remote freelance work through a prepaid hotspot, a few clients, cash. A supply run every ten days to a town thirty minutes away. Keep the brambles by the creek: he chose the place because its natural mana hides a little leakage.
