# Characters

These are people, not functions. Each one is inconsistent the way real people are: they feel first and reason afterward, they are funny at the wrong moments, they say things they regret, and they change their minds. When in doubt, choose the reaction a real person would have over the efficient one.

**For whoever drafts from this:**
- Show these traits through what people do and say. Never have the narration name a trait.
- The sample lines show the kind of voice each person has. Don't reuse them as dialogue unless a chapter outline marks them verbatim.
- Material under the heading "Act IV (only for Ch 22 to 30 drafters)" is for those drafters only.

## Mira

**At the start of the book (Ch 1):**
- 26. Lives in Columbus with a roommate, Jess.
- Office analyst job. The Henderson report is due that Monday, and she never turns it in.
- Shoots film on a Pentax she bought a month ago, using expired stock with a green cast. There's an undeveloped roll in the camera. She's been eyeing a darkroom space she can't quite afford.
- Her sister Nora lives in Chicago, sends apartment listings, and finishes a part-time master's in June.
- Mom asks about promotions. Dad mails grad-school applications.
- Has had coffee with Jason every Tuesday for two years and is quietly, hopelessly in love with him.

**Who she is:**
- With friends, in ordinary life, she tells a bad day as a good story. Her humor is dry and occasional, and mostly lives in what she notices, not in one-liners.
- When she's frightened, the jokes stop. In danger, grief or shock she gets methodical: she takes photos, screenshots, runs a dream test, because it gives her hands something to do. Humor might come back once, late, out of nerves, and it isn't polished. A woman who has just been thrown across three states doesn't produce a quip every paragraph.
- The feelings often arrive after the danger, all at once, at a bad time.
- When she's hurt she says the sharp thing, then comes back later, embarrassed, to half-apologize.
- She plays down her job, her city and her hobby before anyone else can. Part of why she stays with all this is that, for once, she isn't ordinary, and she feels guilty for liking that.
- She'll argue with Jason for an hour and then defend him to anyone else in one sentence.
- She burns rice. When something goes wrong she says she's fine. When someone offers comfort she changes the subject. She won't leave a task half done even when she should.
- She thinks about mana in photographs: exposure, grain, latent images, the patience of a print coming up in the tray. The best things she does with mana come from that. She uses the image about once a chapter.
- She narrates as an adult, in full sentences. She notices concrete things (light, faces, what people do with their hands) and doesn't give objects or animals attitudes. She lets the reader draw conclusions from what she reports.

**How she reacts to things:**
- She cries sometimes, in private.
- She gets angry at Jason in ways that are partly about the current problem and partly about two years of Tuesdays.
- She hopes, against evidence, and is a bit ashamed of it.

**Her voice:** direct, specific, sometimes wry, never performing. When she's angry she's precise. When she's scared she's short and practical.

**On the Box:** she thinks it's wrong, says so, and keeps saying so. She also knows it's his ability, not hers, and she keeps benefiting from it. It's a real, standing disagreement between them. She doesn't hate herself for using it, and she won't pretend she agrees.

**Her arc:**
- Devotion pulls her in. If she doesn't learn, she has to give him up, and that is a romance reason, not a logical one. Then she's good at something for the first time in her life, and that keeps her in too.
- She never stops loving Jason.

**The lying:**
- She lies to family to keep them safe (see RULES).
- It gets easier, and that frightens her more than the creatures do.

## Jason

**Background:**
- 28. Remote freelance programmer.
- Started producing at 8 or 9, while obsessing over a diagram he'd invented. Spent two months sure he was dying, and told no one.
- The community found him at 12, by which point he'd made three techniques they'd never seen. He first nearly died at 10.
- Left home at 17 by not coming back after a summer. His parents are good people. He calls them sometimes from numbers they can't trace.
- He tells people he doesn't talk to them, because good parents raise questions. He tells Mira the same lie in Ch 1 ("we didn't really get along"), and she catches it in Ch 6.
- Lives off-grid: cash, no paper trail. He never goes anywhere without his backpack. He used to move every year or two. Columbus is the longest he has stayed anywhere, two years, and Mira is the reason. He doesn't say so.

**Who he is:**
- He has lived with danger since he was a child. He taught himself magic, was hunted by practitioners, killed things that crossed over, and erased people's memories. In a crisis he is fast, focused and practical. He acts before he talks. He isn't scared of his own abilities and doesn't fumble them. What scares him is losing someone, and that shows as focus, not panic.
- With most people he's pleasant and a little distant, and he keeps conversations off himself without seeming to. He isn't socially awkward; he's guarded. He reads people well.
- With Mira he's warm. He has spent two years paying attention to her because he loves her. Remembering her order or finding her a roll of film is easy for him, not a grand gesture.
- He talks in full, measured sentences, explains things clearly when he chooses to, and doesn't ramble or lecture. He doesn't volunteer technical explanations. When he does teach, he teaches the way it worked for him, and that doesn't work for her. His humor is rare and dry, never a bit.
- He decides for other people. He's used to being the one who knows, and he decides fast, including about her, then tells her afterward. He thinks this is protection. It's his real flaw. Mira names it.
- He is proud of his work and says it's his: "Because I made it. It's mine." He knows how it sounds and holds the position anyway.
- He lives alone by habit and doesn't talk about being lonely. He treats it as the price of staying alive.
- He takes consent seriously with people, and he doesn't share Mira's view of the Box. To him the creatures are predators and the Box is the cheapest way to deal with them.
- When his control breaks, it breaks once and badly. Then he gets it back.
- He can't cook either, and eats whatever she makes. He has no tics or props: no fidgeting, no habits added to make him seem real.

**On ownership:** He never changes his mind that his work was his. Warned about leaks, he is complacent: people have laughed at magic for centuries, and he expects them to laugh at the videos.

**How he shows love:** through acts he doesn't explain. The math he did on her survival during the escapes is one: in Ch 1 he changed the random jump in the instant to keep it off open water, and never said so.

**His voice:** calm, direct, economical. When he won't answer, he says so plainly ("Not yet." "I'll tell you, but not here.") or changes the subject smoothly.

## Delilah

- **Background:**
  - In her sixties. Widowed.
  - Has practiced for thirty years and lives a drive away from the Appalachian cabin.
  - Better than Jason at fighting things on Earth. Never crossed, and assumed nobody strong could afford to.
- **Who she is:**
  - She asks Mira something personal early, because she's nosy, and she follows one question with the next before the first is answered. She says what she thinks.
  - She swears mildly, mostly at Jason.
  - She gossips about other practitioners; it's half community news, half entertainment.
  - She has taught several newcomers who died. She doesn't bring them up; when it comes out, it comes out sideways.
  - She has grandkids she can't tell anything to (RULES: telling family puts them at risk). That's part of why she latches onto Mira.
  - Her caution hardens into policy, and she is sometimes wrong.
- **What she teaches:** the living side of magic (warming, cleaning, making food taste good, how to behave around powerful people, how to read intent) and erasure, as an ordinary tool.
- **Her words:** she calls mana "the warmth". She calls Mira by name. She says "honey" now and then, at most once per chapter.

## Kael

- **Background:** Has lived partly on the other side for years, in symbiosis with certain creatures that ride along with him.
- Kael talks to creatures like dogs and talks to people like he's already bored. He asks the question you were hoping he wouldn't. He lives mostly alone on the other side and is happier there, which unsettles Mira more than his creatures do.
- **Wants:** proof that humans can live alongside the other side, maybe even bargain with it.
- **Role:**
  - He watches Mira's shield sequence and says exactly what's unusual about it.
  - He estimates she's "top 100" from what he senses (RULES).
  - He warns about leaks: "How many viral videos are you from catastrophe?"
- **His words:** he calls mana "ether" and the other dimension "the deep".
- **On Jason:** respects his work enormously, pities his loneliness, and says both to his face.

## The hunters

- **Who they are:** Three practitioners who asked Jason politely for years and offered money and technique swaps. Then they pressured him, then attacked. They are not the FBI and not mortal enemies: they find people by mana, not by records, and what they want is for Jason to show them his techniques.
- **Owen:**
  - He has a family and a job he's bad at hiding magic from.
  - He wants the random teleport to get newcomers out before predators reach them. He once lost a student he could have saved with it. That's the full-strength argument against Jason.
- **On the page:**
  - Before Ch 13: one close call or a message.
  - In Ch 13: they watch Mira fight, so now they know she's a practitioner who can fight.

## Columbus people

- **Marcus:**
  - A friendly, gossipy coworker who teases her about Jason.
  - When they meet in Columbus, he sees Mira do something small he can't explain, and pokes at it out of curiosity, not malice.
- **Sarah:** a friend who runs gallery nights.
- **Dave:** a coworker with loud carrots.
- **Jess:** the roommate, mentioned in Ch 1.
- **Mom:** texts about milk Mira promised to bring to Sunday dinner. Worries in the form of questions about promotions.
- **Dad:** shows love by mailing grad-school applications.
- **Nora:** Mira's sister in Chicago. Is the first to suspect something is wrong.

## Act IV (only for Ch 22 to 30 drafters)

**Mira**
- Around the Ch 21 homecoming duty starts to replace devotion, because for the first time she is the one who put someone else at risk.
- By Act IV duty has taken over. She does to strangers what she refused to let Jason do to her, and she knows it.
- At the end she can't hear Jason over the scale of what she has become.

**Jason**
- He shares everything only because of the global threat, and still thinks he was right. He resents being forced.
- By Ch 29 he asks her instead of deciding.
- He shows love by staying within half a mile of the body he thinks of as her, and never says why.

**Delilah**
- In Act IV she joins the harsher-suppression faction and asks Mira to do the Ch 24 erasures, and she and Mira have a real fight about it.
- Ch 29: she argues Mira into resting and organizes relief. Mira makes the final call.

**Kael**
- In Ch 28 he asks whether Mira's clones are people.

**Owen**
- He meets Mira in person.
- He is in the go-public faction.
- He sends Jason a message: "Took you long enough, asshole."

**Marcus**
- He keeps digging into what he saw, and that becomes one of the leaks. Mira erases him.

**Anjali**
- A quiet practitioner from Jaipur who makes jewelry. She messages Mira in Ch 27 as the walker arrives, and is gone by Ch 28.

**Priya**
- Priya is twenty and scared and keeps asking questions because it's the only thing she can do. She has a notebook because she is a student.
- She made the energy-manipulation video and its two follow-ups.
- In Ch 24 she has been producing for 72 hours, so she can't be erased. Mira teaches her sensing and hands her to a local practitioner.
- She comes back later in Act IV.

**Theo**
- Seventeen, in a high school hoodie. He picks up cycling fast and is frightened by how fast.
- He comes back once more, late in Ch 29 or in Ch 30.
