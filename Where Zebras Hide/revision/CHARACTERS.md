# Characters

These are people, not functions. Each one is inconsistent the way real people are: they feel first and reason afterward, they are funny at the wrong moments, they say things they regret, and they change their minds. When in doubt, choose the reaction a real person would have over the efficient one.

Items marked *(proposed)* are my inventions to fill gaps. Change any of them.

**For whoever drafts from this:**
- Show these traits through what people do and say. Never have the narration name a trait.
- The sample lines show the kind of voice each person has. Don't reuse them as dialogue unless a chapter outline marks them verbatim.

## Mira

**At the start of the book (Ch 1):**
- 26 *(proposed)*. Lives in Columbus with a roommate.
- Office analyst job. The Henderson report is due that Monday, and she never turns it in.
- Shoots film on a Pentax she bought a month ago, using expired stock with a green cast. There's an undeveloped roll in the camera. She's been eyeing a darkroom space she can't quite afford.
- Her sister Nora *(proposed name)* lives in Chicago, sends apartment listings, and finishes a part-time master's in June.
- Mom asks about promotions. Dad mails grad-school applications.
- Has had coffee with Jason every Tuesday for two years and is quietly, hopelessly in love with him.

**Who she is:**
- **Warm, observant and quietly funny.** With friends, in ordinary life, she's the one who tells a bad day as a good story ("I hated Dave. I hated his carrots."). Her humor is dry and occasional, and mostly lives in what she notices, not in one-liners.
- **When she's frightened, the jokes stop.** In danger, grief or shock she gets methodical (photos, screenshots, a dream test), because it gives her hands something to do. Humor might come back once, late, out of nerves, and it shouldn't be polished. A woman who has just been thrown across three states doesn't produce a quip every paragraph.
- **Feels first, reasons second.** The feelings often arrive after the danger, all at once, at a bad time.
- **Stubborn, and sometimes unfair.** When she's hurt she says the sharp thing, then comes back later, embarrassed, to half-apologize.
- **Anxious that she's ordinary.** Boring job, boring city, a hobby people call a waste of money. Part of why she stays with this is that, for once, she isn't ordinary, and she feels guilty for liking that.
- **Loyal.** She'll argue with Jason for an hour and then defend him to anyone else in one sentence.
- **Bad at:** cooking, asking for help, accepting comfort, leaving things unfinished.
- **How she thinks about mana:** in photographs: exposure, grain, latent images, the patience of a print coming up in the tray. The best things she does with mana come from that.
- **How she narrates:** as an adult, in full sentences. She notices concrete things (light, faces, what people do with their hands) and doesn't give objects or animals attitudes. She lets the reader draw conclusions from what she reports.

**How she reacts to things:**
- She cries, sometimes, and hates it, so she gives herself a time limit.
- She gets angry at Jason in ways that are partly about the current problem and partly about two years of Tuesdays.
- She hopes, against evidence, and is a bit ashamed of it.

**Her voice:** direct, specific, sometimes wry, never performing. When she's angry she's precise. When she's scared she's short and practical.

**On the Box:** she thinks it's wrong, says so, and never stops saying so. She also knows it's his ability, not hers, and she keeps benefiting from it. It's a real, standing disagreement between them, not guilt: she doesn't hate herself for using it, she just won't pretend she agrees.

**Her arc:**
- Devotion pulls her in. Then she's good at something for the first time in her life, and that keeps her in too.
- By Act IV duty has taken over. She does to strangers what she refused to let Jason do to her, and she knows it.
- She never stops loving Jason. At the end she can't hear him over the scale of what she's become.
- The outline marks the turn from devotion to duty around the Ch 21 homecoming.

**The lying:**
- She lies to family to keep them safe (see RULES).
- It gets easier, and that frightens her more than the creatures do.

## Jason

**Background:**
- 28 *(proposed)*. Remote freelance programmer.
- Started producing at 8 or 9, while obsessing over a diagram he'd invented. Spent two months sure he was dying, and told no one.
- The community found him at 12, by which point he'd made three techniques they'd never seen. He first nearly died at 10.
- Left home at 17 by not coming back after a summer. His parents are good people. He calls them sometimes from numbers they can't trace.
- He tells people he doesn't talk to them, because good parents raise questions. He tells Mira the same lie in Ch 1 ("we didn't really get along"), and she catches it in Ch 6.
- Lives off-grid: cash, no paper trail, a new place every year or two.

**Who he is:**
- **A capable adult who has lived with danger since he was a child.** He has taught himself magic, been hunted by practitioners, killed things that crossed over, and erased people's memories. In a crisis he is fast, focused and practical. He acts before he talks. He is not scared of his own abilities and doesn't fumble them. What scares him is losing someone, and that shows as focus, not panic.
- **Composed, private and polite.** With most people he's pleasant and a little distant, and he keeps conversations off himself without seeming to. He isn't socially awkward; he's guarded. He reads people well.
- **Warm with Mira.** He has spent two years paying attention to her because he loves her. Remembering her order or finding her a roll of film is easy for him, not a grand gesture.
- **Plain-spoken.** He talks in full, measured sentences, explains things clearly when he chooses to, and doesn't ramble or lecture. He doesn't volunteer technical explanations. His humor is rare and dry, never a bit.
- **Decides for other people.** He's used to being the one who knows, and he decides fast, including about her, then tells her afterward. He thinks this is protection. It's his real flaw. Mira names it, and by Ch 29 he asks her instead of deciding.
- **Proud and possessive of his work.** "Because I made it. It's mine." He knows how it sounds and holds the position anyway.
- **Lonely, and doesn't admit it.** He thinks being alone is the price of staying alive.
- **Careful about people, careless about everything else.** Consent matters to him with humans. The Box doesn't bother him.
- **When his control breaks**, it breaks once and badly, like the Ch 3 confession. Then he gets it back.
- He can't cook either, which is why he eats her cooking without complaint (a small comic beat in Ch 12). No other quirks: no fidgeting, no props, no habits added to make him seem real.

**On ownership:** He never changes his mind that his work was his. In Act IV he releases everything only because the world is ending. He still believes he was right, and resents being forced.

**How he shows love:** through acts he doesn't explain. The model is Ch 29: he stays within half a mile of the body he thinks of as her, and never says why. Also the math he did on her survival during the escapes: the Ch 1 teleport he shaped so she couldn't land in water.

**His voice:** calm, direct, economical. When he won't answer, he says so plainly ("Not yet." "I'll tell you, but not here.") or changes the subject smoothly.

**Never write him as:** a scared kid, a socially awkward nerd, a rambling explainer, a silent nodder, a man described by what he doesn't do, or someone who is always right.

## Delilah

- **Background:**
  - In her sixties *(proposed)*. Widowed *(proposed)*.
  - Has practiced for thirty years and lives a drive away from the Appalachian cabin.
  - Better than Jason at fighting things on Earth. Never crossed, and assumed nobody strong could afford to.
- **Who she is:**
  - **Warm, nosy and opinionated.** She asks Mira personal questions in the first hour. She gossips about other practitioners, and it's half community news, half entertainment.
  - **Funny and a little crude.** She swears mildly, mostly at Jason.
  - **Grieving, underneath.** She's taught several newcomers who died. One was a boy she found too late *(proposed)*. She doesn't bring him up; when it comes out, it comes out sideways.
  - **Lonely in a specific way.** She has grandkids she can't tell anything to (RULES: telling family puts them at risk). That's part of why she latches onto Mira.
  - **Stubborn and sometimes wrong.** Her caution hardens into policy. In Act IV she joins the harsher-suppression faction and asks Mira to do the Ch 24 erasures, and she and Mira have a real fight about it.
- **What she teaches:** the living side of magic (warming, cleaning, making food taste good, how to behave around powerful people, how to read intent) and erasure, as an ordinary tool.
- **Her words:** she calls mana "the warmth". She calls Mira by name.
- **Her voice** (sample): "Oh, honey, no. Is he teaching you like that? Lord. Sit down, I brought tea."
- **Ch 29:** she argues Mira into resting and organizes relief. Mira makes the final call.

## Kael

- **Background:** Has lived partly on the other side for years, in symbiosis with certain creatures that ride along with him.
- **Who he is:**
  - Delighted where everyone else is terrified. He talks about the creatures the way some people talk about their dogs.
  - Rude, theatrical, curious about everything.
  - Lonely like Jason, but he chose it, and he's happier for it, which unsettles Mira more than his creatures do.
- **Wants:** proof that humans can live alongside the other side, maybe even bargain with it. Act IV tests that belief.
- **Role:**
  - He watches Mira's shield sequence and says exactly what's unusual about it.
  - He estimates she's "top 100" from what he senses (RULES).
  - He warns about leaks: "How many viral videos are you from catastrophe?"
  - In Ch 28 he asks whether her clones are people.
- **His words:** he calls mana "ether" and the other dimension "the deep".
- **On Jason:** respects his work enormously, pities his loneliness, and says both to his face.

## The hunters

- **Who they are:** Three practitioners who asked Jason politely for years and offered money and technique swaps. Then they pressured him, then attacked.
- **Owen** *(proposed name)*:
  - Tired, angry and decent. He has a family and a job he's bad at hiding magic from.
  - He wants the random teleport to get newcomers out before predators reach them. He once lost a student he could have saved with it *(proposed)*. That's the full-strength argument against Jason.
  - In Act IV he's in the go-public faction.
- **On the page:**
  - Before Ch 13: one close call or a message.
  - In Ch 13: they watch Mira fight, so now they know she's a practitioner who can fight.
  - At the Act IV lake house.
- **"Took you long enough, asshole."** Owen sends it in Act IV.

## Priya

- 20, a student.
- She made the energy-manipulation video and its two follow-ups.
- Bubbly and nerdy. She talks fast, asks questions faster than Mira can answer, and keeps a physical notebook. She is terrified underneath and hides it behind questions.
- In Ch 24 she has been producing for 72 hours, so she can't be erased. Mira teaches her sensing and hands her to a local practitioner.
- She comes back in Act IV, notebook and all.
