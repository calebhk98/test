# Creatures: what they look like

Material under the heading "Act IV (only for Ch 22 to 30 drafters)" is for those drafters only.

## Design principles that follow from the rules

1. **They evolved without light.** Nothing on the other side needed eyes, color or camouflage. They are closest to cave fish and deep-sea animals: pale or colorless, often eyeless. Each one is either translucent or matte, and the sections below say which. Under Earth's light they look unfinished, as if nobody decided what color they should be.
2. **They sense with mana, so their bodies are built around it.** Expect sensory surfaces instead of eyes and ears: frills, plates, fine filaments, ridges that ripple toward whatever they're sensing.
3. **Their strength isn't their size.** A kitten-sized stray can fell a tree because it hits with mana, not muscle (Ch 9). Bodies are lighter and stranger than their power suggests.
4. **They look wrong to a photographer.** This is Mira's way in. Light falls on them badly:
   - the matte ones soak it up and show no highlight where a living thing should have one
   - the translucent ones let it through and show no solid edge

   Her eye keeps trying to fix the picture and can't.
5. **Heat only moves by touch where they come from.** On Earth they lose heat to the air fast. They feel cool to the touch, about the temperature of the air around them.
6. **Nothing is evil.** They behave like animals with niches: they hunt, retreat, conserve energy, and give up when the math stops working.

## The other side's plants

- **Hull-moss:** ankle-deep mats that press on any shield passing through, like water on a hull. It's what she feels first in Ch 16.
- **Lantern stalks:** tall, pulsing stems that pull mana from the air. They are the other side's version of plants growing toward sunlight. They don't glow; the name is Mira's joke.
- **Something that feeds on leaks:** a vine that wraps creatures that leak mana and drinks from them, without killing them. This is the one concrete ecological relationship for the merged Ch 17-18.

## The scout (Act III; intelligent, a servant)

- Roughly human-sized, matte, and built wrong:
  - too many joints in the arms
  - a smooth head with a sensory ridge instead of a face
  - a body that shifts its density, so it sometimes seems heavier than it should be and sometimes barely there
- **How it talks:** it shapes the air with mana, so the words come from the space around it, not from a mouth. Its voice sounds slightly behind and to the left of where it stands. It has learned human language by listening; it's been watching longer than anyone thought.
- It's afraid of its masters, which is the first time Mira realizes the intelligent ones have a hierarchy.

- **Nobody ever sees an intelligent one on its own side.** We only ever sense them there, as distant pressure.

## Act IV (only for Ch 22 to 30 drafters)

Wolves also cross in packs into cities in Act IV.

### The living landscape (the walker, Ch 27)

**What it looks like:**
- One colossal body, hundreds of meters tall: pale, eyeless, matte, built like the other creatures but at the scale of a hill that walks.
- Light sits on its grayish-white hide wrong, so it looks flat even at a distance.
- Phone footage shows it clearly. That's what ends denial.

**How it kills:**
- Its mana pulls in and consumes every producing person for kilometers around it as it walks. They fall where they stand, or are dragged toward it.
- Ordinary people die in its wake: buildings it walks through, roads jammed with people fleeing, fires, crushes, and the collapse of everything the dead were running.
- It crosses near the Haryana border and walks toward Delhi, through a dense, connected region, for days. India has about 1.48 billion people, so a hundred million is under 7%. That is how the toll reaches about a hundred million by the end of the book.

**How it ends:**
- It doesn't hurry and it doesn't notice resistance. Practitioners throw everything at it and barely mark it.
- It leaves on its own, full, which is worse than being driven off.
- "It moves like weather. Like a natural disaster with intent." (Keep this line.)

### The scout's master (Ch 30)

**What it looks like:**
- Nearly human-sized, the same kind of being as the Act III scout, but its master: taller, with more joints, and the same smooth head with a sensory ridge where a face should be.
- It is matte. Like the scout, it can shift its density. At the edges of Mira's sight it seems to blur.

**Why it came:**
- It's looking for the scout that never came back (RULES). It knows Jason's signature, because that's what the scout went to investigate.
- That makes Ch 30 a payoff of Act III: the thing Jason locked in his Box is why this one crossed.

**How it talks:**
- Like the scout, by shaping the air, so its voice comes from beside it.
- It speaks to Jason first, and then, curious, to Mira.
- It's intelligent, polite in a way that's worse than threats, and tactical.
- Jason says: "It isn't here." The master answers: "Then where is it?" The Ch 30 outline has the rest of the exchange.

**How the fight goes:**
1. It tests them.
2. It kills the first two hundred clones almost casually. They are bait: as many as Mira could fill in the seconds she had.
3. The next round takes minutes to fill, and the creature doesn't use them.
4. It tries to send a message home. Jason's lockdown blocks it, which is Jason's role in the fight.
5. It retreats, then commits.

It is far stronger than any single body of Mira's, and each clone dies in seconds. It loses because it is one body with one set of reactions, and the colony comes at it from every direction at once.

**Its soul:**
- The creature is dead, as far as Mira can tell. It will not stay dead: under the rules it comes back eventually, with its memories, including the memory of her.
