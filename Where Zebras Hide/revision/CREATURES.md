# Creatures: a first pass at what they look like

These are options to react to, not decisions. Pick, mix or throw out.

## Design principles that follow from the rules

1. **They evolved without light.** Nothing on the other side needed eyes, color or camouflage. They are closest to cave fish and deep-sea animals: pale or colorless, often eyeless, sometimes translucent. Under Earth's light they look unfinished, as if nobody decided what color they should be.
2. **They sense with mana, so their bodies are built around it.** Expect sensory surfaces instead of eyes and ears: frills, plates, fine filaments, ridges that ripple toward whatever they're sensing.
3. **Their strength isn't their size.** A kitten-sized stray can fell a tree because it hits with mana, not muscle (Ch 9). Bodies are lighter and stranger than their power suggests.
4. **They look wrong to a photographer.** This is Mira's way in. Light falls on them badly:
   - surfaces too matte to show their shape
   - or glassy in the wrong places
   - shadows that don't match their outline
   - no highlight where a living thing should have one

   Her eye keeps trying to fix the picture and can't.
5. **Heat only moves by touch where they come from.** On Earth they lose heat to the air fast. They feel cold to the touch, and they may steam in the cold or frost the grass they stand on.
6. **Nothing is evil.** They behave like animals with niches: they hunt, retreat, conserve energy, and give up when the math stops working.

## The other side's plants

- **Hull-moss:** ankle-deep mats that press on any shield passing through, like water on a hull. It's what she feels first in Ch 16.
- **Lantern stalks:** tall, pulsing stems that pull mana from the air. They are the other side's version of plants growing toward sunlight. They don't glow; the name is Mira's joke.
- **Something that feeds on leaks:** a vine that wraps creatures that leak mana and drinks from them, without killing them. This is the one concrete ecological relationship for the merged Ch 17-18.

## The scout (Act III; intelligent, a servant)

- Roughly human-sized, but built wrong:
  - too many joints in the arms
  - a smooth head with a sensory ridge instead of a face
  - a body that shifts its density, so it sometimes seems heavier than it should be and sometimes barely there
- **How it talks:** it shapes the air with mana, so the words come from the space around it, not from a mouth. Its voice sounds slightly behind and to the left of where it stands. It has learned human language by listening; it's been watching longer than anyone thought.
- It's afraid of its masters, which is the first time Mira realizes the intelligent ones have a hierarchy.

## The massive ones (Act IV)

**What kills a hundred million (old Ch 28).**
- It isn't a monster with claws. It's more like a weather front that eats.
- Kilometers across. On camera it looks like heat shimmer, or a fog bank with structure inside it, slowly rotating.
- As it moves, it drains every producing person in a wide radius, the way a drought kills a field. That's how the numbers get so large, so fast, in a dense region where thousands have just started producing.
- Ordinary people die in the collateral: buildings and vehicles fail when the people holding them up drop, plus panic, crushes and fire.
- It moves slowly and doesn't hurry. Everything that was producing is gone behind it.
- Practitioners can't stop it. It leaves on its own, full, which is worse.
- "It moves like weather. Like a natural disaster with intent." (Keep this line.)

**The Ch 30 early mover.**
- An intelligent one, much smaller than the weather front: building-sized.
- It doesn't want to be seen. It bends light around itself, and the camera eye in Mira's head can't hold it in frame.
- **Its defense:** it drinks the mana in any attack aimed at it, and takes damage only when the attacks arrive faster than it can drink. That's why numbers win, and why two hundred clones and then forty thousand aren't enough.
- **Its tactics:** it tests her, retreats when the colony grows, tries to send a message back (Jason's lockdown stops it), then commits.

## Open questions for you

- Do any creatures on Earth glow, or reflect light strangely, in a way that would show up on phone footage? This decides how convincing the Act IV videos look.
- How do the intelligent ones look on their own side? We only sense them there. A single scene of one passing in Act III could be:
  - only pressure and sound
  - or Mira making a flare of light and seeing a small piece of it
- Do the creatures have names among practitioners, or does everyone use their own (like the vocabulary for mana)?
