# Chapter 12: Progress

**When and where:** Around May 10 (three months in) to late May. The cabin. Delilah drives over most afternoons.
**Length:** about 3,800 words. There is no old draft.

## Where things stand

- Mira can cycle without thinking, shield her scent and sense creatures. Her clones produce their own mana and are all her. They are still useless in fights: they start nearly empty.
- Delilah, a warm, nosy practitioner from over the ridge, now teaches her in the afternoons. She calls mana "the warmth".
- Mira worked out, alone and at night, that a clone can be built mature instead of copied from her tired self. She told nobody. She has been trying it for days.
- Jason has a Box: a captured creature fighting a construct made of his mana on a loop. Mira thinks it's a prison. That's unresolved.
- Her phone stays off except in town on the supply run.
- Mira and Jason are close and not together.
- In the motel in February Jason told her the witch trials were worse and said someone else should tell the full story.

## What this chapter does

She survives the window, and learns it was only the first one. Delilah teaches her the history and erasure. Her clones become useful. At dinner, a half-joke starts an argument that goes nowhere.

## Scenes

### Scene 1: Three months (about 300 words)
- **Beats:**
  1. She wakes, realizes it's been three months, and goes to find Jason, almost giddy.
  2. He's glad, really glad. He also tells her the truth: three months gets you quiet, and the first few years get you safe. She is past the first deadline, not the last.
  3. She's deflated, then decides she'll take it.

### Scene 2: Delilah's afternoons, and the witch trials (about 900 words)
- **Beats:**
  1. Jason teaches survival in the mornings. Delilah teaches practical things Jason has never bothered with, in the afternoons. Show two.
  2. **The history, told once, through a person.** This is the first time Mira hears why. Delilah tells the story her own teacher told her, of a village in the 1600s:
     - people started looking for witches
     - the looking taught some of them
     - the ones who learned got eaten
     - the bodies looked like proof of witches, and the looking got worse

     Afterward, the people who knew pushed everyone hard toward believing magic was nonsense. Delilah says that's why Mira's science teachers were so sure of themselves, and that her people made sure.
  3. Mira's reactions carry the scene. It's one told story.

### Scene 3: Erasure (about 800 words)
- **Mira wants** never to learn this, and learns it anyway.
- **Beats:**
  1. Delilah teaches erasure as normal cleanup. If someone gets too close to it, a neighbor or a kid, you take the last hour, or with care the last few weeks, and they go on with their lives. That's how her people have kept the lid on since the trials.
  2. Mira says no. Delilah says she'll need it someday. Mira says her mind is her own, and Delilah says that's why she'll be careful with other people's.
  3. **Practice.** Delilah has her practice on one of her own clones: take the last five minutes from the clone before she reabsorbs it.
  4. She does it. When she reabsorbs the clone there's a gap, five minutes of herself she can feel the edges of and can't get into.
  5. She goes outside and is sick in the bushes.
- **Changes:** she can now do the thing she refused to let Jason do to her.

### Scene 4: Full clones (about 800 words)
- **Beats:**
  1. Box training. Jason partitions his Box so her clone can go in and fight the captured creature while his construct steps back. She can't build or change the Box. She's a tenant in it.
  2. She doesn't last at first. She reacts to movement instead of mana, always half a second behind. Attempt after attempt, until by the twelfth she lands something that counts as a counterattack before her clone dies. Before the first round she tells him she still thinks the Box is wrong, and he says noted.
  3. **The breakthrough.** Working from her own darkroom idea, she builds clones mature instead of copying her tired, drained self. They take nothing from her, start nearly empty, produce at her own current rate from the first second and fill within minutes, where her old clones trickled.
  4. She shows up to training and her clone fights twice as long. Jason stops and asks how. She explains. He's delighted, and jealous of her idea, because he can't do the technique at all.
  5. The precision costs her: she could hold about nine before; she can hold three of these.

### Scene 5: Dinner (about 800 words)
- **Beats:**
  1. Dinner, all three of them. Delilah has stayed late.
  2. Mira complains: three isn't enough, and she can't hold more.
  3. A three-way exchange with interruptions, built on the verbatim lines below. Delilah says it's impossible. Mira says she already gets headaches with three.
  4. Jason asks, mouth full, why they can't. Nobody answers him, because Delilah is already telling him not to start. The talk moves to dessert.
  5. Jason offers to make the dessert he promised and burns it. They end up on the kitchen floor in the smoke, laughing, and Mira learns he can't cook either.

### End (about 200 words)
In bed, three clones spread through the cabin, their senses in hers. She does the math on doubling it. Dismisses it. Does it again. End on the math, not on a door image.

## Rules in play
- Three months to get quiet, years to get safe.
- Once someone has started, erasure can take memories but can't undo the understanding or stop production. That's why it's a cleanup tool for people who haven't started. Practicing on her own producing clone works because she's only taking five minutes of memory.
- The Box: only Jason builds it.
- Mature clones start empty and fill fast, and produce at her own current rate from the first second. Nine drops to three. Don't use stage numbers in narration.

## Verbatim lines (keep exactly)
- "Too bad your clones can't just make their own clones." / "Yeah. That'd be nice." / "Why can't they?" Delilah says the first line to Mira, half joking, in answer to her complaint that three clones isn't enough. Mira answers. Jason asks the last.
