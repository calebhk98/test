# Chapter 14: Why He Won't Share

**When and where:** June, about two weeks. A short-term rental in a village in Galicia, Spain.
**Length:** about 3,000 words. There is no old draft.

## Where things stand

- The hunters (three practitioners, one named Owen) watched the cabin for a day and closed in at dawn. Jason teleported them out: ocean, ocean, Spain.
- Mira won her first real fight using three clones from three angles. The hunters saw her.
- She learned Jason shaped the teleport range in February to keep her alive, and what it cost him. She took his hand.
- She knows the hunters want something he made, and that he won't give it.
- Her phone is off in her coat, and her bag with wallet and keys is with her. Jason has his backpack, with his laptop and the cash he always keeps in it. They have no passports.
- Jason sent Delilah a warning by mana message before they left.

## What this chapter does

Jason's position, at full strength and at its worst. The reader should come out unsure whether he's wrong. Mira takes a position of her own.

## Scenes

### Scene 1: Logistics (about 600 words)
- **Beats:**
  1. The room is rented off the books through the baker's cousin, above the bakery, and a little damp. Jason pays in cash from his backpack. There is no account.
  2. Jason has bad Spanish and the baker is patient about it. Language is a phrasebook, and the landlady's Galician, which neither of them speaks. Nothing magic about it.
  3. A mana message arrives from Delilah, two sentences, and Jason reads them to Mira. Delilah is safe. The hunters found the cabin through her: she mentioned Jason's girl, and which hills, to someone she trusted, and it travelled. She is sorry. Jason adds that she is a gossip, and that she said so herself.
  4. ID: they can't fly. Mira asks how they get home. Jason says the same way they got here, eventually. She doesn't like the sound of that.

### Scene 2: The whole story (about 700 words)
- **Mira wants** the full version.
- **Beats:**
  1. Over breakfast he tells her:
     - Years ago, before Columbus, he got known for a few new techniques. Owen and others came to him, polite.
     - They wanted the teleport. They offered money, which he refused, and swaps of their own techniques, which he also refused.
     - Politeness became pressure, pressure became threats, and threats became what she's now survived twice.
  2. **Owen's reason, which Jason knows and tells her himself:** Owen finds newcomers, as Delilah does, and too often he's too late. He once lost a student he could have gotten out with a random teleport. He wants it to save people.
  3. Mira asks why not give it to him.

### Scene 3: Whose it is (about 900 words)
- **Beats:**
  1. They go back and forth on the verbatim exchange below.
  2. He argues for it, and he's good. Nobody helped him; he did it alone, at nine and twelve and seventeen. If you spent three years writing something, nights and weekends, with no help, and someone said they needed it, does needing it make them entitled to it?
  3. He gives a concrete harm. A technique that moves a person anywhere is also a way to take one out of a locked room, and some of the people Owen travels with are people Jason would not trust with that.
  4. Owen's side, which Jason states fairly: there aren't enough Jasons to be on call.
  5. Mira says lives are at stake. Jason answers with the second verbatim line below.
  6. **Mira's strongest argument, which is her own:** if you cured cancer, for your mom, alone, in your garage, would you keep it? He doesn't have a quick answer, and tells her so.
  7. Mira says Delilah told her people keep a lot of things that could save lives. Jason says yes.
  8. Mira points out that he taught her. He says he did. She doesn't ask why. Leave the reason unspoken.

### Scene 4: Selfish (about 500 words)
- **Beats:**
  1. Mira asks whether he thinks he's selfish. He says yes, and says selfish and right can be the same thing sometimes. He won't pretend he has a noble reason when he doesn't.
  2. **Mira takes a position.** She thinks he's wrong about Owen. She thinks if she had made it, she'd want to keep it too, and she doesn't know what that makes her.

### End (about 300 words)
An ordinary afternoon. She buys a cheap disposable camera at a tabac and shoots the village for no reason she can explain. She washes dishes, he answers a client's email, the radio plays in a language neither of them speaks, and bread smells come up from downstairs. End on an image.

## Jason in this chapter
- **Wants:** her to understand him without needing her to agree. He finds he needs her to agree a bit more than he thought.
- **Defensive:** gets prickly, then apologizes for getting prickly.
- **His view that the work is his never changes** in this book, even when he gives it away. Write it so it is coherent and costs him something to hold.
- **What he owns:** his teaching sequence, the teleport, the Box and the lockdown. The teleport is the one Owen wants.

## Romance beat
She points out he taught her, and he says he did. Let it breathe, and don't explain it.

## Old-life beat
None this chapter.

## Rules in play
- Jason's techniques are his alone: his teaching sequence gets most people cycling in days instead of weeks, only very strong practitioners could learn the teleport, and nobody else can do the Box or the lockdown.
- Erasure and teaching can't be undone.
- Cash only. No account, no ID.

## Verbatim lines (keep exactly)
- "Because I made it. It's mine." / "That's it?" / "That's it." Jason answers Mira's question about why he won't give Owen the teleport; she asks whether that's all, and he says it is.
- "Everybody's got somebody who'll die without something. That doesn't make it theirs." Jason says it to Mira, in answer to her saying lives are at stake.
- "You taught me." / "Yeah. I did." Mira says it to Jason, in answer to his argument that nobody is entitled to what he built. He answers.
- "Do you think you're selfish?" / "Yeah. I do." Mira asks it near the end of the argument. Jason answers.
- "I think selfish and right can be the same thing sometimes." Jason says it to Mira, following up his answer.

## Limits for this chapter
- Triplets: at most 1.
- No more than one analogy in any scene.

## Do not
- Let either of them win.
- Make Owen a villain. Through Jason's own telling, he's sympathetic.
- Use magic for translation.
- Turn Mira's phone on.
