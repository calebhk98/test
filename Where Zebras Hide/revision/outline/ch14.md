# Chapter 14: Why He Won't Share

**When and where:** June, about two weeks. A short-term rental above a bakery in a village in Galicia, Spain.
**Length:** about 3,000 words. There is no old draft.

## Where things stand

- The hunters (three practitioners, one named Owen) watched the cabin and closed in at dawn. Jason teleported them out: ocean, ocean, Spain.
- Mira won her first real fight using three clones from three angles. The hunters saw her.
- She learned Jason shaped the teleport range in February to keep her alive, and what it cost him. She took his hand.
- She knows the hunters want something he made, and that he won't give it.
- No passports. Her phone plan doesn't work here. Jason has euros from an account he set up years ago, "for if".
- In February, soon after the attack, the clock on her phone read 4:52 in Tennessee. They had left Columbus at 5:40 and the attack was about 5:47. She has never been able to explain the clock, and she and Jason have never talked about time zones.

## What this chapter does

Jason's position, at full strength and at its worst. The reader should come out unsure whether he's wrong. Mira takes a position of her own.

## Scenes

### Scene 1: Logistics (about 600 words)
- **Beats:**
  1. The rental is cash-friendly and a little damp, above a bakery. Jason pays from the old account, in euros.
  2. Language: a phrasebook and Jason's terrible Spanish, plus the landlady's Galician, which neither of them speaks. Nothing magic about it. Make it funny: Jason ordering at the bakery badly every morning, and the baker visibly enjoying it.
  3. At breakfast her phone switches itself to Spanish time when it finds a network, and she suddenly understands the clock in Tennessee: the time zone. They left at 5:40 in Ohio and the attack was about 5:47, which is why the phone read an hour earlier. She laughs out loud at the table, and has to explain to Jason why a four-month-old mystery just solved itself.
  4. They can't fly, with no passports. She asks how they get home. He says the same way they got here, eventually. She doesn't like the sound of that.

### Scene 2: The whole story (about 800 words)
- **Mira wants** the full version.
- **Beats:**
  1. Over breakfast he tells her:
     - Years ago, before Columbus, he got known for a few new techniques. Owen and others came to him, polite.
     - They wanted the teleport. They offered money, which he refused, and swaps of their own techniques, which he also refused.
     - Politeness became pressure, pressure became threats, and threats became what she's now survived twice.
  2. **Owen's reason, which Jason knows and tells her himself:** Owen finds newcomers, as Delilah does, and too often he's too late. He once lost a student he could have gotten out with a random teleport. He wants it to save people.
  3. Mira asks why not give it to him.

### Scene 3: "It's mine" (about 1,000 words)
- **Beats:**
  1. They go back and forth on the first verbatim exchange below.
  2. He argues for it, and he's good. Nobody helped him; he did it alone, at nine and twelve and seventeen. If you spent three years writing something, nights and weekends, with no help, and someone said they needed it, does needing it mean they're entitled to it?
  3. Mira says lives are at stake. Jason answers with the second verbatim line below.
  4. **Mira's strongest argument, which is her own:** if you cured cancer, for your mom, alone, in your garage, would you keep it? He takes too long to answer. She sees it hit.
  5. He comes back with the practical problem: you can't unteach anything. Once Owen has it, Owen's friends have it, and so do their friends. Right now he knows exactly who has it.
  6. He says "So could a lot of things people choose to keep", and she says Delilah said that. He says Delilah stole it from him. It's a small laugh in the middle of a hard talk.
  7. Mira says, "You taught me." He says he did. She waits. He holds her gaze, then looks at his coffee. She doesn't ask why. Leave the reason unspoken.

### Scene 4: Selfish (about 500 words)
- **Beats:**
  1. Mira asks whether he thinks he's selfish. He says yes, and says selfish and right can be the same thing sometimes. He won't pretend he has a noble reason when he doesn't.
  2. **Mira takes a position.** She thinks he's wrong about Owen. She thinks if she had made it, she'd want to keep it too, and she doesn't know what that makes her. That's honest, it's hers, and it's unresolved.

### End (about 100 words)
An ordinary afternoon. She buys a cheap disposable camera at a tabac, because she wants proof of a normal week, and shoots the whole roll of the village for no reason she can explain. She washes dishes, he looks at property listings, the radio plays in a language neither of them speaks, and bread smells come up from downstairs. End on an image.

## Set up in this chapter
- Her phone changes its own clock when it finds a network in Spain. It happens at breakfast, with no one watching.
- Owen lost a student he could have gotten out with a random teleport. Jason tells it in one or two lines.
- Mira's cure-cancer argument. Jason has no quick answer, and says so.
- Jason says "right now I know exactly who has it." One line.
- Jason says "So could a lot of things people choose to keep". Mira answers that Delilah said it first.
- Mira buys a cheap disposable camera at a tabac and shoots the roll.
- "You taught me." / "Yeah. I did." Let it stand, with the reason unspoken.

## Jason in this chapter
- **Wants:** her to understand him without needing her to agree. He discovers he needs her to agree a bit more than he thought.
- **Defensive:** gets prickly, then apologizes for getting prickly.
- **Funny:** the bakery Spanish.
- **His position never changes** in this book. Write it so it is coherent and costs him something to hold.

## Romance beat
"You taught me." / "Yeah. I did." Let it breathe, and don't explain it. Earlier, at breakfast, he laughs with her about the clock.

## Old-life beat
The disposable camera and the whole roll of the village, shot because she wants proof of a normal week. It comes after the argument, when she needs to be somewhere that isn't in her head.

## Rules in play
- Jason's techniques are his alone.
- Erasure and teaching can't be undone, so once he shares something, it spreads.
- Hunters find Jason by mana, not by records. An account, a rental or a phone doesn't give him away.

## Pay off
- The Ch 2 clock: it was the time zone.

## Verbatim lines (keep exactly)
- "Because I made it. It's mine." / "That's it?" / "That's it." Jason answers Mira's question about why he won't give Owen the teleport; she asks whether that's all, and he says it is.
- "Lives are always at stake. That's not a special condition that overrides ownership, Mira. That's just a more emotionally compelling version of the same argument." Jason says it to Mira, in answer to her saying lives are at stake.
- "You taught me." / "Yeah. I did." Mira says it to Jason, in answer to his argument that nobody is entitled to what he built. He answers.
- "Do you think you're selfish?" / "Yeah. I do." Mira asks it near the end of the argument. Jason answers.
- "I think selfish and right can be the same thing sometimes." Jason says it to Mira, following up his answer.

## Limits for this chapter
- "She doesn't have an immediate answer to that": 0. She answers, and loses, or doesn't.
- "He looks at her steadily": 0.
- "Maybe both. Probably both.": 0.
- Triplets: at most 1.
- No more than one analogy in any scene.

## Do not
- Let either of them win.
- Make Owen a villain. Through Jason's own telling, he's sympathetic.
- Use magic for translation.
- Say that any set-up item will matter later.
