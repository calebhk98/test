# Chapter 14: Why He Won't Share

**When and where:** June, about two weeks. A short-term rental in a village in Galicia, Spain.
**Length:** about 3,000 words. There is no old draft.

## Where things stand

- The hunters (three practitioners, one named Owen) watched the cabin and attacked at dawn. Jason teleported them out: ocean, ocean, Spain.
- Mira won her first real fight using three clones from three angles. The hunters saw her.
- She learned Jason shrank the teleport range in February to keep her alive. She took his hand.
- She knows the hunters want "something he made", and that he won't give it.
- No passports. Her phone plan doesn't work. Jason has euros: "an account I set up years ago, for if."

## What this chapter does

Jason's position, at full strength and at its worst. The reader should come out unsure whether he's wrong. Mira takes a position of her own.

## Scenes

### Scene 1: Logistics (about 500 words)
- **Beats:**
  1. The rental is cash-friendly and a little damp, above a bakery.
  2. Money: the old account. Language: a phrasebook and Jason's terrible Spanish, plus the landlady's Galician, which neither of them speaks. Nothing magic about it.
  3. Her phone switches itself to Spanish time when it finds a network, and she suddenly understands the clock in Tennessee: Central Time. She laughs out loud at breakfast, and has to explain to Jason why a four-month-old mystery just solved itself.
  4. ID: they can't fly. "How do we get home?" "Same way we got here, eventually." She doesn't like the sound of that.
- Make this funny: Jason ordering at the bakery badly every morning, and the baker visibly enjoying it.

### Scene 2: The whole story (about 800 words)
- **Mira wants** the full version.
- **Beats:**
  1. Over breakfast he tells her:
     - Years ago, before Columbus, he got known for a few new techniques. Owen and others came to him, polite.
     - They wanted the teleport. They offered money, which he refused, and swaps of their own techniques, which he also refused.
     - Politeness became pressure, pressure became threats, and threats became what she's now survived twice.
  2. **Owen's reason, which Jason knows and tells her himself:** Owen finds newcomers like Delilah does, and too often he's too late. He once lost a student he could have gotten out with a random teleport. He wants it to save people.
  3. Mira: "Then why not?"

### Scene 3: "It's mine" (about 1,000 words)
- **Beats:**
  1. "Because I made it. It's mine." "That's it?" "That's it."
  2. He argues for it, and he's good. Nobody helped him; he did it alone, at nine and twelve and seventeen. "If you spent three years writing something, nights and weekends, no help, and someone said they needed it, does needing it mean they're entitled to it?"
  3. "Lives are always at stake. That's not a special condition that overrides ownership, Mira. That's just a more emotionally compelling version of the same argument."
  4. **Mira's strongest argument, which is her own:** "If you cured cancer, for your mom, alone, in your garage. Would you keep it?" He takes too long to answer. She sees it hit.
  5. He comes back with the practical problem: you can't unteach anything. Once Owen has it, Owen's friends have it, and so do their friends. Right now he knows exactly who has it.
  6. He says "So could a lot of things people choose to keep", and she says, "Delilah said that." He says, "Delilah stole it from me." It's a small laugh in the middle of a hard talk.
  7. "You taught me." "Yeah. I did." She waits. He holds her gaze, then looks at his coffee. She doesn't ask why. Leave the reason unspoken; the reader knows it.

### Scene 4: Selfish (about 500 words)
- **Beats:**
  1. "Do you think you're selfish?" "Yeah. I do." "And you're fine with that." "I think selfish and right can be the same thing sometimes. I'm not going to pretend I have a noble reason when I don't."
  2. **Mira takes a position:** "I think you're wrong about Owen. I think if I'd made it, I'd want to keep it too. I don't know what that makes me." That's honest, it's hers, and it's unresolved.

### End (about 300 words)
An ordinary afternoon. She washes dishes, he looks at property listings, the radio plays in a language neither of them speaks, and bread smells come up from downstairs. End on an image. Cut "Maybe both. Probably both."

## Jason in this chapter
- **Wants:** her to understand him without needing her to agree. He discovers he needs her to agree a bit more than he thought.
- **Defensive:** gets prickly, then apologizes for getting prickly.
- **Funny:** the bakery Spanish.
- **His position never changes** in this book. Write it so it is coherent and costs him something to hold.

## Romance beat
"You taught me." / "Yeah. I did." Let it breathe, and don't explain it.

## Old-life beat
She buys a cheap disposable camera at a tabac and shoots the whole roll of the village, for no reason she can explain. Maybe to have proof of a normal week.

## Rules in play
- Jason's techniques are his alone.
- Erasure and teaching can't be undone, so once he shares something, it spreads.

- "She doesn't have an immediate answer to that": 0. She answers, and loses, or doesn't.
- "He looks at her steadily": 0.

- Triplets: at most 1.

## Do not
- Let either of them win.
- Make Owen a villain. Through Jason's own telling, he's sympathetic.
- Use magic for translation.
