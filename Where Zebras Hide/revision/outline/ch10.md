# Chapter 10: The Box

**When and where:** Late April. The cabin and the woods around it.
**Length:** about 3,200 words. There is no old draft.

## Where things stand

- Mira has seen one creature: small, eyeless and translucent. It felled a tree, and Jason killed it. The photo she took is still on her phone.
- She learned he'd killed several more without telling her, and named his pattern: deciding about her and telling her later. He apologized. They're close again, but she's watching him now.
- Jason can make clones. He taught her how, and she can make a handful that move a little on their own. She fixes their small flaws because they bother her.
- Her quiet window closes in about two weeks.

## What this chapter does

- Jason's best invention, and the first moral fight between them that isn't about her safety.
- Her clones change: they become her, all of them.

## Scenes

### Scene 1: The capture (about 800 words)
- **Beats:**
  1. A stronger stray crosses near the creek. It's dog-sized and low, with a broad faceless head like a dish and a grey, matte hide that gives back no highlight.
  2. Jason doesn't kill it. He fights it for a minute, then does something: she sees a boxed wall of pressure close around it and squeeze smaller and smaller until it's gone from sight.
  3. Inside, he explains, it is still fighting. It thinks it is still in the woods. A clone of him is fighting it on a loop.

### Scene 2: Watching him die (about 900 words)
- **Mira wants** to understand what she's watching. Then she wants him to stop.
- **Beats:**
  1. He lets her sense into it, as if pressing her ear to a wall. She feels his clone fight the creature, die, reset and fight again. Dozens of times.
  2. Each time the clone does something a little differently and lasts a little longer.
  3. She realizes this is how he got so good: by dying hundreds of times on purpose.
  4. She asks, "Do you feel them? When they die?" He hesitates, then tells the truth: "A flinch. Like a dream where you fall. You get used to it." Don't make that a throwaway answer. It's the most frightening thing he's told her.
  5. He is excited explaining the design, how the loop works and how it learns. It's the first time she sees how much he loves what he built. That makes the next scene harder.

### Scene 3: The fight (about 700 words)
- **Mira wants** him to see it the way she does.
- **Beats:**
  1. She calls it a prison. He calls it a training tool. She says it's a prison the creature doesn't know it's in.
  2. He also calls it a battery. She asks what it powers. He says it helps with some things and goes back to talking about the loop.
  3. **A real fracture.** He thinks she's being sentimental about an animal that would have eaten her. She thinks he's comfortable with something he shouldn't be. Both of them have a point.
  4. They don't resolve it. She asks to try the Box herself. He says no, not yet, and gives real reasons: she needs to survive a fight on her own for at least a few seconds before a clone of hers can learn anything in there.
  5. She goes to bed angry and can't sleep. She scrolls to the creature photo on her phone, types it into a message to Nora as a joke she couldn't explain, and deletes the draft.
  6. At midnight Jason comes to her door and tells her he isn't going to let it go, but that he heard her. She says that isn't the same thing. He agrees it isn't.

### Scene 4: More of her (about 800 words)
- **Beats:**
  1. Over the next days, Jason sees that her clones' mana is growing. That shouldn't be possible. He is thrilled and unsettled, and tests it three ways.
  2. His theory: she's been fixing the small flaws for weeks. The bodies got complete enough that they started producing mana on their own, the way a person does when they understand.
  3. At the same time, she tells him she can't keep their senses apart anymore. When she's in the kitchen and a clone is on the porch, she's on the porch too. Fully.
  4. **One mind.** She tries to explain what it is like, and he asks her to say it plainly. He tells her that isn't how it works for him, since his clones are separate from him. She says it's how it works for her.
  5. A small new thing: for a second, she can't tell him which body she "started" in this morning, and finds she doesn't care.

## Set up in this chapter
- The Box is a battery as well as a prison and a training tool. Jason says "it helps with some things" and moves on. Mira notices he moved on and lets it go.
- Jason says "I'm not going to let it go. But I heard you." at her door. Let it stand as what he said.
- Mira's clones are one mind. All of them are her, and she has no main body.
- Jason's clones die in the Box and he feels it as a flinch. He says it lightly.

## Jason in this chapter
- **Wants:** her to see why he's proud of the Box.
- **Is wrong about:** how much the prison matters to her.
- **Proud:** explaining the loop's design, clearly and with real pleasure. It's the one thing he talks about without being asked.
- **Hides:** what the battery powers. That's his pattern again, and the reader should notice it even though Mira lets it slide.

## Romance beat
The midnight doorway. "I heard you."

## Old-life beat
She looks at the creature photo on her phone and almost sends it to Nora, as a joke she couldn't explain. She deletes the draft. It follows the fight with Jason, when she can't sleep and wants to talk to someone from before.

## Rules in play
- The Box is a prison, a training tool and a battery, and only Jason can build one. A clone of Jason fights inside it on a loop, dies, resets and fights again.
- Jason's clones are copies of him, separate from him. He feels one die as a flinch.
- Mira's clones produce mana, and they are one mind. Don't use the word "Stage" in narration.
- She doesn't experience a main body. That's just beginning here.

## Verbatim lines (keep exactly)
- "It's not that they're like me. They're me. There's just more of me." Mira says it to Jason when he asks her to put into words what it is like to be in two bodies at once.
- "I'm not going to let it go. But I heard you." Jason says it at her door at midnight, answering her argument that the Box is a prison.
