# Chapter 16: Arrival

**When and where:** About June 28. The other side, for about an hour.
**Length:** about 3,000 words. There is no old draft.

## Where things stand

- Mira is about four and a half months in. She can cycle, shield, sense crossings and erase memories, and holds three or four full-strength clones, which are all her.
- She has beaten a worn-out mid-tier on Earth.
- She said yes to crossing because she wants to be good at this, and to see it.
- Jason held her hand and kissed her forehead just before they crossed. Both pretended it was nothing.
- She knows the other side runs on mana and has creatures. **She doesn't know there is no light there.**

## What this chapter does

Strip away her most basic sense in the first line. Make the place alien without making it evil. Then give her a photographer's act: she makes light where there has never been any.

## Scenes

### Scene 1: Nothing for her eyes (about 700 words)
- **Beats:**
  1. Her eyes are open and there's nothing for them to do. No sky glow, no edges, nothing at all. A dark room still has some light in it. This has none.
  2. She grabs Jason's arm. He lets her.
  3. **The mana.** Everything produces it: the ground, the air, things moving nearby. It isn't loud like a sound; it's as if someone removed the idea of quiet.
  4. She clamps her shields. "Tighter. Everything you have." It becomes bearable.
  5. Existing here costs mana constantly, the way staying warm costs calories.

### Scene 2: Feeling the shape of things (about 600 words)
- **Beats:**
  1. Through mana sense she starts to read it:
     - the density of the ground cover
     - different textures of different plants
     - things moving at different distances
  2. Describe what it is, not what it isn't: a whole-body sense of pressure and texture, as if her skin had learned to read a room.
  3. **The plants:** ankle-deep moss that presses on her shield the way water presses on a hull. Taller stalks that pull mana out of the air the way plants on Earth lean toward a window.
  4. Jason is unsettled too, and she can feel it. His suppression makes sense to her now: even quiet, he's the loudest thing for a long way.

### Scene 3: Light (about 700 words)
- **Mira wants** to see, just once.
- **Beats:**
  1. She asks whether she can make light. He says practitioners can, but it's expensive here. Nothing here knows what light is, so it won't help her see anything that matters.
  2. She does it anyway: a small light cupped in her palm, like shielding a match.
  3. For about three seconds she sees:
     - Jason's face, closer than she thought
     - the moss under her feet, colorless, the grey of something that never needed to be any color
     - a stalk beside her, pale as a root
  4. Her photographer's brain fires: exposure, no shadow detail, nothing has a highlight. She thinks this might be the first light this patch of ground has ever seen, and doesn't know if that's true.
  5. It costs her far more than she expected. She lets it go.
  6. Jason, quietly: "Okay. That was worth it."

### Scene 4: Something that lives here (about 800 words)
- **Mira wants** to survive without seeing.
- **Beats:**
  1. Ten minutes in, she feels it. It's about the size of a large dog, but much denser than anything on Earth. There's a ridge along its back that thrums before it strikes. It's coming straight at them.
  2. "Don't drop your shield. Match whatever it does."
  3. It hits hard, a committed strike. She staggers and rebuilds the side that took it.
  4. **A different shape from her Earth fights:**
     - She can't see and can't feel the edges of anything, so she puts a clone a few steps to her left. Through two bodies she can feel where it is, the way two ears tell you where a sound is.
     - That's her idea, a new one, and it works.
  5. She finds the gap between strikes and pushes everything through it at once.
  6. It doesn't die. It decides she isn't worth it and withdraws.

### Scene 5: After (about 300 words)
- **Beats:**
  1. "You good?" "I'm blind. I want to be clear that I'm blind." He laughs, a real laugh. "You're not blind. You're using the wrong sense." "I've had this sense for four months. I've had eyes for twenty-six years." "You'll adjust." A beat. "Your shield held." "It held expensively." "It held. That's the standard here."
- **End:** following him through the dark by the feel of his mana, the way you'd follow someone's voice through a dark house. Keep to the rule: no flashlight comparison, because light doesn't exist here.

## Jason in this chapter
- **Wants:** her to come out of this alive and wanting to come back.
- **Fears:** everything here, still, after all these years. Let her feel that.
- **The laugh:** it's his first real laugh over here. It means something.

## Romance beat
She grabs his arm and he lets her. She sees his face in her three seconds of light.

## Old-life beat
She reaches for the phone in her pocket to check the time, out of habit. There's no screen she can see and no time to check.

## Rules in play
- No natural light. Practitioners can make it, at a cost.
- Heat moves only by touch.
- Existing here costs mana constantly.
- Plants press on shields.
- Everything sensed through mana.

## Limits for this chapter
- "It's not X. It's not Y.": 0.
- "And hostile." as a one-word paragraph: 0. Nothing here is hostile on purpose.
- Light similes in narration: 0. No "like a flashlight" or "like sunlight".
- "She files that away": 0.
- Triplets: at most 1.

## Do not
- Show any creature with eyes.
- Describe what things look like, except during the three seconds of light.
- Have the creature be evil.
