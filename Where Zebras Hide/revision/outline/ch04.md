# Chapter 4: The Walk

**When and where:** Friday night, February 9, into early Saturday. Woods, a county road, a small town near Crossville, Tennessee, and a motel.
**Length:** about 2,600 words. This is a transition chapter. There is no old draft; write it new.

## Where things stand

- Jason has told her:
  - he can do things others can't
  - people attacked him to get what he knows
  - he loves her
  - he can erase her memory, but only because she hasn't started
  - most who learn the truth die within months, and most figure it out alone: kids, loners
- She refused erasure and has until Ohio to decide.
- She does **not** know how any of it works, and he won't tell her the mechanism. She has no word for it yet. Tonight he gives her one: **mana**.
- She has her bag with her wallet and keys, and her phone. Jason has his backpack with the cash.
- It is dark. She is in office shoes and a light office coat.

## What this chapter does

A quieter chapter after the blast. The world gets wider through what he can safely say. Lying starts to feel easy, and she starts deciding.

## Scenes

### Scene 1: The walk (about 1,100 words)
- **Mira wants** to understand the shape of what she's facing without being changed by it.
- **Jason wants** to be honest without saying one thing too many.
- **Beats:**
  1. Phone flashlights and tripping on roots. Cold. She is shivering in her thin coat, and he gives her his heavier jacket. He walks on the side nearer the road.
  2. He names it: mana. That's his word. Other people use other words.
  3. **Why some things are safe to say.** He explains the line he's walking: history and what's out there are safe, how it works inside you is not. Knowing there's gold isn't the danger, knowing where to dig is.
  4. **The community:** others like him, a few thousand worldwide, no organization, no one in charge. People who know, trying not to die.
  5. **The history, briefly.** Towns that figured it out together got wiped out, and the witch trials were part of it. She asks for more, and he gives the verbatim answer below. Her reaction: she has thought all of this was nonsense her whole life, and she isn't sure whether she should be offended.
  6. She asks how he found out. He says not tonight.
  7. **Checking her.** At the road he stops and asks, oddly, whether she has felt anything: warmth, a headache. He is worried the gold analogy said too much. He goes inward for a moment, then tells her nothing is there, and is relieved. She realizes he just checked inside her somehow, and doesn't know how she feels about that.
- **Optional texture:** pick none or one that fits what you've written.

### Scene 2: Town and motel (about 600 words)
- **Mira wants** a bed, a phone charger, and to stop lying.
- **Beats:**
  1. A gas station that's still open, where Jason buys a phone charger. A motel with a buzzing sign. Jason pays cash from his backpack. Two beds.
  2. She plugs the phone in and it lights up with a new text from her mother about Sunday dinner. That leads her to make the calls. Jason goes out to the ice machine to give her privacy. She calls Sarah and tells her she's gone on a spontaneous road trip and will be away for a few days. She calls her mother and says she'll miss Sunday dinner, and apologizes about the milk. These are bigger lies than the ones she told earlier that evening, and they cover more days.
  3. The lies come out perfectly, and she sits on the edge of the bed afterward, a little sick at how good she is at it.
- **Optional texture:** pick none or one that fits what you've written.

### Scene 3: What dying looks like (about 600 words)
- **Mira wants** the real version of "most people die".
- **Beats:**
  1. Jason comes back in. She is still sitting on the edge of the bed, thinking about how good she is at lying, and how many people she is lying to for something she doesn't understand. That leads her to ask the verbatim question below.
  2. He tells her: things find you. Afterward there can be anything from a normal body to nothing at all, because sometimes they take you somewhere else. She asks where. He says that's as far as he'll go tonight.
  3. She asks the other question: were the people on the street among those things? No, they were people. That's worse, somehow.

### Scene 4: The ceiling (about 300 words)
- **Beats:**
  1. Lights out. His breathing in the other bed.
  2. **Show her deliberating.** She thinks through the real choice: the apartment, the job, Nora, versus a life without him in it, and always knowing what she turned down. She argues both sides with herself the way she argues with Marcus.
  3. She doesn't decide out loud, but by the end the reader can tell which way she's leaning.
- **End:** on an image, such as the motel sign's light through the curtain, not a statement of her decision.

## Jason in this chapter
- **Wants:** to give her enough to decide well, without one word too many.
- **Fears:** that he already said too much in the woods.
- **Shows care by:** giving her his jacket and walking on the side nearer the road.

## Romance beat
The jacket. Lying awake listening to him breathe.

## Old-life beat
The calls to Sarah and Mom, and missing Sunday dinner.

## Rules in play
- What's safe to say versus the mechanism.
- Erasure only works because she hasn't started.
- Different people have different words for it.

## Verbatim lines (keep exactly)
- "There's a lot of history. Someone else should tell it." Jason, on the walk, answering Mira's request for more about the towns and the witch trials.
- "I need to understand what 'most people die' actually looks like. Not the mechanics. Just, what happens to them?" Mira to Jason in the motel, before lights out, asking what happens to the people who die.

## Limits for this chapter
- History exposition: at most one paragraph of Jason talking, then interruption or reaction.
- "Two years": at most 1. Coffee images: 0.
- Triplets: at most 1.
- Jokes: 0.

## Do not
- Explain the mechanism.
- Describe the other side or the creatures in detail. A creature is "things", and the other side is "somewhere else", and that is enough tonight.
- Let her announce a decision.
- Give the full history. He stops where the verbatim line puts him.

## Set up in this chapter
- Jason says mana is his word for it and other people use other words. He says it plainly, as a fact.
- The history he gives is short: towns wiped out, the witch trials. He stops where the verbatim line puts him, and says someone else should tell it.
- "Somewhere else" is all he says about where the bodies go. She asks and he doesn't say more.
