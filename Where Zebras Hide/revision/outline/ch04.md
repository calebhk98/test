# Chapter 4: The Walk

**When and where:** Friday night, February 9, into early Saturday. Woods, a county road, a small town near Crossville, Tennessee, and a motel.
**Length:** about 3,000 words. There is no old draft; write it new.

## Where things stand

- Jason has told her:
  - he can do things others can't
  - people attacked him to get what he knows
  - he loves her
  - he can erase her memory, but only "before you've started"
  - most who learn the truth die within months, and most figure it out alone: kids, loners
- She refused erasure and has until Ohio to decide.
- She does **not** know how any of it works, and he won't tell her the mechanism. She has no word for it yet. Tonight he calls it **mana** for the first time.

## What this chapter does

A quieter chapter after the blast. The world gets wider through what he can safely say. Lying starts to feel easy, and she starts deciding.

## Scenes

### Scene 1: The walk (about 1,000 words)
- **Mira wants** to understand the shape of what she's facing without being changed by it.
- **Jason wants** to be honest without saying one thing too many.
- **Beats:**
  1. Phone flashlights and tripping on roots. Cold. She's in office shoes.
  2. He names it: "Mana. That's what I call it. Other people call it other things."
  3. **Why some things are safe to say.** He explains the line he's walking: history and what's out there are safe, how it works inside you is not. "Knowing there's gold doesn't find it. The map does."
  4. **The community:** others like him, a few thousand worldwide, no organization, no one in charge. People who know, trying not to die.
  5. **The history, in broad strokes only.** Towns that figured it out together got wiped out. The witch trials were worse. Afterward, people like him pushed everyone toward thinking magic was nonsense. Let Mira react: she's been the person who thinks it's nonsense her whole life, and she isn't sure whether she should be offended.
  6. Keep the history to one paragraph of his talk plus her reaction. The full story comes later, from someone else.
  7. She asks how he found out. He says, "Not tonight." That's different from his old deflection, and she notices.
- **Detail to use:** her breath fogging in his phone light.

### Scene 2: Checking her (about 400 words)
- **Beats:**
  1. He stops on the road and asks her, oddly, whether she's felt anything: warmth, a headache.
  2. He's worried the gold analogy said too much. He does something she can't see, a pause and an inward look, then says, "No. Nothing. Good." Relieved.
  3. She realizes he just checked inside her somehow, and doesn't know how she feels about that.

### Scene 3: Town and motel (about 700 words)
- **Mira wants** a bed, a phone charger, and to stop lying.
- **Beats:**
  1. A gas station that's still open. A motel with a buzzing sign. Jason pays cash. Two beds.
  2. She calls Sarah and invents a spontaneous road trip. She calls Mom and says she'll miss Sunday dinner, sorry about the milk.
  3. The lies come out perfectly and she sits on the edge of the bed afterward, a little sick at how good she is at it.
- **Detail to use:** the motel soap, wrapped in paper.

### Scene 4: What dying looks like (about 600 words)
- **Mira wants** the real version of "most people die".
- **Beats:**
  1. "I need to understand what 'most people die' actually looks like. Not the mechanics. Just, what happens to them?"
  2. He tells her: things find you. Afterward there's anything from a normal body to nothing at all, because sometimes they take you back across.
  3. She asks about across what. He says, "Somewhere else," and won't go further tonight.
  4. She asks the other question: were the people on the street "things"? No, they were people. That's worse, somehow.

### Scene 5: The ceiling (about 300 words)
- **Beats:**
  1. Lights out. His breathing in the other bed.
  2. **Show her deliberating.** She thinks through the real choice: the apartment, the job, Nora, versus a life without him in it and a door she would always know was there. She argues both sides with herself the way she argues with Marcus.
  3. She doesn't decide out loud, but by the end the reader can tell which way she's leaning, and so can she.
- **End:** an image (the motel sign's light through the curtain) rather than "She's already made her decision."

## Jason in this chapter
- **Wants:** to give her enough to decide well, without one word too many.
- **Fears:** that he already said too much in the woods.
- **Shows care by:**
  - giving her his jacket without comment, then pretending he isn't cold
  - walking on the side nearer the road
- **His humor:** dry about the motel. "Five stars. The ice machine works."

## Limits for this chapter

- "Two years": at most 1. Coffee images: 0.

## Do not
- Explain the mechanism.
- Describe the other side or the creatures in detail. "Things" and "somewhere else" is enough tonight.
- Let her announce a decision.
