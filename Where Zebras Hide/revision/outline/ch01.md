# Chapter 1: Normal

**When and where:** Friday, February 9. The office around 4:30 pm, the coffee shop from 5:00 to 5:40, then a walk of several blocks toward a Thai restaurant.
**Length:** about 2,500 to 3,000 words. Shorter is fine; never pad.
**You also get:** the old draft of this chapter, as raw material. Keep what works, cut the padding, and fix what this outline changes.

## Where things stand

- Nothing supernatural has happened yet. Mira doesn't know magic exists, and the reader shouldn't be told.
- Mira is 26, an analyst, and lives in Columbus with her roommate Jess. The Henderson report is due Monday.
- She has had coffee with Jason every Tuesday for two years and is quietly in love with him.
- **Jason does not know anyone is coming for him tonight, and he has no plan to leave Columbus.** This is an ordinary evening for him too.
- He asked to meet on a Friday because he found something for her and didn't want to wait until Tuesday. She doesn't know that; she just notices it's not their day.

## What this chapter does

It shows the life she's about to lose and makes the reader like her in it. Jason's distance is visible without being underlined. Then something breaks the evening open, and neither she nor the reader understands what.

## Scenes

### Scene 1: The office (about 600 words)
- **Mira wants** to finish the Henderson report and stop wondering why Jason asked for a Friday.
- **Beats:**
  1. Flickering lights, a pivot table that shifted three pixels, and Dave eating baby carrots too loudly.
  2. Marcus leans on her cubicle and teases her about Jason. He says she cancelled girls' night last month "to have dinner with him".
  3. Jason's text: "Still on for coffee? I'm heading over now."
  4. She leaves the report unfinished.

### Scene 2: The coffee shop (about 1,300 words)
- **Mira wants** a normal evening with him, and to know why tonight.
- **Beats:**
  1. Her latte is ordered and waiting when she sits down. Show this once, without comment.
  2. **Conversation that sounds like two real adults who know each other well.** She talks about the Pentax, the expired film with its green cast, the undeveloped roll still in the camera, and the darkroom space she can't afford. He asks good questions and actually listens.
  3. Nora's Chicago apartment listings. Mom wants a promotion. Dad mails grad-school applications.
  4. She asks about his parents. "We didn't really get along," he says, and moves the conversation back to her darkroom so smoothly she barely notices. This is a lie she'll catch later; write it so it slides by.
  5. **The gift.** He gives her a small paper bag: a roll of the discontinued film stock she complained about months ago, once, in passing. He found it at a camera shop across town. "I saw it and thought of you." She's ridiculously happy and tries not to show it. It goes in her coat pocket.
  6. At the next table, two students start arguing about free will and physics, loudly, and keep getting louder. It's just noise that makes the coffee shop unbearable. Jason doesn't react to the content. It's the reason they leave.
  7. Mira suggests the Thai place her friend Sarah keeps recommending, a few blocks away. He says yes.
- **Detail to use:** her green-tinted bridge photo, described the way a photographer would.

### Scene 3: The walk, and the attack (about 800 words)
- **Mira wants** to make him finish a sentence for once.
- **Beats:**
  1. They walk several blocks through the February evening, past the park. The restaurant isn't in sight yet.
  2. Under the orange streetlights, Jason says her expired green film would look strange in this light, and asks whether she'd shoot it anyway. One or two lines, about her and her photos, not about physics. It's a small, sweet moment of him paying attention.
  3. Wind makes her stumble. His hand stops six inches from her elbow, then drops. This is the only hovering-hand moment in the chapter.
  4. "Do you ever get lonely?" "Sometimes." "You're different." "Different how?"
  5. **The attack. Nobody is visible.** The air cracks. There's a flash of blue light right in front of them, and something invisible slams into it with a force she feels in her chest. She never sees who did it.
  6. **Jason reacts faster than he could speak.** He says nothing. His hand is already on her arm before she understands anything has happened.
  7. Pressure, ringing, the world lurching sideways. Her skin feels peeled off and put back on wrong (keep this image from the old draft).
  8. Then ground, dead leaves, trees.
- **End line:** short and sensory. Bare branches against a grey sky. Don't end on "Trees."

## Jason in this chapter
- **Wants:** an ordinary evening, and to see her face when she opens the film.
- **What he's like here:** warm, easy, attentive. He asks about her and deflects questions about himself smoothly, without awkwardness.
- **At the attack:** instant and silent. He's done this before.

## Romance beat
The film he found for her. The hovering hand. "You're different."

## Limits for this chapter
- "Two years": at most 2. The hovering hand: once.
- Body-language clichés (face warming, chest fluttering, face lighting up): at most 1 in the chapter.
- Jokes or banter lines: at most 4 in the chapter, and only in Scenes 1 and 2.
- Jason looking at doors, windows or the street: 0. He doesn't know anything is coming.
- Repeated information ("listened to it twice"): 0.

## Do not
- Explain the attack, mention mana, or let Mira guess at magic.
- Show the attackers.
- Have Jason say anything during the attack.
- Have Jason do experiments, give technical explanations, or smirk at the students.
- Give Mira a coffee lid or any other object to carry through the scene for later.
