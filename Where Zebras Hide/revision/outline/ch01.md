# Chapter 1: Normal

**When and where:** Friday, February 9. The office around 4:30 pm, the coffee shop from 5:00 to 5:40, then a walk of several blocks toward a Thai restaurant, about ten minutes if you went the whole way. All of this is Eastern time.
**Length:** about 2,700 words. A chapter under 2,300 has probably skipped texture the scenes need; add observation, not jokes or side beats.
**You also get:** the old draft of this chapter, as raw material. Keep what works, cut the padding, and fix what this outline changes.

## Where things stand

- Nothing supernatural has happened yet. Mira doesn't know magic exists, and the reader shouldn't be told.
- Mira is 26, an analyst, and lives in Columbus with her roommate Jess. The Henderson report is due Monday, and two sections are left.
- She has had coffee with Jason every Tuesday for two years and is quietly in love with him.
- Jason does not know anyone is coming for him tonight, and he has no plan to leave Columbus. This is an ordinary evening for him too.
- He asked to meet on a Friday because he found something for her and didn't want to wait until Tuesday. She doesn't know that. She only knows it's not their day.
- Where things are. Mira: crossbody bag with her wallet and keys, a scarf, her coat, her phone in the coat pocket. Jason: a backpack he never goes without, holding his laptop, some cash and a few things he always keeps in it. The laptop stays in the backpack for the whole chapter.

## What this chapter does

It shows the life she's about to lose and makes the reader like her in it. Jason's distance is visible without being underlined. Then something breaks the evening open, and neither she nor the reader understands what.

Beats are events and purposes. The drafter finds the details, images and actions.

## Scenes

### Scene 1: The office (about 600 words)
- **Mira wants** to finish the Henderson report, and to stop wondering why Jason asked for a Friday.
- **Beats:**
  1. The office is a place she is tired of tonight: one small thing about the report or the room is going wrong, and Dave at the next desk is eating baby carrots (this connects to the verbatim line). The time is about 4:30; Marcus gives it in beat 2.
  2. Marcus stops at her cubicle and teases her about Jason. He reminds her she skipped girls' night last month to have dinner with him. He tells her the time in his own words and says she should go if she is meeting Jason at five. He also makes one specific observation about Mira's week, not an explanation.
  3. Jason has texted to confirm coffee at five. She answers yes. A text from Mom arrives too, asking her to bring the milk to Sunday dinner. She answers in a few words. This is the only place the milk comes up.
  4. She leaves the report unfinished, puts the phone in her coat pocket, and puts the bag across her body.

### Scene 2: The coffee shop (about 1,300 words)
- **Mira wants** a normal evening with him, and to know why tonight.
- **Beats:**
  1. Jason is at the counter collecting two cups when she walks in. By the time she sits, her latte is in front of her. Mention this once. Placement: his backpack is on the chair beside him, her bag is on her own chair, and she takes off her scarf and leaves it on her chair. Everything stays where it is until they leave.
  2. **The film and the darkroom.** Jason asks how the Pentax is going. That leads Mira into the expired film with its green cast, the undeveloped roll still in the camera at home, and the darkroom space she can't afford. She talks across several exchanges, with one follow-up question from Jason, one observation of his own, and her own tangents. No speech runs over about 80 words, and it all stays inside quotation marks. She then apologises, and Jason says the verbatim line below. Once in this scene Jason brings up something Mira told him weeks ago, unprompted: he asks whether the co-op above the print shop on the east side is still the one with the enlarger.
  3. **Her family, spread across the scene.** Each item is no more than a clause, at a different point, never close together. The milk is already done in Scene 1.
     - Early: her phone buzzes face-up on the table. It is Nora in Chicago, sending another apartment listing. Mira turns the phone over and tells Jason she is not going. (On the walk her thoughts show she isn't sure.)
     - Later, after the film talk has moved on: Jason asks what she is doing this weekend. She answers with Sunday dinner at her parents', and one passing remark about what they think she should do next. Asking about his parents (beat 4) follows from that.
     - Jess comes up as one clause, when the students at the next table get loud (beat 6): the argument sounds like her and Jess arguing about the dishwasher.
  4. She asks about his parents, because she has just talked about hers. He says he and they didn't really get along, and steers the talk back to her darkroom. She follows the change of subject.
  5. **The gift.** With the talk back on her darkroom, he takes a small paper bag from the backpack and gives it to her: a roll of the discontinued film stock she mentioned wanting to try once, months ago, back when she was only thinking about buying a camera. He found it at a camera shop across town and says he saw it and thought of her. She is ridiculously happy. Show it in something she does or says too much of, and let her catch herself. The narration does not tell the reader what it meant. This also answers why he asked for tonight. The roll goes in her coat pocket, and the paper bag stays on the table. She thinks, in narration, the verbatim line below.
  6. At the next table, two students start arguing loudly about something trivial and get louder while she and Jason are still in the shop. It is noise and nothing more. It is the reason they leave.
  7. Mira suggests the Thai place her friend Sarah keeps recommending, and says it is about a ten-minute walk. He says yes. They leave at 5:40. She puts her coat back on and Jason shoulders the backpack. The scarf stays on her chair, and the paper bag and cups stay on the table.
- **Photography images** appear once in the whole chapter. Use none elsewhere. A one-sentence mention of her green-tinted bridge photo in Scene 2 is allowed, and no more.

### Scene 3: The walk, and the attack (about 800 words)
- **Mira wants** an easy walk with him, and she is working up to asking him something personal.
- **Beats:**
  1. They walk several blocks through the February evening, past the park. The walk would take ten minutes, and the attack comes a few minutes in. It is dusk and the streetlights are on along the park. The restaurant isn't in sight yet. Someone else is on the street, so it isn't empty. The park path runs down to a river, and Mira notices the water once. About a minute or two after they leave the shop she checks her phone and it reads 5:42. That is the only clock reading in the chapter. The attack comes a minute or two later, at about 5:44. No narrated minute for the attack.
  2. Jason asks whether she'd shoot her expired green film at night under streetlights anyway. One or two lines, about her and her photos.
  3. She stumbles. Jason reaches toward her elbow and stops short of touching her, then lowers his hand. This is the only hovering-hand moment in the chapter.
  4. She asks if he ever gets lonely. Don't explain why. The reader may connect it to Nora, or to him. (Her private worry on the walk is the loneliness question only. Nora's offer may show once in her thoughts as a sign she isn't sure she is staying, with no explanation. Friday is not mentioned.) He says sometimes. Then the verbatim exchange below. She asks what he means, and the attack comes before he answers.
  5. **The attack. Nobody is visible.** The air cracks. A flash of blue light appears right in front of them, and something invisible hits it with a force she feels in her chest.
  6. **Jason's reaction.** Show only his hand: it is on her arm before she understands that anything has happened.
  7. Pressure, ringing, the world lurching sideways, and a sensation across her whole skin that she can't compare to anything she has felt. Partway through, for about a heartbeat, the movement changes direction, and then it continues as before. She feels this as a sensation only, and the narration moves on. (Drafter: find your own physical detail; do not use the word "peeled".)
  8. Then ground, dead leaves, trees. She is on her back or sitting, with the sky visible through bare branches. Jason is a few feet away, his hand released from her arm.
- **End line:** short and sensory, on what she sees overhead: bare branches and a grey sky. Don't end on a single word like "Trees."

## Jason in this chapter
- **Wants:** an ordinary evening, and to see her face when she opens the film.
- **What he's like here:** warm, easy, attentive. He asks about her and turns questions about himself aside smoothly.
- **At the attack:** instant and silent. His hand is on her arm, and that is all the narration gives.

## Romance beat
The film he found for her. The hovering hand. The exchange about being different.

## Old-life beats
- the job and Marcus (Marcus's visit leads into Jason's text; the milk is a Mom text)
- the film and the darkroom (Jason's question about the Pentax leads into it)
- the family: Nora (her listing buzzes in), Jason's weekend question (leads to Sunday dinner, her parents' opinions, then her question about his parents) and Jess (the students' argument)

Scene 1 carries the job (Marcus) and the milk text. Scene 2 carries two: the film and darkroom, and the family. Each family item takes no more than a clause at a different point in the scene, never together. Scene 3 carries only a callback to Nora.

## Verbatim lines (keep exactly)
These are the only sentences the outline supplies.
- "I hated Dave. I hated his carrots." Mira's narration in Scene 1, about Dave and his baby carrots.
- "Most people are boring." Jason says it in Scene 2, answering Mira's apology after she has run on about film and the darkroom (see Scene 2 beat 2). He means she isn't.
- "He remembered everything except to ever ask me on an actual date." Mira's narration in Scene 2, right after he gives her the film.
- "You're different." / "Different how?" Jason says the first line to Mira on the walk, after saying he sometimes gets lonely. She asks the second. The attack interrupts before he answers.

## Limits for this chapter
- "Two years": at most 2, including any verbatim line.
- The hovering hand: once.
- Jason's hands are described at most once in the chapter, by what they do. No description of their colour, skin or knuckles.
- Body-language clichés (face warming, chest fluttering, face lighting up): at most 1. The cap covers emotional reactions only. The impact in Scene 3 is a physical event and doesn't count.
- Jokes or banter lines: at most 4, only in Scenes 1 and 2.
- Jason looking at doors, windows or the street: 0. He doesn't know anything is coming.
- Information given to the reader twice: 0.
- Short-line volleys: put a full sentence of action or thought between short lines of dialogue.

## Do not
- Explain the attack, mention mana, or let Mira guess at magic.
- Show the attackers, or have the narration remark that she didn't see them. She just doesn't see anyone.
- Have Jason speak during the attack.
- Have Jason do experiments, give technical explanations, or react to the students.
- Give Mira anything to carry through the chapter other than the bag, the phone and the film. None of them is a symbol.
