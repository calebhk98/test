# Chapter 1: Normal

**When and where:** Friday, February 9. The office around 4:30 pm, the coffee shop from 5:00 to 5:40, then the walk outside.
**Length:** about 3,000 words. This is shorter than the old draft, which ran long.
**You also get:** the old draft of this chapter, as raw material. Keep what works, cut the padding, and fix what this outline changes.

## Where things stand

- Nothing supernatural has happened yet. Mira doesn't know magic exists, and the reader shouldn't be told.
- Mira is 26, an analyst, and lives in Columbus with her roommate Jess. The Henderson report is due Monday.
- She has had coffee with Jason every Tuesday for two years and is quietly in love with him.
- Jason texted her today asking to meet. Friday is not their day. She notices this and is secretly hopeful.

## What this chapter does

It shows the life she's about to lose, and makes the reader like her in it. It also lets the reader see Jason's distance clearly, along with one or two oddities about him that will make sense later. Then it breaks.

## Scenes

### Scene 1: The office (about 700 words)
- **Mira wants** to finish the Henderson report and stop thinking about why Jason asked for a Friday.
- **Beats:**
  1. Flickering lights, a pivot table that shifted three pixels, and Dave eating baby carrots too loudly.
  2. Marcus leans on her cubicle to tease her about "the mysterious Jason".
  3. She deflects badly.
  4. Jason's text arrives: "Still on for coffee? I'm heading over now." She pretends to Marcus it's nothing.
  5. She leaves the report unfinished.
- **Detail to use:** the carrots.

### Scene 2: The coffee shop (about 1,400 words)
- **Mira wants** to know why he asked her here on a Friday, and doesn't ask.
- **Beats:**
  1. Jason has her latte ordered before she sits down. He's attentive and funny.
  2. Show that he remembers her order once. Don't explain it.
  3. She talks about the Pentax, the expired film, the green cast, the undeveloped roll still in the camera, and the darkroom space she can't afford.
  4. Nora sends Chicago listings. Mom wants a promotion. Dad mails grad-school applications.
  5. She asks about his parents. He says, "We didn't really get along," and changes the subject to her darkroom. This is a lie; she'll catch it later. Write it so it slides by.
  6. Two students at the next table argue about whether the universe is predictable. Keep it to two or three lines, as background. Jason glances at them with an odd half-smile, as if he knows something funny about the argument.
  7. Twice he looks at the window or the door for a beat too long. She reads it as him being distracted by something he wants to say.
  8. He seems about to tell her something serious, then doesn't. He's here to say goodbye, though she won't learn that until Chapter 3.
- **Changes:** she leaves sure that something is different about tonight.

### Scene 3: The walk, and the attack (about 900 words)
- **Mira wants** to make him finish the sentence he started.
- **Beats:**
  1. They walk through the February evening, quiet streets, the park on one side.
  2. Wind makes her stumble. His hand stops six inches from her elbow, then drops. This is the one hovering-hand moment; it carries the whole motif.
  3. "Do you ever get lonely?" He says sometimes, and that he's not great with people. "You're different." "Different how?"
  4. Before he answers, a man steps out from a doorway across the street and raises a hand.
  5. **Make the attack visible.** The air cracks. A curved sheet of blue light snaps into being in front of them, and something invisible slams into it. She sees it ripple like a windshield taking a rock. Then a second figure. Jason grabs her arm, hard. She doesn't understand any of it.
  6. Pressure, ringing, the world lurching sideways. Her skin feels peeled off and put back on wrong (keep this image from the old draft). Then ground, leaves, trees.
- **End line:** short, sensory, disoriented, and different from the first lines of Chapter 2. Something like: bare branches, grey sky, her coffee lid still in her hand. Don't end on "Trees."

## Jason in this chapter
- He came to say goodbye: the hunters are close, and he's leaving Columbus tonight. He wants one more normal hour with her, and he's ashamed of wanting it.
- He's warm and teasing and won't talk about himself.
- He's distracted and checks the street.
- At the attack, he moves before she understands anything is happening.

## Romance beat
The hovering hand, and "You're different." Her hope, which she mocks in her own narration.

## Old-life beats
All of it: the job, Marcus, the film, Nora, her parents, the roommate. Mention Jess once. Mira has a Sunday dinner at her parents' this weekend and promised to bring milk; one line.

## Limits for this chapter
- "Two years": at most 2. "Twelve inches": at most 1. The hovering hand: once.
- The door metaphor: at most 1.
- "Something shifted in his expression": 0. "He grinned": at most 2.
- Triplets: at most 1. "The way you ___" similes: at most 2.
- Explaining what a moment means after showing it: 0. Cut "He gave me his attention, but he wouldn't give me anything else."

## Do not
- Explain the attack, mention mana, or let Mira guess at magic.
- Have Jason say anything about who the attackers are.
- Repeat the same beat of distance more than once. One clear instance beats five.
- Leave a duplicated action, like getting up to order twice.

Mira on the ground in Tennessee woods, ears ringing, about ten feet from Jason. Get the transition right: he let go of her arm when they landed.
