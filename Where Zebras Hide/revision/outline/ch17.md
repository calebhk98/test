# Chapter 17: The Ecosystem

**When and where:** July. Back and forth between the other side (a few hours at a time) and the Vermont base.
**Length:** about 4,000 words. There is no old draft.

## Where things stand

- Mira has crossed once. There is no light there, and everything produces mana. She made a light for three seconds and saw colorless moss. She fought a dense, dog-sized native creature by placing a clone to her left to locate it.
- She holds three or four full-strength clones, all of them her.
- Jason told her he makes himself lighter to cross. He didn't say how.
- She knows the Box is a prison, a training tool and a battery. The Box holds captured creatures; a clone of Jason fights each one on a loop. He has never said what the battery powers.
- Jason held her hand and kissed her forehead before the first crossing. They hold hands. Nothing is named.
- She said yes to crossing so she could practice, and she is practicing.
- Her phone is with her on Earth and she uses it in town like anyone.

## What this chapter does

Learning the other side as an ecosystem. Hiding from something vast. Learning what has been paying for her trips. Then a surprise attack shows how good she has gotten, and a stranger explains it.

## Scenes

### Scene 1: Back and forth, and how it lives (about 1,100 words)
- **Mira wants** it to make sense.
- **Beats:**
  1. They cross for a few hours and come back, again and again. She is surprised they come back so often. He says she is not ready to sleep there: her shields have to hold while she sleeps, without her.
  2. On Earth she stands at the window and looks at the sun.
  3. Jason teaches her to read energy flows, territories and safe paths. It is ecology, not magic.
  4. **One relationship she witnesses:** a creature leaking mana from an old wound wanders into a patch of vine. The vine wraps it and drinks from the leak, slowly, without killing it. The creature lets it happen and seems to rest. She can't decide whether it is horrible or kind.
  5. It sends her thinking to the Box. She asks him a question about it, and he answers with a lesson about territory markers.
  6. She misreads a territorial marker and walks them toward something's den. Jason turns her by the shoulders. She is mortified, and he is kind about it.
  7. On a day between crossings they drive into town for groceries. At the drugstore photo counter she picks up the Spain roll she dropped off the week before. Back at the house she pins the prints up in the order she shot them: an afternoon in a village.
- **By the end:** she can read the place well enough to be wrong in a useful way, and she has a question about the Box that he hasn't answered.

### Scene 2: Something passes (about 700 words)
- **Beats:**
  1. Far off, something vast moves. She feels it as pressure, like a ship passing far out at sea. Then it comes closer.
  2. Jason pulls her down. They go completely still, shields locked tight, for minutes. Her hand finds his and they hold on hard.
  3. Its attention passes over them and moves on without stopping.
  4. They see nothing. Nobody ever sees one of these on its own side.
  5. Afterward, shaking, she tells him what she understands now that she didn't before. He says some of them know about Earth and don't care, because it isn't worth what crossing would cost them. He adds nothing to that.
- **By the end:** the thing she was told about in the car is real to her.

### Scene 3: What pays for it (about 800 words)
- **Mira wants** the truth about the cost.
- **Beats:**
  1. Back in Vermont, Jason is grey with exhaustion. Every trip is worse. She pushes until he admits it.
  2. The toll takes a bigger share the stronger you are. To make himself lighter, he pushes part of himself into the Box.
  3. **The battery:** he has been drawing on the captured creatures to carry her across. Every trip is paid for partly by the things in the prison.
  4. She is angry twice: for them, and because he left it out. She names his pattern in his own words from the cabin: "You said you'd tell me when you were deciding things about me." This is the second time he has hidden something about her since he promised.
  5. She has told him once that she thinks the Box is wrong. She says it again, plainly. He says it is his ability, he built it, and he isn't going to stop using the one thing that makes crossing possible.
  6. **Where they land:** they cross less often, and he will tell her every time he draws on them. She keeps crossing because she wants this practice and it is his call how he pays for it. She doesn't pretend to agree, and he doesn't ask her to. Neither of them is wrong about the facts.
- **By the end:** a disagreement they will carry, not a wound. She doesn't tell him it's all right.

### Scene 4: The attack (about 700 words)
- **Beats:**
  1. The next crossing. They are tense with each other. Jason is tired.
  2. Something attacks her from nowhere. Write it fast, in her POV, with her body moving before her thinking. The hit lands, she is thrown, she answers before she has finished landing. Use no more than three shield actions.
  3. It turns and leaves.
  4. She thinks that was just the basics Jason drilled into her.
- **By the end:** she has done something well without deciding to.

### Scene 5: Kael (about 700 words)
- **Beats:**
  1. Someone laughs nearby: a man, with small cool things riding on him that she can feel against his mana.
  2. This is **Kael**. He talks to the creatures on him the way you talk to dogs, and to people as if he is already bored. He says he has followed them for two crossings, and the thing that hit her was one of his. He called it off at the last second.
  3. Kael asks who taught her that. Mira says she supposes Jason did. Kael turns to Jason and asks whether he let her think that was the beginner course. He says it is either cruel or the best teaching he has ever seen.
  4. Let the scene carry that she is not a beginner anymore, and that Jason has been quietly very good at teaching her. Narration doesn't state either.
  5. Kael calls mana ether and the other side the deep. He knows Jason by reputation. He invites them to visit and leaves before they can answer.
- **End:** Mira, still on the ground, turned toward Jason. She asks if that was expert level, and Jason answers flatly.

## Jason in this chapter
- **Wants:** her strong enough to survive here without him. He has been hiding what it costs him.
- **His flaw, again:** leaving out the battery. He is sorry he hid it and not sorry he uses it.
- **Answers** Mira's last question flatly, as a fact about what he told her.

## Romance beat
Lying still together while the vast thing passes. Her hand finds his and they hold on so hard it hurts.

## Old-life beat
She picks up the roll she shot in Spain from a drugstore in town and pins the prints to the wall in the order she shot them: a normal afternoon in a village. She looks at them for a long time. Lead: they are in town for groceries between crossings and she passes the photo counter (Scene 1).

## Rules in play
- The other side is an ecosystem.
- The intelligent ones are only sensed, never seen.
- The toll takes a bigger share the stronger you are.
- The Box is a battery. A clone of Jason fights each captive on a loop.
- Sleeping there needs shields held in sleep.
- People use different words for mana: Kael says ether and the deep.

## Verbatim lines (keep exactly)
- "You told me this in the car. That they're out there. I didn't understand it until now." Mira says it to Jason, shaking, after the vast thing has passed. In the car, months ago, Jason had told her that things on the other side know about Earth and mostly don't care. Jason answers in his own words.
- "Who taught you that?" Kael says it to Mira, laughing, after her fight.
- Mira: "Expert level?" Jason: "I may have rounded down." Mira asks it from the ground after Kael has left; Jason answers flatly.

## Set up in this chapter
- The compromise: fewer trips, and Jason tells her each time he draws on the Box. Say it once, flatly, at the end of the argument.
- Kael's invitation: he says to come and see his place, and leaves before they answer. Mention that he has followed them for two crossings, as a fact he gives about himself.
- The crossings are frequent. Show it by counting them in passing (a few hours, home, again). Nobody says what the crossings might draw.

## Limits for this chapter
- Triplets: at most 1.
- "The way you ___" similes: at most 2.
- Analogies: one per scene.

## Do not
- Describe the intelligent one beyond pressure and its attention.
- Have Kael explain history.
- Resolve the battery argument.
- Narrate that Kael is impressed. He says it.
