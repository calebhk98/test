# Chapter 30: Threshold

**When and where:** Day 11, late afternoon. A field and tree line near the Vermont base. Theo's teaching group, moved here by Jason this morning, is half a mile away.
**Length:** about 3,000 words.
**You also get:** the old draft of this chapter. It's the best-written prose in the old draft, so keep its rhythm and its best lines, and fix what this outline changes.

## Where things stand

- Day 11 of the end. Three days since a colossal creature crossed in Rajasthan. It killed about a hundred million and left.
- Mira has held about 110 of herself for nine days. Yesterday she rested ten hours, and it cost eleven lives.
- She can make clones that make clones. Once, in August, she reached about a thousand in a field on a Tuesday afternoon, and reabsorbing them knocked her out for hours. Last night, pulling back ninety was the worst since then.
- In July, a talking scout crossed into Vermont. Jason boxed it. It said: "They will want to know where I went." It never went home.
- She and Jason are a couple. "I couldn't stand you forgetting me."
- Theo, the seventeen-year-old natural, is in a group Jason moved here this morning.

## What this chapter does

The scout's master comes looking for its scout and finds them. Mira does the math and becomes a colony of eight million. She wins. To pull herself back fast enough, she breaks. The last five of her can see Jason. Then dark.

## Scenes

### Scene 1: It arrives (about 600 words)
- **Beats:**
  1. "The thing arrived the way the first one had, wrong." It's heavy, like the pressure before a storm.
  2. It came through here, in rural nothing, because it's looking for something specific. **It knows Jason's signature**, because that's what its scout came to investigate in July.
  3. **What it is** (CREATURES.md):
     - nearly human-sized, the same kind of being as the scout
     - taller, with more joints, and a smooth head with a ridge for a face
     - it shifts its density, so it blurs at the edges of her sight; "it didn't want to be seen clearly"
  4. It speaks, its voice from beside it, politely. To Jason first: where is the one I sent? Then, curious, to Mira.
  5. It's intelligent, patient, and polite in a way that's worse than a threat.

### Scene 2: The math (about 400 words)
- **Beats:**
  1. She does the math practically.
  2. Jason could maybe contain it, and it would wreck him. More things are coming; she can feel pressure at the edge of her range.
  3. Theo's group is half a mile away, and fifty untrained producers there are a feast.
  4. **Jason's job:** he locks the area down. No teleporting, no crossing, no messages home. That's his whole fight, and it's huge.
  5. She starts with two hundred.

### Scene 3: Two hundred, then forty thousand (about 800 words)
- **Beats:**
  1. Two hundred of her in the field, the tree line and the road. One walks forward and dies in four seconds, and in those four seconds the second round finishes.
  2. **Its tactics:**
     - It kills the two hundred almost casually.
     - It recognizes the danger when the forty thousand appear.
     - It tries to send a message home. Jason's lockdown stops it, and Jason pays for that.
     - It pulls back, considering, then commits.
  3. Jason is shouting and running toward her. He saw two hundred and thought that was her ceiling. Five of her look at him; the rest watch the creature.
  4. **What only numbers can beat:** it's far stronger than any one of her. It is one body with one set of reactions. Forty thousand from every side is still not enough.

### Scene 4: Eight million (about 600 words)
- **Beats:**
  1. The third round: each of the forty thousand makes two hundred.
  2. "I was not one person anymore. I was not forty thousand people. I was a colony of eight million." Keep this.
  3. **Where they are:** eight million bodies spread for miles across fields, woods and roads, as far as her sense reaches, standing shoulder to shoulder on the county highway.
  4. It attacks and breaks against layered shields like a wave against a breakwater. The colony answers: eight million of everything she knows, from every direction at once.
  5. **One reversal.** For a moment it finds the weak point: it starts tearing through bodies faster than they can close the gap. It is winning. Then the colony shifts all at once, and the gap closes.
  6. Then it's gone.
  7. **The beacon thought.** One line: is a colony of eight million the thing that finally tells everything over there that Earth isn't worth it, or the brightest signal this planet has ever sent? She doesn't know.

### Scene 5: Pulling back (about 500 words)
- **Mira wants** to pull them back slowly. She can't.
- **Beats:**
  1. **Why she has to hurry:** eight million bodies, each producing mana, are the loudest thing on Earth. Every second they stay up invites the next crossing toward Theo's group and everyone else. She has to come down fast.
  2. She knows what pulling back ninety did last night, and what a thousand did in August. She does it anyway.
  3. Four million, then three million, then two million. The headache becomes a sound.
  4. "I kept pulling because the alternative was leaving them and I was not going to leave them out there." Keep this.
  5. One hundred thousand. Ten thousand.

### Scene 6: The last five (about 200 words)
- "The last five of me were the ones who could see Jason." He's right there. He's holding something, or maybe holding her. The sentence breaks off.
- **Make one thing he's doing concrete** for a sequel: she feels a familiar technique close around her, the edges of a box, his architecture. Then she loses it. She can't tell what it means, and neither can the reader.
- She pulls the last of them back.
- "And then I couldn't see."

## Mira's voice in this chapter
- She is still Mira inside the colony.
- One photographer's observation: eight million of her across the fields looks like a contact sheet, the same frame over and over, each slightly different.
- One flash of her humor, such as a dry thought about how Mom would want a head count.

## Jason in this chapter
- **His role:** the lockdown, then reaching her.
- He's frightened in a way she's only seen a few times.
- The box closing around her at the end is his last act on the page.

## Romance beat
The five of her who can see him. "Somewhere that was still Mira and still his and still ours." Keep it.

## Old-life beat
The contact-sheet thought, and Mom's head count.

## Rules in play
- The intelligent ones come back eventually with their memories, so "gone" is for now.
- Lockdown blocks messages.
- Clones are all her, with no main body.
- Reabsorbing at scale can kill her.
- Many clones are loud.
