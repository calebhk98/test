# Chapter 9: First Contact

**When and where:** About April 12, at night. The cabin porch and the woods below it, near the creek.
**Length:** about 2,800 words. There is no old draft.

## Where things stand

- Two months in. Mira can cycle without thinking and shield a little. She can make a handful of clones that move a little on their own, and she fixes their small flaws because they bug her.
- Since the hand scene, she and Jason are close but not together. They touch easily and haven't named anything.
- She knows things cross for meals. She has never seen one.
- Her phone works the way anyone's does. Mom and Nora call and text. Mira has told Mom she quit her job and is traveling with Jason.
- Jason promised in Chapter 5 to tell her when he decides things about her.
- Jason chose this cabin partly for the brambles by the creek. They give off a little mana of their own, which hides some of the leakage that comes off the two of them.

## What this chapter does

The danger becomes real and specific. It is small, it fells a tree, and Jason kills it at once. Then she learns he has been doing this without telling her.

## Scenes

### Scene 1: The slip (about 600 words)
- **Beats:**
  1. Evening at the cabin, after dinner. Her phone rings on the table. It is Nora, who is upset that Mira skipped her birthday dinner in Chicago, and keeps asking where Mira really is. Mira takes the call out on the porch and lies. Nora says she sounds like someone reading from a script. They hang up badly.
  2. Mira stays on the porch and cries, and for a minute she stops cycling without noticing. Her shield loosens.
- **Optional texture:** pick none, or one that fits what you've written (the spring peepers in the creek, for instance).

### Scene 2: It comes (about 700 words)
- **Mira wants** to see it, then to be anywhere else.
- **Beats:**
  1. Jason comes out fast and tells her to stay where she is. She feels it before she sees it: something small and bright to her mana sense, coming up the slope from the creek. It was eating the brambles. Then it noticed her.
  2. Show the creature through her photographer's eye, using this scene's one image. Pick two details: its size and shape, its whiskers, or the porch light passing through it with no highlight where a living thing would shine.
  3. It lashes out at the air near her, and a young tree splits and comes down across the path.
  4. She takes a photo on her phone without thinking. It shows up clearly: pale, eyeless, wrong.
  5. Jason kills it in two quick lines of action. It drops.

### Scene 3: Over the body (about 500 words)
- **Beats:**
  1. Its heat goes fast. Within a minute the body is the same temperature as the night air, and nothing on it steams. Her hands shake and she sits on the porch steps. Jason's hands shake a little too, and she notices.
  2. He crouches by the body and, while it is still there, tells her in a few plain sentences what to feel for: how this is different from the brambles, and that this is what alive feels like. He says why it noticed her: she went loud for a minute.
  3. Her brain grabs for a practical question, whether they bury it. They do. It weighs almost nothing, as light as paper. This is the scene's one analogy.

### Scene 4: The other thing he didn't say (about 1,000 words)
- **Mira wants** to know if that's what is coming for her.
- **Beats:**
  1. After the body is buried, they sit on the steps. She asks Jason whether that is what would come for her.
  2. He says that one came for a weed. It noticed her because she went loud for a minute. The ones that come for people are bigger, and they come later, when she is louder.
  3. She asks how he knew where to look so fast. He wipes his hands on his jeans before he answers. It is the fourth one this month. He has been feeling them cross and killing them before they got near the cabin, while she slept.
  4. She explodes: "You said you'd tell me when you were deciding things about me." This was a decision about her. He says he didn't want her scared while she was still learning to cycle. She says it wasn't his call.
  5. He apologizes badly, then well.
  6. She says she hasn't forgiven him, and stays sitting next to him anyway. He goes inside and comes back with a basin of warm water and a dish towel, because she still has grave dirt on her hands. He sets it on the step by her feet and goes back to wiping his own hands. He says nothing, and she washes hers.
- **End:** the two of them on the steps, the brambles down by the creek, her phone with the photo in her lap. Don't explain the feeling.

## Set up in this chapter
- The brambles by the creek give off a little mana of their own. Jason says in passing, while they sit on the steps, that he picked the cabin partly for them. One line. Mira doesn't comment.
- Jason makes decisions about her alone and tells her afterward. Show it as the plain fact of the fourth kill this month. This is the first time she says it to his face.
- The photo of the creature stays on her phone. She doesn't delete it.
- Things on camera show up clearly, even when the eye can't make sense of them.

## Jason in this chapter
- **Wants:** her safe, and her not scared, in that order. That order is the problem.
- **Flaw, on display:** deciding alone and telling her later. This is the first time she names it.
- **Not cold:** after the kill his hands shake a little too. She notices.

## Romance beat
She is angry and stays sitting next to him anyway. He brings out warm water and a towel for the dirt on her hands, sets it by her feet without a word, and she uses it.

## Old-life beat
Nora's call, and "reading from a script". It comes in the evening because Nora has been trying to reach her for days.

## Rules in play
- Any plant or animal can produce mana naturally, so the brambles draw strays.
- Small things notice by scent.
- Things on camera show up clearly.
- They lose heat fast on Earth.

## Verbatim lines (keep exactly)
- "Is that what would come for me?" Mira asks it of Jason on the porch steps, after the body is buried, in answer to what she has just seen.
- "You said you'd tell me when you were deciding things about me." Mira says it to Jason when he admits the fourth kill.

## Limits for this chapter
- One body reaction per scene.
- Jason's teaching over the body: at most four sentences.
- Similes for the creature: at most 2. Use specific visual details instead.
- Triplets: at most 1.

## Do not
- Make the creature evil. It was eating, then it was startled.
- Let the teaching over the body turn into a lecture.
- Name the creature type in dialogue. If Mira names it in her head, it's her own nickname.
- Say that the brambles or the photo will matter later.
