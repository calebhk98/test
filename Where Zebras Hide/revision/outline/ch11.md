# Chapter 11: Visitors

**When and where:** Late April, a few days after Chapter 10. The cabin.
**Length:** about 3,000 words. There is no old draft.

## Where things stand

- Mira is about ten weeks in. She can cycle and shield. Her clones produce their own mana and are all her, one mind in several bodies.
- She and Jason fought about the Box (a prison it doesn't know it's in) and didn't resolve it. He told her "I heard you".
- He calls the Box a battery and won't say what it powers.
- She doesn't know any other practitioner exists in person. She knows a few thousand exist worldwide.

## What this chapter does

A third person arrives and the world gets wider. Delilah is warm, nosy and funny. She also, without meaning to, hands Mira half of an idea.

## Scenes

### Scene 1: The car (about 600 words)
- **Beats:**
  1. A car stops on the gravel. An older woman gets out with a thermos. Jason goes very still and then very fast. He is genuinely alarmed: nobody is supposed to find this place.
  2. **How she found them:** she lives forty miles over the ridge and has known these hills for thirty years. For weeks she's felt "a new warmth" out this way, then something big and strange (the Box) that rattled her teeth. She came to check on whoever was new, the way you do.
  3. The people hunting Jason don't know these hills. She does.
  4. Jason checks her somehow, then relaxes, slightly.

### Scene 2: Tea (about 1,000 words)
- **Mira wants** another person to tell her how this works. Then she wants a friend.
- **Delilah wants** to make sure this girl isn't going to die.
- **Beats:**
  1. Delilah calls it "the warmth". She brought actual tea, in a thermos, like a normal person.
  2. She asks Mira personal questions in the first ten minutes: how old are you, how'd you meet him, are you two together. Mira chokes on her tea.
  3. **The community, through people, not numbers:**
     - "There's a woman in Tucson who proofs bread with it. Thirty years. Never fought anything in her life."
     - A man in Ontario who keeps bees.
     - Most people are like that, small and careful, living ordinary lives.
     - The strong ones are rare, and mostly alone.
  4. She knows Jason by reputation: the boy who won't share. She says it to his face, cheerfully.
  5. "You're brilliant, Jason, but you're a terrible teacher." He points out that Mira's ahead of schedule. Delilah: "She's ahead because she's stubborn. You're still terrible."
  6. **Grief, sideways:** Delilah mentions a boy she once found too late. One line, quickly covered, and Mira notices.

### Scene 3: The question about Jason (about 700 words)
- **Beats:**
  1. Jason goes outside. Mira asks Delilah why he won't share. Delilah: "Because he made it. Same reason an artist won't give away their paintings for free."
  2. Mira: "These could save lives." Delilah: "I don't disagree, honey. But so could a lot of things people choose to keep."
  3. **Romance advice, written out.** Mira asks, obliquely, what Delilah would do if someone screamed that they loved her in the woods as a reason to leave. Delilah, bluntly: "Be mad for a month. Then decide whether the reason was any good. And make him say it again to your face when he's not shouting."
  4. Mira laughs, and then thinks about it for the rest of the day.

### Scene 4: Empty clones (about 600 words)
- **Beats:**
  1. Delilah offers to come teach "the boring stuff that keeps you alive and fed". Jason starts to refuse. Mira says yes before he can.
  2. Mira complains about her clones: when she makes them she has to fill them from her own reserve, so they start at her own tired level, refill slowly, and leave her drained. "They're useless in a real fight."
  3. Delilah, stirring sugar into her tea, offhand: "Well, cloning isn't really copying, is it? It's creation. There's no reason what you create has to match your current state." Then she moves straight on to asking whether the cabin's woodstove draws properly.
  4. Mira doesn't get it yet. She'll chew on it for weeks.
  5. Later, Jason admits he never thought of it that way. To him a clone was always a copy of himself as he is. Let him be a little put out that Delilah saw it first.

## Jason in this chapter
- **Wants:** Delilah gone, then grudgingly to keep her.
- **Fears:** that being found once means being found again.
- **He's funny about it:** territorial, sulky, then caught agreeing with her. Delilah teases him and he takes it.

## Romance beat
Delilah's advice, and Mira looking at Jason across the porch afterward, thinking about "say it again to your face".

## Old-life beat
Delilah has grandkids she can't tell anything to. "My daughter thinks I'm in a book club." That lands hard on Mira.

## Rules in play
- No vocabulary is standard: Delilah says "the warmth".
- A local practitioner checks on newcomers.
- How she found them: local knowledge, and the Box is loud.
- Small useful magic: Delilah's territory.

## Plant for later
- "Cloning isn't really copying."
- The boy Delilah found too late.
- The book-club grandkids.
- Mira's yes to Delilah's lessons.
- "So could a lot of things people choose to keep." Jason will echo this in Chapter 14, and Mira will call him on it.

## Verbatim lines (keep exactly)
- "You're brilliant, Jason, but you're a terrible teacher."
- "Well, cloning isn't really copying, is it? It's creation. There's no reason what you create has to match your current state."

## Limits for this chapter
- "Delilah gives her a look": 0.
- Calling Mira "child": 0. She says "honey" or "Mira".
- Community exposition: through people only, at most three examples.
- "Two years": at most 1.

## Do not
- Have Delilah figure out the clone technique. She makes the remark and moves on.
- Have Jason be calm about being found.
- Explain the Box's battery.
