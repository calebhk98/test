# Chapter 5: The Choice

**When and where:** Saturday, February 10, morning. The motel room near Crossville, Tennessee.
**Length:** about 2,700 words. There is no old draft.

## Where things stand

- Mira knows the world's shape:
  - mana, which is Jason's word for it
  - a few thousand people who use it, with nobody in charge
  - towns wiped out in the past, and the witch trials
  - most new learners die within months, killed by things that sometimes take bodies somewhere else
- She doesn't know how it works. Jason checked last night: she hasn't started.
- He gave her until Ohio to decide. She is deciding now, sooner, and she knows that breaks their deal.

## What this chapter does

This is the point of no return. She chooses on the page, for reasons the reader can follow. He explains, the reader doesn't hear the explanation, and her body starts. Then she has to accept that she can't go home.

## Scenes

### Scene 1: Bad coffee (about 800 words)
- **Mira wants** to tell him before she loses her nerve, and to win.
- **Jason wants** her to change her mind.
- **Beats:**
  1. Lobby coffee in paper cups. She tells him flatly that she's staying, and that she knows she's breaking their deal by deciding early. He argues.
  2. Her argument, in the verbatim lines below: he said most people who learn this die, but also that most figure it out alone. She wouldn't be alone.
  3. He tries the worst case: what happens if she can't learn. She answers it. Nothing guarantees anything, and this way she would at least die understanding why.
  4. Underneath both of them is the thing neither says yet. If she doesn't learn, she has to give him up, and she won't. That is her real reason. It is not a logical one, and Jason can tell. He also doesn't want to be the reason she dies.

### Scene 2: Ground rules (about 400 words)
- **Mira wants** to be a partner, not a pupil.
- **Beats:**
  1. He agrees on one condition: he sets the pace and decides when she learns each next thing.
  2. She sets hers, in the verbatim line below. He agrees a little too fast.
  3. Each gives something. State the terms; leave the back and forth to the drafter.

### Scene 3: The explanation (about 600 words)
- **Beats:**
  1. The walls are thin. He does something with his hand, and sound changes: the TV next door goes muffled, the air goes still and close. A silence curtain. He makes it look easy and she's annoyed by how cool it is.
  2. He asks if she's sure. She says yes. He asks once more, and she tells him to stop asking.
  3. He explains. **The reader does not hear it.** Cut straight to forty minutes later: her pen has torn the motel notepad, the coffee is cold, and her head is full of something she can't put down.
  4. This cut is deliberate. The book never teaches the reader the mechanism.
- **Optional texture:** pick none or one that fits what you've written.

### Scene 4: It starts (about 400 words)
- **Beats:**
  1. A faint warmth in her chest, like a fever coming on. Not comfort. Wrong in a small way.
  2. She looks up. Jason has put down his pen and is looking at her chest, not her face. Then he looks at her face. His own has gone very still, and he is frightened.
  3. He says the verbatim line below.
  4. She feels it. She answers in her own words, not with a bare acknowledgment.
  5. **First voluntary touch.** He puts his hand over hers on the notepad and leaves it there. She turns her hand over under his.

### Scene 5: Not going home (about 500 words)
- **Mira wants** to go home and pack, at least.
- **Beats:**
  1. He says they can't go back to Columbus.
  2. Two reasons. The people from the sidewalk know Columbus. And she is now a signal: near her family she's a risk to them, because things that come for her don't check who is standing next to her.
  3. She takes it badly. She takes stock of what she's losing. Show two: her apartment, Jess, her job, Sunday dinners, or the Pentax on her shelf with the undeveloped roll inside.
  4. She writes a list of what's back there on the torn notepad. Then she turns the page, writes what she needs to know to stay alive, and underlines it twice.
- **End:** on the new page, not a speech.

## Jason in this chapter
- **Wants:** her to change her mind. Then, once she won't, to be good at this for her.
- **Fears:** this exact moment, his face as it starts.
- **He drops** his careful manner once: the hand over hers.
- **He doesn't tell her** that he's afraid he can't keep her alive.

## Romance beat
His hand over hers.

## Old-life beat
The list of what she's leaving. She writes it because she asked to go home and pack, and he said they can't.

## Rules in play
- Understanding causes it, and onset is small and feverish.
- Erasure could still take her memories now, but it can't undo the understanding or stop production.
- Scent risks the people near her.

## Verbatim lines (keep exactly)
- "You said most people who learn this die. But you also said most of them figure it out alone. Kids stumbling into it. Loners teaching themselves. I wouldn't be alone. I'd have someone who already knows what he's doing." Mira to Jason in Scene 1, answering his case for her to go home and forget.
- "Nothing guarantees anything. At least this way I'd die understanding why." Mira to Jason in Scene 1, right after.
- "You decide when. I decide whether. And you tell me when you're deciding something about me." Mira to Jason in Scene 2, as her condition for his.
- "It's started." Jason to Mira in Scene 4, when he sees the onset in her face.

## Limits for this chapter
- Bare "I know" as a reply to a fact: 0. It's fine as an answer, not as filler for a feeling.
- Coffee as an image: at most 1. Don't use coffee for the warmth.
- One body reaction per scene.
- Triplets: at most 1.

## Do not
- Write out the explanation of mana.
- Have the onset be dramatic: no glow, no wind, no light.
- Have Jason trail off mid-sentence. Let him finish.

## Set up in this chapter
- Mira's condition, said plainly: he tells her when he is deciding something about her. Jason agrees to it quickly. Nobody remarks on how quickly.
- Mira writes a list on the motel notepad of what she is leaving behind. She keeps the page.
