# Chapter 5: The Choice

**When and where:** Saturday, February 10, morning. The motel room near Crossville, Tennessee.
**Length:** about 3,000 words. There is no old draft.

## Where things stand

- Mira knows the world's shape:
  - mana, which is Jason's word for it
  - a few thousand people who use it, with nobody in charge
  - towns wiped out in the past, and the witch trials
  - most new learners die within months, killed by "things" that sometimes take bodies "somewhere else"
- She doesn't know how it works. Jason checked last night: she hasn't started.
- He gave her until Ohio to decide. She is deciding now, sooner, and she knows that breaks their deal.

## What this chapter does

This is the point of no return. She chooses on the page, for reasons the reader can follow. He explains, the reader doesn't hear the explanation, and her body starts. Then she has to accept that she can't go home.

## Scenes

### Scene 1: Bad coffee (about 900 words)
- **Mira wants** to tell him before she loses her nerve, and to win.
- **Jason wants** her to change her mind.
- **Beats:**
  1. Lobby coffee in paper cups. She tells him flatly that she's staying, and that she knows she's breaking their deal by deciding early. He argues.
  2. Her argument, in her voice:
     "You said most people who learn this die. But you also said most of them figure it out alone. Kids stumbling into it. Loners teaching themselves. I wouldn't be alone. I'd have someone who already knows what he's doing."
     Then: "Nothing guarantees anything. At least this way I'd die understanding why."
  3. He tries the worst argument he has: what happens if she can't learn. She answers it.
  4. Underneath both of them is the thing neither says yet. She doesn't want to lose him, and he doesn't want to be the reason she dies.
- **Cut** "I could get hit by a bus tomorrow." It's a cliché.

### Scene 2: Ground rules (about 500 words)
- **Mira wants** to be a partner, not a pupil.
- **Beats:**
  1. He agrees on one condition: he sets the pace and decides when she learns each next thing.
  2. She pushes back: "You decide when. I decide whether. And you tell me when you're deciding something about me." He agrees, too fast. The reader should doubt he'll keep it.
  3. Make it a negotiation. They each give something.

### Scene 3: The explanation (about 700 words)
- **Beats:**
  1. The walls are thin. He does something with his hand, and sound changes: the TV next door goes muffled, the air goes still and close. A silence curtain. He makes it look easy and she's annoyed by how cool it is.
  2. He asks three times if she's sure. The third time she says, "Ask me again and I'm leaving."
  3. He explains. **The reader does not hear it.** Cut from his first sentence ("Okay. It starts with how your brain notices things...") to forty minutes later: her pen has torn the motel notepad, the coffee is cold, and her head is full of something she can't put down.
  4. This cut is deliberate. The book never teaches the reader the mechanism.
- **Detail to use:** the notepad with the motel's logo, her handwriting getting worse.

### Scene 4: It starts (about 400 words)
- **Beats:**
  1. A faint warmth in her chest, like a fever coming on. Not comfort. Wrong in a small way.
  2. She looks up. His face has gone very still, and he's frightened.
  3. "It's started."
  4. She can feel it. Instead of "I know", she says something of her own, for example: "Yeah. It's... yeah. That's in me."
  5. **First voluntary touch.** He reaches over and puts his hand over hers on the notepad. Not dramatic. He doesn't let go for a minute. Neither of them comments on it.

### Scene 5: Not going home (about 500 words)
- **Mira wants** to go home and pack, at least.
- **Beats:**
  1. He says they can't go back to Columbus.
  2. Two reasons: the people from the sidewalk know Columbus. And she's now a signal. Near her family, she's a risk to them, because things that come for her don't check who's standing next to her.
  3. She takes it badly. She thinks through what she's losing:
     - her apartment, Jess, her job
     - Sunday dinners
     - the Pentax on her shelf with the roll inside
  4. She makes a list on the torn notepad of what's back there. Then she turns the page and writes "What I need to know to stay alive", and underlines it twice.
- **End:** that underlined line, or her folding the list into her pocket. Don't end on a speech.

## Jason in this chapter
- **Wants:** her to change her mind. Then, once she won't, to be good at this for her.
- **Fears:** this exact moment, his face as it starts.
- **He breaks** his own professional calm once: the hand over hers.
- **He doesn't tell her** that he's afraid he can't keep her alive. The reader should be able to see it anyway.

## Romance beat
His hand over hers.

## Old-life beat
The list of what she's leaving.

## Rules in play
- Understanding causes it, and onset is small and feverish.
- Erasure is now impossible for her.
- Scent risks the people near her.

## Plant for later
- "you tell me when you're deciding something about me", which he will break
- the silence curtain
- the notepad list

## Verbatim lines (keep exactly)
- "It's started."
- Her argument in Scene 1, as quoted.

## Limits for this chapter
- "I know." as a reply: 0.
- Coffee as an image: at most 1. Don't use coffee for the warmth.
- One body reaction per scene.
- Triplets: at most 1.

## Do not
- Write out the explanation of mana.
- Have the onset be dramatic: no glow, no wind, no light.
- Have Jason say "That doesn't guarantee..." and trail off. Let him finish his sentences.
