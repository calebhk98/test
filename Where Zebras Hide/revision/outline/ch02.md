# Chapter 2: Displaced

**When and where:** Friday, February 9, at about 4:47 pm local time. Woods on the Cumberland Plateau near Crossville, Tennessee, which is on Central Time.
**Length:** about 2,800 words. This is shorter than the old draft.
**You also get:** the old draft of this chapter, as raw material.

## Where things stand

- Seconds ago they were on a Columbus sidewalk, and someone attacked them. Mira saw blue light and something hit it. Then the world lurched.
- She knows nothing about magic and believes in none of it.
- Jason is in love with her and has never said so. She doesn't know.

## What this chapter does

Disorientation, written like a car accident, not a chosen-one moment. Mira copes the way she copes: by documenting things and checking them. Jason is frightened, and the reader should be able to see that. The chapter turns on one decision: she doesn't call 911.

## Scenes

### Scene 1: Waking up wrong (about 700 words)
- **Mira wants** to know if she's hurt, and where she is.
- **Beats:**
  1. Ringing ears, scraped palms, nausea. She checks herself: fingers, head, can she stand?
  2. Jason is ten feet away, turning in a slow circle, watching the trees like he expects something to come out of them.
  3. She tries a bomb theory, then a gas line, then a concussion.
  4. Her phone works. The GPS says Tennessee, near Crossville.
- **Detail to use:** dead leaves stuck to her coat. One brief description of the woods. Don't repeat Chapter 1's last lines.

### Scene 2: The clock (about 500 words)
- **Mira wants** facts.
- **Beats:**
  1. Her phone says 4:47 PM. They left the coffee shop at twenty to six, and the attack was minutes later. An hour has gone missing, backward.
  2. She stares at it, decides the phone is wrong, decides she might be wrong, and screenshots it anyway.
  3. She doesn't solve it. She won't for a long time. Don't hint at the answer. (It's the time zone; she works it out much later.)

### Scene 3: Twenty minutes (about 800 words)
- **Mira wants** answers now.
- **Jason wants** time to make sure nobody followed them, and to decide how much he can say.
- **Beats:**
  1. She demands to know what happened. He's scared, and it shows: his hands won't stay still, his voice is too fast, he keeps losing the thread.
  2. He doesn't say "I don't know" over and over. Give him one "I don't know" at most. After that, have him deflect in different ways: "Give me a minute." / "Not here." / "Can you do something for me first?" / "Whether anyone followed us." That last one should land as a clue she notices.
  3. They bargain: thirty minutes, then fifteen, then twenty. "Fine. Twenty minutes. But then you tell me everything."
  4. She sets a timer on her phone. Use the timer: she checks it twice during the chapter, and the last scene ends near zero.

### Scene 4: The texts and the 911 call she doesn't make (about 800 words)
- **Mira wants** to keep her life from noticing she's gone, and to know whether she should call for help.
- **Beats:**
  1. She texts Sarah ("Something came up, can't make it tomorrow"). Sarah: "You ok? You never skip gallery stuff." She texts Jess ("Staying with a friend tonight"). Mom asks about the milk for Sunday. Mira lies smoothly and is startled by how smoothly.
  2. She runs the dream test: types "The quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog", looks away, looks back, and the text hasn't changed. Keep this; it's the best characterization in the old draft.
  3. **The 911 beat.** She types 911 and holds her thumb over call. She imagines the call ("I was in Ohio and now I'm in Tennessee, no, I'm not hurt, yes, I'm sober"). She looks at Jason, who is scared and watching the woods for something. She deletes it. Her reason, in her own words, is that whatever this is, the police can't help with it, and Jason knows something. Partly trust, partly a sense that calling would make it worse. Don't rest it on trust alone.
- **End:** the timer near zero, and Jason walking back toward her. End on a concrete action, not a mood summary.

## Jason in this chapter
- **Wants:** to know nobody followed them, and to put off a conversation he has dreaded for two years.
- **Fears:** that they were followed, and what he's about to have to tell her.
- **Shows it by:** scanning the trees, picking a stick up and snapping it, starting sentences and abandoning them. One honest moment: "I'm scared too."

## Romance beat
She trusts him enough not to call. She notices that his hand, when he finally reaches toward her, is shaking.

## Old-life beat
The texts. Sarah, Jess, Mom and the milk.

## Plant for later

## Verbatim lines (keep exactly)
- "This wasn't a panic attack. This was a legitimate reason to panic. But the breathing still helped."
- The dream-test sentence: "The quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog."
- "I'm scared too."

## Limits for this chapter
- Jason's "I don't know": at most 1. "I know.": at most 1.
- Descriptions of him scanning the woods: at most 2.
- "Two years": at most 1. Twelve inches: 0.
- Lists like "No path. No people.": at most 1.
- One body reaction per scene. Don't pile shaking hands, a dropping stomach and not breathing into one moment.

## Do not
- Explain anything about magic.
- Solve the clock.
- Have her sit on a log before the log exists in the scene.
- End on "twenty minutes that felt like they would never end".
