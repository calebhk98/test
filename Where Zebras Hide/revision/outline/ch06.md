# Chapter 6: Orientation

**When and where:** Saturday, February 10, to Monday, February 12. A driveway car sale outside town, highways, rest stops, then a cabin in the Appalachian foothills (east Tennessee).
**Length:** about 3,500 words. The old draft is about 600 words and covers only Scene 3. Keep its best lines and build the rest.
**You also get:** the old draft, which is the car conversation about the zebras.

## Where things stand

- Mira started producing mana this morning. She has a faint fever-warmth in her chest.
- She agreed that Jason sets the pace of what she learns, and that he tells her when he's deciding something about her.
- She can't go back to Columbus. She has a list in her pocket of what she left.
- She knows no details yet about the "somewhere else", the "things", or how anyone detects her.

## What this chapter does

A road trip that's really a set of revelations. Her body changes, she learns her first skill, she learns why Earth is safe and why she isn't, and Jason tells her who he really is. The car gives them intimacy: there's nowhere to go.

## Scenes

### Scene 1: Cash (about 500 words)
- **Beats:**
  1. Jason buys a fifteen-year-old sedan from a man in a driveway, counting out cash. He has more cash than she's ever seen anyone carry.
  2. She asks. "Years of practice not leaving a trail. It's mostly boring. Prepaid everything, cash jobs, never the same bank twice." Don't make this magic. It's his skill.
  3. Monday morning, from a rest stop, she emails her boss that she's quitting for a family emergency. She stares at "Send" for a long time. Marcus texts twenty minutes later: "??? are you ok". She sends one word back: "fine".

### Scene 2: Headaches and the first lesson (about 700 words)
- **Mira wants** the headache to stop.
- **Beats:**
  1. Headaches, fatigue, and a strange hunger. Jason explains it plainly: her body is restructuring around something new. Eat more than you think. It gets worse before it gets better.
  2. At a rest stop he teaches **sensing**: just feeling what her body is now making.
  3. His first explanation ("like hearing a frequency you've always been deaf to") doesn't help. His second does: "Like suddenly being able to feel your own heartbeat, except it's not your heart and you can't make it stop."
  4. She feels it, sitting on a picnic table in the cold. It's horrible and fascinating.
  5. Mira tries her own version: it's like the moment in a darkroom when the image starts coming up in the tray. He says, "Huh. Yeah. Better than mine."

### Scene 3: The zebra talk (about 900 words; based on the old draft)
- **Mira wants** to know what's coming for her.
- **Beats:**
  1. "How long before something notices me?" "A while. Weeks, months. The problem is it gets louder the better you get."
  2. "So learning to protect myself also makes me more of a target." Give this room. Let her react, then him.
  3. The other side: another dimension overlapping ours, running on mana the way ours runs on sunlight. Things live there.
  4. **The toll.** Crossing over costs them, and the stronger they are, the bigger the share it takes, like a tax bracket. Small things cross for a small meal. Big things would need a huge meal to make it worth it. Ordinary humans produce nothing, so Earth has never been worth it for the big ones.
  5. Mira's model: "So we're zebras. Seven billion of us, hiding in tall grass, and they can't see us because we don't smell like food." He doesn't correct her.
  6. "Were invisible." / "Were," he agreed. Keep it, but expand it slightly with a look or a gesture rather than a third one-word line.
  7. Her line: "Tell me what I need to know to stay alive in the next three months and I'll have an existential crisis about the rest of it later."
  8. His reply, warmly: "That might actually be the healthiest response I've ever seen to this conversation." Keep it, but make it a laugh, not a grade.
- **Details to use:** the billboard for a personal injury lawyer, and the dog with its ears flattened in a minivan window. Keep these from the old draft, and don't spell out what they mean.

### Scene 4: Who he is (about 1,000 words)
- **Mira wants** the real story, and to catch the lie.
- **Beats:**
  1. She brings up his parents: "You told me you didn't get along." He's quiet, and she can see him decide. He tells her the truth.
  2. **His origin, told by him and interrupted by her questions:**
     - He was eight or nine, obsessed with a diagram he'd invented about something that didn't make sense. One afternoon it fit.
     - Two months sure he was dying. He told no one, because a child knows some things you don't tell adults.
     - Years teaching himself in secret. The community found him at twelve. By then he had three techniques nobody had seen.
     - He left at seventeen by not coming back after a summer.
     - His parents are good people. He calls them from numbers they can't trace. He tells everyone they don't get along, because good parents invite questions about why you vanished.
  3. "How old were you when you first almost died?" "Ten."
  4. **The train**, for how fast people figure it out. She asks why it took her one morning and him two months. He explains: the same train can take a mechanic an hour and someone else two years, and having someone walk you through it is the fastest of all. "You had me. I had a crayon."
  5. She is quiet after this. Instead of a null beat, she does something: she reaches over and turns the radio off, or offers him the last of the gas-station almonds.
- **Changes:** Jason is no longer a mystery to her. He's a lonely kid who grew up.

### Scene 5: The cabin (about 400 words)
- **Beats:**
  1. Monday evening. A cabin with a woodstove, a porch, and trees on every side.
  2. He scouted it a year ago, "for if." She asks for if what. "For if I ever had to leave Columbus fast."
  3. She stands in the doorway looking at the woods.
- **End:** an image of the doorway and the woods. No summary.

## Jason in this chapter
- **Wants:** to be honest with her for the first time in two years. It's terrifying and a relief.
- **His voice:**
  - Warm in the car, and funny about the sedan's broken heater.
  - Excited when explaining the toll; he draws it in the air with one hand while driving, and she makes him put it back on the wheel.
  - Awkward about his parents.

## Romance beat
Two people in a car, telling each other the truth. She says, "You should call them," about his parents. He doesn't promise.

## Old-life beat
The resignation email, and Marcus's text, which she doesn't answer.
