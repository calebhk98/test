# Chapter 6: Orientation

**When and where:** Saturday, February 10, to Monday, February 12. A driveway car sale outside town, two days of back roads, a rest stop, a stop in a town far from the cabin, then a cabin in the Appalachian foothills of east Tennessee.
**Length:** about 3,500 words. The old draft is about 600 words and covers only Scene 3, the car conversation about the zebras.
**You also get:** the old draft, as raw material.

## Where things stand

- Mira started producing mana this morning. She has a faint fever-warmth in her chest.
- She agreed that Jason sets the pace of what she learns, and that he tells her when he's deciding something about her.
- She can't go back to Columbus. She has a list in her pocket of what she left.
- She knows no details yet about the other side, the things that cross, or how anyone detects her.
- Jason has a backpack with cash in it. Mira has her bag with her wallet and keys, and her phone.

## What this chapter does

A road trip that's really a set of revelations. Her body changes, she learns her first skill, she learns why Earth is safe and why she isn't, and Jason tells her who he really is. The car gives them intimacy: there's nowhere to go.

## Scenes

### Scene 1: Cash, and the phone (about 500 words)
- **Beats:**
  1. Jason buys a fifteen-year-old sedan from a man in a driveway, counting out cash from his backpack. She has never seen anyone carry that much.
  2. She asks. He says it's years of practice not leaving a trail: prepaid everything, cash jobs, never the same bank twice. It isn't magic. It's his skill.
  3. He tells her to keep her cards in the bag, and to turn her phone off and leave it off. He says why: phones can be traced. They spend Sunday and Monday on back roads, doubling back, because he wants to be sure nobody is behind them. On Sunday he buys her clothes with cash.
  4. Monday morning, in a town well away from the cabin, she turns the phone on for a few minutes while he waits in the car. Two voicemails from her mother. She listens to them and doesn't call back yet. She emails her boss that she is quitting for a family emergency, and stares at Send for a long time. Marcus texts twenty minutes later asking if she's okay. She sends one word back. Then she turns the phone off.

### Scene 2: Headaches and the first lesson (about 700 words)
- **Mira wants** the headache to stop.
- **Beats:**
  1. Headaches, fatigue, and a strange hunger. Jason explains it plainly: her body is restructuring around something new. Eat more than you think. It gets worse before it gets better.
  2. At a rest stop he teaches **sensing**: just feeling what her body is now making.
  3. His explanation is that it's like suddenly being able to feel your own heartbeat, except it isn't your heart and you can't make it stop. This is the scene's one analogy. It lands.
  4. She feels it, sitting on a picnic table in the cold. It's horrible and fascinating.
  5. **Something far off.** In the middle of the lesson Jason goes still. Something small has crossed somewhere, miles away, probably nothing to do with them. He has them back in the car in under a minute anyway, and drives faster than he should for an hour. She never sees anything. It's the first time the danger is real and present for her.

### Scene 3: The zebra talk (about 900 words; based on the old draft)
- **Mira wants** to know what's coming for her.
- **Beats:**
  1. She asks how long before something notices her. He says a while, weeks or months, and that the problem is she gets louder the better she gets.
  2. She says the verbatim line about protection making her a target. Give this room. Let her react, then him.
  3. The other side: another dimension overlapping ours, running on mana the way ours runs on sunlight. Things live there.
  4. **The toll.** Crossing over costs them, and the stronger they are, the bigger the share it takes. Small things cross for a small meal. Big things would need a huge meal to make it worth it. Ordinary humans produce nothing, so Earth has never been worth it for the big ones. Explain it plainly, with no simile.
  5. Mira's model, in the verbatim line below. He doesn't correct her.
  6. She says that makes humans invisible to them. He agrees, and says she isn't anymore. Give this a look or a gesture, not a string of one-word lines.
  7. She says the second verbatim line. He tells her that's the right order to do it in.

### Scene 4: Who he is (about 1,000 words)
- **Mira wants** the real story, and to catch the lie.
- **Beats:**
  1. She brings up his parents and reminds him he told her they didn't get along. He is quiet, and she can see him decide. He tells her the truth.
  2. **His origin, told by him and interrupted by her questions:**
     - He was eight or nine, obsessed with a diagram he'd invented about something that didn't make sense. One afternoon it fit.
     - Two months sure he was dying. He told no one, because a child knows some things you don't tell adults.
     - Years teaching himself in secret. The community found him at twelve. By then he had three techniques nobody had seen.
     - He left at seventeen by not coming back after a summer.
     - His parents are good people. He calls them from numbers they can't trace. He tells everyone they don't get along, because good parents invite questions about why you vanished.
  3. Mira asks the verbatim question below, and he answers it.
  4. **The train**, for how fast people figure it out. She asks why it took her one morning and him two months. He explains: the same train can take a mechanic an hour and someone else two years, and having someone walk you through it is the fastest of all. He had nobody.
  5. She is quiet after this, and she does something small and specific instead of staying silent.
  6. She tells him he should call his parents. He doesn't promise.
- **Changes:** Jason is no longer a mystery to her. He's a lonely kid who grew up.

### Scene 5: The cabin (about 400 words)
- **Beats:**
  1. Monday evening. A cabin with a woodstove, a porch, and trees on every side.
  2. He scouted it a year ago, in case he ever had to leave Columbus fast. The land around it gives off a little mana of its own, especially by the creek, and that hides some of what she gives off. It's one reason he picked it.
  3. He works remotely for a few clients through a prepaid hotspot, paid in cash. Every ten days he drives to a town thirty minutes away for supplies.
  4. She stands in the doorway looking at the woods.
- **End:** an image of the doorway and the woods. No summary.

## Jason in this chapter
- **Wants:** to be honest with her for the first time in two years. It's terrifying and a relief.
- **His voice:**
  - Warm in the car.
  - Clear and patient explaining the toll, the way someone explains a thing they've had to explain before.
  - Quiet and careful about his parents; this is the part he's never told anyone.

## Romance beat
Two people in a car, telling each other the truth. She says he should call his parents. He doesn't promise.

## Old-life beat
The stop in town: her mother's voicemails, the resignation email, Marcus's text and the one word she sends back.

## Rules in play
- Sensing.
- Output rises with skill.
- The toll grows with the creature's strength.
- Ordinary humans produce zero.
- The train rule: exposure, frequency, mind.

## Verbatim lines (keep exactly)
- "So learning to protect myself also makes me more of a target." Mira to Jason, after he tells her she gets louder the better she gets.
- "So we're zebras. Eight billion of us, hiding in tall grass, and they can't see us because we don't smell like food." Mira to Jason, after he explains the toll and why Earth isn't worth it for the big ones.
- "Tell me what I need to know to stay alive in the next three months, and I'll deal with the rest later." Mira to Jason, after the zebra exchange, when it's all too much.
- "How old were you when you first almost died?" / "Ten." Mira interrupts his account of teaching himself in secret to ask when something first came close to killing him. Jason answers in one word.

## Limits for this chapter
- Analogies: one per scene at most, and Mira makes at least one herself.
- "Two years": at most 1.
- Jokes or quips: 0.
- Photography images: at most 1.

## Do not
- Have Jason explain how mana works in the body.
- Name or describe the people who attacked them beyond what she already knows.
- Tell the origin story in one uninterrupted monologue. She asks, guesses wrong and pushes.
- Give her phone any more time on in this chapter than the one stop in town.
