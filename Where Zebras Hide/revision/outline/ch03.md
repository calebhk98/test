# Chapter 3: The Ultimatum

**When and where:** Friday, February 9, dusk, about 5:15 pm local time. The same woods in Tennessee. About half an hour of usable light is left.
**Length:** about 3,400 words.
**You also get:** the old draft of this chapter, as raw material. Its spine is good: the options, the confession, the erasure refusal, the gold analogy. Its repetition is bad.

## Where things stand

- The attack (an invisible force against a flash of blue), the teleport and the clock are all unexplained. Mira called time on Jason's twenty minutes.
- She knows nothing about mana. **Jason must not explain how it works.** Explaining the mechanism would start her change.
- Jason wasn't planning to leave Columbus. He is deciding it now, because the people who attacked them found him in Columbus by his mana.
- They have no car. They are about three hundred miles from Columbus. Jason has cash in his backpack, so tomorrow they can take a bus.
- Mira has her bag, her phone, and the roll of film Jason gave her in her coat pocket. The Henderson report still has two sections left.

## What this chapter does

The emotional core of Act I.
- Jason explains what he safely can, calmly and plainly, and the dilemma takes shape: knowing changes you, so nobody can consent in advance.
- He confesses he loves her, by accident, as the reason he has to leave her behind.
- He offers to erase her memory and she refuses.
- He gives her the choices he sees. She cuts him off, and demands time.

Put physical action and thought between stretches of dialogue. The chapter is mostly talk, so give her hands, her body and the ground something to do, and give Jason plain things to do, without making any of them symbols.

## Scenes

### Scene 1: What he is (about 800 words)
- **Mira wants** the truth, all of it.
- **Jason wants** to tell her enough that she'll let him go, and no more.
- **Beats:**
  1. He tells her plainly, without fumbling:
     - People attacked him. She didn't see them because they didn't want to be seen. "You just happened to be there."
     - He can do things most people think are impossible. He isn't the only one. There are others, and some of them want what he knows.
  2. She reacts badly rather than calmly: a laugh, a demand for proof, or a question that misses the point. Pick one.
  3. She asks whether that was teleporting. He says yes, more or less. He could have aimed it, but an aimed jump can be followed, and a random one can't. He chose a random jump so nobody could follow, which is why it landed in Tennessee and not somewhere he picked. He has done it many times. He doesn't explain how it works, and when she asks, he says he won't.
  4. Getting home: he won't aim a jump back to Columbus, because an aimed jump can be followed and Columbus is where they would look. Another random jump would put them somewhere else again. So it's a bus or a rental car tomorrow, and either is fine.
  5. He has to leave Columbus now. He says it like a man revising plans he'd hoped never to need.

### Scene 2: The confession (about 700 words)
- **Mira wants** to go with him.
- **Beats:**
  1. She asks to come with him. He says no. She argues. He argues back, controlled, then less controlled.
  2. His control breaks once, in the verbatim line below.
  3. She is furious, gutted and, underneath, elated, and ashamed of being elated. Show the elation in one line of what she does, without naming it, before the anger wins. Don't use an outside event to stand for it.
  4. She says the second verbatim line.
  5. She puts her hand in her pocket and finds the roll of film. She tells him he found her discontinued film and never once asked her to dinner. He has no answer.
- She had imagined this moment in ordinary places, like the break room or walking to her car, and never like this. Use that once.

### Scene 3: The erasure offer (about 700 words)
- **Mira wants** to keep her own mind.
- **Beats:**
  1. He tells her, in his own words, that he can make her forget. There are two ways.
     - Just tonight. She would wake at home remembering coffee and nothing after it, and he can leave her remembering that he moved away for work, so there is a reason he's gone.
     - Or all of him, the whole two years. Most skilled people can take years, and he can.
  2. Neither has side effects. He has done it before to other people, when he had to.
  3. He can still do it to her only because she hasn't started. One line. Don't explain what "started" means.
  4. She asks how she would know he hasn't already done it to her. Let the question sit.
  5. He says he hasn't, ever. She believes him, and she can find nothing in her memory of those coffees that would prove it either way. End the beat there; don't state the point.
  6. She refuses, in the verbatim line below.
  7. He accepts it. He doesn't raise it again.

### Scene 4: The choices, and why she can't consent (about 900 words)
- **Mira wants** a real choice.
- **Beats:**
  1. Jason gives her the choices in his own words, once, and she interrupts him before he finishes.
     - He leaves, and she never sees him again.
     - He erases tonight, or him, as he has just said, and leaves.
     - She comes with him knowing nothing, which is what she just asked for. He answers that request: she would probably be found and killed. She argues for it once more. He shuts it down.
     - He tells her everything, and it changes her. She cuts in here, and he explains why that one is not simple.
  2. Why he can't just explain: the gold analogy, in the verbatim line below.
  3. Then the verbatim line about consent.
  4. The odds. She asks how long people who start usually last. He answers. She asks who they are. He answers. Let it stop the conversation. She thinks of two things she would lose, such as the summer, Nora's graduation in June, or the roll she never developed. Show two, one line each.
  5. She asks why the attackers want what he has. He says they want it and he won't give it to them. He won't say more tonight.
  6. She sees the shape of it. Every choice except the last one means giving him up. She doesn't say so. Show it in what she does, not in a statement.

### Scene 5: Time (about 300 words)
- **Beats:**
  1. She refuses to decide tonight.
  2. He gives her until they are back in Ohio. Once she is home, she chooses.
  3. He says they should start walking toward a road before it's dark. Say it once.
- **End:** on an action: her standing and brushing off her jeans.

## Jason in this chapter
- **Wants:** to leave, and to keep her safe and whole.
- **Fears:** that she'll say yes.
- **What he's like:** calm, plain-spoken and honest within limits. He doesn't joke, ramble or explain technique.
- **His one break:** the confession. Afterward he's quieter, not flustered.

## Romance beat
The confession, a moment of joy inside the anger, and the film in her pocket.

## Old-life beats
Show two of the plans she is afraid to lose (the summer, Nora's June graduation, the undeveloped roll, the Henderson report she won't finish). One line each, in different scenes. Each comes because the talk leads there: the odds in Scene 4 make her count what she'd lose, and the film in her pocket in Scene 2 leads to the undeveloped roll at home.

## Verbatim lines (keep exactly)
- "It wouldn't be fair of me to risk your life just because I love you!" Jason, shouting, in answer to Mira's insistence that she come with him.
- "You love me so you've been lying to me for two years?" Mira to Jason, right after the confession.
- "My mind is mine. You don't get to edit me for my own good." Mira to Jason, refusing the erasure.
- "If I told you there's gold buried in a field somewhere, that's useless. But if I said it's twenty-five feet down near yellow oak trees? Now every time you see an oak tree, your brain checks." Jason to Mira, explaining why he can't just tell her.
- "There is no informed consent here." Jason to Mira, right after the gold analogy.
- "A few months, usually. Sometimes less." Jason, answering Mira's question about how long people who start usually last before something finds them.
- "Kids stumbling into it. Loners teaching themselves." Jason, answering Mira's question about who those people are.

## Limits for this chapter
- Jokes or quips: 0.
- "Two years": at most 3, including the verbatim line.
- One body reaction per scene.
- Props or repeated gestures: 0, other than the film in her pocket, once.
- Outside events or objects standing for feelings (quiet, light, cold, furniture): 0.
- Exchanges that end on a neat closing line: let two or three run on, overlap or go wrong instead.

## Do not
- Explain how anything works, or use the word "mana". He says "it", "this" or "what I can do".
- Make Jason scared, awkward, rambling or childish.
- Suggest the teleport was new or experimental, or that he was too slow.
- Have Mira restate the gold analogy back to him.
- Have the narration point at the "started" line.
- Have Jason say the attackers saw Mira, or that she is in this whether she learns or not.

## Set up in this chapter
- "You haven't started." Say it once, plainly, as the reason erasure can still work. Nobody comments on it.
- Jason has erased people before, when he had to. He says it as a plain fact.
- The attackers want what he knows, and he won't give it to them.
- Most people who start work it out alone. It comes up as part of the odds.
