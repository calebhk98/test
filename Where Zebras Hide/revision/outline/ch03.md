# Chapter 3: The Ultimatum

**When and where:** Friday, February 9, dusk. The same woods in Tennessee.
**Length:** about 3,500 words. The old draft is about 4,100 and padded. Cut about 40%, then rebuild with the new beats.
**You also get:** the old draft of this chapter, as raw material. Its spine is good: the options, the confession, the erasure refusal, and the gold analogy. Its repetition is bad.

## Where things stand

- The attack, the teleport and the clock are all unexplained. The timer just ran out.
- Mira knows nothing about mana. **Jason must not explain how it works.** Explaining the mechanism would start her change.
- They have no car. They're about 370 miles from Columbus.

## What this chapter does

This is the emotional core of Act I.
- Jason explains what he safely can, and the dilemma takes shape: knowing changes you, so nobody can consent in advance.
- He confesses he loves her, by accident, as the reason he has to leave her behind.
- He offers to erase her memory, and she refuses.
- He lays out four options. She demands time.

## Scenes

### Scene 1: What he is (about 1,000 words)
- **Mira wants** the truth, all of it, now.
- **Jason wants** to tell her enough that she'll let him go, and no more.
- **Beats:**
  1. The people on the street were attacking him. "You just happened to be there."
  2. He can do things. He's not the only one. Some people want what he knows.
  3. The teleport was random: "I didn't pick Tennessee specifically. That's what randomized means." Don't explain yet that he narrowed the range.
  4. Getting home: he can't aim it, so it's a bus or a rental car tomorrow. "Tomorrow, probably."
  5. He has to leave Columbus. He came tonight to say goodbye. That's why it was Friday. She takes this badly.

### Scene 2: The confession (about 700 words)
- **Mira wants** to go with him.
- **Beats:**
  1. "Can I come with you?" "No." She argues. He argues back, louder.
  2. "It wouldn't be fair of me to risk your life just because I love you!"
  3. Silence. A bird takes off. She's furious, gutted and, somewhere underneath, elated, and ashamed of being elated.
  4. Let her feel the elation for one full breath on the page before the anger wins.
  5. "You love me so you've been lying to me for two years?"
- **Keep** the old draft's paragraph about imagining this moment "in the break room, walking to my car" and never like this. Use it once.

### Scene 3: The erasure offer (about 700 words)
- **Mira wants** to keep her own mind.
- **Beats:**
  1. He can make her forget: tonight, the attack, all of it. "I do it sometimes. When I have to."
  2. He can still do it to her because "you haven't started yet". This plants the rule: erasure only works before production begins. One line. Don't explain what "started" means.
  3. "How would I know?" Could he have already done it? Let the question sit for a moment. It's the scariest thing in the chapter.
  4. He swears he hasn't. She half believes him.
  5. "My mind is mine. You don't get to edit me for my own good."
  6. Cut his second offer to erase "all of it" if she hates him. It reads as manipulative.

### Scene 4: The options, and why she can't consent (about 900 words)
- **Mira wants** a real choice.
- **Beats:**
  1. He lays out the options once, clearly, in dialogue:
     - he leaves and she never sees him again
     - he erases tonight and leaves
     - she follows him without knowing, and is probably found and killed
     - he tells her everything, and it changes her
  2. Don't have her re-list them in her head. If you want her mind looping, use one short broken line, as in the old draft's "Option two" stutter, used once and clearly on purpose.
  3. Why he can't just explain: the gold analogy. "If I told you there's gold buried in a field somewhere, that's useless. But if I said it's twenty-five feet down near yellow oak trees? Now every time you see an oak tree, your brain checks."
  4. "There is no informed consent here."
  5. The odds: "A few months, usually. Sometimes less." Most of them figure it out alone: "Kids stumbling into it. Loners teaching themselves." Most who start alone die. Let the line stop the conversation for a moment. She thinks of the summer, Nora's graduation in June, and the roll of film she never developed.
  6. The pet analogy, briefly, once.
  7. She asks why the attackers want what he has. He gives her the short version, "they want it and I won't give it", and won't say more. That's for later.

### Scene 5: Time (about 400 words)
- **Mira wants** time to choose properly.
- **Beats:**
  1. She refuses to decide tonight.
  2. He gives her until they get back to Ohio: "once you're safe at home, you need to choose."
  3. They sit as the light goes. He says they should start walking toward a road. Say it once.
- **End:** on an action or an image, such as her standing up and brushing leaves off her jeans, or the first headlights through the trees. Don't end on a sentence explaining how she feels.

## Jason in this chapter
- **Wants:** to leave, and to keep her safe and whole. He's caught between those.
- **Fears:** that she'll say yes.
- **His voice:**
  - He's emotional here, not lecturing.
  - When he explains he gets excited despite himself, talks with his hands, and catches himself.
  - When he's cornered emotionally he blurts out the true thing.
- **He is not** a calm, careful ethics professor. He's a scared man who has rehearsed this and is doing it badly.

## Limits for this chapter
- "Two years": at most 3 (the old draft has more than 10). "Twelve inches": at most 1.
- "He closed his eyes": at most 1. "We should move soon": once.
- One body reaction per scene. Hands shaking: at most twice in the whole chapter.
- Profound one-line paragraphs ("It didn't work. / Nothing worked."): at most 1.
- The door metaphor: 0.
- No thesis line at the end.

## Do not
- Explain how mana works, or use the word "mana". He says "it", "this" or "what I can do".
- Have Mira quote things Jason never said. Cut "You said they don't care about me. That I'm useless to them."
- Duplicate paragraphs.
