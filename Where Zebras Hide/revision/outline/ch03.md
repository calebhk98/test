# Chapter 3: The Ultimatum

**When and where:** Friday, February 9, dusk. The same woods in Tennessee.
**Length:** about 3,000 to 3,500 words. Shorter is fine; never pad.
**You also get:** the old draft of this chapter, as raw material. Its spine is good: the options, the confession, the erasure refusal, the gold analogy. Its repetition is bad.

## Where things stand

- The attack (an invisible force against a flash of blue), the teleport and the clock are all unexplained. Mira called time on Jason's twenty minutes.
- She knows nothing about mana. **Jason must not explain how it works.** Explaining the mechanism would start her change.
- **Jason wasn't planning to leave Columbus. He is deciding it now**, because the people who attacked them know where he lived.
- They have no car. They're about 370 miles from Columbus.
- The roll of film he gave her is in her coat pocket.

## What this chapter does

The emotional core of Act I.
- Jason explains what he safely can, calmly and plainly, and the dilemma takes shape: knowing changes you, so nobody can consent in advance.
- He confesses he loves her, by accident, as the reason he has to leave her behind.
- He offers to erase her memory and she refuses.
- He lays out four options. She demands time.

## Scenes

### Scene 1: What he is (about 900 words)
- **Mira wants** the truth, all of it.
- **Jason wants** to tell her enough that she'll let him go, and no more.
- **Beats:**
  1. He tells her plainly, without notes or fumbling:
     - People attacked him. She didn't see them because they didn't want to be seen. "You just happened to be there."
     - He can do things most people think are impossible. He isn't the only one. There are others, and some of them want what he knows.
  2. She asks whether that was teleporting. He says yes, more or less, and that it's random: "I didn't pick Tennessee specifically. That's what randomized means." He's used it many times. He doesn't explain how it works, and when she asks, he says he won't.
  3. Getting home: he can't aim it, so it's a bus or a rental car tomorrow.
  4. He has to leave Columbus now. They know where he lived. He says it like a man revising plans he'd hoped never to need.

### Scene 2: The confession (about 700 words)
- **Mira wants** to go with him.
- **Beats:**
  1. "Can I come with you?" "No." She argues. He argues back, controlled, then less controlled.
  2. His control breaks once: "It wouldn't be fair of me to risk your life just because I love you!"
  3. Silence. She's furious, gutted and, underneath, elated, and ashamed of being elated. Give the elation one full breath on the page before the anger wins. Don't use an outside event (a bird, the wind) to stand for it.
  4. "You love me so you've been lying to me for two years?"
  5. She puts her hand in her pocket and finds the roll of film. "You found me discontinued film, and you never once asked me to dinner." He has no answer, and he knows it.
- **Keep** the old draft's paragraph about imagining this moment "in the break room, walking to my car" and never like this. Use it once.

### Scene 3: The erasure offer (about 700 words)
- **Mira wants** to keep her own mind.
- **Beats:**
  1. He tells her, plainly, that he can make her forget. There are two ways:
     - just tonight (the attack, the woods, this conversation), so she'd remember him moving away
     - or all of him, the whole two years
  2. It leaves no gap she'd notice and no side effects. "I do it sometimes. When I have to."
  3. He can still do it to her because "you haven't started yet". One line. Don't explain what "started" means.
  4. "How would I know?" Could he already have done it? Let the question sit. It's the most frightening thing in the chapter.
  5. He says he hasn't, ever. She believes him and understands that believing him proves nothing.
  6. "My mind is mine. You don't get to edit me for my own good."
  7. He accepts it. He doesn't raise it again.

### Scene 4: The options, and why she can't consent (about 900 words)
- **Mira wants** a real choice.
- **Beats:**
  1. He lays out the options once, clearly:
     - he leaves, and she never sees him again
     - he erases tonight, or him, and leaves
     - she follows him without knowing, and is probably found and killed
     - he tells her everything, and it changes her
  2. Why he can't just explain: the gold analogy. "If I told you there's gold buried in a field somewhere, that's useless. But if I said it's twenty-five feet down near yellow oak trees? Now every time you see an oak tree, your brain checks."
  3. "There is no informed consent here."
  4. The odds: "A few months, usually. Sometimes less." Most figure it out alone: "Kids stumbling into it. Loners teaching themselves." Most who start alone die. Let it stop the conversation. She thinks of the summer, Nora's graduation in June, the roll she never developed.
  5. She asks why the attackers want what he has. "Because they want it and I won't give it to them." He won't say more tonight.

### Scene 5: Time (about 400 words)
- **Beats:**
  1. She refuses to decide tonight.
  2. He gives her until they get back to Ohio: once she's home, she chooses.
  3. He says they should start walking toward a road before it's dark. Say it once.
- **End:** on an action or image: her standing and brushing off her jeans, or the first headlights through the trees.

## Jason in this chapter
- **Wants:** to leave, and to keep her safe and whole.
- **Fears:** that she'll say yes.
- **What he's like:** calm, plain-spoken and honest within limits. He's had to think about this kind of conversation before. He doesn't joke, ramble, read notes or explain technique.
- **His one break:** the confession. Afterward he's quieter, not flustered.

## Limits for this chapter
- Jokes or quips: 0, except at most one nervous, unpolished line from Mira.
- "Two years": at most 3.
- One body reaction per scene.
- Props or repeated gestures (sticks, phones, notes): 0.
- Outside events standing for feelings: 0.
- The door metaphor: 0.

## Do not
- Explain how anything works, or use the word "mana". He says "it", "this" or "what I can do".
- Make Jason scared, awkward, rambling or childish.
- Say the teleport was experimental or new, or that he was too slow.
- Duplicate paragraphs.
