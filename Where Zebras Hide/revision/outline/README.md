# How the chapters get drafted

Each chapter is drafted by one Haiku agent that sees only what's listed here, not the rest of the book.

## What each drafter gets

1. `outline/chNN.md`: its own chapter. Every chapter file is self-contained. It opens with "Where things stand", which says what has happened and what Mira knows at the start.
2. `outline/BOOK_MAP.md`: the acts up to and including the drafter's own, plus the "What follows" line of its own act. Strip every later act. For a Ch 28 to 30 drafter, keep the full Ch 30 line; for everyone else, strip it.
3. `RULES.md`, `CHARACTERS.md`, `CREATURES.md`, `STYLE.md`. In RULES, CHARACTERS and CREATURES, strip the section headed "Act IV (only for Ch 22 to 30 drafters)" for any chapter before Ch 22.
4. The packet from earlier chapters: the full text of the previous drafted chapter (two chapters if they're short), plus the critic's fact log from the previous chapter, when there is one. The fact log covers clothes, objects, times, plans and what was said, so the join lines up.
5. For Chapters 1, 2, 3, 6 and 30: the old draft of that chapter, as raw material.

Drafters never get `PLANTS.md` or `TIMELINE.md`. They are planning files.

## What each drafter must do

- Write only its own chapter, in Mira's first person, past tense.
- Length: hit the beats. If you're 15% short of the word count given, stop. If you're 20% over, cut. No other length rule applies.
- Follow the chapter's "Limits for this chapter". These are counts, not suggestions.
- Copy only the lines marked **verbatim**. Everything else in the outline describes intentions, not sentences.
- If a beat seems to break RULES.md, follow the rules and add a note at the end of the draft.

## Drafting order

1. Draft in order, 1 to 30, so each drafter has the previous chapter's ending.
2. If the user wants speed, chapters can be drafted in parallel in act-sized batches, then smoothed at the joins afterward.

## After drafting

Each chapter should get a check against its "Limits" and "Do not" lists before the next one starts. A Sonnet reviewer, or three Haiku, can do this. The reviewer also writes the chapter's fact log for the next drafter.
