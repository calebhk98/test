# Author rulings (these override DOC_FIXES.md and every review)

## Who decides what

- **The author decides the book-level plot:** what happens, why characters do it, how the world and the abilities work.
- **The outline (Claude) decides the chapter level:** which scenes a chapter has, the order of the beats, what each chapter sets up, the romance beat, the length.
- **Haiku writes the prose.**
- **Reviewers flag problems.** A reviewer finding that would change the book-level plot goes to the author as a question. It is never applied directly.

The docs review and consistency pass broke this rule: several book-level changes were applied without the author. The rulings below undo them. For anything below that says "restore", the version to go back to is the one at git commit `eec2723` (`git show eec2723:"Where Zebras Hide/revision/<file>"`), which is from before the reviews.

## Rulings

1. **Clones.** Jason can make clones. He teaches Mira how. She goes on to Stage 4, clones that make clones, which he can't do, and she is better at it than he is, but he still has ordinary clones. Inside a Box, a clone of Jason fights the creature on a loop. Delete the "construct" and every "Jason can't clone" line.
2. **Erasure.**
   - Most skilled practitioners can erase years of memory.
   - Jason can also manipulate memories, for example leaving her remembering that he moved away for work.
   - The only limit is that once a person has started producing mana, erasing their memory, even years of it, doesn't stop it.
   - Ch 3's two options stay: tonight only, or all of him. Neither has side effects.
3. **Teleport.**
   - Jason can aim a teleport.
   - An aimed teleport can be blocked or tracked. A random one can't, which is why he jumps at random to escape.
   - In Ch 1 he changed the random jump in the instant to keep it off open water.
   - Delete every "can't aim" and "can't pick the spot" line. Where a chapter needs a reason he doesn't simply teleport somewhere, the reason is that an aimed jump can be followed.
4. **Mira's reason to learn.** If she doesn't learn, she has to give him up. It's a romance reason, not a logical one. Restore the Ch 3 and Ch 5 reasoning to the pre-review versions. Delete "the attackers saw her, so she's in it anyway".
5. **The time zone.** Keep it a mystery. Ordinary adults don't know offhand where time zones change. They leave at 5:40, she sees 5:42 on her phone on the walk, and the attack is about 5:44. Her phone reads 4:47 in Tennessee. She can't explain it, and works it out in Spain (Ch 14). Restore the Ch 14 payoff.
6. **The hunters.**
   - They aren't the FBI. They look for Jason by mana, not by records, so names, phones, rentals, hotels and accounts don't give him away.
   - They aren't mortal enemies. They want him to show them his techniques, they pressure him, and they have to set up before they can attack.
   - Jason doesn't run the moment hunters are near, or he'd be teleporting all the time. He waits until there's a real threat, just as he didn't run when Delilah showed up.
   - They found the cabin through its mana, not through Delilah. Delete Delilah's gossip as the leak.
   - Nobody needs to warn Delilah. The hunters don't care about her.
   - Restore the pre-review phone handling. The "phone stays off" rule is deleted.
   - Restore the Ch 13 premise to the pre-review version, with this added reason for staying: it's easier to wait until it becomes a threat.
   - Ch 22: Jason goes to the meeting because these are not people who would kill him at a dinner. Delete the host's safety promise.
   - The Vermont house under another name is fine. Delete "buys months, not years".
7. **The walker and 100 million.** India has about 1.48 billion people, so a hundred million is under 7%. Restore the pre-review mechanism. Keep only the geography fix: it crosses near the Haryana border and walks toward Delhi.
8. **Ch 15's reason for crossing.** It's so she can practice. Hiding isn't the reason. Restore the pre-review reason, and the pre-review travel (the random jumps).
9. **Ch 29 and Ch 30.** On hold until the author says what they should be. Don't edit them except for rulings 1 to 8.
10. **Other book-level changes from the reviews.** Any change that came from DOC_FIXES.md or the consistency check and alters what happens or why gets restored to the pre-review version, and listed for the author. That covers motives, abilities, who is an enemy, causes, and Jason's arc, for example "agrees with Kael" against "complacent". Changes to how a scene is staged, wording, stage directions, verbatim context, budgets and joins stay.

## Chapter-level rules (the outline's job)

11. **Set-ups.** Each outline has a section "Set up in this chapter". It says plainly what to establish and how lightly: for example, "Show the pickup slowing on the road and driving on. Mira doesn't comment." It never says the thing matters later and never names a later chapter. The content comes from `PLANTS.md` and from the pre-review "Plant for later" sections.
12. **Romance in every chapter.** Every outline has a romance beat: one concrete moment between Mira and Jason, small or large.
13. **Beat order.** Haiku writes beats in the order the outline lists them, so the outline lists them in the order a scene would really go. Old-life items come up because the conversation leads to them, and each beat says what leads to it.
14. **Length.** If the beats can't fill the length, the outline adds a real scene (social, romance, or a quiet moment that matters) instead of leaving the chapter short. Haiku never pads.
