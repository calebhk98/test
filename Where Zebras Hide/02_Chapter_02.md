## Chapter Two

My ears were ringing. High-pitched and constant, like someone had stuck a tuning fork directly into my skull. I tried to push myself up and my arms shook. Everything hurt. My palms were scraped raw, stinging where dirt had ground into the skin. My knees throbbed where they'd hit the ground. There was dirt under my fingernails and leaves stuck to my jacket.

I blinked hard, trying to clear my vision. The world kept tilting slightly to the left, like I was on a boat. My stomach lurched and I had to swallow hard against the nausea.

What happened?

The last thing I remembered was walking with Jason. Talking about something. His work maybe. Then that blue flash. That pressure in my chest. That feeling like I'd been hit by something I couldn't see.

A bomb. That's what it had to be. Some kind of explosion. That would explain the pressure wave, the flash of light, being thrown backward. We must have been near the park when it went off. Maybe a gas line. Or something worse.

I moved my arms experimentally. They worked. Sore, but nothing felt broken. I touched my head, checking for blood or soft spots. My hair was full of leaves and dirt but my skull seemed intact. No wet warmth that would mean bleeding. I ran my hands down my sides, my legs. Everything hurt but in that general bruised way, not the sharp specific pain of something broken or torn.

Okay. I was banged up but functional. That was something.

"Jason?"

My voice came out rough and too quiet. I cleared my throat and tried again.

"Jason?"

Movement to my left. I turned my head carefully and saw him about ten feet away. He was on his feet already, turning in a slow circle, his head tilted like he was listening for something. Looking for something.

He wasn't hurt. At least not visibly. No blood, no limping. His jacket had dirt on it like mine probably did, and there was a leaf caught in his hair, but he was standing steady. More steady than I felt.

"Are you okay?" I asked.

He stopped turning and looked at me. His face was pale, his jaw tight. "Yeah. You?"

"I think so. Nothing broken." I managed to get to my knees. The ground felt solid but my head was still swimming. "What happened? Did you see what it was?"

He didn't answer. Just went back to scanning the area around us, his eyes moving systematically from left to right and back again.

I looked around properly for the first time.

Trees. Trees everywhere. Bare branches against grey sky. Dead leaves covering the ground in thick drifts. No path. No people. No buildings. No street.

Where were the buildings?

We'd been on a sidewalk. Next to the park, yes, but there had been that row of closed businesses across the street. The park itself had benches, trash cans, that little playground on the far side. And beyond it, more buildings. The constant backdrop of the city.

Now there was nothing. Just trees and sky and silence except for wind through branches and the occasional bird call.

"Where's the road?" I asked.

Jason was still scanning the woods. "I don't know."

"What do you mean you don't know? We were just on a sidewalk." I tried to point in what I thought was the direction we'd been walking. "The park was right there. The businesses were across the street."

"I know."

"Then where did they go?"

"I don't know."

His voice was tight. Controlled. He was worried about something but trying not to show it.

I got to my feet slowly. My legs were shakier than I wanted them to be but they held. I took a few steps in what I thought was the right direction. More trees. Turned and walked the other way. Still trees. Nothing but trees in every direction.

No road. No buildings. No park benches or street signs or any indication we were in a city at all.

"This doesn't make sense," I said. My voice sounded too loud in the quiet. "How far did we get thrown? Where are all the people? Why can't I hear sirens?"

"I don't know yet."

"You keep saying that."

He didn't respond. Just kept looking around like he was searching for something specific.

My hands were shaking. I shoved them in my pockets and felt my phone there. Thank god. I pulled it out and checked the screen. No cracks. The display came on normally when I pressed the button.

4:12 PM.

I stared at the time. That was wrong. We'd left the coffee shop after five. I'd checked my phone right before we left. It had definitely been past five.

The explosion must have damaged something. Or maybe I was misremembering. Concussed people got confused about time. That was a thing, wasn't it?

I swiped over to my location services. Maybe the GPS would tell me something useful. Show me where the nearest hospital was.

The map loaded slowly. I watched the little blue dot appear, then the label underneath it.

Cumberland Gap National Historical Park, Tennessee.

I read it again. The words didn't change.

Tennessee.

"Jason." My voice came out sharper than I intended, high and thin in a way I couldn't control. "My GPS says we're in Tennessee."

He turned to look at me. "What?"

"My phone. The GPS. It says we're in Tennessee. Cumberland Gap National Historical Park." I walked over and held out the phone. "We were in Ohio. We were in Columbus. How does my phone think we're in Tennessee?"

He looked at the screen. His face was carefully neutral. Too neutral. "I don't know."

"You don't know? That's all you're going to say?" I pulled the phone back. "Jason, we were just in Columbus. We were walking to dinner. And now my phone thinks we're three hundred miles south and there's no road and no buildings and you won't tell me what's going on."

"I'm trying to figure it out."

"Figure it out faster."

"I need time."

"We don't have time. We need to call 911\. We need to get help. Something happened and I don't understand what and you're just standing there like you're looking for your car keys."

"We can't call 911 yet."

"Why not?"

"Because I don't know what we're dealing with." He was still scanning the woods, not looking at me. "I need to make sure we're safe first."

"Safe from what?"

"I don't know yet. Just give me some time to figure this out."

My heart was pounding so hard I could hear it in my ears. I dug my fingernails into my palms, using the pain to focus. I needed to think. I had to think. "How much time?"

"Thirty minutes. Maybe less."

I stared at him. "Thirty minutes? Jason, I need answers right now. Not in thirty minutes. Now."

"I know. But I don't have answers yet. I have ideas but I need to check some things first."

"What things? What are you checking?"

"Whether we're alone. Whether anyone followed us. Whether it's safe to stay here or if we need to move." He finally looked at me properly. "Please. I know you're scared. I'm scared too. But I need you to trust me for a bit."

"Trust you to do what? You're not explaining anything."

"I will. I promise. But I need to make sure we're safe first and I need to figure out how to explain this because it's not simple."

"Then make it simple."

"I can't. Not yet." There was something desperate in his voice now. "Please. Just give me a little time."

I wanted to scream at him. Wanted to call 911 myself and let them figure it out. But he was scared. Actually scared. And he'd never asked me to trust him before. Not like this.

"How much time?" I asked again.

"Thirty minutes."

"No. That's too long. Fifteen."

"I need at least twenty."

"Fine. Twenty minutes. But then you tell me everything whether you've figured it out or not."

"Okay. Twenty minutes."

I pulled up my timer app and set it for twenty minutes. Hit start. The numbers began counting down. "I'm holding you to that."

"I know."

He went back to scanning the woods. I stood there holding my phone and trying to think through the anger and fear.

I needed to document this. Whatever this was. If I was concussed, if something had actually happened, I needed evidence. Something to show doctors or police or someone later when I tried to explain.

I opened my camera and took a picture of the trees. Then another from a different angle. Then one of the ground, the leaves, the grey sky visible through the branches. I took a screenshot of my GPS showing Tennessee. Another screenshot of the time.

Then I opened my messages. I should tell someone what was happening. Even if I didn't understand it yet.

I pulled up my conversation with Sarah and typed: **Something came up, can't make it tomorrow. Will explain later.**

Sent it before I could second-guess myself.

Then my roommate: **Staying with a friend tonight, back tomorrow**

My mom had texted an hour ago asking me to pick up milk on my way home. I typed back: **Can't tonight, will get it tomorrow**

Short. Vague. Nothing that would make anyone panic but enough that they'd know I was alive and responsive.

I thought about calling someone. Actually explaining what was happening. But what would I even say? There was some kind of explosion or something and now my GPS says I'm in Tennessee and Jason won't explain and I don't know if I'm concussed or hallucinating or if this is actually real?

That sounded insane. They'd call 911 for me. And then what? What would I tell the dispatcher that made any sense?

I put my phone away and tried to breathe slowly. In through my nose, out through my mouth. Like my therapist had taught me for panic attacks.

This wasn't a panic attack. This was a legitimate reason to panic. But the breathing still helped.

I went back to my photos and looked at the pictures I'd just taken. Trees. Leaves. Sky. All clear and detailed. I could zoom in and see individual branches, the texture of bark.

I navigated away and then back. The pictures were the same. Consistent.

I opened a text message and typed: The quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog.

Read it. Navigated away. Came back. Read it again. The words were exactly the same. Nothing changed or scrambled.

In dreams, text didn't stay consistent. You couldn't read the same thing twice and have it be identical. I'd read that somewhere. It was a test lucid dreamers used.

So either I wasn't dreaming, or my brain was being extremely detailed about maintaining the illusion.

Or I was hallucinating. That was possible too. A head injury could cause hallucinations. I could be lying in the park right now with a concussion, hallucinating this entire scenario. The woods, the GPS reading, Jason acting strange. All of it could be my brain misfiring.

But it felt real. The cold air, the sting of my scraped palms, the rough bark of the log when I sat down on it. It all felt too detailed, too consistent to be a hallucination.

My phone buzzed. Sarah had responded.

**Sarah: You ok? You never skip gallery stuff**

**Me: Yeah fine, just got caught up with something. Promise I'm ok**

**Sarah: Call me later?**

**Me: Will do**

I put my phone away before she could ask more questions I couldn't answer.

Jason was deeper in the woods now, maybe thirty feet away. Still doing that slow scanning thing. His hands were at his sides but his fingers kept twitching slightly.

I checked the timer. Still had time left. Too much time.

I should call 911\. That was the logical thing to do. Something had happened, I didn't understand what, and I needed help. That's what 911 was for.

But Jason had asked me not to. And what would I even tell them? I didn't know where I was. My GPS said Tennessee but that couldn't be right. I didn't know what had happened or if anyone else was hurt or if there was any actual danger.

And Jason had promised to explain. In less than twenty minutes now. I could wait that long. I could give him that.

Even though every logical part of my brain was screaming at me to call for help.

I looked at my phone again. Pulled up the dial pad. Typed 911 but didn't hit call. Just stared at the numbers on the screen.

I could press it. Jason wouldn't stop me. He was too far away and too distracted.

But I didn't press it.

Because as angry as I was, as scared as I was, I trusted him. Two years of coffee and conversation and careful distance, and I still trusted him more than I trusted my own understanding of what was happening.

I deleted the numbers and put my phone away.

The sun was lower now. The grey afternoon light was fading toward something darker. It would be evening soon. We needed to figure out what we were doing before it got dark.

I sat on the log and waited and tried not to think about all the ways this could be going wrong.

Jason stayed in the woods doing whatever he was doing. Checking. Making sure we were safe. Or so he said.

The timer counted down. I didn't look at it. Looking at it made the time go slower.

My phone buzzed again. My mom this time.

**Mom: Did you get the milk?**

**Me: Not yet, will get it tomorrow**

**Mom: Everything ok?**

**Me: Yeah fine, just busy**

She didn't respond to that. Good. I didn't want to lie to her more than I already was.

The woods were quiet except for wind and birds. No traffic sounds. No voices. No indication anyone else existed anywhere nearby.

Just me and Jason and trees and a GPS that insisted we were in Tennessee and twenty minutes that felt like they would never end.
