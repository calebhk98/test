# Allocations — who gets what

Everything here is **invented**. The manuscript is Chloe's point of view, so almost
nothing exists about anyone else's home, family, languages, electives or body. That is a
gap to fill, not a fact to preserve.

This document exists for one reason: twelve people writing sheets in parallel will
otherwise give four characters the same language and three the same dead grandfather.
Allocations are assigned here so they cannot collide. Sheet writers **expand** these into
real detail; they do not replace them.

`CURRICULUM_GRID.md` is **Chloe's** timetable, not the school's. Everyone takes eight
academic hours and two afternoon blocks, and the six permanent tracks are shared, but the
language each year, the two rotating academic slots, and both afternoon blocks are
individual. Two students in the same year are doing different subjects in a different
order. Ruth took Portuguese; Chloe never did.

ten-page report was about, which one they use when angry, which one they think in, which
two they mix up, who they argue with in which language.

## One private thing each

Nobody else knows. This is the detail that makes a person feel real, and none of it is in
the book because Chloe cannot see it.

- **Ruth** rewrites her own old work, years later, and has never shown anyone.
- **Sam** writes to his grandmother every week, by hand, and has since he was seven.
- **Kavi** keeps a list of every question he has been unable to answer.
- **Nadia** sends money home from her first real pay and does not mention it.
- **Eli** has never told anyone he was the one who found the fence gap in second year.
- **Theo** keeps a photograph of the cohort he has never shown any of them.
- **Priya** is the only one who writes to Owen after he leaves.
- **Odile** is afraid of the water and has never said so.
- **Fen** knows the name of every person who has had her room before her.
