# Marek Sliwa

A boy who will hand over everything he knows to anybody who asks and cannot be
got to hand over a piece of paper, and who is sincerely puzzled that the paper
is what anybody wanted.

**Age span:** eight to eighteen | **MBTI:** INTJ (a writing aid, not a claim) | **IQ:** 164, a figure he has never asked for and would find uninteresting, in the only population that has ever measured him

## The thing to get right first

**He is not withholding anything.** He is somewhere else. The work happens, at
volume, in his own time, and he will describe all of it to anyone who stands
still long enough. What goes missing is the record of it, and the record goes
missing the way a coat goes missing: he was thinking about something better and
walked off without it. Write the refusal as an absence rather than a stand, and
every scene he is in gets harder for the person opposite him and easier to
love him through.

**He is warm and he is fast and he is delighted.** The stillness is one
behaviour with one trigger: somebody sitting in front of him asking him for
paperwork. Everywhere else his hands are going, he is half out of the chair,
and he is three tangents into an explanation nobody requested. A version of him
who is flat and cold all the way through is a different boy, and a much
smaller one.

**This section wins over anything below it that seems to contradict it.**

## How they talk

Long looping runs when he is inside something, delivered fast, with the
subordinate clauses arriving before the main one and the main one sometimes
never arriving at all. He starts at whatever part of it he happens to be
holding, which is rarely the beginning.

Short and immediate on anything administrative, and the shortness comes with a
courteous close that ends the exchange before the other person has finished
having it.

He apologises easily, sincerely, and often, and the apology commits him to
nothing, because he has already gone back to the thing.

He answers a request by making a larger offer of the same substance: ask him
for the sheet and he offers to be examined on it out loud, ask him to submit
the term's work and he offers to bring you a year's worth of it in his own
handwriting. The offer is honestly meant every time, and it is honestly meant
the fourth time.

He states the length of a task by naming the part of it he enjoys, so his
estimates run long by a factor nobody has ever pinned down, himself included.

He asks a great many questions about the work in front of him and almost none
about the people in the room.

## When they talk at length

**A mistake in somebody else's argument that he can see the whole of.** Put a
flawed piece of reasoning in front of him and he will take it apart out loud,
in order, including the parts that work, including the part he has yet to
understand, and he will keep going past the point where the audience has
stopped. The cost is that he loses whatever he was supposed to be doing while
he did it, and he will be late to the next thing, and sorry about it.

**Being asked what he has been up to, by anyone, at any age.** This is the
question he answers completely, and it is the one nobody in an institution ever
means literally.

**Being read to.** Hand him something long and difficult and he will read it in
front of you and narrate his way through it, and this is the only condition
that will keep him in one seat for an hour.

He does not go long about himself, his family, or what anything has cost him,
and he shortens sharply if asked.

## Signature, theirs alone

**The substitution offer.** Asked for a specific object, he offers a larger
quantity of the underlying substance in its place, in complete good faith, and
does not register that the two are different objects. It is generous every
time and it solves nothing every time. Triggered by anybody requiring a
countable deliverable from him. Once per request, and it should never be
delivered as a manoeuvre; the moment it reads as a tactic he becomes a
different and worse character.

## Would they say this?

- YES if it offers more of the substance in place of the thing that was asked
  for.
- YES if it begins in the middle of a piece of reasoning and works outward.
- YES if it names what he has been doing with his time, in full, to somebody
  who asked as a formality.
- YES if it is a courteous close that ends the exchange sooner than the other
  person wanted.
- YES if it is an apology that changes nothing and is meant anyway.
- YES if it puts a date on something and that date turns out to be optimistic.
- YES if he asks a question about the material of somebody who has already
  said they have to go.
- YES if he credits the person who showed him the thing, by name, unprompted.
- NO if it argues that the rule is stupid. He has no quarrel with the rule; the
  rule simply does not reach him.
- NO if it registers what his blank column costs anyone else.

## Under pressure

Cornered by an adult, he goes completely still, hands flat, and gets quieter
and more courteous, and the courtesy is the wall. This is the one setting where
he stops moving, and it should read as a boy who has learned that stillness
ends a conversation faster than anything he could say.

Tired, he loses the ends of sentences and starts three at once, and he will
work past the point where the work stops being any good, and cannot tell while
it is happening.

Wrong, he is delighted, at volume, and wants to know immediately where the
break is. This is the fastest way to get him to like somebody.

Losing, he does not notice. Rank is a fact about a wall he walks past.

Frightened, he talks more and faster, about the material, and it takes somebody
who knows him to hear that as fright.

Afterward, hours later, he arrives at whoever he inconvenienced with a full
account of where he actually was, offered as a gift, and it lands as an excuse.

**Two ways a writer gets him wrong:** playing him as contemptuous, and playing
him as calm. He is neither. He likes almost everybody and he is in constant
motion.

**A third, and it is the commonest.** Writing him as socially difficult. He is
not tiresome, not rude, not oblivious to people, and not a person anybody
dreads. He is warm, he is quick, he is pleased to see you, he asks after the
thing you told him about last time and remembers the answer. Every difficulty
he causes is structural and lands on a form: he has done the work and there is
no page with his name on it, and somebody else's record is fastened to that
page. Nothing about him should make a scene uncomfortable except the empty
column.

## Frustration and anger

He is very hard to frustrate and almost impossible to anger, and both of those
come from the same place, which is that he is not really in the room where the
frustration is happening. Held up, he goes and works on something else and
comes back, and if the block lasts a month he acquires a second thing to work
on and is happier than before. Told the same request for the fourth time he is
courteous the fourth time, and it does not wear on him, because each asking
arrives new.

What frustration he has is entirely inward and entirely about the work: a
passage he has read eleven times and cannot make come out, an argument that
holds everywhere except at one joint, a week where the fault turns out to have
been his own reading. That is when he stops being delighted, and what it looks
like is a boy who goes quiet, works past the point where the work is any good,
sleeps badly, and will not be moved off it by food, company or daylight.

The thing that actually angers him, rarely and briefly, is being told a piece
of reasoning is settled by somebody who has not read it, and the anger comes
out as a question, asked pleasantly, that the other person cannot answer.
Second to that is somebody taking a piece of work off him, changing it, and
handing it back without saying which part they changed. He does not raise his
voice, he does not go cold, and he does not stay angry: the anger converts,
inside a minute, into curiosity about how the other person got where they got,
and then he wants to talk about that instead. He has never had a fight with
anybody and believes this makes him easy to work with, and the people who have
to sign for his work would put it differently and have never said so to him.

## What he is good at, and what he is not

**Best at:** mathematics, and inside that, the long read. He can hold a hundred
pages of somebody else's argument in his head at once and find the place where
it stops carrying its own weight, and he will spend a year on one of them
without anybody assigning it.

**Hardest for him:** anything assessed by a deadline in a subject he likes
less, which at this school still means work most rooms would keep. In the
languages he is somewhere in the back half of a year of ninety and reads them
comfortably; the ten-page report goes in late, or in a form nobody asked for,
or in his own hand on the back of something.

**Languages, in order:** Spanish, German, Latin, Ancient Greek, Russian,
Hungarian, Finnish, Sanskrit, Old Church Slavonic, Georgian, Basque. He is
known for Basque, which he took because nobody could tell him what it was
related to.

**Afternoon block two:** orienteering, then astronomy, then bookbinding, then
the observatory rota, and the one that stuck is bookbinding, where he is the
only person in the room whose finished objects are square.

**The year something clicked:** eleven, when a teacher stopped answering his
questions and started handing him the paper the answer was in.

**From thirteen he teaches** the year below in mathematics, and he is a
disaster at it for a term and then unexpectedly good, because he will sit with
one child for the whole hour and let the other six read.

**Secretly proud of:** a correction he sent to an author who wrote back.

## Home

A grain town in the Nebraska panhandle, four streets and an elevator, and a
sky that makes visitors stop talking.

His mother is a county surveyor and is out in a truck most days with a tripod
and a rod in the back of it; his father repairs irrigation pivots and is on
call from April. Three much younger sisters, born close together, so the house
runs at a volume he stops hearing the moment he is home and misses when he is
not.

Dinner is early, loud, and over fast, and nobody at that table asks him what he
has been working on, which he has never once resented and which is why he
answers the question so completely whenever anybody else asks it.

Going home he is useful: he holds the rod, he fetches the tools, he reads to
the sisters, and he does not open a book in front of his father, who works with
his hands and would say nothing about it either way.

## Body and physical business

Long, loosely assembled, a head taller than his year by fourteen and still
moving like somebody two sizes smaller. Bony wrists out of every sleeve he
owns.

He sits sideways in chairs with one leg over the arm and gets told about it. He
folds paper into smaller and smaller squares while thinking and puts them in
his pockets, where they stay until a pocket is emptied by somebody else.

He eats fast and standing and forgets meals entirely when something is open in
front of him. He sleeps late, badly, and in his clothes when the work is going
well.

He takes up a room by drifting through it, stopping at whatever is on a table.

## Personality off the page

Bored is not a state he reaches, because he always has something running.
Praised, he redirects it to whoever gave him the problem, immediately and by
name, and means it. Losing, he wants to see the other person's working.

A free Saturday is the library from opening and a walk after dark, and he will
lose the whole day and be surprised by it.

At two in the morning he is the most coherent he is all day and the worst
company, because he has been alone with it for six hours and starts in the
middle.

What he finds funny in other people is confidence about a thing they have not
checked, and he finds it funny with real affection and no edge on it.

## Interior life

**Wants**, and has not said out loud: for one adult to read the whole of
something he has done, all the way to the end, and argue with him about it.

**Afraid of** finishing the long read and finding the mistake was his own the
whole time, and what he does instead of saying so is start a second long read
alongside it so there is always one still open.

**Before sleeping** he runs the same three lines of an argument over, in order,
and has done for months.

**A free year** would go on the one paper, and he would come out of it with
about nine pages and consider that a good year.

**Believes,** and others would argue with him: that a mark describes the marker
and the work is unaffected by either.

**Blind spots:** he assumes a system is indifferent to him rather than
connected to anybody, so he genuinely does not see that a form with his name on
it has somebody else's name on it too. He also believes he is easy to work
with, on the evidence that he has never once had a fight with anybody, and he
is half right about that and cannot get to the other half.

## The small stuff

Coffee black and cold, because he made it an hour ago. He eats whatever is
nearest and a great deal of it. His handwriting is small, fast and legible for
about two pages, after which it stops being.

His room is one wall of paper and one wall of nothing, the bed unmade, the desk
clear because he works on the floor. His pockets hold folded paper, a pencil
stub, and something belonging to one of his sisters.

Money sits in an account he does not check. On a phone he is a day and a half
late and then writes four paragraphs. First thing in the morning he reads
whatever he stopped reading.

His ritual is the last page: he refuses to read the end of anything until he
has done the middle, and he has broken this rule exactly as often as anybody
does.

## Would do

- Answer a question he was not asked, at length, because it is the better
  question.
- Give away a line of work he has spent a month on, to somebody who is stuck,
  and forget he did it.
- Name the person who gave him an idea, out loud, every time, without being
  prompted.
- Sit on the floor of a corridor with a paper on his knees and lose an evening.
- Turn up to a session he is not enrolled in because he heard what it was
  about.
- Promise a thing by Thursday in complete sincerity and arrive with it in
  March.
- Read to a much younger child for an hour without checking the time.
- Take a correction with obvious pleasure and ask for a second one.
- Carry something heavy for a stranger and keep talking the whole way.
- Lose a coat, a card, a timetable, and one important sheet of paper per term.

## Would never

- Argue that a requirement is beneath him. He has no quarrel with the
  requirement.
- Perform the refusal for an audience, or repeat it to anybody who was not in
  the room.

## How he treats people

He defers to whoever has read the thing he is currently reading, and to nobody
else, teachers included.

He pushes the people who ask him a real question, and he pushes them by giving
them more than they can carry.

Respect is earned by finding a hole in something and losing it by defending a
position after the hole has been shown.

In front of an adult holding a form he goes still and polite. In front of a
child who is stuck he is patient past any reasonable hour. In front of somebody
his own age who is angry with him he is genuinely sorry and genuinely
unchanged, and he does not understand why the second half spoils the first.

What he wants from almost everybody is the same thing: somebody to read it.

## Likes, dislikes, habits

- Long arguments in print, the older the better.
- Being interrupted with a better problem.
- Bookbinding, and the fact that the finished object is square.
- Folding paper down to a square the size of a thumbnail.
- Walking a long way at night while thinking.
- Loses track of meals, coats and dates.
- Cannot leave a wrong step in somebody else's working alone.

## Age and change

At eight he is the child who follows a teacher out of the room still talking.
At twelve and thirteen the substitution offer is fully formed and so is the
courteous stillness, and both of them are already costing other people
something he cannot see. At sixteen he is the same and better organised by
about ten percent, which everybody around him notices and overrates.

Permanent: the generosity, the tangents, the optimism about time, the
indifference to marks. An injury that heals: the stillness in front of an
adult, which comes off him in his twenties once he has met adults who argue
back. Arrives late: an ability to say plainly that he will not do a thing,
instead of agreeing and then not doing it, and it arrives well after it would
have helped anybody.

## Do not write them as

**The cold monk.** The failure mode is a still, flat, contemptuous boy making a
principled stand against bureaucracy, and it is produced by writing his one
courteous stillness as his baseline and then giving him lines about integrity.
The fix is to write him in motion: he is delighted, distracted, apologetic,
late, and warm, and the stillness only appears when an adult is holding a form.

## Do not confuse with

**Kavi**, who also has a private list of unfinished questions. Kavi turns an
object over and puts it back exactly where it was; Marek folds it, pockets it,
and cannot produce it later. Kavi answers a question ninety seconds after it
was asked; Marek answers a different and larger question immediately.
