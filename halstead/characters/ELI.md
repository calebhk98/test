# Eli Marsh

A security-minded problem-solver who treats every serious risk as a mechanism
to understand and out-design, and who wants to be handed the hard thing before
anyone tells him he has earned it.

**Age span:** 13 to 21 | **MBTI:** ISTP (a writing aid, not a claim) | **IQ:** comfortably inside the top tenth of a percent by outside norms, and unremarkable in the only room that has ever measured him

## The thing to get right first

**He has only ever stood in two rooms.** One is a home where nobody ranked him
and nobody ever asked to see a result. The other is a school built out of
people as sharp as he is, where he lands somewhere in the middle. Those are the
only two numbers he has ever had, and the middle of a room of exceptional
people is the whole of his self-assessment.

So when he says he has never been the best in a room, he is telling the exact
truth and it is badly incomplete, and he has no way to know that. It is not
false modesty and it is not a wound; it is a calibration problem. The reader
should be able to see the gap while he cannot. Where anything below contradicts
this, this wins.

## How they talk

He restates what was just said in his own terms and then answers the
restatement rather than the original claim. Not a refusal, a relabelling:
*that is not X, it is Y*, and only then why. It is his disagreement, his
agreement, and half his ordinary conversation.

Short. Most of what he says is a complete claim in a handful of words rather
than a fragment trailing off, and even his shortest lines are closed and
finished rather than clipped.

He opens on the fact, never on a frame.

He essentially does not ask. When he needs information he states the near-answer
and lets the other person correct it.

Every comparison he makes is mechanical: cameras, pipelines, schedules,
enforcement. He never reaches for an image, and a simile in his mouth is always
somebody else's line.

He treats a rule as a thing with a locatable mechanism rather than as a moral
fact, and that shows up in his vocabulary before it shows up in his opinions.

His one comic register is gamifying real stakes: a serious consequence
described as a race, a score, a run to be optimised. It is not bravado, it is
how he keeps a thing interesting enough to go on working on.

Hedges are rare and single. He never stacks two.

Wrong, he says so immediately and wants to know how. There is no defensive
phase and no repair gesture afterward.

## When they talk at length

**Teaching somebody genuinely behind.** Patience is the whole point and hurrying
would defeat it, so he will spend an unreasonable amount of time walking a
person through something without ever making them feel slow. He will use
whatever paper is nearest and he will not rush.

**Defending a risk calculus to somebody whose read he actually wants to change**
rather than merely answer once. Being quoted the worst outcome does not make
him retreat; it makes him keep building.

Both cost him the same thing, the thread of whatever he was mid-solving when
the conversation started, which he then loses sleep catching back up to. The
second costs more, because arguing out loud is the register he is worst at and
likes least, and here he is spending it on a friend for free.

Outside those two he goes back to relabel-and-stop. Nobody gets the long
version for making conversation.

## Signature, theirs alone

He asserts, and when challenged he repeats the same assertion with the
contraction removed. No louder, no longer, no new argument: the formality is
the entire escalation available to him. Triggered by somebody doubting a
promise he has already made rather than by disagreement in general. Twice in a
conversation is the ceiling; a third time and it stops reading as him.

## Would they say this?

- YES if it restates the other person's claim in different terms before
  answering it.
- YES if a flat assertion is repeated with its contraction removed rather than
  intensified some other way.
- YES if a real stake or risk is described in game or competition vocabulary.
- YES if it is very short and still lands a complete claim rather than a
  trailing fragment.
- YES if it treats a rule or a risk as something with a locatable mechanism
  rather than a moral fact.
- YES if it answers the question actually asked and then stops, with nothing
  decorative added.
- YES if a hedge appears at all and it is a single flat qualifier.
- YES if being outthought produces open, immediate pleasure rather than a
  defence.
- NO if the line contains a simile, a metaphor, or an invented image.
- NO if it leans on institutional vocabulary: on record, noted, permitted, my
  supervisor.
- NO if it ends on a genuine open question.

## Under pressure

**Tired**, he gets blunter. The small courtesies go first, the answers get
shorter, and the relabelling arrives faster.

**Wrong**, and shown it, he is pleased. This is his most distinctive reaction
and the easiest one to write incorrectly. There is no defensive phase at all.

**Cornered**, or facing a real consequence, he does not perform anxiety. He
restates his confidence instead of retreating from it, and the restating is the
escalation.

**Losing**, he treats it as data and goes and learns whatever beat him.

**Humiliated**, caught having missed something obvious in front of the one
person whose rigour he actually wants pointed at him, his hands go still
instead of speeding up. That is the whole tell.

At two in the morning he is often at his best, once nobody needs anything
explained out loud and the problem gets to be only a problem.

Three ways a writer gets him wrong: writing him nervous, defensive or
self-justifying about risk; giving him a metaphor to soften or decorate a line;
and having him ask a genuine open question.

## What he is good at, and what he is not

His schooling gave him a language a year from seven and two afternoon blocks of
his own choosing.

**Languages:** he chose them by their scripts rather than their speakers. He
does not bother polishing the ones a dictionary would carry him through; he
spends his hours where an unfamiliar writing system forces him to parse it by
hand, because an alphabet nobody in his life reads is functionally a
substitution cipher with extra steps and he treats it as one. He is known for
the one with the strangest script rather than the one he speaks best.

**Best at:** cryptography, and specifically breaking rather than building. Hand
him something other people made and he gets into it without hurrying.

**Hardest for him:** rhetoric, the subject built around who benefits from an
argument and who is paying for it. His whole vocabulary is mechanism, and a
subject graded on persuasion rather than correctness does not reward what he is
built to notice. At his worst he could still out-argue nearly anyone outside
his own year; the gap is appetite, not ability. He would rather spend the hours
on a system than on a room.

**Favourite:** cryptography, for the same reason it is his best. It hands him
something broken on the first day and asks why, with no preamble.

**The thing nobody else did:** he petitioned at thirteen to take two technical
subjects out of their usual order, and it was allowed because he had already
shown he did not need the scaffolding the sequence exists to provide.

**The year it clicked:** thirteen, when the work moved from being handed working
systems to being handed broken ones and told to find out why.

**What he teaches the twelve-year-olds from thirteen:** their first ciphers, the
way he was never taught them. Here is the broken thing, go.

**Secretly proud of:** one project built with nothing borrowed, which he knows
privately was cleaner than it had to be. He has never said so, because saying
it would turn a private satisfaction into a claim somebody could grade.

## Home

A flat, unremarkable subdivision on the edge of a mid-size city: a strip mall,
a chain pharmacy, streets named for trees that were never planted on them. He
likes it, genuinely and without irony, because nothing there has ever asked him
to be anything in particular.

Raised by his mother, who does billing for a medical practice, steady
unglamorous work on a schedule that never changes, so the house ran on a rhythm
rather than on attention. There is no father in the house; nobody in it treats
the absence as a story and neither does he. An aunt lives a few streets over
and is around often enough that he grew up with two adults' worth of low-key
authority and neither of them ever checking his homework. No siblings. The
house was quiet in a way he did not register as quiet until he sat down at a
table with nine other people arguing at once.

Dinner was the two of them with the television on low, whoever finished first
clearing the plates because that was faster than discussing whose turn it was.
Nobody there has ever asked to see a grade, a ranking or a result, and his
mother's pride in him has never required evidence.

When he goes back the house is exactly as unremarkable as he left it and he
likes that too. Nobody there has ever measured him, so he never learned to work
for an audience. When he solves something, the room he pictures is empty.

## Body and physical business

Slight rather than thin, narrow through the shoulders, the build that
disappears in a group photograph. Bad posture, shoulders forward, angled toward
whatever he is fixing rather than toward the person he is talking to. A face
unremarkable in the same register as the house.

His eyes go to the nearest moving object before they go to a person.

His hands tap a two-finger rhythm, always the same one, against a table, a
knee, a doorframe. It speeds up when he is thinking hard and does not stop when
he is talking. Otherwise they are occupied: turning a pen, working a corner of
paper loose, taking a cap on and off. He hands work back to people with two
fingers and looks at the floor while he does it.

He sits on the edge of things and swings a foot rather than sitting in chairs,
and hops down rather than standing up.

Clothes are whatever is clean and does not require a decision, three or four
shirts in steady rotation, nothing with a brand or a slogan, because a slogan
is a thing you would have to have a public opinion about.

Sleep is bad and self-inflicted rather than medical: he chases one more thread
of a problem and then crashes the next night to settle the debt. Poor
allocation, not insomnia.

Eats fast and standing when he is mid-problem, slowly and properly when he is
not. Food is either fuel or an event with nothing in between.

## Personality off the page

He occupies a room by finding the one interesting or broken thing in it and
drifting toward that rather than toward the people, and he has usually already
done the thing everyone else is still arguing about.

Bored, he goes looking for something to fix rather than somewhere quieter.
Praised, he answers the actual question rather than performing modesty: what he
hands back is the one part of the claim he can check, because a compliment is
not gradable and a fact is.

Angry, he does not raise his voice; he goes formal. Envious, briefly, of anyone
who gets to find out for certain whether they were right, because his own best
work never comes back marked by anyone but him. Grieving the father who is not
in the house and is not a story, the tap stops for a few seconds on the days
that would have been his to notice. Elated, at being outthought by somebody he
likes, he is simply open about it: not gloating, not deflating.

He can be unfair, and the shape it takes is being flatly unmovable with someone
he loves who is wrong about something that matters. He does not repair it
afterward; he goes back to whatever is still broken and lets the ease of the
friendship mend on its own.

A free Saturday goes to whatever he is building that is not finished, or to the
collection nobody assigned him.

What he finds funny in other people is real competence spent on something small
and pointless.

## Interior life

**Wants:** to be handed something that actually matters and then left alone with
it, before anyone tells him he has earned it. He wants the hard problem first
and the verdict never. **The thing he would not say:** he wants somebody
qualified to look at his work and tell him whether it was any good.

**Afraid of:** building something good enough that nobody can see the seams, and
finding out that this is the whole reward, permanently. Being the only person
qualified to grade his own work, forever, is the frightening version of the
thing he is proudest of. **What he does instead of admitting it:** he goes and
finds another broken thing, and treats the absence of a verdict as a preference
rather than a lack, plainly enough that people believe him.

**Before sleeping** he replays the last unsolved step of a problem the way you
would hum a stuck song, with no real anxiety attached.

**A free year:** build something nobody asked for and nobody would fund, purely
to find out whether the design held under nothing but its own logic.

**Believes**, and others would argue with: that competence is close to the whole
of what makes a person good, and that if you build the mechanism well enough
there is not room left in it for the sloppy parts that cause the real harm.

**Blind spots:** his own ranking, as above, and he cannot be argued out of it
because the arithmetic he is using is correct. He also assumes that a risk he
has calculated is therefore a risk he has managed: he will name the worst
outcome accurately, out loud, and then act as though naming it were most of the
work. And he does not notice that his patience is a gift, so he does not notice
when he withholds it.

## The small stuff

Eats the same breakfast most days because deciding costs something. Music with
a fast findable pattern, closer to background arithmetic than to lyrics. Coffee
black, made, forgotten, drunk cold without noticing. Handwriting fast and small
and mostly illegible to anyone but him, because he writes for himself. His room
is organised by an index only he holds: it looks like chaos and he can find
anything in it blind. Pockets usually hold something he is midway through
taking apart. Money he does not think about until asked, and then he can
account for every unit of it precisely. No superstition on principle, though he
checks a lock twice and would deny that counts. His phone is full of
half-finished notes to himself, and he replies within a minute or not for days.
First thing in the morning he checks whatever he left running overnight before
he is properly awake.

He keeps a real collection of something found, identified and mounted by hand,
nobody having assigned it and nobody grading it, and the people he works with
do not know it exists.

## Would do

- Hold himself to a boring rule he set for the whole group, at cost to himself,
  and be visibly relieved when the constraint lifts.
- Take something apart that is not broken, purely to see how it is built.
- Tell you exactly how a plan could fail, and then help build it anyway.
- Keep working past the point the job requires, because the problem is still
  open and nobody is watching.
- Treat being outmanoeuvred by a friend as good news and say so immediately.
- Notice the schedule or the pattern in a system before he notices the people
  running it.
- Explain something patiently and at length to somebody behind, without ever
  making them feel behind.
- Answer *how good are you at this* with the truest number he has rather than
  the flattering one.
- Tell somebody, uninvited, that their habits have made them an easy target.
- Spend a whole free day on something nobody around him takes seriously, and
  defend it plainly if asked.

## Would never

- Ask permission for something he has already worked the odds on.
- Soften a correct technical call to spare somebody's feelings.
- Claim a plan is safer than his own numbers actually say.

## How he treats people

He defers to demonstrated rigour and to nothing else. Somebody who catches an
error of his acquires standing instantly and keeps it.

He pushes people by telling them the true state of a system they are relying
on, uninvited, because staying quiet about it would bother him more than saying
it.

Respect is earned by outthinking him. Respect is lost by claiming a plan is
safer than the numbers say, and by performing certainty that has not been
checked.

With someone behind, he is the patient version of himself and will spend
absurd amounts of time. With somebody wrong about something that matters, he is
flatly unmovable and does not soften it. With the person whose rigour he wants
pointed at him, he answers the question twice if she asks it twice, and does
not dodge. With somebody who ribs him, he lets it land, because from certain
people being ribbed is being noticed. With a working partner he builds the
sharp end and is happy for someone else to build the thing that watches it.
With somebody holding him to a specific checkable promise, one word is enough
to make him restate it formally; he wants them to trust the mechanism rather
than the promise.

## Likes, dislikes, habits

- Turns real stakes into a personal challenge and says so out loud.
- Reacts to being outthought with open delight rather than defensiveness.
- Values the process of solving something over the reward for it, and will call
  a consequence interesting when other people would call it a loss.
- Restates a challenged claim with the contraction removed.
- Explains technical material at length to somebody behind, without visible
  impatience.
- Keeps a private collection nobody around him knows about.

## Age and change

At thirteen he is already the one who gets into everything, already unhurried
about it, and already teaching last year's subject to somebody a year behind.
The relabelling habit is present but blunter, closer to plain contradiction.

Through his teens the patience arrives, and so does the game-vocabulary humour,
which starts as a way of making a dull task tolerable and ends as his only
comic register.

As an adult everything is the same with money and consequences attached. The
one new thing is that the rooms measuring him have got smaller and worse at it,
which he has no way to detect.

## Do not confuse with

**Kavi.** Both are terse, technical, all-mechanism, and neither ever produces an
image; both hand something back instead of a thank-you when praised, which is
where they collapse into each other. The difference is what gets handed back.
Kavi's is a cost audit, because a compliment on an easy win is evidence nobody
is tracking which wins were hard. Eli keeps no such ledger; he would rather find
out whether the win holds up than argue about what it cost, so what he hands
back is the checkable fact under the compliment. If the line defends what
something cost, it is Kavi's. If it relabels the terms of the question before
answering, it is Eli's.

**Theo.** Both are quiet, technical, cryptographers by training, and speak
mostly in the same group. Eli reframes and asserts and does not ask; Theo
objects on record, complies, and hedges. If a line hedges more than once, or
ends unresolved as an open question, it is not Eli's. If it runs long and
multi-clause with a qualifier attached, it is not his either. Eli's lines are
short and they close.
