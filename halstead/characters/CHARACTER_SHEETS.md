# Character sheets — index

32 characters, one file each. Every sheet is standalone: hand it to someone with one
chapter and they can find the lines that character would not say, and the scenes where
they are not acting like themselves.

Each sheet carries: how they talk, described as properties, an exclusive signature, a
would-they-say-this test written as properties of a line, **subjects** (best at, worst
at, favourite and why, least favourite, the one they are secretly proud of, the one they
would drop), **personality off the page**, **would do / would never do**,
**body and habits**, **relationships** one line per person, **under pressure**, **age and
change**, and who they blur into. Continuity facts sit in one clearly separated block at the
bottom, and the sheet has to still be complete if that block is deleted.

Start here: **`_CALIBRATION.md`** — how good these people actually are, what a weakness
means at a school where everyone is a genius, and why the teachers never let a student
know anything is impressive. Every sheet is written against it.

Every cohort sheet now opens with a one-line **Engine**, directly under the age and IQ
line: the single sentence to have in mind before anything else on the sheet. The ten are
collected in `_DIFFERENTIATION.md` Axis 8, and no two of them produce the same behaviour
in the same room. Axis 9 does the same for what a compliment does to each of them, which
is where the sheets had converged worst, and Axis 10 assigns each character one register
word that nobody else may use.

Then **`_DIFFERENTIATION.md`**, which leads with how much similarity is correct —
a cohort taught by the same adults from age seven should sound alike, and the parents are
married — then lists the shared baseline to keep and the margins that sit on top of it.
It also records the rule that **nobody in this book is evil**. **`_TEMPLATE.md`** is the
structure every sheet follows.

**`_SHEET_RULES.md` is the specification.** A sheet describes a person, not this book, and
carries **no quotations from the manuscript and no line references**: the draft moves, a
quotation does not, and most of the quotations these sheets used to carry had already gone
stale. Sheets state things plainly, without tags, without measured statistics, and without
citing a page to prove a trait.

**On shared answers.** Eli and Kavi are both best at cryptography; Chloe and Ruth are
both best at mathematics; Kavi and Ruth would both drop politics. Those are correct.
Nine people on one timetable will overlap, and the sheets distinguish by degree and
reason rather than by forcing different answers. The one place a shared answer was
actually wrong is recorded in `ELI.md`, where two agents independently landed on the same
least favourite for the pair that already blurs most.

No measured dials below. Words per line and percentage-terse figures used to sit in this
table; they described a draft that has since been rewritten and writers read them as
targets, so they are gone and each sheet now carries the tendency the number was pointing
at. MBTI is a writing aid, not a claim, and appears nowhere in the book. IQ figures are
carried from the synopsis, which states they are a reader's inference.

## Halstead staff

Every sheet carries the same test. A staff line passes only if praise attaches to a
mechanism or a correct answer and **never to the child**; a correction is a flat
statement, never cushioned; age is never a reason for leniency; an extraordinary result
is treated as expected; and a teacher's own error is fixed the same flat way a student's
is. A generic-teacher line fails by warming up the delivery.

## Adults outside Halstead

Shared test: **kindly-and-inadequate versus unkind.** A line is kindly-and-inadequate if
the adult does their job competently and the failure is structural — the frame was never
built to reach what Chloe needs. It is unkind the moment it stops being about the task
and becomes about Chloe herself. The book's argument depends on none of them crossing it.

## Manuscript and reference-document notes

These are defects in the draft or in the older reference documents rather than facts about
anybody, so they do not live on character sheets. They are recorded, with their chapters and
line numbers, in `passes/fix/SHEETS_DELINK_CHLOE.md`, which is where they can be worked and
closed out.

In summary:

- Nine places where the older notes disagree with the manuscript, all verified against the
  book. Four are gender or naming errors about staff. One attributes an invented image to
  Chloe that another character actually says. One says the father is unnamed when he is
  named repeatedly. Where the notes and the book disagree, the book wins.
- Two collisions the manuscript makes on itself, where two students land the same short
  sentence within one chapter.
- Two different characters are called Deb, twelve years apart and with no connection between
  them. This is not an error and needs no fixing.
- One unresolved contradiction about which activity a departing student did or did not
  complete, stated two ways sixty-six lines apart. It may be deliberate, since the second
  line is shaped as a correction, but nothing signposts it.
