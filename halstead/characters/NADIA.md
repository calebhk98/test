# Nadia

Runs on proportion: fixes exactly what's broken and no more, and wants to build something an actual person uses instead of something built for other companies to buy.

**Weakest sheet in the cast.** Swap-test failure rate 59% — most of her lines would survive being moved into another character's mouth unnoticed. The profile below rests on roughly 13 substantive lines, 9 of them in a single chapter. Past that chapter, treat everything as thin.

**Speaks:** 41 lines total, badly front-loaded. Only 5 lines in the first 20 chapters. Of the 36 later lines, 22 are one- or two-word filler ("yeah," "hang on," "since when"). The substantive core is ~13 lines, 9 of them in CHAPTERS_23_30_v2.md's chapter titled "Nadia."

## Voice in one paragraph
Short independent clauses with no connectives — she doesn't join ideas with "and," "but," or "so," she sets one fact next to the next one. Near-imperative even outside actual commands: "Go back and fight the waitress." She opens on the fact, never on a lead-in ("so," "well," "look"). She essentially never asks — questions measure 0% of her lines, and her one rhetorical exception answers itself in the same breath ("why would a machine have a port"). She disagrees by flat refusal or correction, proportionate to the offense, and then moves on without relitigating. Zero figurative language anywhere. Most characteristic line: "if youre going to come at me, come at me. dont insult me with a cleaner" (CHAPTERS_23_30_v2.md, "Nadia," line 47) — paired imperatives, no adjective, no explanation of why a cleaner counts as an insult. The reader supplies that.

## Would they say this?
- NO if the line joins two clauses with "and," "but," or "so" — she sets facts beside each other, she doesn't connect them.
- YES if it's two or more short independent clauses in a row, stated flatly, with nothing linking them.
- NO if the line hedges — "maybe," "I think," "probably," "I guess." Hedging should read as a near-absent habit for her.
- NO if it's a genuine information-seeking question waiting on someone else's answer. A rhetorical question that answers itself in the same breath is fine.
- NO if her response to a slight or a threat is bigger than the slight itself — she matches the size of the punishment to the size of the offense and nothing more.
- YES if she refuses something outright and the scene moves on within a line or two, with no follow-up justification.
- NO if the line contains a simile, metaphor, or any invented image — figurative language isn't available to her, ever.
- NO if she apologizes, compliments, or softens a judgment for someone else's comfort.
- YES if the line names the person under discussion instead of using "he/she/they," including where the pronoun would be more natural. [signature, proposal — see above]
- NO if she explains her reasoning before stating the decision. The flat statement comes first; if a reason follows, it's one short clause, not a case being built.

## Are they acting like themselves?
- Under a real threat she takes the unglamorous cleanup task rather than the dramatic one — resets a dislocated joint in the aftermath of a fight without being asked or announcing it (chapters/20_the_parking_lot.md).
- Organizes people fast and gets compliance without persuading anyone — splits a floor into working teams in about four minutes; eleven people take orders from her in a row before questioning it (chapters/14_sixty_degrees.md).
- Goes from asleep to mid-sentence with no visible transition — falls asleep sitting up for four minutes, wakes, finishes the sentence she was on (chapters/20_the_parking_lot.md). Treats her own body as another task to manage, not something to explain.
- Investigates before punishing, and does it herself rather than delegating or calling in an outside authority (CHAPTERS_23_30_v2.md, "Nadia" — traces who paid for a theft against her in a week, goes in person, alone, declines to involve police).
- Failure modes for a rewriter: (1) giving her doubt or self-justification before a decision — she decides, then acts, any explanation is short and after the fact; (2) letting her escalate a small provocation into a large response — the proportionality is load-bearing, breaking it breaks the character; (3) having her banter or open a joke — her humor closes a beat someone else started, it doesn't originate one.
