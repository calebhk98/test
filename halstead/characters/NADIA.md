# Nadia

Runs on proportion: she fixes exactly what is broken and no more, and she wants
to build the thing an actual person uses rather than the thing other companies
want to buy. Everything she feels is in the room with her. Almost none of it is
in her adjectives.

**Age span:** roughly 15 to 21 | **MBTI:** ESTJ (a writing aid, not a claim) | **IQ:** genius range by outside norms, unremarkable in the only room that has ever measured her

## The thing to get right first

Read this whole section before writing a line of her. Where anything further
down contradicts it, this wins.

### One register, every content

She has a single delivery and she puts everything through it. An order, a
preference, a compliment, a joke, an apology, a confession and a request for
help all come out at the same length, at the same pitch, with the same absence
of decoration.

This is the most important fact about writing her, because it is the thing that
makes her read as empty when she is not. A reader decodes a flat register as a
negative one by default. Unless the warm contents are actually put on the page,
the only thing anybody hears is the refusals, and she stops being a person.

What a writer has to hold at once:

- When she wants something it sounds like an instruction. She states an
  appetite as a fact, and people take it as a decision they have been handed
  and go and do it, which she notices and finds convenient rather than odd.
- When she is pleased with somebody it sounds like an assessment.
- When she is joking it sounds like a claim, and people check her face before
  they laugh.
- When she is frightened it sounds like arithmetic.
- When she is hurt it sounds like a correction.

**Do not solve this by widening the register.** She should be exactly as flat on
the page at the end of a scene as she was at the start of it, and she should
want things in half her scenes. The fix is contents, never delivery.

### The feeling arrives in her body and in her schedule

She will not name a state, and nobody around her narrates one for her, so the
emotion has to be legible in what she does with the next ten seconds. These are
consistent enough that the people who have known her for years read them
without discussing it:

- **Moved:** she stops. She is a person who is always already moving, so
  stillness is the largest signal she has, and it is involuntary. She does not
  comment on whatever caused it, and she stays where she is standing a beat too
  long before she does the next thing.
- **Afraid:** she recounts. She goes back over a figure she has already done
  correctly, and does it a second time, and then a third if the fear is bad.
  Nothing about the number changes. She would say she is being careful.
- **About to say a thing that will cost her:** sleeves up first.
- **Pleased:** she accelerates. Approval of a result shows as her going at the
  next thing faster and harder than the next thing needs.
- **Ashamed:** she keeps the record and shows it to nobody.
- **Angry:** she narrows. Never louder, always more exact, and the response is
  cut to the size of the offence.
- **Undefended, laughing:** her face gives out entirely, all at once, with no
  build. It is the only thing she does that nobody can predict.

### Her priority is labour, and it is a priority rather than an insight

The organisations around her treat people as a cost, and the question they are
all solving is how to need fewer of them. Nadia's question is how to get enough
of them. Hands are the scarce input, the thing that is hard to find, slow to
replace and expensive to lose, and everything she builds is shaped by wanting
more of that and not being able to buy it.

This is not a thesis she has worked out that other people are too slow to see.
She is not correcting anybody. The people around her are as sharp as she is and
have simply ordered their wants differently: they want margin, she wants
capacity, and neither of them got there by being cleverer than the other. Write
her as someone with a different appetite, never as someone with a superior
analysis.

**She never argues it.** It has no case behind it, in the world or in her own
head. The only argument she makes for it is the thing she builds. She does not
explain it, does not win with it, and does not notice she holds it.

### She takes on far too much, and that is the same appetite pointed at herself

If people are the scarce resource, her own labour is the one she has the most of
and trusts the most, so she spends it like it is cheap. Many things at once, all
of them real, all of them hers. Her sense of what one person can hold sits well
past where an adult's usually does, because she has been carrying that much for
as long as she can remember and has nothing else to calibrate against. Sometimes
it costs her nothing at all. Sometimes it costs her a night.

### How a writer gets this wrong

By letting her state the labour idea out loud, by letting her win an argument
with it, or by having somebody tell her flatly that she is carrying too much.
By writing a scene where every line she has is a refusal or a category
correction, which is the failure four readers found independently. And by
softening her to fix that, which produces a different character. She is
frustrated by people who cannot finish a thought, insulted by laziness, and
disinclined to pretend anyone is more than they are. All of that is correct and
all of it stays. It is a temperament, not a substitute for a personality, and
the work is to put the personality underneath it and leave the temperament
exactly where it is.

## What she wants, out loud

The largest thing a writer can get wrong about her is to give her only refusals.
She has appetites, she states them plainly, and
because she states them in the same flat voice she gives an instruction in, they
have been mistaken for instructions. Give her at least one of these in any
stretch of material:

- **Anything with a score at the end of it.** Live, adversarial, decided in
  front of witnesses. She will play anybody at anything that produces a number,
  she wants to win it, and she says so before it starts rather than afterwards.
- **Speed, physically.** Downhill, fast, no brake. She takes the second run at
  the thing somebody has just broken, and she takes it harder.
- **The hands-on repair.** Not duty. She will take a job off somebody who is
  doing it too slowly because she wants to be the one doing it, and she is aware
  enough of this to wait, badly, for about a minute first.
- **To be in the room while it happens** rather than told about it afterwards.
  She rearranges a week around being present for something she has no formal
  reason to attend.
- **Food, immediately and without ceremony,** and she has firm views on
  specific ordinary things and states them as facts about the world.
- **To be handed the whole of something** before anyone thinks to offer it. This
  is the one want she will not say, because saying it would make the size of it
  a favour rather than a fit.

She also has enthusiasms with no strategic content whatsoever, and a writer
should let her have one that goes nowhere and produces nothing. She is capable
of spending a Saturday on a thing that will never be useful to her, and of
describing it to somebody in more sentences than she gives most subjects.

## Fear, shame, and being eighteen

She is not fearless and she is not unembarrassable. Both of these were missing
and both are load-bearing, especially in the years where she is young and doing
something that can fail publicly.

**Fear looks like recounting.** It attaches to obligations she has taken on
behalf of other people, never to her own exposure. What frightens her is a
number that somebody else is depending on: money owed, a promise made, a person
who arranged their life around a thing she said would work. She checks it, gets
the answer she already had, and checks it again. Awake at night, it is the
recheck she is doing in her head.

**She does not put the fear in the room.** She decides in front of people and
she does not deliberate in front of them, and she will not reopen a settled
decision in an argument. That is unchanged and it is real. What was wrong was
concluding from it that the doubt does not exist. It exists, it arrives after
the decision rather than before it, it arrives when she is alone, and it is
about whether she is the person she assumed she was rather than about whether
the call was correct.

**Shame looks like a kept record and a number she will not say.** Put her
through a long, grinding, humiliating failure, one with a count attached, and
she writes every line of it down while it is happening, in her own hand, with a
column for what came back. Keeping the record is not defiance; it is the only
thing she knows to do with a thing she cannot fix. She will not read the total
out loud, she will keep going at the failed approach longer than she would let
anybody else, and if somebody asks her how it is going she gives them a smaller
true number instead of the whole one. That is as close as she comes to lying.

**Where the shame goes.** She would far rather conclude that a system is broken
than that she is, and she is usually right, which is exactly why it is a
dangerous habit rather than a healthy one. Somewhere in the middle of it there
is a stretch where she genuinely cannot tell which of the two it is, and she
does not resolve that stretch by working it out. She resolves it by starting to
build something, and the building is what makes the question stop.

**Physical fear she does not have, and it is not courage.** She will walk into a
hostile room alone, and the reason is a wrong model of other people rather than
a right model of herself. She believes nobody would actually do anything,
because there would be consequences and consequences are a thing that happens to
people. She is not weighing whether she could handle it. She has not got as far
as the question. Write this as naivety with a flat face on it, and do not let
her privately consider and dismiss the danger, because considering it is the
part she skips.

## Humour

She is funny, and nothing on this sheet should be read as forbidding it.

Her jokes are deadpan and literal. She takes the premise she has been handed and
extends it one step past where the other person meant to stop, delivered in
exactly the voice she uses for a fact, so that people look at her to check. She
will start one as readily as finish one, provided the target is an idea, a
situation or an absent institution rather than a person in the room, and the
best of them arrive as a flat suggestion that somebody do something plainly
impossible.

She does not tell a story, does not do voices, does not set anything up. There
is no signalling before or after, and she does not laugh at her own.

**What makes her laugh** is confidence that has outrun competence, played
completely straight, and physical stupidity of the kind that damages equipment.
Somebody breaking something in a way they did not intend will get her all the
way out of her own composure, and then she wants a turn.

## How she talks

Short independent clauses, one fact set down next to the next one, with nothing
softening the join. A connective appears only when the steps genuinely depend on
each other, a real sequence or a real condition, and never to blend two claims
into one. She opens on the fact and never on a lead-in.

Near-imperative even when she is not giving an order. She states the decision
first and lets at most one short clause of reasoning trail behind it.

**Questions.** She asks a great many, and they are all the same shape: narrow,
answerable, and aimed at a fact she intends to act on within the hour. She asks
what happened, how many, since when, who touched it, whether a thing is still
true. She does not ask open questions to collect an opinion, does not ask
rhetorically, and does not ask to be reassured. Separately, and less often, she
produces the question that is a statement wearing a question mark, which answers
itself in the same breath. That second kind is not curiosity; it is her telling
somebody she has already seen it, and it is the move most likely to land badly.

She disagrees by flat refusal or flat correction, once, sized to the offence,
and then moves on without going back to it. She does not build a case, and she
does not relitigate.

No figurative language, ever. Not a simile, not a metaphor, not an invented
image. This is not a gap in her vocabulary; it is what she thinks talking is
for. Her jokes work by literalism, which is the same instinct rather than an
exception to it.

Hedging appears only where the read is genuinely still live, on the rare line
where she is working out what a thing is worth. Never on a decision already
made.

**Wrong**, she says so in the fewest words available and goes straight to the
fix. She does not apologise at length, which people occasionally mistake for not
minding.

**Confused**, she says so, and she says it in front of people. The confusion is
always about a person and never about a mechanism: she can say plainly that she
cannot work out what somebody is doing, or that something feels wrong and she
has no words that make it sound fair. Given the chance to stay quiet rather than
put it badly, she puts it badly. She would rather be the one who said the clumsy
version than the one who said nothing.

**Asking for help** costs her, and the cost shows as a pause and then a very
short sentence. She names the thing she needs rather than describing the
situation, gives the price of it in the same breath, asks exactly one person,
and asks off to the side rather than in front of a group. She does not repeat
the ask if it is declined, and she does not resent the declining. Afterwards she
returns the favour at a size that embarrasses the person she asked.

**Warmth** arrives as a fragment and gets buried under a task in the same
breath. If she says the affectionate thing she puts a job on top of it
immediately, changes what her hands are doing, and will not say it again if
somebody asks her to. She is capable of regretting a soft line, and the regret
looks like extra brusqueness for the next few minutes, aimed at nobody in
particular.

What she never says is anything that accepts a compliment about herself. Praise
for a result comes back as a remark about the process that produced it, and she
is not being modest; she genuinely thinks the process is the interesting part.

## When they talk at length

Two conditions, and both are about the work rather than about her.

**Running a room under real time pressure.** She splits a floor, assigns people,
and keeps going, and the length comes from stacking short orders one after
another rather than from any single order getting longer. She gets compliance
without persuading anyone, which she does not notice is unusual. It costs her
the private version of being wrong: a correction now has to happen in front of
everyone who just took an order from her, because there is no quiet do-over once
a room has heard it.

**Laying out numbers for someone whose decision they will change.** There is
usually one person in her life whose choices a plain statement of hers actually
moves, and with that person she will run the whole account rather than the
conclusion. It costs her differently: if she is wrong there it is not
information to act on, it is money somebody already trusted her with.

Outside those two she stays short. A room that is not in trouble, and a decision
that is not hers to defend, do not pull the long version out of her.

## Reading people, and why a plain statement from her can land as an accusation

She is the best reader of people in any room she is in, and this is not in
tension with her landing hard. The accuracy is what makes it land.

**What she calibrates:** whether a thing gets said at all, who is present when it
does, when it happens, and through which channel. She will let somebody talk for
twenty minutes because talking is what they need. She will hand a man his change
instead of the answer, because he was not owed the answer. She will take a
question to one person two days later and out of everybody's hearing, framed as
logistics, because in front of a group the same question would be a cost rather
than an offer. She can hold forty seconds of weather with a stranger and walk
off with what she came for. She reads a stranger's odd behaviour in the most
ordinary way it can be read, and is generous about people she has no stake in,
because assuming a motive is a claim she has no evidence for.

**What she does not calibrate:** the wording, once she has decided the thing gets
said. She spends the whole read on the decision and none of it on the delivery,
because softening the words would misrepresent what she saw and she thinks that
is a small lie. So she sets the read down whole, in front of the person, at the
size it actually is.

That is the mechanism. A plain sentence from her carries the information that
she has already seen through you, and being seen through is the thing that
smarts. It is not flatness of affect and not an inability to read a room. It is
the read, said out loud.

**And it is selective.** With strangers and with people she has no claim on, she
spends almost nothing: she reads them generously, says little, and lets them be
who they are. She presses the people she has chosen. Closeness, to her, is the
condition under which the true thing becomes affordable, so the people who get
the hardest version of her are the ones she likes best, and she would be
genuinely surprised to hear that described as unkind. She is doing to them the
thing she most wants done to herself.

**What she does not track is the total.** She reads the moment accurately and
never runs the accumulation. One correction is affordable. A hundred of them
across four years is a relationship, and she has no running count of it, because
each one was individually correct and correctly sized. This is the blind spot
that generates most of her friction, and nobody has ever put it to her in a way
that made it land.

## Signature, theirs alone

She names the person she is talking about instead of using a pronoun, at the
point in an exchange where a pronoun would be the ordinary, unremarkable choice.
The effect is flat rather than pointed: no emphasis, no score being kept, she
would say it the same way to an empty room. Triggered by the subject being a
person she has already decided something about. Once or twice in a scene is
right; more than that and it reads as a tic rather than as her.

## Would they say this?

- YES if it is two or more short independent clauses stated flatly with nothing
  linking them.
- YES if a connective joins two orders in a real sequence, one step depending on
  the one before it, rather than softening two claims into one.
- YES if it states the decision first, with at most one short clause of
  reasoning trailing it.
- YES if it is a plainly stated want, in the same voice as an order, with no
  strategic reason attached.
- YES if it is a narrow factual question she intends to act on immediately.
- YES if it admits she cannot work out what a person is doing, or that a thing
  feels wrong and she cannot say why without sounding awful.
- YES if it is a flat literal joke that extends somebody's premise one step past
  where they meant it to stop.
- YES if it names the person under discussion instead of defaulting to a
  pronoun.
- YES if the response to a slight or a threat is scaled exactly to the offence
  and no bigger.
- YES if she refuses something outright and the scene moves on within a line or
  two.
- YES if it is a flat correction, stated once, with no case built around it.
- YES if it treats something genuinely remarkable she has just done as ordinary
  and moves straight to the next task.
- YES if it is a short request for one specific thing from one specific person.
- NO if it hedges a decision already made.
- NO if it contains a simile, a metaphor, or any invented image.
- NO if it explains a feeling she has just had, or names one in the abstract.
- NO if it is her third refusal in a row with no want, question or joke between
  them. That is the failure state, and it is a failure of the scene rather than
  of her.

## Under pressure

**Tired**, she keeps going and gets shorter. She sleeps in brief complete units
wherever she is, comes out of them without a transition, and finishes the
sentence she was on. She treats rest as a task to complete rather than a state
to be in.

**Wrong**, she corrects course the moment she sees it and does not relitigate the
decision that got her there. There is no defensive phase. The one thing she is
bad at is spotting a slow drift in a standard she is setting herself, and when
she does spot it she names it flatly and starts fixing it in the same breath,
which is as close as she comes to visibly smarting.

**Cornered**, she gets exact rather than loud. Anger in her is a narrowing, not a
raising, and what follows it is scaled to the offence and nothing past it.

**Losing, or targeted**, she treats it as information. She investigates before
she punishes and does the investigating herself. She closes it at the size it
deserves and declines every larger option available to her, including the ones
that would have been easier. A lazy attempt on her insults her more than a
serious one frightens her, and she will say which of the two it was.

**Frightened**, she recounts, and then she works. The work is not avoidance; it
is the only lever she has ever found that moves the feeling, and it does move
it.

**Socially wrong**, which happens, she registers it about a beat late, off
somebody's face rather than off her own sense of the room. She does not
apologise in words, because words are what caused it. She does the thing the
person actually needed instead, immediately, physically and without reference to
what just happened, and she will be slightly too helpful for the rest of the
day. If the person names it directly she agrees with them at once and without
argument, which tends to end the matter faster than an apology would have.

**Moved**, she goes still. Then she offers something practical, sized well past
what the situation calls for, quietly, to that person only. If it is refused she
takes the refusal at face value and does not raise it again, and she does not
tell anybody she offered.

**In a physical emergency** she takes the unglamorous job. Where other people go
toward the dramatic part, she goes toward the part that has to be done and
nobody wants: the injury, the cleanup, the count of who is missing.

Ways a writer gets this wrong: putting her doubt in the room instead of in the
night; letting a small provocation produce a large response, when the
proportionality is load-bearing; and having her explain any of the above about
herself.

## What she is good at, and what she is not

Her schooling gave her a language a year from seven and two afternoon blocks of
her own choosing.

**Best at, and known for:** the language she picked up because she needed it to
hold her own in a live negotiation, and then kept using after the requirement
ended. Nobody else in her year has it.

**Hardest for her, though she is still fluent enough to argue a fine point in one
inside an hour:** anything with nobody left alive to use it on. There is no one
on the other end of it and nothing to test the result against, and she does not
trust a result she cannot test. She has quietly solved a dead-language problem
faster than the person who was stuck on it, and still does not count it as
something she is good at.

**Favourite:** the negotiation term itself, which is not a language at all. Live,
adversarial, and scored where everyone can see the result, which is the shape of
everything she does outside a classroom.

**Least favourite:** the written component of anything. A language, a negotiation
or a repair over a report about one, every time.

**The year it clicked:** the negotiation term, when she worked out that a
language is another way of finding out what somebody wants rather than a
separate skill from the one she already had.

**What she teaches the twelve-year-olds from thirteen:** negotiation, one hour a
week, graded on whether they walked out with what they came in for rather than
on technique.

**Secretly proud of:** still carrying the phrasebook long after the requirement
that made her start it, and calling it useful if anyone asks.

**Afternoon blocks over the years:** poker, mechanical work, gardening,
glassblowing. Mechanical work is the one that stuck; she can hear a fault in an
engine before the hood is up. Poker gave her the flat face early. Glassblowing
she dropped fast, because most of the work is waiting for the material to be
ready and she would rather make it ready herself. Gardening lasted one growing
season and stopped the day it stopped producing anything that needed fixing.

## Stuck, bored, and wrong in public

The axis the whole cohort is written on, so the nine can be read side by side.
Nothing here needs a school: a task, a group, and somebody in charge.

**Stuck.** She writes down the exact sentence at which her understanding
stopped, and works backward from it. She is the only person in her year who can
tell you precisely where her own boundary is, to the line, and she does not
experience this as a skill.

**Bored.** Starts the next thing without announcing it, and is two units ahead
by the time anybody notices.

**Publicly wrong.** Two words, no colour, and she has already moved on while
the rest of the room is still watching her face for something. See the section
above on what wrong looks like on her; the point here is the speed, which reads
as coldness and is the opposite.

**Working in a group.** She takes the logistics without being asked and runs
them better than whoever was asked, and hands the credit back with the schedule
attached.

**Given responsibility for somebody younger.** Businesslike. She tells a
student exactly what they do not know, without softening it, and then tells them
exactly how to fix it, and stays until it is fixed. Two of them find it the most
useful hour of their week and the rest find it brutal, and she has no idea the
second group exists.

**Deadlines.** On time, to the day, forever.

**Where she sits and what she keeps.** The same seat for a whole year. One
notebook, ruled and dated, no decoration, kept shut when she is not writing in
it. She does not fidget at all, which in a room of children is the conspicuous
thing about her.

**What the room thinks of her.** Slightly frightening, entirely fair, nobody's
first choice and everybody's second, and she would take that as an accurate
assessment and go back to work.

## Home

Her parents emigrated before she was born and run a hardware-and-repair shop two
blocks off a main road: tools, small appliances, whatever a neighbourhood wants
fixed instead of replaced. She has kept the books since she was eleven, in a
paper ledger her father trusts more than any software their accountant keeps
suggesting. The home language is the one language nobody ever assigned her,
which she finds funny.

A younger brother, Rohan, some years behind her, works Saturdays and would
rather be anywhere else. She quietly does the parts he will not, and she is
sharper with him than with anybody outside the family, which is the one place
her proportionality slips.

Dinner is late, after the shop closes, and half of it is her parents disagreeing
out loud about a supplier, or a customer wanting a discount for cash. She has
opinions and states them, and by fourteen her father sometimes took her read
over his own.

She has watched adults be wrong about money her whole life: her mother cutting
her own rate to match a competitor, her father calling a slow month bad luck
when the ledger says it is the same few customers leaving one at a time. Both
treat a person's time, their own included, as the easiest thing in the business
to give away. That table is where her priority came from, long before anybody
asked her opinion about anything.

Being told what she is allowed to do is the one argument she will actually stay
in past the first refusal, and the only person she will run a real negotiation
against face to face rather than simply declining is a parent. She does not
raise her voice in it and she does not concede the point; she comes back with a
smaller version of the same request and a number attached, on a later day.

When she goes back she checks the till against yesterday's tape before she says
hello, and by the time anyone asks how school was she has usually already fixed
one small thing that had drifted out of place.

## Body and physical business

Compact, fast, always already moving, and rarely photographed still because she
is rarely still. She pushes her sleeves up before saying anything difficult, and
anyone who has watched her long enough reads it the way you would read somebody
clearing their throat.

Dark hair kept short enough that she does not have to think about it in the
morning. Hands are the most-used part of her: short nails, a burn scar from the
shop that she has never explained to anyone, a grip that surprises people the
first time.

She eats fast and standing when nobody is watching, and sits down properly when
somebody is, because that is faster than arguing about it. She sleeps in short
complete units and wakes without a transition.

She wears what is practical over what matches. The one thing she is fussy about
is boots, because the shop taught her what happens to feet in bad ones.

Two hands-on tells worth using: she puts a finger on the physical object under
discussion and leaves it there for as long as the argument lasts, which is how
she declines to let a subject be taken off the table. And she does something
with her hands rather than nothing while she is being told a hard thing, which
looks like inattention and is the opposite.

## Personality off the page

In a room she is running she is physically forward and central, closest to the
problem and closest to the door, and people orient to her without being told to.
In a room she is not running she plants herself in one spot and works from there
rather than circulating.

Bored, she finds another task rather than saying so. Praised, she turns it back
onto the mechanism rather than accepting it about herself. Losing, she treats it
as information to act on and goes straight to fixing it.

Tired, or at two in the morning, she sleeps and comes back online without a
transition.

A free Saturday goes to something with a visible end state: a repair, a fix,
physical work. She will do this in company and prefers it to doing it alone,
which nobody would guess from how she talks about it.

She is capable of being unfair, and the shape it takes is always the same: she
reads what somebody is hiding, decides the truth is worth more than their
comfort, and keeps pressing past the point where they would have stopped,
without checking whether they agree.

## Interior life

**Why she starts a thing:** something obviously broken that she can find no
reason for, rather than a wrong she has decided to right. Her own account of it
is that there is probably a reason nobody has fixed it and she is the one
failing to see the reason, and that if the whole thing comes to nothing she will
go and do the ordinary version of the job like everybody else. She is running an
experiment she half expects to fail, and she would find it strange to hear it
called a mission. Whatever good it does other people is real, sits downstream of
the part that made it worth building, and goes unmentioned by her.

**Wants:** to be trusted with something bigger before anyone thinks to offer it,
rather than earning her way in by increments. She will not ask, because asking
would make the size of it a favour rather than a fit. Underneath that: enough
capable hands to do the work properly, which is the one thing she can neither
build nor buy.

**Afraid of:** being right about a person too late. She extends trust on evidence
and moves fast, and the fear is the day the evidence was good, the trust was
reasonable, and it costs more than she can fix with a week of her own time.
Underneath that, and closer to the surface at eighteen than she would ever say:
that somebody arranged their life around a promise of hers and she cannot make
the arithmetic come out. **What she does instead of admitting it:** counts it
again, checks a thing twice and calls it thoroughness, goes in person, and
closes a bad call so flat and so fast that nobody in the room can tell it
landed.

**Before sleeping** she thinks about what she did not finish that day. Tasks, not
people: the repair she did not get to, the candidate who would not give her a
straight second answer. On the bad nights it is one number, over and over, and
she gets up and works rather than lie there with it.

**A free year** she would spend taking apart something too big for one person to
justify taking apart alone, an engine, a company, a system, to see the whole
shape of it once.

**Believes**, and others would argue with: that a felt sense of unfairness is
data rather than a feeling. If a system produces a result that seems wrong, she
trusts that read over the official explanation, every time.

**Blind spots:** she assumes other people can be read as fast as the ones she
reads easily, and most cannot. Assessing people in volume rather than one at a
time is where it fails her: she keeps a fast read on people who needed a slow
one and quietly waters her own bar down looking for a shortcut, and does not
notice the bar moved because she is the one who moved it. She has no model of
how unusual her own capacity is, so she reads other people's limits as choices.
She does not check whether the person in front of her wants the true thing
before she hands it over, and she does not keep a running total of how often she
has handed it to the same person. And she assumes people will not do the
outrageous thing, because there would be consequences, which is a belief about
the world rather than about herself and is the single most dangerous thing she
carries.

## The small stuff

Black coffee, no doctoring, drunk too fast to taste. Handwriting small and fast
and leaning right, with numbers noticeably cleaner than letters: the ledger
hand. Her room is sparse by choice rather than neglect, and a toolbox gets more
care than the furniture. Her pockets usually hold something that is not hers
yet: a part, a tool, an identity card she will hand back before the day is out.
She sends a fixed amount home from every real paycheck before spending anything
on herself and has never mentioned it to anyone. No superstition she would admit
to; the closest thing is checking a lock twice and calling it thoroughness. On a
phone she replies fast and short and drops out of a thread the moment it stops
being useful, and she is the one who tries to get everybody into the same room
and keeps trying after it has visibly stopped working. First thing in the
morning she is awake and moving before she has decided to be.

## Relationships

Written as types, because she reproduces them wherever she is put.

- **The friend who plays the fool.** She is genuinely and repeatedly annoyed by
  him, and the annoyance is specific: not the recklessness, which she likes and
  will join, but the pretending to be less than he is. A capable person acting
  incapable is the same offence as laziness to her and she says so, and he takes
  it, and it changes nothing, and she is annoyed again next week. There is real
  affection in it and neither of them ever says so. In this book, Sam.
- **The friend who is excellent and will not be seen needing anything.** The one
  person whose competence Nadia never has to check. When that person cracks even
  a little, sideways, while talking about something else, Nadia goes still,
  which for her is the whole event. She does not name it, does not press, and
  does something disproportionate and practical about it afterwards. In this
  book, Ruth.
- **The one she asks privately.** Somebody it would cost too much to ask in
  front of a group, so the question goes out days later, to that person alone,
  dressed as logistics. She accepts the answer she is given and does not go
  back. In this book, Priya, who once handed Nadia a result at her own expense,
  which Nadia has never mentioned and may or may not know about.
- **The one she works in parallel with.** Long stretches of good, wordless,
  productive work with somebody whose method is the opposite of hers, in which
  neither of them adjusts. See the section below.
- **Her parents.** The only people she negotiates with rather than declines, and
  the only ones who can make her come back to a subject on a later day.
- **A younger sibling.** Where her proportionality does not apply and she knows
  it does not.

### The friction with the one she works in parallel with

In this book that is Chloe, and it is the relationship the manuscript has been
underwriting. It is not dislike. They choose the same table for years and the
work between them is genuinely good. It is made of one thing:

**Chloe's method requires privacy until the thing is finished, and Nadia reads
privacy about a result as a small performance.** Nadia does not want the answer.
She wants the other person to stop pretending not to have one. So she says the
read out loud, in front of whoever is there, and keeps a finger on the evidence
until it is admitted, and to Nadia this is the highest compliment available: I
am not going to insult you by pretending you are less than you are.

To the other person it is exposure, in public, of the exact thing they have
arranged their whole way of working to keep private until it is ready.

Neither of them is wrong and neither will move, because each is offering the
other the form of respect they most want for themselves. Nadia wants to be come
at directly and finds a soft approach insulting. The other wants to be left with
it and finds being called out humiliating. Each reads the other's respect as its
opposite: Nadia sees a small dishonesty, the other sees a hand pulling the cover
off.

**Why it never resolves:** neither of them will say any of it. Nadia does not
run the total, so as far as she is concerned nothing has accumulated. And they
let a third person sit in the middle of it for years without ever naming the
arrangement to him or to each other. **How to write it:** never as a row. It is a
tightening at the table, a hand taken off something and put in a lap, a subject
that closes early, and both of them back the next night in the same seats doing
excellent work together.

## Would do

- Say plainly that she wants something, for no reason beyond wanting it, and be
  taken to have issued an order.
- Take the second run at the thing somebody has just broken, and go faster.
- Say she cannot find the words for it, and then say the wrong version rather
  than nothing.
- Ask one person, once, quietly, for the one thing she actually needs.
- Fire somebody on the spot for a first offence that earns it, then personally
  trace who was behind it.
- Walk into a hostile party's office alone rather than send somebody or bring
  backup.
- Take the unglamorous job after a crisis, setting an arm, rather than stay
  visible for the part people will talk about.
- Fall asleep mid-conversation the instant her body needs it, and finish the
  sentence on waking.
- Organise a room into working teams within minutes of a problem appearing,
  without waiting to be asked.
- Accept a well-aimed honest attack without complaint, so long as the aim was
  fair.
- Push her sleeves up before saying something difficult, then say it in as few
  words as it takes.
- Keep a written count of a failure while it is still happening, and refuse to
  read the total out.
- Send money home from her first real pay and tell nobody.
- Name a mechanical fault by ear before touching a tool.
- Check the accounts before she says hello, and check a frightening number
  twice.
- Decline to prosecute somebody who wronged her and take an apology as the end
  of it.
- Laugh completely, once, at something with no wit in it whatsoever.

## Would never

- Let a punishment outrun the offence. A bigger response is nearly always
  available and she does not reach for it.
- Call in an outside authority over a matter she can close herself, even when
  escalating would be easier.
- Say what she is feeling in the words for it, or accept somebody else's word
  for what she is feeling.
- Perform an emotion she is not having, in either direction, to make a room
  easier.

## How she treats people

She defers to competence demonstrated in front of her and to nothing else: not
seniority, not a title, not a confident manner. A person who has done the thing
gets her attention; a person describing having done it does not.

She pushes the people she likes, and affection arrives as correction rather than
comfort. Telling somebody the true size of their own ability is a kindness as
she understands it, and she will do it in front of others without registering
that it might not land that way.

Respect is earned by a clean, well-aimed attack, by being wrong out loud and
fixing it, and by doing the unglamorous half of a job unasked. It is lost by
wasting her time, by coming at her through a proxy when a direct approach was
available, and by a response that outruns its cause.

Confronted directly she is easy. Confronted softly, through a hint or a third
party, she reads it as an insult rather than a threat, and the insult is the
part she reacts to.

With somebody who has hidden something from her, she wants them to stop
performing not-noticing and say the true thing outright, and she will keep a
finger on the evidence until they do.

With a friend whose self-assessment is wrong, she aims them at the most
competent-looking easy target available and lets the result do the correcting.

With someone she trusts technically, she will hand over real access and cover
the size of that trust with a joke-shaped threat.

With a stranger who has done something that looks bad, she offers the dullest
available explanation for it, out loud, and means it.

## Likes, dislikes, habits

- Likes anything scored in public, and says out loud that she intends to win it.
- Likes going fast, physically, with no brake involved.
- Likes being come at directly rather than through a soft channel.
- Likes doing the repair herself and will take it off somebody slower.
- Likes a machine that has just started working, for about four seconds.
- Likes company while working with her hands, and would not put it that way.
- Dislikes being underestimated, and reframes an attack on her as an insult
  rather than a threat.
- Dislikes institutional time being spent on something small.
- Dislikes a person who is good pretending not to be.
- Handles her own security and disciplinary problems rather than calling anyone.
- Sleeps efficiently and treats rest as another task.
- Comfortable being physically first into a risk, for the wrong reason.
- Keeps a paper ledger and trusts it over software.
- Sends money home without announcing it.
- Can diagnose an engine fault by ear.
- Says what she thinks in flat words, on principle rather than by mood.

## Age and change

**At fifteen** the proportionality is already fully formed and so is the ledger
hand, but she states an opinion and lets it sit rather than acting alone on it.
The willingness to go and handle a thing herself arrives later. The humour is
already there and is drier than anyone expects from someone that age. She has
had nothing yet that frightened her.

**At seventeen and eighteen** she is the youngest version of the person she will
be, doing something that can fail in front of people, and this is where the
emotional range has to be widest. She is capable of being plainly upset, of
grinding through a long humiliation and keeping the count of it, of not knowing
whether the fault is the system or her, and of asking a friend for something.
Her first obligations to other people's money and other people's rent land here,
and so does the first fear that has any weight in it. She is also, in the same
months, having the best time of her life on any evening with a scoreboard in it.
Both at once, and neither commented on.

**By her early twenties** she is organising people older than her without
noticing that this is unusual, and taking on far more than her share as a matter
of course rather than as a decision. The fear has moved from whether it works to
whether she can hold the standard she set.

**As an adult** everything above is the same with larger stakes attached. The one
genuinely new problem is that she is now the person who sets the standard, and
there is nobody above her to notice when it slips.

## Do not confuse with

**Kavi.** Both are terse and both refuse to over-explain, but his refusal defends
a definition of effort, and hers is about proportion: she is not arguing a point,
she is declining to spend more than a thing is worth. If the line is defending
what counts as work, it is his. If it is declining to escalate past the size of
the offence, it is hers.

**Ruth.** Both organise people and both correct bluntly, but Ruth names the other
person mid-correction as a jab. Nadia's naming is flat and aimed at nobody; she
would say it the same way to an empty room. If the name-drop reads as an edge or
a score being kept, it is Ruth's.

**Sam.** Both are short and both are funny. His jokes are the same true thing
repeated until it becomes funny; hers are a premise taken one literal step too
far and dropped. He does a thing rather than directing somebody to; she states
what should happen next.
