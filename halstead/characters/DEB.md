## Subjects and schedule

Not a teacher or clinician, so this is adapted to her actual job. Role: project coordinator at the translation company — the person who intakes client requests, sets deadlines, and routes documents to translators, rather than a translator herself. Ten years at the company by the time Chloe starts. Best at: holding a colleague's attention with an ongoing, detailed, personal account over months. Worst at: taking in a plain, first-pass explanation of something at work without a concrete reframe. What she's known for around the office: organizing the small stuff nobody else wants to — the office birthday calendar, the shared coffee fund. Secretly proud of: that in ten years she's never missed a deadline she personally owned, even during the two years she was raising Tyler essentially alone.

## Home

Divorced eight years before Chloe joins the company; her ex-husband lives out of state and sees their son twice a year. Raising her son, Tyler, now eleven, largely on her own, which is most of why he comes up constantly — he is, practically speaking, most of her life outside the office. Lives in a two-bedroom apartment fifteen minutes from work, close enough to make Tyler's Little League games on weeknights. Her own parents live nearby and help with childcare on the days her schedule runs long. Dinner is usually something built around Tyler's schedule — quick, kid-approved, eaten early.

## Body

Mid-forties, average build, dresses comfortably and a little brightly — she likes color in an office that runs mostly gray and navy. Talks with her hands, especially about Tyler. Keeps a framed photo of him at every age on her desk, updated every school year. Moves through the office at an easy, unhurried pace compared to some of her younger colleagues. Sleeps reasonably, though Tyler's sports schedule cuts into it some seasons.

## Personality off the page

Genuinely likes people and assumes, correctly most of the time, that they'll like her back. Bored: fills any lull with a story, usually about Tyler. Wrong: takes correction easily, doesn't get defensive. Praised: enjoys it openly, doesn't deflect the way some of her colleagues do. Losing: rare in an office job with little real conflict; the closest is a missed deadline, which she takes personally and fixes fast. Tired: talks a little less, still asks about your day. A free Saturday: a Little League game, then errands, then something easy on the couch. Finds funny in others: earnestness about something trivial — she finds it endearing.

## Interior life

Wants, unsaid: for Tyler's father to be more present than twice a year, though she's stopped pushing for it and built a full life without waiting on it. Afraid of: that she's the only adult really paying attention to Tyler's actual day-to-day, and that something will slip through because of it. Thinks about before sleep: whether she remembered everything on Tyler's schedule for the week. A free year: she'd spend most of it with Tyler before he's a teenager and doesn't want to anymore. Believes something colleagues would argue with: that the office overcomplicates simple explanations on purpose sometimes, to sound more essential than the task requires. Wrong about: she assumes Chloe's patient, repeated reframing of things is just Chloe being naturally good with people — she has no idea it's a learned habit, and Chloe has no reason to tell her.

## The small stuff

Coffee, milky, one sugar, refilled twice a morning. Handwriting: loopy, fast, covers a sticky note with more words than it should hold. Her desk: photos of Tyler, a mug he made her in art class, a candy dish she restocks every Monday. Keeps her phone face-up on the desk, checks it at every lull for a text from Tyler's school. Money: careful but not anxious about it — a single income covering two people teaches you to plan without it ruling you. Superstition: none, though she always says "knock on wood" and actually knocks. First thing every morning: texts Tyler to make sure he's up before she's even out of bed herself.
