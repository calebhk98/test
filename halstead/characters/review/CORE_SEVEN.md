# Core Seven — capacity-for-complexity review

Read against `_CALIBRATION.md`, `_DIFFERENTIATION.md`, `_TEMPLATE.md`, all seven sheets, and
chapters 15, 17, 24 and 34. This is not a voice-consistency audit — the sheets already run their
own known-problems sections for that. This is asking one question: if the next pass lengthens
sentences, deepens subordination, and lets scenes and dialogue run longer, which of the rules
already written into these seven sheets will actively fight that pass, and which characters have
sheet-given room to absorb it without going out of character.

---

## 1. Verdict

This cast can carry a more complex book, but not evenly, and not without the sheets themselves
being edited first — several of the current rules are written as absolutes that will read as a
character violation the moment a sentence gets longer. The raw material for complexity is already
there: every one of the seven has a real interior life with wants, fears and a specific
self-deception (Ruth's ten months building an alternate theory rather than accept she'd been
outranked; Sam's blind faith that an honest answer is always safe; Eli's fear of being the only
person who can grade his own work), and at least three characters — Kavi in his unbroken technical
runs, Theo throughout, and Sam in the one tactical monologue chapter 15 already gives him — prove
on the page that longer, multi-clause, reasoned speech is compatible with this cast's flat,
unhedged, non-showy institutional register. The problem is not that these people are incapable of
a longer sentence. The problem is that five of the seven sheets contain an explicit rule banning
the specific register a maturity pass would reach for first — medium-length lines, connective
words, hedges, figurative language — and the two characters best built to carry that register,
Theo and, in person, Ruth, are either almost absent from the book (Theo has 26 lines total and
none before adulthood) or almost never shown using the capacity the sheet itself says they have
(Ruth "otherwise willing to talk at length in person," RUTH.md:125, appears in none of the four
sampled chapters as sustained argument — she is mostly one-line corrections). The interior lives
that would give a longer scene something to be about are, by the sheets' own description, hidden
from every other character on the page (Kavi's list "he doesn't show... to anyone," KAVI.md:184;
Ruth's improved-and-refiled work that "nobody has ever seen," RUTH.md:107). A pass that lengthens
dialogue without first loosening the rules below, or without finding a way to let that interiority
surface, will be fighting the sheets on every page.

`_DIFFERENTIATION.md` is right that a shared register is evidence the institution is real — "These
nine were selected by one person, taught by the same adults from seven... A shared register is
evidence the institution exists" (`_DIFFERENTIATION.md:24-26`). That argument holds for the
baseline: no praise-language, no hedging, no small talk, reasoning before conclusion. It stops
holding where two sheets assign the *same specific move* rather than the same baseline — Kavi and
Eli both getting "names the real cost instead of accepting the compliment" is a duplicated
signature, not a shared culture, and both sheets already say so.

## 3. Per character

### Chloe
**Can already carry:** sustained, unhedged, multi-beat argument-building — already licensed at
length with her father and Ruth (the fractions retelling, the Thanksgiving tariffs argument run
across a whole table, CHLOE.md:141). Her "If you..." parallel-scenario device is itself a
subordination engine: it exists specifically to let her build a conditional, multi-clause argument
without ever hedging.
**Sheet forbids:** "NO if it hedges: 'maybe,' 'I think,' 'kind of,' 'sort of,' before a claim."
(CHLOE.md:45) — closes off the single most natural way prose usually signals nuance, though her
parallel-scenario device is a workable substitute the sheet already supplies.
**Single highest-leverage change:** stop confining the full unhedged version to family only. The
sheet already licenses it ("gives the people she loves the long version, unprompted," CHLOE.md:50)
— extending that license to peers under real stakes, not just Dad and Ruth, adds capacity without
adding a trait that isn't already hers.

### Ruth
**Can already carry:** the sheet states outright that she "otherwise" talks "at length in person"
and reserves only chat for economy (RUTH.md:125). That's a built-in permission slip for exactly the
kind of longer, argued dialogue a maturity pass needs, and it is presently almost unused on the
page — none of the four sampled chapters show her sustaining an argument past two or three lines.
**Sheet forbids:** hedging target is being driven to 0% (`_DIFFERENTIATION.md`, RUTH.md:23,
"hedging is Theo's exclusive trait; strip it from her"), and questions to 0% — removing two of the
most ordinary tools a longer, more exploratory line would reach for.
**Single highest-leverage change:** write the "room gets the argument" version of her that the
sheet already promises but the sampled chapters never show — let her actually run the case out loud
in person, not only report the conclusion.

### Sam
**Can already carry:** more than his own dials admit. The chapter 15 tactical plan is already a
compound, conditional sentence in his mouth: "They're in the loading corridor, so they've got the
stairs or they've got the long way round past the kitchens, and if we push from the north they take
the stairs, so —" (chapters/15_twelve.md:183). That is a subordinate clause, on the page, under his
name, in a scene the sheet cites approvingly elsewhere.
**Sheet forbids:** "NO if it contains a subordinate clause built to qualify or explain something.
That belongs to Theo." (SAM.md:43) — a rule the manuscript itself has already broken once, in a
scene his own sheet treats as core evidence of his character. Terseness is also being pushed
further up, to 40%+ (SAM.md:21), the wrong direction for a pass that needs more from him, not less.
**Single highest-leverage change:** name tactical/procedural planning explicitly as the register
where his subordinate clauses live — write the rule to match the evidence instead of overriding it.

### Kavi
**Can already carry:** his "one long unbroken technical run" mode (KAVI.md:26) is already
dense, subordinate-clause-rich prose — "That's a heat exchanger problem and I want to know which
part of it is the limit" (chapters/15_twelve.md:83) is not a simple sentence.
**Sheet forbids:** "NO if the line sits at ordinary conversational length, one or two medium
sentences of six to fourteen words... That length is the one range his voice may never occupy."
(KAVI.md:52) — this is the single most direct rule-conflict in the whole cast. A pass that raises
subordination will land constantly in the six-to-fourteen-word range, which is explicitly
off-limits for him.
**Single highest-leverage change:** delete the medium-length ban and let his existing bimodal
range (three words / unbroken run) widen at the edges into that middle zone, rather than staying a
hard binary.

### Nadia
**Can already carry:** real command competence under pressure — she organizes a floor into working
teams in four minutes and gets compliance from eleven people (NADIA.md:47) — but that competence is
"written entirely in narration with zero dialogue in the scene," a problem the sheet already flags
against itself (NADIA.md:164).
**Sheet forbids:** "Short independent clauses with no connectives: she doesn't join ideas with
'and,' 'but,' or 'so'" (NADIA.md:11) — an explicit ban on the basic joining words that build
subordination and multi-step instruction, which is exactly the kind of line her own command scenes
need and don't yet have.
**Single highest-leverage change:** write the spoken command sequence the sheet's own Known
problems #1 calls for. A real multi-step instruction ("do X, then Y, unless Z") necessarily uses a
connective; loosening the no-connectives rule for exactly this register fixes the sheet's
self-identified gap and adds capacity in the same move.

### Eli
**Can already carry:** the highest hedge tolerance of any non-Theo character (7%, ELI.md:24) and
the only "playful" register in the cast (jokiness 7/10, ELI.md:23) — he already has more room than
Ruth, Sam, Kavi or Nadia.
**Sheet forbids:** "NO if it leans on institutional vocabulary... that register belongs to someone
else" and a hard word-count ceiling — "his average sits under nine words" (ELI.md:180) — that caps
how far his existing reframe-then-reassert move can extend before it reads as off-voice.
**Single highest-leverage change:** let the patient-explainer mode already established in his
relationships ("he does it without hurrying," ELI.md:63, twenty minutes on a solved cipher set)
appear on the page as extended explanation, not just be described as a fact about him.

### Theo
**Can already carry:** everything. He is the one character the sheets already built for a more
complex register — longest sentences in the cast (10.6 words/message, THEO.md:21), least terse
(12%, THEO.md:22), and the only one proposed for real hedging (target 15%+, THEO.md:24).
**Sheet forbids:** nothing on the complexity axis — the actual obstruction is that he barely
exists. "Zero dialogue anywhere in chapters 1–20... He then arrives fully formed as an adult" (his
own Known problems #2, THEO.md:178). The character licensed to carry more complexity has no
established voice to carry it from.
**Single highest-leverage change:** this is the one the report weights heaviest — seed his
childhood dialogue now, per his own sheet's proposal, so that when his register gets used more, it
reads as an established voice getting more page time rather than a new voice arriving late.

## 4. What will fight the next pass

Ranked most obstructive first.

1. **Kavi's explicit ban on medium-length lines.** "NO if the line sits at ordinary conversational
   length, one or two medium sentences of six to fourteen words, with nothing else marking it. That
   length is the one range his voice may never occupy." (KAVI.md:52) — this is the exact register a
   subordination-raising pass produces most often, for every character, banned outright for one of
   the cast's most-quoted voices. **Alternative:** keep the three-word clip and the unbroken
   technical run as his defaults, but let the boundary between them widen rather than forbidding the
   middle entirely — the ban should be on ordinary padding ("well, I guess," "kind of"), not on
   length itself.

2. **Nadia's "no connectives" rule.** "she doesn't join ideas with 'and,' 'but,' or 'so'"
   (NADIA.md:11) bans the plainest tools for building a longer sentence, for the character with the
   fewest lines in the book (41 total, NADIA.md:8) and the least established voice to begin with.
   **Alternative:** keep the near-imperative default, but allow connectives specifically in the
   command sequences her own sheet says are currently missing dialogue (NADIA.md:164).

3. **Universal 0% hedging targets.** Sam (SAM.md:23), Kavi (KAVI.md:31), Ruth (target, RUTH.md:23)
   and Nadia (target, `_DIFFERENTIATION.md`) are all being driven to zero hedging, on top of Theo
   being named the cast's only legitimate hedger. Hedging and qualification are among the most
   ordinary ways prose signals a more complex, less certain thought — removing them from five of
   seven voices removes a primary vehicle for exactly the nuance a maturity pass wants.
   **Alternative:** keep 0% as the floor for flat factual claims (their institutional register,
   correctly shared per `_DIFFERENTIATION.md:169-176`), but allow a qualifier where a character is
   reasoning toward a claim rather than stating a settled one — that's a different use of the same
   word and doesn't cost them the "assumes any question has a findable answer" baseline.

4. **Figurative language banned for five of seven.** "Ruth, Kavi, Nadia, Eli, Theo, Odile | none,
   ever. The refusal is characterising." (`_DIFFERENTIATION.md:158`) removes the single most common
   device a maturity pass reaches for — image, metaphor, comparison — from most of the cast, leaving
   it concentrated in one character (Chloe) plus one narrow channel (Sam's deflating literalism).
   **Alternative:** this one should probably stay. It is explicitly "characterising," not
   accidental, and `_CALIBRATION.md` warns against making anyone "showy" — the fix here isn't to add
   imagery to these five, it's to build their complexity through mechanism and consequence instead
   (which their sheets already do well; see §5).

5. **Sam's terseness target moving the wrong direction.** Currently 31%, target 40%+ (SAM.md:21),
   alongside "flat declarative, no subordinate clause, ever" (SAM.md:18) — a rule the manuscript
   itself already contradicts once (chapters/15_twelve.md:183, cited above). **Alternative:** cap
   the terseness target where it is rather than raising it, and carve out his tactical/procedural
   speech as the register where longer clauses are already textually attested.

6. **Interiority walled off from every other character on the page.** Kavi's list of unsolved
   questions "he doesn't show... to anyone" (KAVI.md:184); Ruth's habit of quietly improving old,
   already-graded work, of which "nobody has ever seen an improved version" (RUTH.md:107); Eli
   "never told anyone" about his private satisfaction in the no-libraries project (ELI.md:69). The
   richest, most complex material on every sheet is explicitly built to never surface in dialogue,
   which means a pass that wants longer, more interior-feeling scenes has to invent new material
   rather than draw out what these sheets already contain. **Alternative:** none of this needs to
   become confessional — that would break calibration — but one or two of these private habits could
   surface as *action* (Ruth is caught mid-revision; Kavi's notebook is seen, not read) without
   anyone narrating the feeling behind it.

7. **Axis 2's disagreement moves are all quick-resolution.** Correct-and-done (Ruth), restate-louder
   -or-act (Sam), mechanism-correct-and-stop (Kavi), flat-refusal-then-move-on (Nadia),
   reframe-once (Eli), object-then-comply (Theo) — every assigned disagreement move in
   `_DIFFERENTIATION.md`'s Axis 2 table (lines 83-95) is built to close a disagreement within a line
   or two, not sustain one. A pass that wants scenes to "breathe" and characters to "talk more" needs
   at least some of these seven able to stay inside a disagreement without resolving it immediately.
   **Alternative:** this doesn't require a new move per character — Theo already has one built for
   it ("objects procedurally, on record, then complies," which the character's own Interior life
   notes never actually changes an outcome, THEO.md:120) — that structure could extend to one or two
   more characters without inventing anything.

## 5. What is already working — protect this

- **The shared institutional baseline is correct and should not be diluted for "more complex."**
  Reasoning before conclusion, no praise-language for people, no small talk, comfortable silence
  (`_DIFFERENTIATION.md:169-176`) is what makes this read as one institution rather than seven
  random smart teenagers. A maturity pass should raise *register*, not add chattiness, hedging-as-
  nervousness, or self-awareness about being gifted — `_CALIBRATION.md` is explicit that none of
  them "know any of this" (`_CALIBRATION.md:21`) and that should hold exactly as written.

- **The interior-life sections are already genuinely novelistic.** Every one of the seven has a real
  want that contradicts a real fear, and a specific thing they're wrong about — Ruth's ten months
  spent building an alternate theory rather than accept a plainer, worse one about herself
  (RUTH.md:121); Sam's blind faith that honesty is always safely received (SAM.md:116); Eli's fear
  that being the only person qualified to grade his own work is "the frightening version of the
  thing he's proudest of" (ELI.md:111). This is exactly the material a maturity pass needs and it
  already exists — the work is finding ways to let it surface, not inventing it from nothing.

- **Existing, already-published proof that longer register survives in this voice.** Kavi's
  unbroken technical runs, Theo's whole voice, and Sam's one tactical monologue in chapter 15 are
  already on the page and already approved by their own sheets. These are load-bearing evidence
  that complexity and this cast's flatness are not actually in conflict — they're the templates to
  extend, not exceptions to explain away.

- **Chloe's parallel-scenario device.** "If you walked all the way around this table..."
  (chapters/05_behind.md:95) is already a sophisticated rhetorical structure — a hypothetical built,
  then argued on its own terms — and it's exclusively hers by the differentiation matrix. It should
  be extended, not replaced with something showier; it already does the work a subordinate-clause
  pass would want from her.

- **The sheets' own Known Problems sections have already found several of these gaps.** Theo's
  missing childhood, Nadia's defining trait living only in narration, Eli's missing years 14-17,
  Chloe's thinning signature — these aren't things this review discovered, they're already flagged
  by name on the sheets themselves. That is real scaffolding: the fixes this report proposes mostly
  extend fixes the sheet-writers had already started, rather than requiring new diagnosis.
