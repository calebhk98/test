# Staff review — nine people or one manner?

Scope: `BELL.md`, `HEARN.md`, `KOWALCZYK.md`, `AMBERG.md`, `BAPTISTE.md`, `DOYLE.md`,
`PRUITT.md`, `SINCLAIR.md`, `SANDOVAL.md`, checked against `_CALIBRATION.md`,
`CHARACTER_SHEETS.md`, and the manuscript in `chapters/`. All quotes below were verified
with `grep -n`, not `cut`.

## 1. Verdict

These are five people and one manner wearing four more coats. Bell, Kowalczyk, Amberg,
Baptiste, and Sandoval each have a real, load-bearing mechanism that is theirs alone —
holding the object, doing arithmetic with her hands busy, placing the document, throwing
the question back, naming her own authority out loud — and each of those mechanisms
already produces multi-clause, causal, sometimes long sentences on the page. But Hearn,
Doyle, Pruitt, and Sinclair are built almost entirely out of the shared institutional
test rather than out of a person: their "voice" sections describe what they withhold, not
what they say, and three of the nine sheets themselves flag each other as easily confused
(`BELL.md:111`, `PRUITT.md:105`, `AMBERG.md:108`, `HEARN.md:108`, `BAPTISTE.md:111`,
`DOYLE.md:107`, `SINCLAIR.md:108`). The tell that the test has hardened into a personality
is structural, not impressionistic: the paragraph defining "the Halstead-staff test" is
reproduced **verbatim, word for word, in all nine sheets** (`BELL.md:29`, `HEARN.md:29`,
`KOWALCZYK.md:29`, `AMBERG.md:29`, `BAPTISTE.md:31`, `DOYLE.md:29`, `PRUITT.md:29`,
`SINCLAIR.md:29`, `SANDOVAL.md:29`), and the measured dials confirm it: jokiness is 0/10
in **9 of 9** sheets, hedging is literally 0% in **8 of 9** (Sandoval's is "precision-hedging
only, not an emotional hedge"), and figurative language is "none found" in **8 of 9**
(only Kowalczyk has one permitted simile). A cast can share an institution's rule and still
sound like nine different mouths saying it. Four of these currently don't, because the
sheet never gives them a second thing to be besides the rule.

Read together, this is not nine independent overlaps — it's one axis (flat, unwarmed,
withholding) that every pairing collapses onto, plus five genuinely distinct physical
mechanisms layered on top that keep five of the nine legible anyway.

## 3. Per character

**Bell** — Can already carry 40+ word technical runs built around one held object
(`chapters/14_sixty_degrees.md:11-21`); "wordiness 7/10" is the highest of the near-silent
half of this cast. Forbids: praise of the student ever (`BELL.md:83`), and simile/metaphor
by rule — "NO if it uses a simile or invented metaphor, his comparisons are always
literal" (`BELL.md:41`). Single highest-value change: let his existing long causal
explanations chain further (wind → arc → hand position, one sentence, still purely
mechanical) rather than reaching for figurative language he's explicitly barred from.

**Hearn** — Can carry structured, factual, multi-sentence argument (`chapters/13_ten_pages.md:7`)
and already runs bimodal: long when teaching, terse when correcting. Forbids: "NO if it
explains why an assignment matters or offers encouragement about effort" (`HEARN.md:40`),
and his whole signature is a silence "doing more communicating than a sentence would"
(`HEARN.md:36`). Single change: grow the historical-fact sentences (more clauses of
number and consequence, Federalist-era rhetoric is his actual subject) rather than ever
filling in what a returned paper's silence is currently doing — the silence has to stay
a silence.

**Kowalczyk** — Already the least obstructive teacher in the cast: 13.2 words per line, the
longest speaker in the book (`CHARACTER_SHEETS.md:95`), sustains a live, causal, nested
argument under direct provocation while doing something else with her hands
(`chapters/18_fifteen.md:87-89`), and is the one character the style guide has already
licensed for a simile (`KOWALCZYK.md:23`). Forbids: comforting a student or praising
toughness as a personal quality (`KOWALCZYK.md:40-41`). Single change: almost none needed
— she is the safest place in the cast to add more "because... which means..." nesting,
since she already sustains exactly that shape.

**Amberg** — Can already carry enumerated, multi-clause structure across a scene and
across five years of story time (`chapters/16_thirteen.md`, `19_sixteen.md`,
`22_the_offer.md`). Forbids extending a scene into back-and-forth debate: "NO if it
argues to change a student's mind after they've stated a decision" (`AMBERG.md:40`);
"Would never: Argue with a student after they've stated a decision" (`AMBERG.md:83`).
Single change: deepen the clauses inside one enumerated statement rather than turning the
exchange into multiple turns — complexity through clause depth, not through more dialogue
back-and-forth, which the character's own rule forbids.

**Baptiste** — The lowest-volume teacher after Doyle and Pruitt: "wordiness 2/10, the
shortest average turn of any teacher on this list" (`BAPTISTE.md:20`), half his six lines
are three words or fewer. Forbids explanation directly: "NO if it explains why a wrong
guess was wrong instead of asking what the student thinks now" (`BAPTISTE.md:42`). Single
change: don't lengthen his turns — lengthen the narrated demonstration he repeats
(`chapters/05_behind.md:40`). The complexity budget for a Baptiste scene belongs to the
prose around him, not to his own lines.

**Doyle** — The most under-supported voice in the cast. Real quoted dialogue is two words,
"Mass. Thank you." (`chapters/04_pluto.md:114`); the sheet's own "Known problems" section
already admits "only one scene contains any of his actual speech... His voice under the
more common classroom situation is entirely unattested" (`DOYLE.md:103-104`). Forbids:
"Would never: Get defensive, annoyed, or performatively gracious about being corrected,
the narration explicitly rules out both extremes" (`DOYLE.md:82`). Single change: before
anyone tries to lengthen a Doyle sentence, give him a second scene drawn from material
the sheet already has and the manuscript doesn't quote (the bridge-test, the group-task
deferral) so a rewriter has a second real data point instead of extrapolating warmth from
nothing.

**Pruitt** — The most obstructive single sheet in the cast. Two lines in four years, nine
and eight words, both delivered while already leaving (`PRUITT.md:11,18-19,26`). Forbids
explanation outright: "NO if it explains what to do about the fault, he never does, in
either line" (`PRUITT.md:39`); "Would never: Comment on four years of unrewarded,
unrecorded work, his total spoken output across that span is two sentences, both purely
technical" (`PRUITT.md:83`). Single change: his own sheet already contains long,
subordinated, complex prose about him (Interior life, Home) — move that complexity into
narration around him (how Chloe reads the silence) rather than into his mouth. His two
lines should stay exactly as short as they are; the reading level rises in the sentences
that surround them.

**Sinclair** — The sharpest technical conflict in the whole cast: his defining sentence
shape is explicitly "short independent clauses stacked without connectives"
(`SINCLAIR.md:11,16`). Subordination is the single grammatical feature a reading-level
pass most wants to add, and it is the one thing this character's signature is built to
exclude. Forbids: "NO if it comments on danger, fear, or the children's welfare directly"
(`SINCLAIR.md:40`). Single change: grow him by clause *count*, not clause *fusion* — stack
five flat independent clauses instead of three, don't join them with "because" or
"although." Density through accumulation keeps the Hemingway register intact while still
raising what a sentence has to carry.

**Sandoval** — The model, not the problem. Her sentences already use real subordinate
structure inside the flat register: "I am not going to put a federal NDA in front of a
fifteen-year-old, **because** I do not think anybody should be asking you to sign one"
(`chapters/18_fifteen.md:139`) is a because-clause carrying real argumentative weight
without a single warm word in it. Forbids: minimizing risk to make a request easier to
accept (`SANDOVAL.md:41`). Single change: she needs volume, not complexity — she has one
scene in one chapter (`chapters/18_fifteen.md`). Give her a second appearance and the
"precise rather than warm, structurally complex" register she already owns becomes proof
the whole cast can do this.

## 4. What will fight the next pass — ranked

1. **Sinclair's ban on connective tissue.** "short independent clauses stacked without
   connectives" (`SINCLAIR.md:11`). This is a direct grammatical collision: the revision
   wants more subordination, and this character's signature is defined as the absence of
   subordinators. *Alternative:* add clauses by stacking, not by fusing — more short
   sentences in a row, never "because" or "which" joining them.

2. **Pruitt's zero-elaboration rule, applied to a cast member who already has almost no
   lines to work from.** "NO if it explains what to do about the fault, he never does, in
   either line" (`PRUITT.md:39`). There is no dialogue budget to extend — any added length
   has to go around him, in narration, not through him.

3. **Doyle's dialogue footprint doesn't actually exist to extend.** Verified quoted
   dialogue: two words (`chapters/04_pluto.md:114`). The sheet's second quoted line isn't
   in the manuscript. A rewriter reaching for "how Doyle would say a longer thing" has
   nothing real to extrapolate from and will default to warmth or explanation, both
   explicitly banned (`DOYLE.md:40,82`). *Alternative:* write a second real Doyle scene
   from unquoted sheet material first; extend from that, not from the current one line.

4. **Baptiste's mechanism bans the exact move a "more developed argument" pass reaches
   for.** "NO if it explains why a wrong guess was wrong instead of asking what the
   student thinks now" (`BAPTISTE.md:42`). *Alternative:* lengthen the demonstration he
   silently repeats, not his own sentence.

5. **Zero hedging (8/9) and zero figurative language (8/9) across the cast.** Hedges
   ("although," "given that," "which suggests") and figures ("like," "as if") are the two
   cheapest, most natural vehicles for subordinate clauses in ordinary prose, and this
   cast is built to exclude both by rule (`BELL.md:41`, and the dials tables in all nine
   sheets). *Alternative:* the style guide already has one sanctioned door —
   Kowalczyk's "conventional/inherited simile, permitted under the style guide"
   (`KOWALCZYK.md:23`) — extend that specific exception, a plain, non-invented comparison,
   to one or two more characters instead of inventing new figurative range from nothing.

6. **The verbatim shared test.** Nine sheets sharing one paragraph word for word
   (`BELL.md:29` etc.) means a rewriter under deadline pressure can satisfy "in voice" by
   satisfying the rule instead of the person, which is exactly how four of these
   characters collapsed into one manner in the first place. *Alternative:* when revising a
   scene for complexity, check the character's own **signature** section first (the
   object, the arithmetic, the document, the walking-away) and only the shared test
   second.

7. **Amberg's ban on continuing an argument after a decision.** "Would never: Argue with a
   student after they've stated a decision" (`AMBERG.md:83`). Caps any Amberg scene at one
   exchange. *Alternative:* deepen the single enumerated statement's clauses rather than
   adding turns.

## 5. What is already working — protect this

- **The Interior-life engine.** Every sheet gives its teacher a non-plot, professional
  want (Bell: to see a student shoot past eighteen; Hearn: a student whose writing
  survives real editorial scrutiny; Kowalczyk: a student who needed the training for real;
  Amberg: a student who catches a bad contract; Baptiste: proving structural thinking
  formally; Doyle: keeping fieldwork and classroom both running; Pruitt: the forge staying
  ungraded; Sinclair: the drills staying honest; Sandoval: a student who ends up somewhere
  that rewards precision) plus a "Wrong about" blind spot and a colleague who'd openly
  disagree with them. This is the calibration-safe source of interiority the brief asked
  about — it comes from the character's own craft and its stakes, never from a wound, and
  it's already built into all nine sheets. Scenes that need to develop and run long should
  be built from this material, not from the dialogue register.
- **Proof the flat register already supports complex syntax.** Kowalczyk, Sandoval,
  Amberg, Hearn, and Bell all already produce multi-clause, causal, sometimes long
  sentences without a single warm word (`chapters/18_fifteen.md:89,139`,
  `chapters/19_sixteen.md:41-53`, `chapters/13_ten_pages.md:7`,
  `chapters/14_sixty_degrees.md:11-21`). Whatever the next pass does, it doesn't need to
  warm the register up to lengthen it — the evidence that flat and complex can coexist is
  already on the page in five of nine sheets.
- **Distinct physical staging.** Holding the object vs. arithmetic-while-working vs.
  document-on-the-desk vs. question-thrown-back vs. erasing-and-rewriting vs.
  walking-away vs. watching-before-speaking vs. folder-shifting: these are eight genuinely
  different blocking choices, and they're doing real differentiation work even where the
  words converge. Keep them exactly as specific as they are; they're the thing actually
  holding this cast apart on the page.
