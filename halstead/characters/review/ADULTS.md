# The ten adults: distinctness, passivity, and what will fight a reading-level pass

Scope: MEG.md, DAVE.md, GRANDMOTHER.md, ALDANA.md, VANCE.md, PRAHL.md, PRENTICE.md, AMMONS.md,
WHITAKER.md, DEB.md, checked against `_CALIBRATION.md`, `CHARACTER_SHEETS.md`, and the manuscript
in `chapters/` (all thirty-five files scanned; 01, 02, 03, 06, 07, 08, 09, 12, 15, 17, 19, 23, 25
and 30 read in full for this review). Every manuscript quote below was located with `grep -n` and
read in context before quoting; two places where a sheet's quote does not match the file are
flagged as found.

---

## 1. Verdict

As written, these ten cannot yet carry a longer, more subordinated, more talkative book without a
fight, and the fight is not evenly spread. Meg and Dave have the material to sustain more complex
scenes — married twenty years, recurring across seventeen chapters, already given real interior
lives on their sheets — but they currently sound like one person split across two name-tags, and
their sheets locate the split almost entirely in stage directions (who touches, who photographs)
rather than in anything a reader would hear. The seven outside adults are a harder problem: every
one of the six who has any quoted dialogue is built, identically, on a dial table that reads
**"figurative: not allowed, none used," "jokiness: 0/10," "hedging: 0%."** That is not seven
people converging by accident; it is one register — flat, warm, procedural, undecorated — stamped
onto seven job titles. The kindly-and-inadequate test that makes the book's argument does not
require that flatness, and nothing in the test forbids a joke, an opinion, a stubborn habit, or an
ordinary figure of speech. A pass that tries to lengthen these scenes, let an argument breathe, or
let an adult be specifically funny or specifically wrong will be reaching for exactly the three
tools every sheet in this group has pre-forbidden. The fix is not a rewrite of who these people
are; it is loosening three dials that were set to zero for uniformity's sake, not for the sake of
the test the cast is actually built to pass.

## 2. Meg against Dave

**The recorded failure holds, and the manuscript makes it worse than the sheets say.**

I read chapters 3, 6, 8, and 9 with the speaker tags covered wherever the prose allows it (most of
this cast's dialogue is written in fast alternating exchange with tags only every third or fourth
line, which is exactly what makes the test possible). Stretches like `chapters/03_the_letter.md:19–26` —

> "We didn't apply to this." / "No." / "So how do they have her name." / "I don't know." / "Well,
> how would anybody." / "Ask them, there's a number on it." / "I'm asking you."

— are genuinely unassignable without the tags. Either parent could plausibly say any line in that
stretch. That is defensible as *shared baseline* per `CHARACTER_SHEETS.md:19` and both sheets'
closing "Do not confuse with" sections, which call this deliberate: "They should sound alike more
than any other two people in this book" (`MEG.md:136`, `DAVE.md:132`). The problem is that the
sheets then hand each parent one signature move to keep them apart at the margins — and the
manuscript does not honor the boundary the sheets draw.

**MEG.md:27–34** calls the flat repeat-back "Signature: theirs alone" — *"She repeats the other
speaker's exact phrase back, flat, before she answers or reacts to it"* — and cites
`chapters/17_fourteen.md`. That instance checks out: `chapters/17_fourteen.md:157–159`:

> "Yeah, since September." … "Since September."

is Meg, confirmed. But the same move is not hers alone in the text. `chapters/03_the_letter.md:64–69`:

> "…she said their list is usually pretty good." / "Usually pretty good." / "That's not a
> number." / "Okay. And everything else?" / "…'There's over a hundred kids.'" / "A hundred."

Read against the surrounding attribution ("Her mom takes her hand off the phone" at :64, "Her
husband answers" two lines later at :71), the alternating turn lands "Usually pretty good." and "A
hundred." on Dave — the identical flat-repeat move, twice, in the same phone-call scene the
CALIBRATION doc itself cites as reference material (`_CALIBRATION.md:61–64`). It happens again in
`chapters/06_the_list.md:40–42`:

> "How many days have you been here?" he says. / "Twenty-seven." / "Twenty-seven."

— Dave echoing Chloe's own answer back flat, the same construction MEG.md claims for Meg exclusive
of anyone else in the book. Three instances, two chapters, one construction the sheet says belongs
to her alone. This is the single most concrete, checkable place a reader's ear would fail exactly
the way `CHARACTER_SHEETS.md:81`'s 17-of-20 figure says it does — and it means the sheets'
proposed fix (lean harder on the repeat-back as Meg's tell) will not by itself separate them,
because the manuscript has already given the same tell to both.

**The one place they do split cleanly** is disagreement shape, and it survives scrutiny: Meg
disagrees by escalating a question (`chapters/09_february.md:97–101`, "So what is that." / "I
don't know what that is." / "Four hours away, Dave. And free. And they wrote to her."), Dave
disagrees by naming a cost or a number (`chapters/12_nine.md:149–165`, "A proposal from a vendor.
They want six weeks and about forty thousand dollars… It's very well written, which is what's
bothering me… This is four pages of the same trick, isn't it."). That distinction is real and
already on the page.

**The single most concrete way to separate them without breaking either:** retire the repeat-back
as Meg's signature — the manuscript doesn't actually reserve it for her — and replace it with the
distinction that already survives contact with the text: Meg's repeats are *emotional*, always
landing on a minimizing phrase someone just used to soften bad news (*"Since September."* after
Chloe undersells four months of rifle range); Dave's repeats are *quantitative*, always landing on
a number someone just offered without backing (*"Usually pretty good." … "A hundred."*). Same
surface move, two different jobs — one repeats to make sure a feeling gets heard twice, the other
repeats to flag a number that hasn't been earned. That's a distinction a rewriter can actually use
under pressure, and it doesn't cost either character anything they currently have.

**A secondary note, not central to this brief but grep-verified in passing:** `MEG.md:5` gives

## 4. Per character

**Meg (`MEG.md`).** Can already carry: institutional interrogation at length (the 25-minute call in
ch3), enumerated worry ("what happens if it's worse than a fever"), and one genuinely funny,
specific, stubborn line — *"They are not getting the roof over her head as well"*
(`chapters/09_february.md:105`, confirmed). Forbidden by the sheet: hedging of any kind
(`MEG.md:47`, *"NO if it hedges with 'maybe,' 'I think,' 'probably,' or 'kind of'"*) and any
invented figurative image (`MEG.md:46`). Both bans directly block the two moves a reading-level
pass reaches for first — hedged, layered uncertainty and fresh figurative language. Single highest-
value change: let her keep the dead-idiom joke rule but drop the hedging ban specifically in
private, unwitnessed beats (the parked car, `chapters/09_february.md:137`) — she's allowed to be
composed in front of people and uncertain alone; the sheet currently bans uncertainty everywhere.
Confirmed true and costly: she drops from 10–20 turns/chapter in chs. 1–9 to three lines at her own
daughter's graduation (`chapters/23_the_first_one.md:31–37`, verified: two dialogue turns, three
sentences total) while Dave gets zero (`:40`, verified: *"Her dad fills a memory card and gets none
of her face"* — note this differs slightly from `DAVE.md:126`'s quoted "takes about four hundred
photographs," a minor sheet/manuscript mismatch worth a glance but not a voice problem).

**Dave (`DAVE.md`).** Can already carry: sustained document analysis (ch3, ch12), and real
unspoken feeling that already exists as physical comedy rather than as a line — the extended laugh
at Sam's joke, *"Her father laughs at that, head back, longer than the joke should carry him"*
(`chapters/19_sixteen.md:145`, confirmed). Forbidden by the sheet: any figurative comparison at all
(`DAVE.md:28`, *"figurative: not allowed, none, ever"* — the only dial in this entire batch marked
with an extra "ever") and repeating himself for emphasis (`DAVE.md:49`). The zero-figurative rule
is the tightest constraint on any character in this set and will fight a longer analytical passage
specifically, since extended reasoning almost always reaches for a comparison to carry a technical
point. Single highest-value change: allow him exactly one register where figurative language is
permitted — inside the document-analysis mode itself, a dry, technical comparison ("reads like a
lease with the rent clause missing") rather than an emotional one. That keeps the "never, ever" ban
where the sheet clearly wants it (nothing sentimental) while giving the pass room to lengthen his
best scenes.

**Grandmother / Dorothy (`GRANDMOTHER.md`).** Can already carry: direct physical observation
delivered as settled fact regardless of correction (*"She's put on weight." "I know."*,
`chapters/11_eight.md`, confirmed by sheet quote), which is a real, un-hedged opinion — rare in
this cast. Forbidden by the sheet: pressing past a deflection, and revising her miscalibrated
reading of Chloe's achievements even once corrected (`GRANDMOTHER.md:42,53`). This is the one place
in the whole set where a *lack* of revision is the character trait, so it should probably stay —
but it means she can never anchor a scene that needs to develop or escalate; she is built to
restate, not to move. Single highest-value change: let the restatement itself lengthen and get more
specific ("Once in a whole year isn't very much, is it" could carry one more concrete, wrong-but-
textured comparison to her own working life) without conceding the underlying miscalibration.

**Aldana (`ALDANA.md`).** Can already carry: a single scene, ch1, on the strength of a real,
specific institutional bind (twenty-nine kids, one worksheet, one pace). Forbidden by the sheet:
anything past four words that isn't a direct question (`ALDANA.md:17,34`) and any explanation of a
decision (`:42`). Data-integrity note: the sheet's "most characteristic line," quoted as *"Mrs.
Aldana doesn't say it mean. She says it tired"* (`ALDANA.md:11`), does not appear in the manuscript.
`chapters/01_before.md:16` actually reads *"Mrs. Aldana doesn't look up when she says it. Then she
taps the worksheet twice with one finger and walks back up to the front of the room"* — verified by
grep, no match anywhere in the chapter for "say it mean" or "says it tired." The sheet's tired-not-
mean framing may still be true to the character, but it is not a manuscript line and should not be
quoted as one. Single highest-value change: she never returns after ch1 (`ALDANA.md:123`) — a
second brief appearance, even reported secondhand at a parent-teacher conference, would give a
rewriter room to let her four-word ceiling breathe into five or six words without inventing a new
person.

**Vance (`VANCE.md`).** Can already carry the most physically sustained scene of any outside
adult — a real, held silence at floor level (`chapters/07_the_same_room.md:207–221`, confirmed).
Forbidden by the sheet: sarcasm or impatience (`:42`), and pressing a repeated "no"
(`:43`) — both fine constraints that don't fight complexity. The real constraint is jokiness 0/10
and figurative "not allowed, none used" (`VANCE.md:21,24`), identical to five other sheets. Single
highest-value change: her own sheet already gives her the material for a genuine idiosyncrasy — the
unexplained doorframe-touch habit on her way out every afternoon (`VANCE.md:77`) — that's an
unglossed personal tic nobody comments on. Let one line of dialogue notice it, hers or someone
else's; it's already there, just never spoken.

**Prahl (`PRAHL.md`).** Can already carry a long, unbroken teaching turn (the fifty-word
knights-and-knaves setup, `chapters/08_the_asking.md:93`, confirmed) — she is the only outside adult
whose sheet explicitly permits a turn "over 50 words unbroken" (`PRAHL.md:19`). That's real
capacity already sitting on the page. Forbidden by the sheet: naming the actual constraint out
loud (`:42`) — a good, load-bearing ban, since her whole function is refusal-dressed-as-generosity.
Single highest-value change: she's diagnosed by her own sheet as never getting "the occasion, on
the page, to notice" the mismatch in front of her (`PRAHL.md:51`) — a second beat where she almost
notices, catches herself, and reframes again, would let a longer scene develop around her without
ever crossing into the insight the brief rules out.

**Prentice (`PRENTICE.md`).** The character with the most existing capacity in this batch: ~28
lines, two full registers (short/procedural with a child, long/dense with an adult), confirmed by
`chapters/02_march_4th.md:88`, a 70-plus-word unbroken explanation. He is proof a kindly-inadequate
adult can already sustain a long, complex turn without breaking the test. Forbidden by the sheet:
rating Chloe to her face (`:42`) and any figurative language (`:24`, same universal ban). Single
highest-value change: none needed to his one scene — the highest-value change is structural,
addressed in §5, obstruction #5: he never returns, and his sheet's own "Known problems" section
already proposes the fix (`PRENTICE.md:123`).

**Ammons (`AMMONS.md`).** Can already carry the highest question-density in the batch (57%,
`AMMONS.md:23`) and one real, wordless dramatic beat — the pen stopping (`chapters/08_the_asking.md:31`,
confirmed). Forbidden by the sheet: unprompted reassurance or comfort (`:42`) — again a good,
load-bearing ban. Single highest-value change: her sheet already notes the diagnostic protocol
"matches, unsettlingly, the exact information set a recruiter would want to collect" and that "the
text leaves both readings there" (`AMMONS.md:51`) — a slightly longer version of that exchange,
letting one more of her procedural questions land before she stops writing, would deepen the
existing effect without adding a single degree of warmth the test forbids.

**Whitaker (`WHITAKER.md`).** Thin on the page (4 lines) but the most complex *interior* life
committed to paper for any outside adult — real unease about Halstead's funding that he "carries…
privately, without the authority or the assignment to pull on it further" (`WHITAKER.md:74`).
Forbidden by the sheet: any expressed surprise or suspicion (`:41`) and the universal figurative
ban. Single highest-value change: his own sheet flags that he never learns about Prentice or Ammons
even though a clearance review nominally covers Chloe's whole history (`WHITAKER.md:120`) — one
additional question in his existing scene, closing that gap, would both deepen the only adult scene
set late in the book and give a longer version of ch30 something concrete to develop toward.

**Deb (`DEB.md`).** Zero quoted dialogue, confirmed by direct search of `chapters/25_nineteen.md`
(grep for any quotation mark attached to "Deb" returns nothing) — the entire character is Chloe's
summary plus invention the sheet itself flags throughout (`DEB.md:7,96`). She is evidence Chloe can
have an easy, low-cost relationship, which is valuable, but she currently cannot carry one line of
a longer scene because she has never been staged. Single highest-value change: stage one exchange —
even four or five lines — of the "Deb will not have it, so Chloe will find another way in" pattern
already described in summary (`chapters/25_nineteen.md:57`). The material for a scene already
exists in synopsis form; it just has never been dramatized.

## 5. What will fight the next pass — ranked, most obstructive first

**1. The identical figurative-language ban across every outside adult with dialogue.**
`ALDANA.md:24`, `VANCE.md:24`, `PRAHL.md:24`, `PRENTICE.md:24`, `AMMONS.md:24`, `WHITAKER.md:24` —
all six read, verbatim, **"figurative | not allowed, none used."** Raising a book's reading level
routinely means richer, longer, more specific comparisons; this is a blanket rule that six separate
people share no vocabulary for that move at all. *Alternative:* keep the ban on invented or poetic
imagery (protects the flat, undecorated register that IS the point) but permit one *dead*,
job-specific idiom per character, the way MEG.md already allows Meg exactly this ("domestic
comparisons only, and only when deflecting," `MEG.md:26`) — a nurse's idiom for Grandmother, a
protocol idiom for Ammons, a range-safety idiom for Whitaker. Distinct vocabularies of cliché are
still distinguishing; universal silence is not.

**2. The identical 0% hedging across the same six sheets.**
`ALDANA.md:22`, `VANCE.md:22`, `PRAHL.md:22` (*"0% in what she says aloud"*), `PRENTICE.md:22`,
`AMMONS.md:22` (*"0% in her own speech"*), `WHITAKER.md:22`. Hedged language — "I think," "probably,"
a trailed-off clause — is one of the standard ways longer, more subordinated dialogue signals
uncertainty and interiority. None of these six is permitted it under any circumstance. *Alternative:*
this doesn't need to break the kindly-competent test — a hedge about something *outside* their
competence (Prahl hedging about district budget, not about pedagogy; Whitaker hedging about his own
private unease, which his sheet already grants him internally at `WHITAKER.md:74` but never lets
into a line) would add texture without making anyone look incompetent at their actual job.

**3. The identical 0/10 jokiness across the same six sheets.**
`ALDANA.md:21`, `VANCE.md:21`, `PRAHL.md:21`, `PRENTICE.md:21`, `AMMONS.md:21`, `WHITAKER.md:21`.
The brief is explicit that the goal is capacity to be "funny... in an interesting way" without
becoming unkind — but as written, humor is the one tool banned outright, everywhere, for the entire
class. This is the most direct collision with the stated goal of this whole review. *Alternative:*
a single dry, competence-adjacent joke each (Whitaker's flat government humor about paperwork,
Ammons's clinical deadpan about her own thoroughness) costs nothing structurally — it isn't warmth
directed at Chloe, which the test forbids; it's personality directed at the job, which nothing
forbids.

**4. Meg's signature move is not actually hers alone in the manuscript.**
`MEG.md:27` claims the flat repeat-back as "Signature: theirs alone," but Dave performs the
identical construction at `chapters/03_the_letter.md:65,69` and `chapters/06_the_list.md:42` (see
§2 above, fully quoted and grep-verified). A pass that leans on this sheet to sharpen Meg's voice
will be reinforcing a move the manuscript has already given to both parents, which won't separate
them and may entrench the blur. *Alternative:* adopt the split proposed in §2 — repeat-back as
*emotional* correction for Meg, *quantitative* correction for Dave — which the existing manuscript
instances already support without contradiction.

**5. Six of seven outside adults are single-scene by construction, and the goal statement rules out
the change that would most obviously fix it.**
Aldana: on-page only in ch1. Vance: only ch7. Prahl: only ch8. Ammons: on-page ch8, referenced-only
ch9. Whitaker: only ch30. Deb: zero staged scenes anywhere. Only Prentice has real scene volume
(~28 lines), and his own sheet flags that he never returns (`PRENTICE.md:123`). A longer, more
developed book needs adults who can sustain more than one scene, but the brief correctly rules out
the easy fix (make one of them recur by having them see more than the test allows). *Alternative:*
where a sheet already floats a return — Prentice via Ammons referencing his report
(`PRENTICE.md:123`), Deb's summarized pattern staged as one real scene (`DEB.md`, see above),
Whitaker's file gap closed in his existing scene (`WHITAKER.md:120`) — take it. Each is a same-scene
or same-file deepening, not a new appearance manufactured from nothing.

**6. Both parents' base wordiness sits at the low end of the entire cast, with no permitted range
above it.** Meg: "wordiness 3/10, measured 5.7 words/line" (`MEG.md:20`). Dave: "wordiness 2/10,
measured 4.8 words/line, terser than Meg" (`DAVE.md:22`). Both are lower than five of the seven
outside adults' measured rates. Since Meg and Dave are the two most-recurring adults in the family
and the two most likely to be asked to carry a longer scene, their sheets currently cap them at the
bottom of the cast's own range rather than the top. *Alternative:* the sheets already carry the
lever — Meg's own "Known problems" section flags her question rate as "measured 4% against a target
of 20%+" (`MEG.md:132`) and explicitly invites more of it. Actually using that stated target, rather
than the measured baseline, is already licensed and would lengthen her turns without inventing
anything new.

## 6. What is already working — protect this

- **The married-couple baseline is deliberate and should stay exactly as large as it is.** Both
  sheets say so in identical language (`MEG.md:136`, `DAVE.md:132`): twenty years of shorthand,
  sentences finished for each other, the same worry from two angles. That baseline is correct
  craft, not a flaw to fix — only the two thin margins on top of it need work (see §2).

- **Dave's unspoken physical comedy is real and already on the page** — the suppressed laugh into a
  glass (`chapters/15_twelve.md:419`, "Huh," confirmed), the eyebrow move, and the full unrestrained
  laugh at Sam's joke (`chapters/19_sixteen.md:145`, confirmed). A pass should not convert this into
  spoken jokes; it's already complex characterization that doesn't need a single extra word.

- **The kindly-and-inadequate test itself is sound and should not be touched.** Every outside adult
  sheet applies it consistently, and the "Do not confuse with" sections show the sheet writers
  actively working to keep adjacent characters apart rather than letting them collapse by default.
  The convergence identified in this review is a register problem (§5, items 1–3), not a problem
  with the test's logic.

- **Several already-distinct physical signatures should be protected as-is:** Prentice's chair the
  same size as Chloe's; Ammons's pen going flat on the pad; Whitaker's notebook angled so the
  subject can see it; Aldana's two-fingers-on-the-spine; Vance's crouch-and-stay; Prahl's sheet held
  over her head for the back row. These are all concrete, physical, and genuinely distinguishing —
  they just currently carry more of the differentiation load than the dialogue does, which is where
  §5's fixes should go, not here.

- **Meg's one unguarded, un-composed line already exists and is genuinely strong:** "They are not
  getting the roof over her head as well" (`chapters/09_february.md:105`, confirmed) is specific,
  stubborn, and almost funny in its flatness — exactly the register the brief asks for more of. It
  should be the model for where new lines get added, not an outlier to smooth over.
