# OWEN HALLWORTH

A camper who kept up with everyone else at Halstead's four-week summer camp — swam, built the bridge, was "fine" by his own peers' account — and went home anyway in the second week, because what he wanted most was something camp was never going to give him.

## Home

[Invented in full.] A small town, a modest house. His mother raises Owen and Della largely on her own; his father works long stretches away on a highway construction crew and is home in blocks rather than every night, which is part of why the household's regular rhythms — pancakes, Little League, the dog — matter as much as they do: when everyone is actually home together, it's worth protecting. The household fact that shaped him: he's used to being the reliable one when his dad's away — checking on Della, walking Biscuit, showing up to his own game on time without being told twice — so a month away from all of it wasn't a small gap to him the way it might be to a kid whose house runs the same whether he's in it or not.

## Interior life

[Invented, built around the decision above.] Wants to go home and have things be a normal size again. Afraid, at camp, of being somewhere so full of new things happening every hour that he can't find the edges of it. Thinks about Della and Biscuit before sleep. Would spend a free year doing exactly what his current life already looks like — no changes. Believes a good life doesn't have to be an extraordinary one, which is a belief every other camper in this book, chasing the next thing to get good at, would push back on hard. What he's wrong about: assuming the homesick feeling would pass if he just waited it out — it didn't, and going home wasn't a failure of nerve on his part, it was a correct read of what he actually wanted; a six-year-old doesn't have the words for "I evaluated this correctly," so it just felt like losing.

## Known problems

1. **Direct contradiction between witnesses on capability.** Ruth: "kind of, he's the one who wouldn't do the water thing" (chapters/10_april.md:37). Kavi, unhedged: "He did the water thing and he did the bridge and he was fine." (chapters/10_april.md:103). The scene gives Kavi's account more weight — deliberate, unhedged, a direct answer to Chloe's question — but the contradiction is on the page. **Fix:** soften Ruth's line, or have Kavi's acknowledge and correct it rather than silently supersede it.
2. **Direct contradiction between witnesses on whether he wanted to leave at all.** Sam, in the moment: "He wasn't having fun. He said it wasn't fun." (chapters/05_behind.md:67, 70) — reads as Owen choosing to go. Kavi, months later, explicitly not guessing: "Owen didn't want to go home... he didn't want to go" (chapters/10_april.md:99–103) — reads as the opposite.
