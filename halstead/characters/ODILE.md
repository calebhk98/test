## Voice in one paragraph

There isn't one yet, and that is the finding this sheet exists to state. Across the entire manuscript Odile has three quoted lines. Two are short and flat: *"Ruth was bored in the briefing,"* (chapters/17_fourteen.md:17) and *"That was nearly a whole minute,"* (chapters/17_fourteen.md:53). The third, and chronologically her first line in the book, is not short at all: *"Sunday. My brother's here Sunday, he's been three times and he's never seen anybody do it. I'm making him stand at your end."* (chapters/14_sixty_degrees.md:49) — three sentences, one of them carrying a subordinate clause, the only biographical fact she is ever given (a brother, three prior visits) delivered in the single least terse thing she says in the whole book. On top of the three quotes, four more instances have her "saying" something that the narration paraphrases without quotation marks — what the sound is more like (chapters/14_sixty_degrees.md:51), why a parking company bought a cipher system (chapters/16_thirteen.md:131), who actually broke up with whom (chapters/17_fourteen.md:141), and an unspecified question to Chloe after a bad round (chapters/18_fifteen.md:77). None of those four are quotable. So: a reader cannot currently imitate Odile's voice from the text, because there is not enough of it to generalize from, and the one line long enough to show a pattern breaks the "terse" pattern the other two suggest.

## Would they say this?

- YES if the line is six or seven words and states an observed fact about someone else in the room. Both of her genuinely terse lines do this — Ruth's boredom, Chloe's time.
- NO — check first — if the line reaches past ten words with a subordinate clause. Her one existing example of this ("he's been three times and he's never seen anybody do it") is real, but it is an outlier against her assigned profile, not a pattern to repeat casually.
- NO if the line contains a simile, metaphor, or any invented image. True of everything attributed to her so far, quoted or paraphrased.
- NO if she explains her own feelings or motives in words. This has never happened on the page; what stands in for her interior state is always physical (breathing, shaking, running) and always observed by Chloe, never delivered by Odile herself.
- YES if she initiates physical contact to help or move someone rather than only speaking to them — chapters/17_fourteen.md:53, hauling Chloe up by the wrist while she talks.
- NO if she is shown asking a question and the reader is given the words. Currently one instance exists (chapters/18_fifteen.md:77) and the words are withheld — this is a gap, not a rule, but treat it as untested until a real example exists.
- YES if the line answers a bare number to a direct question with nothing else attached — **this is proposed, not yet in the draft.** Applying it to a line that has never been tested should be flagged, not assumed to already fit.
- NO if she boasts about her own achievement in her own words. The one moment that reads as pride — showing off the ejection-port scar "like it's a medal" — is Chloe's paraphrase to her mother (chapters/17_fourteen.md:147), not anything Odile says or does on the page.

## Are they acting like themselves?

What exists is almost entirely bodily and almost entirely mediated through Chloe's point of view. She runs seventy metres in about forty seconds to reach a broken arrow (chapters/14_sixty_degrees.md:47). She intercepts arrows more than anyone else in the year, "four days out of five" by March (chapters/14_sixty_degrees.md:53). She burns her hand on an ejection port and is still holding the palm up at breakfast weeks later (chapters/17_fourteen.md:21). She hauls a winded Chloe up by the wrist mid-sentence rather than only offering a hand (chapters/17_fourteen.md:53). In her one large set-piece — holding a minute and nine seconds against ten opponents in the self-defense drill, the longest hold recorded anywhere in the reading list — she does not get a line. She comes down on her hands and knees; her breathing "comes in pieces against the mat"; her arms are shaking (chapters/18_fifteen.md:93-97). All of that is observed by Chloe, who stays beside her and later replays the last twenty seconds of the round in her own head, "and every time, she gets to the end of it with nothing in her hands" (chapters/18_fifteen.md:97). The scene belongs to Chloe's incomprehension, not to anything Odile does or says about what just happened to her.

Failure modes a writer should watch for: (1) giving her a reflective or explanatory speech about her own performance — nothing on the page supports her narrating herself, ever; (2) writing the scar-as-pride beat as something Odile performs or says, when currently it is entirely Chloe's secondhand report to her mother, not a scene with Odile in it; (3) making her verbally win an argument — she has never been shown disagreeing with anyone in words, so a debate scene for her currently has no precedent to build from.

## Known problems

1. **She has no voice to protect yet.** Three quoted lines in the entire book is the headline number, and any dial or signature assigned to her (fragments, the number-only answer, disagreeing by action) is a proposal built on almost no material. State this to the author plainly before anything else in this file is used: there is not yet a character here to keep consistent, only a set of physical facts attached to a name.
2. **Her one substantial line contradicts the terseness assigned to her.** chapters/14_sixty_degrees.md:49 is a three-sentence turn with a subordinate clause — the opposite of "fragments and single words." Either that line should be cut down to fit the proposed profile, or the profile should be revised to admit that she can be expansive about specific things (her brother, a spectacle she wants witnessed), and terse about everything else.
3. **She is not shown reacting to holding the best number in the reading list.** The book's engine is that these students never learn how exceptional they are, and Odile holds the clearest hard evidence available to any of them — a full ten seconds past the next-longest hold mentioned in these chapters. The text gives her no line, gesture, or beat that registers this from her own side; the collapse after the record (chapters/18_fifteen.md:93-97) is staged entirely as something Chloe watches and fails to process. This is the single highest-value gap: currently there is no scene, however small, where Odile's own reaction to her own number exists on the page.
4. **The "medal" pride beat is secondhand.** chapters/17_fourteen.md:147 gives the only suggestion that Odile enjoys or is proud of the scar, and it is entirely Chloe's paraphrase to her mother ("now she keeps showing everybody, like it's a medal"), not a line or action from Odile herself. If the author wants that pride to read as hers, it currently needs a scene, because right now it is Chloe's interpretation of her, once removed.
5. **Both of the proposed exclusive signatures are unbuilt.** "Answers a question with a number and nothing else" and "disagrees by doing the thing instead of answering" both have zero instances anywhere in the reading list or the rest of the manuscript searched. That is not a contradiction — nothing on the page rules either out — but it means both are inventions from here forward, not extensions of an existing pattern, and should be introduced as new material rather than assumed to already be "hers."
