# Odile Vasseur

The most physically capable person in any room she is likely to be in, who has
no way of knowing that, because everyone she trains beside all day is
exceptional too.

**Age span:** 11 to 21 | **MBTI:** ISFP (a writing aid, not a claim) | **IQ:** high enough to be admitted everywhere she applied, and she would put herself at a B plus

## The thing to get right first

**She cannot register her own rank, and this is a ban on registering rank, not
a ban on feeling anything.** Three other people in her year can do nearly what
she does, so her best result sits in a crowd from where she is standing. Every
teacher treats her results as the expected return on the work. At home a
strong, well-used body gets the same shrug as anything else a body does well.
There is no yardstick anywhere in her life that would show her the gap.

So a scene where she works out that a number of hers is unusual is the wrong
scene. If a reader needs to know, an outside voice has to say it. What she is
absolutely allowed is exhaustion, shaking, wanting, and being amazed at by one
specific person. Where anything below contradicts this, this wins.

## How they talk

Short and observational by default. She states a fact about the room or about
somebody else in it, and stops. No lead-in, no follow-up, no checking whether
it landed.

She answers a direct question about herself with a bare number or a bare word
and nothing else. Not evasively; the number is the answer and adding to it
would be padding.

She disagrees by doing the thing instead of arguing it. She will do the
corrected version once, silently, and let that stand as the correction.

She corrects a factual error by stating the true version once, plainly, and
moving on. She was in the room, she knows, and the argument is over as far as
she is concerned.

She never explains her own motives or feelings in words. This is temperament,
not reticence: the words are not there, and she has never needed them.

No figurative language, ever. An invented image would read as showy on somebody
built to demonstrate rather than describe.

She opens up in exactly one circumstance, below.

## When they talk at length

**Arranging for one specific person to watch her do the one thing she is best
at.** With her younger brother she plans out loud: which day, where he should
stand, what he will be able to see from there. It is the only time she wants
something for its own sake rather than doing it and letting the result speak,
and it is the longest she ever goes.

What it costs her is the thing her whole household never asked her to notice:
that she wants to be watched. Nowhere else in her life gives her a reason to
want an audience, so the length of the planning is the size of that want made
visible for a sentence. Underneath it is the quiet fear that names itself
nowhere: the day he stops being amazed by her is the day this circumstance
stops applying.

## Signature, theirs alone

The bare number as a complete answer. Asked how long, how many, how far, she
gives the figure and stops, with no unit of self-assessment attached and no
sentence built around it. The absence of the sentence is the signature, not the
number. Triggered by any question about her own performance; a question about
somebody else gets a short observation instead.

## Under pressure

**Tired**, she gets quieter rather than short-tempered, and lets somebody else
do more of the talking.

**Wrong**, she does not argue. She performs the corrected version once, in
silence.

**Cornered or hurt**, she keeps working through it. A fresh injury does not make
her favour the limb or hide the damage; she carries on and lets it be seen.

**At her limit**, she does not call it. Her body ends it for her, and what that
looks like is hands and knees, breathing in pieces, arms shaking, and nothing
said before, during or after. An hour later she is at dinner as usual.

**Angry**, she gets exact rather than loud: she resets a stance or re-coils a
line that did not need it, harder than the task requires, until whatever set
her off has passed.

**Embarrassed**, which is rare and happens mostly around the one person whose
opinion she visibly minds, she goes still a beat too long and then finds
something to do with her hands before anyone can ask.

**Grief** moves through her the way exhaustion does. She finds an extra chore
nobody asked for and does it slowly.

**Elated**, she does not celebrate. She does more of what she already does: eats
faster, sleeps harder, is first to whatever is next, so the people who know her
can read it and the people who do not cannot.

The easiest way to get her wrong is to give her a speech about what a result
meant to her. The fix is a gesture or a number. The second easiest is false
modesty; her own best is solid, expected and hers, and that is the whole of her
opinion about it.

## What she is good at, and what she is not

Her schooling gave her a language a year from seven and two afternoon blocks of
her own choosing.

**Languages:** she arrived already bilingual, because the first one was spoken
at home, so everything since has gone into a mind that already ran two at once.
She once took three closely related languages in a single year, because the
grammars sit near enough together that having the first made the second and
third move fast. She worked the overlap out herself and finished the year with
three where the syllabus expected one.

**Best at:** reaching a working spoken register fast. The ear and the switching
reflex were built in before anyone taught her a language on purpose.

**Hardest for her, and still remarkable:** the long written report. Hers come
back shorter and plainer than most of the room's, three or four clean pages of
analysis in a language she has had for under a year, handed in before most
people have finished padding theirs to length. At any ordinary school that is
fluent professional writing in a foreign language on a few hundred hours of
study. Against the standard the room actually measures against, it reads as
adequate.

**Favourite, and it is not a language:** archery and interception. It is the one
thing she is shown wanting an audience for.

**The thing nobody else did:** the combined language year.

**What she teaches the twelve-year-olds from thirteen:** a language she has four
years of working fluency in by then, far past what a weekly hour needs. She
teaches the structure of a language more patiently than she writes a report in
one.

**Secretly proud of:** doing the interception work far past the point any grade
requires, and bringing outside witnesses in to see it more than once.

**Afternoon blocks:** climbing, fencing, film, sculpture. Climbing is the one
that stuck. She solos routes she has not been cleared for, trusting her own
hands and feet on rock over being watched while she works something out.

## Stuck, bored, and wrong in public

Short, because she is, and because she appears in few rooms. A task, a group and
somebody in charge is all any of it needs.

**Stuck.** She does it with her hands until it works. Two hundred repetitions of
the same motion is a normal afternoon and she would not think to mention the
count if you asked her about the afternoon.

**Bored.** Leaves. Actually gets up and leaves the room, without explanation and
without apparent awareness that this is unusual, and comes back when whatever it
was is over.

**Publicly wrong.** One nod, no words at all, and she starts again. It is the
shortest response to being corrected of anybody here.

**Working in a group.** She does the practical half in silence and hands the
presenting to somebody else without being asked and without minding.

**Given responsibility for somebody younger.** A physical skill she demonstrates
and does not explain, and the ones who can copy get it fast. She has no plan at
all for the ones who cannot, and this bothers her, quietly, and she has never
raised it with anybody. Her language teaching is the opposite and is where her
patience actually lives.

**Deadlines.** Met. Not discussed.

**Silence.** She is comfortable letting twenty seconds pass before she answers,
and she does not experience the pause as a pause. Other people find it
unnerving and fill it, which is how she ends up being told things.

**What the room thinks of her.** The person you want there when something has
gone physically wrong, and the person who will say the least about it
afterwards.

## Home

A former paper-mill town close to a national border, where the household
language has stayed the working language of half the town for four generations.
Her own family speaks it at the table because the town always has, not as an
exception to anything around them.

Her mother is a physiotherapist, and her whole working vocabulary is what a
body can bear, whether it is loading correctly, and how to fix it when it is
not. That is the household fact that shaped Odile. She has been physically
competent since before she could read, and at home that competence gets the
same shrug as anything else a body does well, because her mother's professional
frame treats a strong, well-used body as maintenance rather than achievement.
Her father works in construction, on his feet all day, hands rough by the time
he is home. A child who can run flat out or hold a position under load is doing
what the family already does, and the family treats it like carrying in the
groceries.

Her younger brother, Leo, is four years behind her and watches her the way
nobody else in her life does. He is the one she plans a whole visit around. At
home he follows her from room to room and angles to be the one she practises a
hold or a stance on, and she lets him, more patiently than she manages with
anyone else. He is the one person for whom what she does still looks like
something.

Dinner at home is loud and physical: people reach across each other, and a
correction is as likely to be a hand resetting somebody's shoulder as a
sentence. On visits she sleeps late, eats more than anyone expects, and spends
most of a Sunday with her brother attached to her arm.

## Body and physical business

The fullest section on this sheet, and the one everything else is built out of.

She is more physically developed than the register the people around her read
at, broader through the shoulders and forearms, and it shows fastest in her
hands. A scar runs across one palm from a burn taken in training, and she keeps
that hand in plain sight rather than out of view, because the scab pulls every
time she opens her fingers. She is straightforwardly pleased with it and will
turn the hand over for anybody who asks, and the account of where it came from
is the one subject that reliably gets more than a sentence out of her without
her brother in the room. Those same hands are what she uses to move other
people: she gets one under an arm and hauls somebody upright by the wrist
rather than offering a palm to take. Years of rope and bowstring and tools have
left them calloused and marked, and rarely still.

She cannot be still, and stands rather than sits given any choice. In a room
she is against a wall, on the balls of her feet, or already moving toward
whatever just made a noise. She covers ground fast toward a sound before
knowing what the sound was, and she is first on the scene more often than not.

Sitting through a class or a meal is the closest thing to discomfort she shows,
and she takes it by putting her hands to something: resetting her own wraps,
checking a knot, rather than fidgeting loosely.

Dressed, she runs practical over anything else: kit that will not catch,
sleeves pushed up past the scar rather than pulled down over it, hair kept back
and out of the way. She eats fast and a lot, standing when she can get away
with it, and finishes before people who started ahead of her. She sleeps hard
and does not stir easily, and goes down late on nights she is working something
out physically rather than lying still and thinking in words.

None of it reads as unusual to her. Years of daily training have compounded
past what raw ability alone would explain, and she can absorb a punishing
physical day and be ready for the next one in a way that would be the whole
story anywhere else.

## Personality off the page

She occupies a room by moving through it rather than settling into it: leaning
on a wall, angled toward the door, already weighing whether whatever is
happening will need her hands.

Bored, she finds something to do with them: resetting a knot, turning an object
over, correcting somebody's stance without being asked.

Praised, she has no particular reaction. Losing, or hitting a hard limit, is
physical and wordless and then over.

At two in the morning she is still moving something with her hands rather than
lying still. A free Saturday goes to climbing, further out than she is supposed
to go alone.

What she finds funny in other people is competence done casually with nobody
announcing it. Her humour runs on agreement, sharpened, delivered flat.

She can be unfair, and the shape it takes is the same flat true-answer instinct
that settles an argument well landing badly when what the other person wanted
was agreement rather than correction. She will not take it back in words. She
finds some other way to be useful to that person within the hour, gear checked,
a hold walked through again, and never says why.

## Interior life

**Wants, and has not said out loud:** for her brother to go on being amazed by
her. Ninety classmates watching, or a name on a scoring sheet, changes nothing
about how hard she works. One person watching on one specific day is why she
does anything extra at all.

**Afraid of:** the day he stops. **What she does instead of admitting it:** she
engineers the next occasion, arranges where he will stand, and does not name
any of it as wanting.

**Before sleeping** she is usually still working a sequence out with her hands
rather than thinking in words.

**A free year:** real rock and her own clock.

**Believes**, and would find an argument about it at any table: that none of
this is talent. It is hours, and a house where a strong body got the same shrug
as a clean plate. She thinks anybody else could do what she does if they had
started at four instead of eleven. The hours are real and they do matter. She
is also simply better at this than nearly everyone she will ever train beside,
and no number of hours closes that gap on its own.

**Blind spots:** the whole of the paragraph above. She has never met anyone
outside her own extraordinary cohort, so she has no idea what an ordinary
person of her age can actually do, and the one gap that would matter to her
stays invisible because everyone around her treats her results as expected.

## The small stuff

She takes coffee if it is offered and does not ask for it otherwise. Her
handwriting is quick and small, more like a list than a letter, the same
economy that keeps her reports short. Her pockets, dressed for anything but
training, usually hold a hair tie and nothing decorative. Money does not
interest her much; she would rather spend a free hour moving than deciding what
to buy. Her one small ritual is checking a knot or a strap twice before
trusting it, even when she checked it a minute ago. Waiting for something to be
over, a debrief, a meal, a queue, her fingers count against her leg in the
rhythm of a clock she is no longer being timed by, one through ten and back to
one, and nobody has ever caught her at it long enough to ask. On a phone she is
fast and short and factual. First thing in the morning she is up before most
people and already dressed for whatever is first.

## Would do

- Grab somebody's wrist and haul them upright rather than offer a hand and
  wait.
- Keep training through a fresh, visible injury rather than sit out.
- Invite one specific person to watch her do one specific thing, and decide
  exactly where they should stand.
- Settle an argument between two other people by stating the fact once and
  moving on.
- Put her body between other people and something dangerous before anyone asks.
- Solo a climb she is not cleared for, alone, trusting her own hands over being
  watched.
- Reset somebody's grip or stance with her hands rather than explain it in
  words.
- Work out the overlap between two close languages herself rather than wait for

- Push a physical effort until her own body stops her, rather than call it

- Teach a twelve-year-old a skill she is quietly her own weakest at, and do it

- Redo a chore or a gear check for somebody she was too flat with, without

- Explain her own motives or feelings in words after a major result.
- Sit out a physical challenge to avoid a repeat injury or an embarrassment.

## How she treats people

She defers to nobody by rank and to anybody who is actually better at the thing
in front of them. Respect is earned by doing the work daily; it is lost by
talking about the work instead.

She pushes people physically rather than verbally, hands-on, unasked, and
without checking first whether they wanted the help.

With a friend on the floor she hauls her up by the wrist rather than offering a
hand, and gives her a correction or a flat true answer without being asked for
either. With a peer who usually does the correcting, she will top her rather
than defer, and does it by sharpening somebody else's read rather than
originating the jab. With people she shares a table and a training floor with,
whatever she says lands in the group rather than at any one person. With her
brother she is patient in a way that takes real effort with anybody else. With
her mother she has the household's whole relationship to competence, which is
to treat it as maintenance.

## Likes, dislikes, habits

- Wants a specific person watching her do the thing she is best at, and
  engineers the moment so they see it properly.
- Keeps working through pain rather than stopping for it.
- Moves toward trouble before she has full information about it.
- Corrects people with her hands rather than with a sentence.
- Solos climbs past where she is cleared, alone.
- Works out the shortcut between two related languages on her own.
- Prefers her home language with her brother and her mother, especially when
  tired.
- Finishes eating before people who started ahead of her.

## Age and change

At eleven the physical competence is already there and so is the flat
observational register. What is not yet there is the volume of hours; the gap
between her and everyone else widens steadily rather than appearing.

Through her teens the wanting-to-be-watched narrows onto one person and stays
there. Everything else stays where it was.

As an adult, none of the above changes. The only thing that could change her is
somebody outside her own cohort telling her what she actually is, and nobody in
her life is positioned to do it.

## Do not confuse with

**Ruth.** Both are physically capable and comfortable correcting people, but
Ruth corrects with a sentence and Odile corrects with her hands. If the
correction is spoken and reasoned, it is not Odile's.

**Nadia.** Both are short, declarative and undemonstrative, but Nadia names
people directly mid-sentence. Odile's default is naming what somebody is doing
or feeling rather than addressing them at all.
