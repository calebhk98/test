# Odile

The most physically developed student in the cohort — first to intercept a falling arrow, longest hold ever recorded against ten opponents — who was never told at home that any of that was remarkable, and who is quietly afraid of the one thing at Halstead nobody has ever seen her fail at.

**Age span:** ~11–21 (on-page presence chapters/14–18, roughly ages 11–15; one later mention, narration only, in the 17–21 band)
**MBTI:** ISFP (writing aid, not a claim) — private, physical, protective of the people closest to her, and slow to put anything about herself into words
**IQ:** reader inference only — high, on the strength of admissions results and a negotiated curriculum exception, but the text never shows her reasoning through a problem the way it shows Chloe, Kavi, or Ruth
**Appears in:** chapters/14_sixty_degrees.md, 15_twelve.md, 16_thirteen.md, 17_fourteen.md, 18_fifteen.md; CHAPTERS_16_22_v2.md, Ch16 "The Applications" (two sentences of narration, no scene, no dialogue)
**Speaks:** 3 lines in the entire book. That is a caveat on the manuscript's current draft, not a description of who she is — use this sheet, especially Home, Body, and Interior life, to write her, and treat her dialogue as ground still to be built rather than a ceiling on her.

## Voice in one paragraph

Two of her three lines are short and flat: *"Ruth was bored in the briefing,"* (chapters/17_fourteen.md:17) and *"That was nearly a whole minute,"* (chapters/17_fourteen.md:53). The third and earliest is longer and warmer: *"Sunday. My brother's here Sunday, he's been three times and he's never seen anybody do it. I'm making him stand at your end."* (chapters/14_sixty_degrees.md:49) — the one time the text lets her want something for its own sake, and it comes out in a small run of sentences, not a fragment. That contrast is the real pattern, not a contradiction to sand down: brief and observational about other people, expansive about her brother.

## Are they acting like themselves?

She runs toward the sound of trouble rather than waiting to find out what it was: seventy metres in about forty seconds, first on the scene (chapters/14_sixty_degrees.md:47). She intercepts more arrows than anyone else in the year, "four days out of five" by March (chapters/14_sixty_degrees.md:53). She keeps working with a fresh, visible burn rather than favouring it (chapters/17_fourteen.md:21). She pulls a winded Chloe upright by the wrist mid-sentence rather than waiting for her to get there herself (chapters/17_fourteen.md:53). In her one large set piece — the minute-and-nine hold against ten opponents, the longest in the reading list — she says nothing before, during, or after; she goes to her hands and knees, breathing "in pieces against the mat," arms shaking (chapters/18_fifteen.md:93-97), and the scene is carried entirely by Chloe watching her.

Two ways a writer most easily gets her wrong: (1) giving her a speech about what a result meant to her — she has never once done this, and the fix is a gesture or a number, not a monologue; (2) making the fear of water a visible flinch or a refusal — she has passed the mandatory cold-water unit clean, every time, and the whole point of the fear is that nobody has caught it yet, so it has to read as absence (a beat she avoids, a task she takes on early to control it) rather than a tell.

## Home

French is the language of the house, and has been her whole life — she is the only one of the nine who walked into Halstead already speaking two languages, which is also why the school's yearly-language machine never quite awed her the way it awes everyone else. [invented, from _ALLOCATIONS.md] The family is first generation: her parents built a life somewhere new before she was born, and French at the dinner table is one of the few rooms where nothing has to be translated or explained.

Her mother is a physiotherapist — someone whose entire working vocabulary is what a body can bear, whether it is loading correctly, and how to fix it when it isn't. [invented] That is the household fact that shaped Odile: she has been physically competent since before she could read, and it was never remarkable at home, because her mother's whole professional frame treats a strong, well-used body as ordinary maintenance, not achievement. [text — the household fact is given directly in _ALLOCATIONS.md; the effect on Odile is built from it] Her father works in construction, on his feet all day, hands rough by the time he's home. [invented] Between the two of them, a kid who can run seventy metres flat-out or hold a position under load is just doing what the family already does; nobody claps for it.

Her younger brother, Léo, is about nine — four years behind her — and worships her. [invented name and age; the worship itself and his role are text] He is the one she plans a whole visit around: *"My brother's here Sunday, he's been three times and he's never seen anybody do it. I'm making him stand at your end."* (chapters/14_sixty_degrees.md:49) At home he follows her from room to room, wants to be the one she practices a hold or a stance on, and she lets him, patiently, in a way she is not patient about much else. [inferred, consistent]

Dinner at home is loud and French and physical — people reach across each other, and a correction is as likely to be a hand resetting someone's shoulder as a sentence, her mother's habit leaking off the clinic table and onto the family one. [invented, consistent with the mother's job] Nobody there is measuring Odile against ninety other children, so she doesn't perform for her parents the way she sometimes does for peers who are counting; she sleeps late on visits home, eats more than anyone expects, and spends most of a Sunday with Léo attached to her arm.

## Body

The fullest section on this sheet, because it is the one place the text and the allocation both hand her something concrete to build from.

She is the most physically developed student in her year — broader through the shoulders and forearms than the register the rest of the cohort reads at, and it shows fastest in her hands. [text, from _ALLOCATIONS.md, expanded] Her hands carry a scar from an ejection-port burn taken during marksmanship training, and she does not hide it: she holds the burned palm up in plain sight across the breakfast table for weeks after, "because the scab pulls every time she opens her fingers" (chapters/17_fourteen.md:21). Those same hands are what she uses to move other people — she gets one under Chloe's arm and hauls her upright "by the wrist" rather than offering a palm to take (chapters/17_fourteen.md:53). Between injuries and years of rope, tools, and bowstring, her hands are calloused, marked, and never quite still. [inferred]

She cannot be still, and stands rather than sits given any choice at all. [text, from _ALLOCATIONS.md] In a room she is against a wall, on the balls of her feet, or already moving toward whatever just made a noise — she covers seventy metres at a dead run in about forty seconds toward the sound of two broken arrows, arriving before anyone else on the field (chapters/14_sixty_degrees.md:47). [text] Sitting through a class or a meal is the closest thing to discomfort she shows on the page; she takes it by putting her hands to something — turning a fork, resetting her own wraps, checking a knot — rather than by fidgeting loosely. [inferred]

Dressed, she runs practical over anything else: kit that won't catch, sleeves pushed up past the wrist scar rather than pulled down over it, hair kept back and out of the way rather than styled. [invented] She eats fast and a lot, standing when she can get away with it, and finishes before people who started ahead of her. [invented, consistent with the household and the training load] She sleeps hard and doesn't stir easily once she's down, but drops off late on nights she's working something out physically — replaying a climbing sequence, a hold, a count — rather than lying still and thinking in words. [inferred]

The one exception to all of this competence is entirely invisible on the page: she is afraid of the water, and has never told anyone. [text, from _ALLOCATIONS.md] She has passed every cold-water and towing requirement the school has put in front of her cleanly, on schedule, because her whole method for anything that frightens her is the same one she uses for training — treat the dread as one more rep and do it anyway — and it has never once shown on a scorecard. [invented, built to be consistent with everything else]

## Personality off the page

She occupies a room by moving through it rather than settling into it — leaning on a wall, angled toward the door, already weighing whether whatever's happening needs her hands. [inferred] Bored, she finds something to do with them: resetting a knot, turning an object over, correcting someone's stance without being asked. Wrong, she doesn't argue it — she does the corrected version once, silently, and lets that be the correction. Praised, she has no visible response on the page at all, which is consistent with a household where competence was never treated as news. Losing, or hitting a hard limit — the only real data point is the ten-on-one she can't hold past sixty-nine seconds, and the answer is entirely physical: hands and knees, shaking, no words (chapters/18_fifteen.md:93-97).

Tired, she gets quieter rather than short-tempered, and lets Léo or Chloe do more of the talking. At 2am she is usually still moving something with her hands — a knot, a hold sequence, a piece of kit — rather than lying still. [inferred] A free Saturday goes to climbing, alone, further than she is supposed to. [invented, from the climbing allocation] What she finds funny in other people is competence under pressure done casually, without anyone announcing it — she confirmed Sam's read on Ruth being bored in a briefing (chapters/17_fourteen.md:17) rather than making her own joke, which is the model for her sense of humour generally: agreement, sharpened, delivered flat.

## Interior life

She is afraid of the water, and has never said so to anyone. [text, from _ALLOCATIONS.md] It is not visible because her method for fear is the same as her method for everything else physical: treat it as a rep, do it clean, move on — she has passed every towing and cold-water requirement the school has set without a flinch anyone has caught. Privately, she believes the fear makes her a fraud, because nothing else about her body has ever needed that kind of management, and a household where physical competence was simply assumed gave her no model for a fear that hard work doesn't fix. [invented] She is wrong about that: it is an ordinary fear, not a defect, and no one has ever given her the comparison that would tell her so.

What she wants and has not said out loud: to be told to stop soloing before someone else makes her stop. She knows the climbing is past what she's cleared for and keeps going anyway; on some nights she runs the hold sequence of a route in her head before sleep, and it isn't pleasure, it's checking. [invented, from the climbing allocation]

What she believes that the others would argue with: that none of what she does is talent, only hours and a house that never made a big deal out of any of it — she thinks Ruth or Kavi could do what she does if they'd started at four instead of eleven. [invented] She is wrong about this too, in the same direction the whole school is wrong about all of them: she has no way to see her own outlier status because nobody has ever handed her the other denominator.

Given a free year, she would want somewhere with real rock and real open water and no one timing her — and would very likely still find a reason to avoid the water part while telling herself it wasn't about that. [invented]

## Known problems

1. **She has almost no dialogue to protect.** Three quoted lines in the whole book. That is a caveat for whoever writes her next scenes, not a verdict on who she is — everything in Home, Body, and Interior life above is built specifically so there is a person to write toward, even while her voice on the page stays thin until new scenes give her more.
2. **The fear of water is currently invisible by design, which is also a risk.** If a future scene puts her near water without the dread registering at all, the private fact does nothing; it needs at least one beat — a task volunteered for early, a held breath, a controlled step — that a careful reader could later recognise, without ever naming the fear outright.
3. **The climbing risk-taking (soloing past clearance) has no scene yet.** It is established here as fact but not dramatized anywhere in the reading list; it is a loaded detail — a near-miss or a moment of being caught would use it, and currently nothing does.
4. **Her only long line still reads as an outlier against her default terseness.** chapters/14_sixty_degrees.md:49 is warmer and longer than anything else she says. Keep it — it is now established as her one exception, reserved for her brother — rather than trimming it to match the shorter lines.

## Do not confuse with

**Ruth** — both are physically capable and comfortable correcting people, but Ruth corrects with a sentence and Odile corrects with her hands; a line where the correction is spoken and reasoned belongs to Ruth, a line where it's just done belongs to Odile.

**Nadia** — both are short, declarative, and undemonstrative, but Nadia names people directly mid-sentence as her signature move; Odile's default is naming what someone is doing or feeling ("Ruth was bored") rather than addressing them by name. A flat line that opens with someone's name should default to Nadia.
