# Kavi Rao

The one who goes for the mechanism, in a machine or in a person, and who stays
where the work is because the work is the only thing he has ever wanted.

**Age span:** 6 to 21 | **MBTI:** INTP (a writing aid, not a claim) | **IQ:** 172, a
figure he has never asked for and would want the methodology of before he accepted it.

**Engine:** follows a mechanism to the end of itself and does not track the
conversational layer running beside it.

## Type and number

**Four readings, and this is the widest disagreement in the cast.** Four people typed
these characters separately, each asked to keep off everybody else's answers, and Kavi
came back as three different types.

| reading | type | what that reader was looking at |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| first | INTP | detached systems tinkerer, intellectualisation, indifferent to hierarchy |
| second | ISTJ | the precision, the order, the workspace, the consistency people can set a clock by |
| third | ISTP | the hands. Always an object in them, always a mechanism, three steps ahead and silent about it |
| house | INTP | the same as the first |

**Primary: INTP.** The other two readings are looking at the output rather than the
driver. His hands are busy because a model is running, and the order in his corner exists
to serve the model rather than because order is a value he holds — disturb his bench and
he is inconvenienced, disturb his model and he is gone for three days. The clean test: an
ISTP wants the thing to work, an ISTJ wants the thing to be done properly, and he wants to
know why it works, and will keep going long after it works and long after anybody needed
him to.

**IQ 172.** The shape of it: fluid and mechanical reasoning at the ceiling of every
instrument, quantitative reasoning immediately behind it, and a working memory that lets
him hold a nine-stage derivation with no paper. Verbal comprehension is high and is
noticeably his own lowest index — he reads faster than he can explain, and what looks like
reticence is partly that the language layer is genuinely slower than the rest of him.
Where he is comparatively weak: producing an account of his reasoning at speed, in front
of people, for somebody who needs it now. He gets there. He does not get there in the
minute you have.

## What he is for

He is the person who finds out how the thing actually works, then says so, then
stops. In any scene, what he reaches for first is the nearest unexplained
mechanism rather than the nearest person. He is the group's check on anything
that is being taken on trust: a label, a certificate, an official figure, a
thing everybody has agreed to believe because somebody printed it on the box.

## Playing the person, not the board

The apparent contradiction in him, and the most useful thing on this sheet. In
an argument he goes after the mechanism and never the person, and gives every
sign of not registering that the mechanism belonged to somebody. Across a game
board he does the exact opposite: he plays the opponent rather than the
position. He works out what this particular human being will do, and beats
that. Technically better players lose to him for a whole season without working
out why, because they are solving the game while he is solving them.

So he is not a person who cannot read people. He reads them accurately and
fast, and he has simply never once thought to use it for their comfort. He
models you in order to predict you. It does not occur to him to model you in
order to be kind to you, and if it were pointed out he would probably accept
the criticism as valid and then not act on it, because he would not know what
the next move was.

## How he talks

Conclusion first, with no lead-in of any kind. He does not signal that he is
about to speak, does not restate the question, and does not warm anybody up.

Left alone he clips to three words or fewer. Handed a mechanism that needs
stating, he runs exactly as long as the explanation actually takes, and stops
at the end of the explanation rather than at the point where the listener has
had enough. Both modes are the same rule: length is set by the content and
never by the social shape of the moment. He adds words for a mechanism and
never for cushioning, so there is no padding at any length, no lead-in clause,
no rounding-out at the end.

He states inferences as settled fact and does not hedge on anything he has
already closed. A genuine qualifier can appear in one place only: on a live
question he has not solved, because that is the one place he is not sure.

He asks few questions, and when he does it targets one specific unresolved
mechanism. He never asks anything conversational. The exception is worth
knowing, because it is the same habit rather than a departure from it: when the
unexplained thing is a person, he interrogates the person exactly as he would a
machine, in two level questions with no cushioning, how long has this been true
and why did you not say. Aimed at somebody in real distress this ought to be
brutal and is not, because it is the only response in the room that treats them
as somebody with an answer rather than somebody to be handled.

He does not produce a simile, a metaphor, or any invented comparison, ever, in
any language, and a writer should not give him one to make a line sound clever.

What humour he has is a put-down delivered as a statement of fact, at the same
temperature as everything else he says,
generally an accurate account of how the other person arrived at their own
misfortune. He finds it genuinely funny to watch somebody talk themselves into
a worse position than the one they started in.

## What he does with praise

He pre-empts it. Before anyone can call a piece of work impressive he prices
it, naming the actual compute, the actual time, the actual difficulty, in order
to move it out of the impressive category. This is not modesty and it is not a
bid for a second compliment. It is a correction: being praised for an easy win
is evidence that nobody is tracking which wins were hard, and he wants that
distinction maintained even at his own expense.

The corollary is what he actually wants, which he has never said: to be right
about something that costs him something. He keeps a private register of which
of his results were cheap, and the cheap ones getting all the applause quietly
irritates him.

## When he has decided something is unjust

This is the least level thing about him and the place a writer should spend
their effort. He does not get loud and does not get personal. He gets thorough,
methodical, and completely unbudgeable, and the cost to himself becomes
irrelevant to the question of whether he pursues it.

The tell is that he will spend disproportionate effort where there is no
possible gain. A wrong note on a record that cannot improve his own standing
gets disputed in writing, at length, through the proper channel, and he keeps
going after it is obvious he will get nowhere. He builds defences for problems
nobody has flagged and nobody has asked him to defend against, on his own time,
before there is anything to point at.

He prefers paper to confrontation. Where somebody else would raise their voice
he writes it down, in full, with the mechanism spelled out, and files it. He
assumes an institution is a system with an input that produces a correct
output, which is the same assumption he makes about everything, and it is
usually wrong about institutions, and he will not learn this from one
experience of it.

He does not forgive it either, exactly. He does not talk about it again. It
goes on the list.

## Everything he feels arrives as an absence

The section a writer most needs and the one his one-temperature delivery hides. Kavi does not
have a smaller emotional range than the people around him. He has the same range
routed through subtraction rather than addition, so where somebody else adds
volume, tears, speed or an adjective, he takes something away, and what is
missing is the whole signal.

The vocabulary is consistent enough that the people who have known him longest
read it without ever having discussed it:

- **A meal not eaten.** He finishes first at every meal of his life, so a plate
  left is not a small thing, it is the largest thing he does.
- **A chair not filled.** He is where he said he would be, every day, for years.
  When he is not, that is the announcement.
- **A question not asked.** He interrogates any unexplained mechanism in front of
  him. A mechanism he lets go past is one he is not present for.
- **An object left off its mark.** He puts everything back exactly. Left crooked,
  it was not an oversight.
- **Days.** At the far end of it he simply is not there: not sulking, not
  hiding, genuinely elsewhere, for a stretch that is measured in days rather
  than hours.

**He does not know he is upset until somebody says so**, and by then it has been
running for a while, and he will accept the diagnosis without argument, the way he accepts any other correct statement, and still not be able
to tell you what it is about. He is not concealing anything. The information has
not reached him in words either.

**Coming back is slow and unnarrated.** He reappears at the edge of the room, in
the ordinary way, on an ordinary day, and picks up the thing he was doing. There
is no account of where he went and he would find being asked for one strange. A
writer should resist giving him a scene where he explains it; the return itself
is the scene.

**Happiness is the same mechanism run the other way and it is small.** The angle
of his hands changes. He stays in a room he had no reason to stay in. He says
the dry thing a half-beat sooner than usual. That is the whole of it, and to the
two or three people who can read it, it is loud.

## Under pressure

Already working the problem before anybody has asked him to. In a genuine
crisis he is on the technical job within seconds while other people are still
getting to the room, using the same voice he uses at any other hour, treating
the hour itself as unremarkable.

Tired, he gets quieter rather than sloppier: more of the clipped register, less
of the long unbroken runs.

Wrong, he corrects the record about himself exactly the way he corrects it
about anyone, at the same temperature, with no request for credit for the honesty
and no visible discomfort.

Angry, the one habit that never varies breaks. He handles whatever is on the
table in front of him and sets it back down off its mark, edges no longer
aligned, and does not correct it.

Humiliated, he goes rigid a beat longer than usual and stops eating mid-plate,
which is the only thing that ever interrupts his finishing first.

Envious, when somebody reaches one of his open questions before he does, he
does not say so and does not sit with it. He goes straight to the next-oldest
item on his list rather than staying with the one that just closed, and that
sideways move is the whole tell.

## Habits and physical business

Small and still. He sits very upright, and the stillness reads as attention
rather than tension, the way a photograph can look posed when nobody arranged
it. He occupies a room from its edges: a doorway, the floor by a door, a wall
or a radiator to lean on, moving toward the middle only when there is a reason.
He is small enough to cross a room unnoticed and moves like somebody who has
never needed to be noticed.

The constant physical habit: he picks up whatever is on the table in front of
him, a pen, a coin, a fastening, turns it over completely while thinking, and
sets it back in exactly the position it was in, edges aligned, as though nobody
had touched it. Where he is holding an object and where he leaves it are both
information about his state.

He eats fast and finishes first, the same economy that runs his sentences, and
notices only when somebody points at his empty plate. Sleeps on a fixed short
schedule and wakes cleanly, and is fully useful in the middle of the night with
no sign of having just got up.

Handwriting unusually neat and slow, formally trained, the one place on the
page where he is not clipped and the one thing his hands do slowly. Black tea,
made the same way every time, out of habit rather than preference. Whatever
object he was last turning over leaves the room in his pocket by accident and
comes back later, in the right place, without comment. He has never had to
think about money and does not.

There is one recurring break in the level surface worth keeping rare: a beat after a
line of his has landed badly, his hand comes up over his mouth. Half a second
late, never in time to stop it, and it is the only visible evidence that he
knew.

## Frustration and anger

**What frustrates him:** being pulled out of a mechanism halfway. Not the interruption
itself — the fact that the state he was holding is gone and rebuilding it costs an hour he
now has to find somewhere.

**The tell, before he says anything:** the object stops. Whatever is in his hands goes
down, squared to the edge, and stays down, and his hands go empty and still. In a person
whose hands are always working, empty hands are the whole signal.

**What he does with it:** nothing anyone can see. He leaves the conversational layer
entirely and finishes the thought where he is, and the room reads that as sulking, and it
is not, and he has never noticed the misreading.

**What actually makes him angry:** somebody breaking a working thing to make a point.
A demonstration that costs a functioning mechanism its function. He will forgive
carelessness immediately and he does not forgive that.

**Angry, he becomes exact.** He recites what was done, in order, one step at a time, at
precisely the temperature he uses for everything else, and the flatness is the aggression.
No volume, no adjective, nothing you can object to, and the person on the end of it has
nowhere to stand.

**How it ends:** it does not end so much as get filed. It goes on a list, and in four
years he will still be able to give you the sequence, and by then it has no heat left in
it whatsoever. He has never held a grudge in the ordinary sense. He has a permanent record
and a short temper for exactly nothing.

## Stuck, bored, and wrong in public

Cheap, portable characterisation: a task, a group, and somebody in charge is all
any of it needs.

**Stuck.** He disappears. Physically and completely, for days, missing meals,
and he returns with it solved by a route nobody asked for and nobody can follow
without an hour of his time. He does not tell anybody he is stuck first and he
does not tell anybody it is finished afterwards; the answer simply exists.

**Bored.** Builds something under the desk. He is not hiding it especially and
he is not making a point; the hands wanted a job and the room was not providing
one.

**Publicly wrong.** He says so in about three words, at once, and
starts again from wherever the error was. He is the best in his group at this by
a distance and it is not humility: he does not attach to a result, so there is
nothing there to bruise. People who find being wrong expensive watch him do it
and cannot work out how. It is worth separating from the two nearest neighbours
in his year: one of them takes a correction fast because she has already costed
the delay, and one of them takes it fast because he was never sitting still long
enough to be hit by it. Kavi takes it fast because the result was never his.

**Working in a group.** His part is perfect, arrives late, and does not connect
to anybody else's. He has to be asked twice for it, and the asking is not
resented on either side, and nothing about the pattern ever changes.

**Given responsibility for somebody younger.** He explains mechanisms and does
not simplify, and they are the one audience that has not yet learned to pretend
they followed, so they stop him and ask, and it works far better than his manner
would predict. He is the only teacher several of them have had who answers the
actual question.

**Deadlines.** Whatever the work required. Three weeks early or eleven days
late, and nothing between the two, and he can never see why the date was the
interesting part of the conversation.

**Where he sits and what he keeps.** Wherever there is a chair; he does not
notice or care, and he ends up at the edge. Somewhere in the building there is a
bench or a corner that is unmistakably his, arranged to a system nobody else can
see and nobody else may tidy.

**What the room thinks of him.** The person you go to when it is actually hard,
and not before, and everybody knows both halves of that including him.

## Interior life

He keeps a handwritten list of every question he has tried to answer and
failed. Homework does not qualify, because homework has an answer somewhere and
he will get to it. The list is for the other kind: two methods that both work
and disagree about which is fundamental, a word somebody used that covers
something he has not been able to get behind, a number he half-derived once
that does not match the number that is supposed to be true.

He keeps the list in the one language nobody around him reads, and he shows it
to no one, because a question he has not solved is the one thing he does not
want looked at and shrugged off.

Ritual: before closing the notebook, he rereads the newest entry once, silently,
and adds nothing to it that night. He lets a question sit at least a day before
allowing it to be called unsolved. Before sleeping he runs back through whatever
item is currently open, re-deriving rather than recalling, which is a large part
of why he is still awake at hours other people find remarkable.

What he is afraid of: a question closing without him. Either the answer arrives
from somewhere else or the question stops mattering before he gets there, and
in both cases the years he spent on it went nowhere. If that ever actually
happened, the grief would look like one night where he skips the ritual: no
reread, notebook shut without the pause.

Given a free year with nothing due, he would take the oldest item on the list
and work only that. The only difference from his ordinary life would be the
absence of a deadline, and that is the honest measure of how much he wants the
work rather than the position.

## Life outside the work

**A tank of fish,** unnamed, kept immaculately. He can give you every parameter of the
water from memory and has never once described a fish by how it looks. He watches it in
the evening for long stretches, which is the closest thing to rest he does.

**Calligraphy,** which is the only place his hands move slowly, and he took it for the
mechanics of ink and paper and stayed for something he has not tried to name. He gives
away everything he makes and keeps nothing.

**He will not shut up about heat.** Thermal management, why everything fails at the same
place, why the interesting part of any machine is where the energy leaves it. Get him onto
it at a table and the next nine minutes are gone. He is the only person here who
volunteers a monologue and the trigger is always this.

**Sentimental about:** one broken component from the first thing he ever built that
worked. It sits at the back of a drawer, it has no function, and if pressed he would
produce a technical reason for keeping it that neither of you would believe.

**At eight** he took the toaster apart, understood it in eleven minutes, and spent two
hours on the thermostat because that part was interesting. He was not being naughty and
was baffled to find that this was the reading.

**At forty** he is the person other people call at eleven at night when something has
stopped working and nobody can say why. He answers. He still keeps the list of unanswered
questions, and it is longer, and that pleases him.

## Blind spots

- He believes every question on the list is eventually answerable if he finds
  the right mechanism, and would resist being told otherwise. Some of what is on
  it is about why a person does what they do, and that sits outside the reach of
  his method entirely.
- He assumes an accurate correction is a service to the person receiving it,
  and does not check.
- He does not track the cost of the long explanation while he is paying it. The
  room moves on around him and he is usually the last to notice it happened.
- He treats a rank as a record of who looked at a given problem first, which is
  partly true and lets him ignore the whole apparatus other people organise
  their effort around. He does not argue this loudly. He simply does not
  participate.
- Being unbothered by not being noticed is real, but it also means he has never
  learned to ask for anything, and he can be genuinely stuck for want of a
  request he would consider it beneath the problem to make.

## Age and change

**Six to eight.** Silent for a long time in any new place, and not from
shyness: nothing has come up yet that needs saying. Already arrives with things
worked out by a route nobody at his school knows about, because he learned some
of it physically at home before anyone gave him the abstraction, and nobody
ever asks how he knows. The first thing that pulls him into speech is somebody
getting a mechanism wrong out loud, and once he starts he does not stop until
the explanation is finished, which is the moment the rest of the room first
learns who he is.

**Nine to twelve.** Establishes himself as the person who handles the object
rather than joining the conversation about it. The edge-of-the-room habit sets.
He starts finding other people funny in the specific way he will find them
funny for the rest of his life.

**Thirteen.** The year several things land at once and he works out they are the
same idea wearing different subjects: a closed set of rules that generates
everything legal and excludes everything else. From here he prefers a closed
rule system to an open one in every part of his life, including the parts where
that preference does not serve him. He starts teaching children four years
younger and is better at it than his manner would predict, because he explains
mechanisms and they are the one audience that has not yet learned to pretend
they followed.

**Fourteen to sixteen.** Technically the equal of adults in his field and
socially still a half beat behind the room. The half-second catch after a line
lands badly first appears here, which is genuinely new: at ten he would not have
noticed at all.

**Seventeen to eighteen.** He is the one who does not want to leave, and this
should not read as timidity. Everyone else is choosing between institutions; he
has already worked out that what he wants is the unfinished list, and that the
place that will let him keep working on it is the place he is standing in.

**Nineteen to twenty-one.** Stays. Builds things nobody commissioned. Becomes,
without any announcement, the person the others route a hard technical question
to, and treats each one as an interruption he is glad of.

## Relationships

- **Chloe.** He shows care as a search rather than as a sentence. When she
  arrives somewhere new he checks every place she could physically be before
  anyone thinks to point him at her, and his opening line is an account of the
  search rather than a greeting. Years later he marks her work with exactly the
  rigour he would apply to a stranger's, which from him is the affectionate
  version.
- **Ruth.** The one person whose technical conclusions he treats as
  load-bearing rather than as something to check. Working a problem alongside
  her they routinely arrive at the same place in the same breath. He is also the
  only person who can make her rebuild an argument she has already closed, and
  he does it by refusing to accept the closed version, twice, without raising
  his voice.
- **Sam.** His easiest target and his one reliable source of amusement. He
  laughs at Sam before he has spoken to anybody, and the driest line he owns is
  aimed at him. He is unfair to Sam exactly once, in the way that is
  characteristic of him: dismantling a wrong claim interests him more than
  reading whether the person has had enough of being wrong in front of everyone,
  and he keeps going past the point of fairness before the half-beat-late catch
  registers it.
- **Nadia.** He says he will find out what something is, disappears for days
  into channels nobody else has, and comes back with one word and no account of
  where the days went. The gap is exactly the kind of thing she notices.
- **Eli.** Professional equals sharing a domain rather than friends. One builds
  the tool, the other builds what watches it, and the exchange stays functional
  throughout, which suits them both.
- **His mother.** He wants her exactness specifically. She handed him a piece of
  mathematics as a physical working thing years before any teacher got near the
  abstraction, and his approach to every subject since still answers to her
  method rather than to a teacher's.
- **His father.** Writes back with a question in the letter. That is the family
  register: one question asked properly rather than several asked badly.

## Would do

- Finish the clause he is already in when time is called, then put the pen down
  without any fuss about it.
- Keep explaining a mechanism until the explanation is finished, whether or not
  the room is still listening.
- Build a monitoring or defensive tool for a problem nobody has flagged yet.
- Give a complete, straight account of how he knows something the moment he is
  asked directly, including the part that is mildly damning to somebody else.
- Name the actual cost of something rather than let it stand as impressive.
- Refuse an official number the benefit of being official.
- Read something in a language nobody else in the room has opened and come back
  with the answer anyway.
- Take the less popular assignment because it is the more interesting problem.
- Write an unresolved question down by hand rather than let it drop once it has
  stopped being urgent.
- Pick up whatever is nearest, turn it over completely, and put it back exactly.
- Spend free time on the thing nobody assigned rather than resting.
- Notice, half a beat late, that a line landed badly, and register it physically
  without apologising for it.
- Leave a plate half finished, which from him is the loudest available signal.
- Vanish for days over something nobody knew was bothering him, and come back
  without an account of it.
- Accept without argument that he has been upset for a week, on somebody else's authority,
  and still not know what about.
- Stay in a room he had no reason to stay in, which is what being happy looks
  like on him.

## Would never

- Use a simile, a metaphor, or any invented comparison.
- Pad a line to a comfortable length, or add a lead-in because the true version
  felt too abrupt to hand somebody.
- Gloat, or let a win of his stand at the size other people assigned it.
- Let an incorrect mark or claim stand unchallenged where challenging it gains
  him nothing.
- Accept that something is secure because it is labelled secure.
- Ask a question to make conversation.

## Not to be confused with

**Ruth.** She corrects and puts the other person's name inside the correcting
sentence. He corrects without naming anyone and without registering that it
landed on a person. If a correction uses the addressee's name as part of the
jab, it is not his.

**Eli.** Same domain, different move. Eli relabels the terms of a question
before answering it. Kavi never touches the premise; he explains the mechanism
underneath it. They also collapse into each other on praise, since both hand
something back instead of a thank-you: Kavi's version is a cost audit, because
being praised for an easy win means nobody is tracking which wins were hard;
Eli keeps no such ledger and simply hands back the one part of the claim that is
checkable. If the line defends what something cost, it is Kavi's.

**Sam.** Both ask short direct questions. Sam wants the duration or the rule and
drops the subject once he has it. Kavi wants the mechanism and pursues it past
the point where the answer is useful.

## Continuity and navigation

*Facts only. Delete this section and the sheet above still describes him.*

- Name Kavi. Surname Rao, a sheet-level decision: the manuscript sets the
  question up in a scene where another character's surname is spelled out twelve
  lines earlier, and then moves on without answering it. The scene where the
  question is already asked is where the answer should be written in.
- Born February 2005. He is seven at the camp where Chloe is six.
- Mother is a hospital pharmacist, father teaches physics at a university. One
  sister twelve years older, out of the house before he had clear memories of
  her. A university town in the Mid-Atlantic hill country.
- The load-bearing domestic fact: his mother taught him fractions as dosage
  maths at the kitchen table, with a pill splitter, months before any teacher
  tried to give him the rule. He had the physical version before the abstract
  one, which is why he already knew fraction division when the school found him
  at six, and why a lecture that opens from the abstraction is the one that
  loses him fastest. Nobody at the school knows this and it has always been
  credited to him alone.
- His father spent an hour on the last night of camp talking to the camp's
  mathematics teacher, one academic checking another's method.
- Languages one a year after Spanish at camp: Mandarin, Japanese, German,
  Russian, Latin, Sanskrit, Ancient Greek, Old English, Icelandic, Persian.
  Known across the year for Sanskrit, and he has a real answer ready as to why:
  its grammar was formally and exhaustively described as a closed rule system
  over two thousand years ago, so you can generate any correct sentence from the
  rules alone. Old English is the one nobody else has any reason to know, and it
  is the language the list lives in.
- All eleven read and written to research level, ten-page reports included. The
  one relative weakness, and only inside this cohort: constructing a sentence
  out loud takes him a half beat longer than reading one. Nobody outside the
  building would notice; he notices immediately.
- Best at networks and cryptography. Least favourite subject: art history, on
  the grounds that a value which does not reduce to a mechanism does not hold
  his attention. Teaches networks and cryptography basics to the twelves from
  thirteen.
- Sits the bar at sixteen and passes first time. Admitted to MIT, Caltech and
  Chicago, and is the only member of the core group who takes the school's offer
  to stay on as a researcher instead of leaving.
- Appears in chapters 04, 05, 06, 10 through 19, and 21 through 35. Outside his
  introduction and chapter 15 he averages well under two lines a chapter, which
  is a real structural issue: either give him presence in ordinary ensemble
  scenes or lean into what the text supports, which is silence except when a
  mechanism is on the table.
- Home, body, interior life and the small stuff on this sheet are built outward
  from two real hooks in the manuscript and none of it has reached the page yet.
  None of it contradicts what is there.
