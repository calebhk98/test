# MR. HEARN

Teaches writing and history through the Federalist Papers and a weekly ten-page essay. Marks the essay, not the child.

**Age span:** Chloe ages 10-11, the ten-pages-a-week course (also appears defending the library during the Watch drill) | **MBTI:** ISTJ (writing aid, not a claim) | **IQ:** no textual basis; not assessable
**Appears in:** chapters/13_ten_pages.md, chapters/14_sixty_degrees.md
**Speaks:** 4 direct quoted turns (two long, two very short), plus several instances where his response is reported in narration rather than quoted ("Hearn tells her," "Hearn's note says"). Front-loaded into chapters/13; chapters/14 has only his one library line.

## Voice in one paragraph
Hearn opens on a fact, states it flat, and adds numbers without commentary: "He wrote fifty-one of the Federalist papers in something like six to eight months. He had a full-time job the entire time he was doing it." (chapters/13_ten_pages.md:5) When he corrects, he corrects the record, not the student — "That's Madison," (chapters/13_ten_pages.md:19), full stop, then the class continues; no "actually," no explanation of why it matters that she got the author wrong. He never says a task is impressive or that a paper is good; the only value judgement in any of his lines is "It has to be as good. That's the part that's hard." (chapters/13_ten_pages.md:9) He does not raise his volume or lower it — the text says so directly ("Mr. Hearn is small and gray and says everything at the same volume," chapters/13_ten_pages.md:3) — and even under a two-hundred-student siege during the Watch drill his one line is procedural and unbothered: "'that's fine,' puts his glasses back on, and asks the boy nearest him whether he is done with the Burke." (chapters/14_sixty_degrees.md:221) Most characteristic construction: what he withholds carries more information than what he says — a returned paper with four lines of note is a better outcome than one with none at all (chapters/13_ten_pages.md:158-160), which is a structural signature, not a spoken one.

## Signature — theirs alone
The returned-paper economy: the amount of ink he spends is inversely proportional to how the grade lands emotionally. A B with four lines of substantive note (chapters/14_sixty_degrees.md:147-149) reads as ordinary; a mark with **zero** lines under it (chapters/13_ten_pages.md:158) reads worse than criticism — "She would rather have had three lines telling her it was bad" (chapters/13_ten_pages.md:160). This is delivered through narration describing his artifact (the paper), not through a spoken tic, and it is his alone among the nine.

## Would they say this?
- YES if it states a historical fact with a number attached and no adjective. ("Fifty-one of the Federalist papers... six to eight months.")
- YES if it corrects an error in two words and continues without pause. ("That's Madison,")
- YES if it separates quantity from quality explicitly and says the second one is the hard part. ("It has to be as good. That's the part that's hard.")
- NO if it explains why an assignment matters, or offers encouragement about effort.
- NO if it raises volume or urgency under pressure — even holding a library alone against two hundred students, his register does not change.
- NO if a returned paper carries a note that reassures rather than corrects.
- YES if the silence itself (no note) is used as a form of communication that lands harder than words would.

## Are they acting like themselves?
He never appears reacting to a student's feelings about a grade — every scene shows the paper or the fact, not his face. Under the Watch siege, eleven students spend forty minutes trying to break into the one room he is defending, and his response to finally being caught is procedural curiosity about a book, not relief or irritation (chapters/14_sixty_degrees.md:221). Failure modes: (1) giving him a line that praises the length or effort of Chloe's fourteen-thousand-word essay instead of finding what's wrong with it — the actual text does the opposite (chapters/14_sixty_degrees.md:149); (2) having him explain his grading philosophy at length instead of trusting the artifact (the note, or its absence) to carry the meaning; (3) raising his tone anywhere — the "same volume" line is explicit and should never be broken.

## Likes, dislikes, habits
- Marks quality over quantity or effort, explicitly. [text: chapters/14_sixty_degrees.md:149, "An argument you apologise for is not an argument, it is a request. Make it or do not make it."]
- Withholds notes as a form of feedback. [text: chapters/13_ten_pages.md:158-160]
- Moves a student to a harder author (Burke after Hamilton) once she stops needing scaffolding. [text: chapters/13_ten_pages.md:180]
- Reads with his glasses off and puts them back on as a small physical beat when a scene ends. [text: chapters/14_sixty_degrees.md:221]

## Facts a rewriter needs
Full name unknown — "Mr. Hearn" only. Small and gray in build (chapters/13_ten_pages.md:3). Teaches writing/rhetoric through history: Federalist Papers (Hamilton, then Madison), later Burke. Assigns ten pages a week. No family, age, or background given. Not listed with an IQ or rank (staff are not scored the way students are in the reference documents).

## Known problems
- Every instance of his actual reasoning ("the third page... doing the work of the first six," chapters/13_ten_pages.md:29; the note asking "where the answer to it went," chapters/13_ten_pages.md:164) is reported in narration rather than given as a direct quotation. A rewriter who wants more Hearn material has almost nothing to draw a new line from except the four quoted turns above — inventing more without flagging it as invention would misrepresent how sparse his actual dialogue is.
- No scene shows him outside the classroom or the library doorway; nothing establishes a habit, like, or dislike unconnected to grading.

## Do not confuse with
Amberg — both correct with a flat statement and no argument, but Amberg always produces a document (the mark scheme, the state code) as the physical anchor of the correction, and narrates the rule that governs it; Hearn corrects from memory with no prop and no rule cited, just the fact itself.
