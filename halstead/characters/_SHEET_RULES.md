# What a character sheet is

A sheet describes a person. It does not describe this book.

## The test

> **You should be able to pick the character up off the sheet and drop them into
> a science-fiction lunar colony, a horror novel with ghosts, or a Korean drama,
> and still write them correctly.**

That is the author's test and it decides every question below. If a line on a
sheet would be meaningless on the lunar colony, it is describing the manuscript
and it does not belong.

Chloe on the moon still needs the mechanism before she will accept the rule.
She still sorts adults by whether they are fair. She still feels everything at
full size. None of that needs a classroom to be true. But "she corrects a
teacher about Pluto" only exists in this book, and a writer holding the sheet
in a ghost story learns nothing from it.

## Hard rules

**1. No quotations from the manuscript. None.**

Not as evidence, not as illustration, not as a signature line, not in a table.
This is the rule that was asked for and missed. Quoting the text hard-links the
sheet to a draft, and the draft moves: at the last count **165 of 298
quotations across these sheets no longer matched the manuscript**, because the
dialogue and reading-grade passes rewrote most of the lines they were quoting.
A sheet that cannot be quoted cannot go stale.

If a line seems too good to lose, that is a sign it belongs in the manuscript,
where it already is.

**2. No line or chapter references as the basis of a trait.**

"He does this three times across two chapters" is a count of a draft. Say what
he does and when a person like him does it.

**3. No statistics computed from the draft.**

Percentages of questions asked, mean words per line, counts of turns. These
described a draft that has since changed, and one of them, a false "0%
questions, never a question mark", was written into a sheet as a law of the
character and turned out to be an artefact of a script that could not see
untagged dialogue. If a statistic points at something real, convert it into the
tendency it was measuring, in words a writer can apply to a scene that does not
exist yet.

**4. A single scene is not a rule.**

The commonest defect in these sheets is a trait over-fitted to one moment: a
signature move that happened once, a subject a character will not discuss
because they once did not discuss it. Ask whether it is true of the person or
only of that page. When in doubt, describe the disposition and let the writer
find the moment.

## What a sheet should carry

- **Who they are in a sentence.** What they are for, in the story and in life.
- **What they want**, including the thing they want that they would not say.
- **What they are afraid of**, and what they do instead of admitting it.
- **How they treat people**: whom they defer to, whom they push, what earns
  their respect, what loses it.
- **Under pressure**: what happens to them when they are tired, wrong, cornered,
  or losing. This is the most portable section and usually the thinnest.
- **How they talk**, described as properties rather than quoted: sentence
  length and rhythm, whether they answer the question they were asked, whether
  they hedge, whether they ask questions and what shape those questions take,
  what they do when they are wrong, what they never say and why.
- **Habits and physical business**: what their hands do, how they occupy a room.
- **Blind spots**: what they consistently get wrong about themselves and others.
- **Age and change**: how each of the above differs at the ages they appear,
  since several of these people are on the page from six to twenty-one.

## Nothing book-specific stays

The sheet is about the person, not about this book. It carries no chapter
numbers, no section references, and no navigation. The author's ruling, in his
own words: *"you can't use characters/DAVE.md in a ghost book, if it mentions
this book. The character files are supposed to be about the character, not the
book."*

An earlier version of this file allowed one clearly separated navigation
section at the bottom listing which chapters a person appeared in. Twenty
sheets had one. They are now in `_APPEARANCES.md`, which is the only place
that kind of thing belongs.

Hard facts of continuity may stay, because they are facts about the person and
survive the move: a surname, a birth month, who is related to whom, what
somebody's parents do. Those are not book references. A chapter number is.

The test is unchanged. Read the sheet and see whether you could write the
character into a haunted house.
