# Ruth's Timeline — Chronology Audit

Scope: Ruth Aymar, as given by the manuscript in `halstead/chapters/01_before.md` through `35_nine_minutes.md`. All citations are `file:line`, verified against the chapter files directly (not against `SYNOPSIS_CHARACTERS_TIMELINE.md`, `RUTH.md` (character notes), or `CURRICULUM_GRID.md`, which are used only as background and are explicitly the less-reliable source per the brief). Chloe's dates are included wherever they are the only clock available for a Ruth event, since Ruth is never dated independently of the group.

A note on method: the manuscript ages Chloe (and, by extension, the same-cohort friend group Ruth belongs to) explicitly in most chapters — either in the chapter title ("Twelve," "Thirteen," "Sixteen"...) or in dialogue ("I'm twelve," "you are 12 years old"). Ruth's own age is never given as a bare number; it is inferred from being consistently the same class, the same dorm room, the same graduating cohort (91 names, `chapters/23_the_first_one.md:12,66`) as Chloe. That inference is used throughout and flagged wherever it matters.

## 2. Contradictions

### 2.1 Ruth's networking/surveillance box: built at thirteen, or built "the year before" a sixteen-year-old's night out?

**Chapter 16 ("Thirteen" — Chloe's, and by cohort, Ruth's age-13 year), in scene:**
> "Ruth and Kavi took cryptography and networks last year, which puts Ruth three months ahead of the class by the time Chloe arrives in it. In April she comes into Chloe's room after lights out with a shoebox under her arm and sets it on the end of the bed, grinning, and does not sit down." — `chapters/16_thirteen.md:75`

> "It goes looking for recorders. Anything on any network in range that's recording video, and about half of them are still on the password they shipped with, so it tries all of those first." ... "Then it writes noise into what's being recorded." — `chapters/16_thirteen.md:81,89`

**Chapter 34 ("The Files"), Ruth's own words, first person, years later:**
> "ruth: i built that box in about a weekend when i was thirteen so we wouldnt get detention" — `chapters/34_the_files.md:67`

These two agree with each other exactly: built at thirteen, shown off in Chloe's room in April of that same year (ch. 16), and Ruth's own later recollection confirms the age (ch. 34). The "detention" motive lines up too — the group's midnight kitchen escapade that got them "four hours of counting tomatoes" as a punishment happens in the same age-12/13 window (`chapters/15_twelve.md:377`), giving Ruth a concrete, dated reason to build an anti-detection device right around then.

**Chapter 20 ("The Parking Lot"), describing the *same device* (identical function — finds recorders, tries shipped-default passwords, writes noise into the footage) being used to cover a night out that happens after Chloe's "Sixteen" chapter (ch. 19) and before the college-application chapter (ch. 21):**
> "Ruth brings a box she built for a networking class the year before, which finds video recorders on the networks around it and logs in with the passwords they shipped with, and writes noise into them at intervals." — `chapters/20_the_parking_lot.md:5`

**The arithmetic that fails:** If the box was built "the year before" the events of chapter 20 (age 16, or on the cusp of 17 — see the Underdetermined section for the exact age), it was built at roughly 15–16. But chapters 16 and 34 both put its construction at 13, a two-to-three-year gap, and chapter 16 additionally shows the finished device already in existence and being demonstrated to Chloe *at 13*, i.e. before the events of chapter 20 could have happened at all. A box built "the year before" a night out at 16 cannot be the same box already sitting, built, in Ruth's window drawer three years earlier (`chapters/16_thirteen.md:91`, "The box goes in the drawer under Ruth's window, and it stays there").

**Which reading the rest of the book supports:** Chapters 16 and 34 agree with each other on every detail (age 13, the mechanism, the "why," and — in ch. 34 — Ruth's own unprompted, first-person memory of it years later, given under no pressure to get it wrong). Chapter 20's "the year before" is the outlier and is very likely a drafting slip — the writer needed a plausible one-line reason for Ruth to already own such a device for the parking-lot sequence and reached for "a networking class" without checking back against chapter 16, where the device's origin (age 13, weekend build, no formal class assignment behind it) was already on the page.

### 2.2 The loading-dock intrusion: "six years ago," restated after the story's own clock has moved on

**Chapter 27 ("The File"), Theo, eight months into his first post-graduation job:**
> "He is very good at it and he is happy, and in his eighth month somebody gives him a stack of old material to summarise..." — `chapters/27_the_file.md:6`
> "Six years ago. Four operators, over the fence at the loading side, a little after four in the morning." — `chapters/27_the_file.md:49`
> "He was twelve that year." — `chapters/27_the_file.md:84`

This is internally consistent on its own: the loading-dock intrusion happened when Chloe/Ruth's cohort was twelve (`chapters/15_twelve.md:199,325,377`), and Theo reading about it "six years ago" while eight months into his very first job after graduating at eighteen (`chapters/23_the_first_one.md:96`) computes cleanly: 18 − 12 = 6.

**Chapter 32 ("The Money"), the same fact, restated by the same character, later in the story:**
> "theo: and six years ago they sent people over the fence at four in the morning and four of us put them on the floor" — `chapters/32_the_money.md:124`

Chapter 32 occurs well after chapter 27. Between them: Ruth's freshman-year MIT crisis, which she "holds... for about ten months" before a professor's comment breaks it (`chapters/31_ruth.md:35`), followed by "the following months" of quiet investigation and a confession she says she has been sitting on "about a year" (`chapters/31_ruth.md:103`) — and, on Chloe's side, the two-year wait mandated by the Foreign Service's own age rules (`chapters/23_the_first_one.md:96,99`: "you sit the exam at twenty... the plan for the next two years is: wait"), the exam itself at twenty (`chapters/30_cleared.md:26`), a seven-month clearance process (`chapters/30_cleared.md:40`), and a January start with a twenty-first birthday the following June (`chapters/30_cleared.md:102`). Chapter 33 anchors the immediately following events ("the other one," which grows directly out of "the money" in ch. 32) to Chloe being "four months into a job with a security clearance" (`chapters/33_the_other_one.md:47`) — i.e., her age at this point is ~20, not ~18. Chapter 35, describing this same arc, calls the group "seven people in their twenties" (`chapters/35_nine_minutes.md:105`).

**The arithmetic that fails:** If the cohort is about twenty by chapter 32 (consistent with ch. 30's exam-at-20/clearance/January-start-turning-21-in-June sequence, and with ch. 35's "in their twenties"), then the loading-dock intrusion at age twelve is roughly eight years in the past, not six. Chapter 32 reuses chapter 27's exact figure without updating it for the two-plus years of story time that visibly pass in between (Ruth's MIT arc, Chloe's Foreign Service wait, Nadia's company reaching its first "eleven months," etc.).

**A second, independent check on the same span:** Chapter 34 describes a *different* dated incident — the diner/shopping-cart night from chapter 20 — as "an incident report from six years back" (`chapters/34_the_files.md:12`), matched beyond doubt by the identical details ("Seven men. Three firearms," `chapters/34_the_files.md:18`, against ch. 20's "Seven of them, in a clump... Three have guns," `chapters/20_the_parking_lot.md:23`). That night happens at Chloe/Ruth's age 16 (or on the cusp of 17), several years *after* the loading-dock intrusion at 12. Chapters 32 and 34 are part of the same late arc (the money → the other one → the files, `chapters/32-34`), told within a matter of months of each other in-story. Two source events that are four-to-five years apart (age 12 vs. age ~16) cannot *both* be "six years" before the same later narrative moment — no single value for "the present" in this arc makes both ch. 32's and ch. 34's "six years" true at once.

**Which reading the rest of the book supports:** Chapter 27's "six years ago" is the one that checks out on its own terms (stated when the cohort is freshly graduated, ~18, matching 12 + 6). The repetition in chapter 32 is the stale figure — the elapsed-time count that was correct earlier in the book and was not recalculated for the additional two-plus years chapters 28–31 spend establishing have passed. Chapter 34's "six years back," for the *different*, later (age ~16) incident, is closer to correct for a present age in the low twenties, and is not itself the problem; it simply cannot be reconciled with chapter 32's reuse of "six" for the *earlier* (age 12) incident at the same point in the story.

### 2.3 "The chat is nine years old" — arrives too early for its own math

**Chapter 24 ("The Chat"), the chapter immediately following graduation (Chloe turns 18 in June, `chapters/23_the_first_one.md:96`), opens:**
> "The chat is nine years old. They wrote the encryption themselves at thirteen, mostly so they could arrange getting out of the building without a teacher reading it, and none of them has ever used anything else." — `chapters/24_the_chat.md:3`

The founding age (thirteen) matches the manuscript's own dated scene: the cryptography/networks class year and Ruth's box both sit at age thirteen in chapter 16 (see 2.1 above), so "wrote the encryption themselves at thirteen" is consistent with the rest of the book on its own.

**The arithmetic that fails:** Nine years after thirteen is twenty-two. Chapter 24 sits directly on the heels of the graduation chapter (18) and is full of freshly-started-job material — Theo getting his first round of supervisor feedback, Eli's first months at his company, Sam already enlisted, Ruth failing to buy a school-issued laptop as a recent alum (`chapters/24_the_chat.md:218-227`) — the texture of people who have just left, not people four years into adult careers. It also sits just a few pages before chapter 27, where the same fact (created at thirteen) is implicitly reconfirmed by a present age of about eighteen (Theo, eight months into his first job, computing the loading-dock intrusion as "six years ago" from twelve — see 2.2). Eighteen minus thirteen is five, not nine.

**Which reading the rest of the book supports:** Every other anchor near chapter 24 (graduation at 18 immediately prior in ch. 23; Theo's "eighth month" first job and correct "six years ago" math in ch. 27) points to the chat being about five years old at this point in the story, not nine. "Nine years old" looks like a number that belongs later in the manuscript's own internal clock — closer to where chapters 32–35 sit (cohort in their twenties, per ch. 35) — and was written into chapter 24 instead.

## 3. Underdetermined

- **Exact age of the diner/shopping-cart night (ch. 20).** It falls after chapter 19 ("Sixteen") and before chapter 21 ("The Applications," clearly senior year), with no chapter number or dialogue pinning Chloe/Ruth's age to 16 vs. 17 at that specific point. This affects the precision of the ch. 2.2 "six years back" cross-check but not its core conclusion.
- **Whether Ruth is exactly Chloe's age or a few months apart.** Sam is explicitly "seven and a half" against Chloe's "six" at the first camp (`chapters/04_pluto.md:56-58`), so the friend group is not perfectly age-matched. Ruth's own age is never given as a number; every date in this report for Ruth rests on her being consistently placed in Chloe's classes, dorm, teaching cohort, and the same 91-name graduating list. This is a reasonable inference, not a stated fact, so any of the "six years"-type contradictions above could in principle be narrowed by a year if Ruth's birthday sits at the opposite edge of the cohort from Chloe's — but this would not resolve the internal ch.27-vs-ch.32 restatement problem or the ch.20-vs-ch.16/34 box-age problem, both of which are self-contained within Chloe's own explicitly stated ages.
- **The exam-to-clearance interval in chapter 30.** "She sits the Foreign Service exam three weeks after she turns twenty" (`chapters/30_cleared.md:26`), "the clearance investigation takes seven months... comes through in the autumn" (`chapters/30_cleared.md:40,99`), then "she starts in January. She's twenty-one in June" (`chapters/30_cleared.md:102`). If her birthday is in June (consistent with turning 18 in June at ch.23 and 21 in June here), the exam is ~July; seven months from July lands in February, not autumn; autumn-to-January-start is only 2–4 months, not obviously wrong but not spelled out either. There's no single clean reading that's forced to be wrong, just an unstated gap between "exam" and "clearance investigation starts" that the text doesn't fill in. Not reported as a contradiction because no two explicit statements collide — it's a soft patch, not a hard one.
- **Ruth's Arabic start date.** "Ruth is on Arabic" as an established fact in chapter 13 (Chloe's ~age-10 year, `chapters/13_ten_pages.md:124`), then Ruth defends "I'm doing Arabic" against Chloe's new Latin pick in chapter 14 (~age 11, `chapters/14_sixty_degrees.md:75`) in a conversation framed around that year's new sheet picks. This could mean Arabic was a multi-year commitment for Ruth (not a contradiction — nothing in the manuscript requires every student to swap languages every year, only that Chloe's own slot does), or it could mean the chapter-14 scene was meant to introduce Arabic as new and chapter 13 pre-empted it by a year. The text doesn't disambiguate cleanly enough to call it either way.
- **Ruth's brother's exact age and admission date.** "Ruth's brother, four years behind her, is getting in, which he is" (`chapters/20_the_parking_lot.md:15`) — stated at the ~16/17-year-old diner night. No later chapter follows up on when he actually starts, so there's nothing to cross-check this against.
