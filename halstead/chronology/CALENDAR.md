# Halstead — Calendar

This calendar was derived from the manuscript alone. `BOOK.md` and `RUTH.md` were read first and saved a great deal of re-checking; where anything here differs from them it is noted. `SYNOPSIS_CHARACTERS_TIMELINE.md` was not used as evidence anywhere below. Every citation was verified with `grep -n` against the file in `chapters/` at the time this was written.

## 1. The year anchor

**Chloe Kessler was born in August 2005.** Every other date in this document is derived from that single fact plus the ages and durations the manuscript states relative to it. The anchor was chosen so that the book's final chapter (35) lands in the summer of 2026 — close to the present day, which the brief fixes at August 2026 — and it does: chapter 35 ends within days of Chloe's 21st birthday, itself in August 2026.

### Why August, not the June the back half claims

The constraint is a summer birthday. Two separate, independent, dated statements in Part 1 fix the season:

- On day one of summer camp, "the second week of July" (`03_the_letter.md:9`), Chloe answers "Six." when Sam asks her age (`04_pluto.md:57`).
- The following February, the narration states directly that "she is seven years old" (`09_february.md:27`).

Six in July, seven by February, means the birthday falls somewhere in the window between the two — roughly August through February. Nothing in chapters 5–7 narrates a birthday during camp or in the weeks after it, which rules out the camp itself. That leaves the birthday falling after camp ends (early August) and before the following February. Combined with the author's constraint that it is a *summer* birthday, the only part of that window that is unambiguously summer is late July or August. Late August is the better fit of those two: it keeps Chloe six for the entirety of the four-week camp (which straddles most of July), and it means she starts second grade that September freshly seven — matching a normal school-readiness pattern (turn N in the summer, start the school year already N) that recurs for the rest of the book. I did not find anything to make me second-guess this reasoning; I adopted it as given.

The back half of the book states a June birthday twice, unhedged: "She turns eighteen in June" (`23_the_first_one.md:98`) and "She's twenty-one in June" (`30_cleared.md:104`). This cannot be reconciled with the Part 1 evidence — camp and the following February both fall inside the "not June" part of the year, twice, independently, seven months apart with a full school year of continuous narration in between. Part 1's reading is corroborated twice and never once contradicted anywhere in chapters 1–20; Part 2's June reading is used consistently within Part 2 but has no support anywhere in Part 1 and is never revisited or double-checked against it. **The manuscript's earlier, better-corroborated evidence wins: Chloe's birthday is in August, and the two June lines are the error.** Both are listed in the fix list below, along with a line each depends on (the "twenty-one" appointment claim in `23_the_first_one.md:98`) that turns out to have a second problem once the month is corrected.

### Notes on individual placements

**Chapter 19 spans two school years.** Its bar exam (April, `19_sixteen.md:5`) sits in Chloe's "Sixteen" year (2021–22); its retake ("in October," `19_sixteen.md:99`) and the "comes home in June" line that closes the chapter (`19_sixteen.md:123`) fall in the following, untitled "Seventeen" year (2022–23) — the same year covered from a different angle by chapters 21–23 (applications, offer, graduation). The chapter is not told out of order; it simply keeps going a year past its own title.

**Chapter 20 is placed in April 2020**, not between chapters 19 and 21 where the renumbering puts it. This resolves the ordering problem `BOOK.md` §2.1 and `RUTH.md` §2.1 both flag: the surveillance box Ruth brings to the parking-lot night is built "for a networking class the year before" (`20_the_parking_lot.md:7`), and that is the identical device — same function, same description — that Ruth demonstrates to Chloe in April of the "Thirteen" year, 2019 (`16_thirteen.md:75–91`). "The year before" a 2019 build places chapter 20 in the "Fourteen" year, 2019–20, which is where it now sits. This also lines up independently with chapter 34's dossier, which dates the same night to "six years back" (`34_the_files.md:14`) from a present of roughly June 2026 — six years back from June 2026 is April–June 2020, matching almost exactly. Both of `BOOK.md`'s independent checks (the box's build date and the federal dossier) now agree.

I also checked the secondary worry `BOOK.md` §4 raised about this placement — that "Nadia does not appear anywhere in the story before ch16" — and it does not hold up: Nadia is already present and named in the "Ten" year, three chapters and three years earlier (`13_ten_pages.md:89`). One loose end remains and I'm not able to close it from the text: Sam's fighting in chapter 20 does read as more finished than his self-defense record at fourteen in chapter 17 would suggest. That's a matter of feel, not a citable contradiction, so it isn't in the fix list below, but the author should look at it.

**Chapters 24–29, 31–32 (the post-graduation cluster).** `BOOK.md` §4 already flags this stretch as loosely ordered, with no chapter giving Chloe's own age directly. I dated each chapter from its own strongest anchor: chapter 25's explicit title and its "September"/"April" dates for the blog and for Ruth's silence (`25_nineteen.md:25,125`); chapter 31's internal chain for Ruth's MIT year (`31_ruth.md:37,35,50,103` — October crisis, "about ten months" to the professor conversation, "some months later" in April to the silence, "about a year" from the professor conversation to the confession); chapter 30's exam-and-job-start dates; and each of 26–29's own stated job/training durations. Taken together these pin the cluster later than a first read suggests — chapter 27, for instance, lands in October 2025, not shortly after graduation. Two consequences of that are in the fix list below (items 3–5): the chat's stated age in chapter 24, and the "six years ago" figure Theo uses in both chapter 27 and chapter 32 for the loading-dock intrusion. I built the date table to keep each individual chapter's own math consistent; where that pulls the cluster's absolute year later than the "six years ago" lines assume, the lines are what I'm flagging, not the placement.

## 4. Fix list — every manuscript line that contradicts this calendar

Every line below was checked with `grep -n` against the file in `chapters/` immediately before being written into this table.

| # | File : line | Current text | What it would need to say |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `23_the_first_one.md:98` | "She turns eighteen in June." | "She turns eighteen in August." Chloe's birthday is August, not June (see §1). |
| 2 | `30_cleared.md:104` | "She's twenty-one in June." | "She's twenty-one in August." Same fix, same reason. |
| 3 | `23_the_first_one.md:98` | "You sit the exam at twenty and you're appointed at twenty-one." | This doesn't hold once item 2 is fixed and read against `30_cleared.md:104` itself: she "starts in January," and January comes *before* her August birthday, so she is still twenty when she is appointed, not twenty-one. Either this line should read "...appointed at twenty" or the start date in chapter 30 needs to move to after her birthday (e.g. "starts in September"). This is a real contradiction between the two chapters as written, independent of the June/August question — I checked it both ways and it fails either month. |
| 4 | `30_cleared.md:101` | "The clearance comes through in the autumn." | Doesn't fit the chapter's own arithmetic: the exam is three weeks after her twentieth birthday, i.e. early-to-mid September (`30_cleared.md:101`), and the clearance investigation "takes seven months" (`30_cleared.md:42`) — seven months from mid-September lands in mid-April, not autumn. Either "seven months" should read something closer to "thirteen months," or "the autumn" should read "the spring." (Flagged at lower confidence in `BOOK.md` §3.3; it persists under this calendar regardless of the birthday-month fix.) |
| 5 | `24_the_chat.md:5` | "The chat is nine years old." | Under this calendar the chat is founded when the cohort is thirteen, in the 2018–19 school year (the same year Ruth's box is built and shown off, `16_thirteen.md:75`), and chapter 24 sits shortly after graduation, September 2023 — about five years later, not nine. Suggested fix: "The chat is five years old." |
| 6 | `27_the_file.md:51` | "Six years ago." | The loading-dock intrusion falls in Chloe's "Twelve" year, winter 2017–18 (`15_twelve.md:199,325,377`); chapter 27 sits in October 2025 by its own "eighth month" job marker and its place in the post-grad cluster (see §3 note above). That's roughly eight years, not six. Suggested fix: "Eight years ago." |
| 7 | `32_the_money.md:126` | "and six years ago they sent people over the fence at four in the morning" | Same event, same fix, restated later in the book: should also read "eight years ago." (`RUTH.md` §2.2 already flags this line as stale relative to chapter 27's figure; under this calendar both instances need the correction, not just the second one.) |
