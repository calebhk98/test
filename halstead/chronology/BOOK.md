## 2. Ordering problems

### 2.1 Chapter 20 ("The Parking Lot") cannot be securely placed after Chapter 19 — its own internal evidence points to a much earlier date

This is the join the brief flagged, and it is where the book's chronology actually breaks down.

**Where the renumbering puts it:** Chapter 20 sits immediately after "Sixteen" (ch19) and immediately before "The Applications" (ch21, senior-year college admissions). Read that way, the diner night and the fight behind the hardware store happen when Chloe is about 16–17.

**What the chapter's own content says:**

> "Ruth brings a box she built for a networking class the year before, which finds video recorders on the networks around it and logs in with the passwords they shipped with, and writes noise into them at intervals."
`20_the_parking_lot.md:5`

That box — finds recorders on the local network, logs in with shipped-default passwords, writes noise into the recording — is not a generic gadget; it is the exact device Ruth builds and shows Chloe in Chapter 16 ("Thirteen"):

> "It goes looking for recorders. Anything on any network in range that's recording video, and about half of them are still on the password they shipped with, so it tries all of those first."
`16_thirteen.md:81` (built "In April," `16_thirteen.md:75`, the year Chloe is 13)

If the box in ch20 was built "the year before" ch20's present, and it was built when Chloe/Ruth were 13, chapter 20 should be set when they are **14** — the "Fourteen" year (ch17) — not 16–17.

**What a later chapter says, independently:** Chapter 34 ("The Files") is a federal dossier read by the group as adults, well into their careers (it follows directly from ch33, "four months into a job," i.e. Chloe is 20, about to turn 21). That dossier dates the same diner/shopping-cart night explicitly:

> "And there's an incident report from six years back. Four students left campus at night without permission, walked into town, sat in a Waffle House for two hours, and afterwards were in the car park pushing each other around in a shopping trolley. Seven men. Three firearms."
`34_the_files.md:14–15,18`

Six years before a ~20–22-year-old present puts the diner night at roughly **age 14–16** — consistent with the "year before" the age-13 box, and *not* comfortably consistent with the 16–17 placement the renumbering gives it.

**Conclusion:** two independent pieces of internal evidence (the box's build date, and the federal dossier's "six years back") both pull chapter 20 earlier than its current position after chapter 19 — toward the "Fourteen" year (ch17) rather than after "Sixteen." The renumbering that put it after ch20 is not supported by the chapter's own content. This is exactly the kind of position-is-ambiguous case the brief asked to flag: chapter 20 does not have a securely dateable slot in the current running order.

### 2.2 Chapter 6 → Chapter 7 boundary: the goodbye is narrated after the drive home it precedes

Minor, but a genuine backward step across a chapter break. Chapter 6 ends mid-drive, already an hour from camp:

> "An hour into the drive she's got her head against the cold part of the window with her eyes shut. Her dad checks the mirror twice and then calls her mom on the car speaker."
`06_the_list.md:80`

Chapter 7 opens with the goodbye at the flagpole — an event that has to happen *before* getting in the car, i.e. before the scene that just closed chapter 6:

> "Ruth finds her at the flagpole and hugs her hard enough to hurt."
`07_the_same_room.md:7`

This is a small, single-scene rewind (not a whole chapter told out of order), so it sits close to the "flashback inside a chapter is fine" carve-out — except the rewind spans a chapter boundary with no signal that it does. A reader who has just finished the drive-home scene in ch6 restarts at the flagpole in ch7. Worth a look, low stakes.

### 2.3 Chapters 13 ("Ten Pages") and 14 ("Sixty Degrees") carry no explicit self-stated age

Both chapters are placed by position alone (between the confirmed "Nine" of ch12 and the confirmed "Twelve" of ch15) rather than by a line inside them that states Chloe's age the way ch4, ch9, ch12, ch15, and ch16 do. Nothing found in either chapter contradicts ages 10 and 11 respectively, so this is not reported as an error — but it means the spine for those two years rests on inference, not statement. See Underdetermined.

No other chapter-to-chapter regressions were found. The age-titled run from Chapter 7 through Chapter 19 (grade 2 through the bar exam) advances forward consistently, chapter by chapter, with no other gaps or overlaps.

## 3. Dating contradictions

### 3.1 Chloe's birthday: the book gives it two incompatible seasons

This is the headline finding, and it sits right at the ch20/21 join the brief pointed at — it's the seam between the two separately-drafted halves of the book.

**Part 1 (ch1–9) fixes Chloe's birthday to a window that excludes March–July.**

- Day one of summer camp, early-to-mid July, Chloe states her own age:
  > `"Six."`
  `04_pluto.md:57`
- The following February (same continuous stretch of narrative — the camp is followed by grade 2 that autumn, then this February), the narration states directly:
  > "…and she is seven years old with the word for it nowhere in her."
  `09_february.md:25`

For Chloe to be 6 in July and 7 by the following February, her birthday must fall somewhere between (roughly) August and February. It cannot fall in March, April, May, or June — those months are already accounted for as "still 6" (ch2's March 4th appointment, ch3's July letter) or come after the "already 7" February statement in the same stretch of story.

**Part 2 (ch21–35) requires Chloe's birthday to be in June.**

- Graduation: > "Chloe is going into the Foreign Service, and finds out in May that she can't yet. You sit the exam at twenty and you're appointed at twenty-one. **She turns eighteen in June.**"
  `23_the_first_one.md:98`
- The same June anchor recurs, internally consistently, through the rest of Part 2:
  > "She sits the Foreign Service exam three weeks after she turns twenty."
  `30_cleared.md:28`
  > "She starts in January. **She's twenty-one in June.**"
  `30_cleared.md:104`

**The arithmetic:** a birthday window of roughly August–February (established with two explicit, dated, verified statements in ch4 and ch9) cannot also be June (stated explicitly and reinforced twice more in ch23 and ch30). These are not approximate or "eyeball" statements on either side — "Six," "seven years old," "turns eighteen in June," and "twenty-one in June" are all direct, unhedged statements of age tied to a specific month. One side of the book is wrong about which month Chloe was born in.

**Which reading the rest of the book supports:** Part 1's winter/autumn birthday is corroborated twice (July camp, February statement, independently established seven months apart with a school year of continuous narrative in between) and is never contradicted anywhere in chapters 1–20. Part 2's June birthday is used consistently *within* Part 2 (18 in June, 21 in June) but has no support anywhere in Part 1. Given the brief's steer that the manuscript should win over less-reliable material, and that Part 1's evidence is earlier, more redundant, and never revisited or corrected, Part 1's autumn/winter birthday reads as the intended one, with Part 2's "June" the likely error — but this is a genuine either-side-could-be-"right" contradiction and the author should decide, not this report.

### 3.2 Chapter 21's "sixteen" — RESOLVED, not a contradiction

> "The Caltech rep reads for six hours, comes out, and asks whether the third author is available to talk. He's told **she's sixteen**, she's in class until four, and that no, she isn't."
> — `21_the_applications.md:101`

This was reported as a contradiction on the reading that the third author is Chloe, which would make her sixteen in application season and eighteen the following June.

**The author has confirmed the third author is a different person.** The chapter says only "Three applicants list research" (`21_the_applications.md:95`) and never identifies which of the three the Caltech rep is asking about. Nothing here bears on Chloe's age, and the line needs no change.

### 3.3 "Seven months" of clearance investigation does not reach "the autumn" from the stated start point

> "She sits the Foreign Service exam three weeks after she turns twenty."
`30_cleared.md:28`
> "The clearance investigation takes seven months and involves a man named Whitaker…"
`30_cleared.md:28`
> "The clearance comes through in the autumn."
`30_cleared.md:101`

Taking the exam three weeks after a June birthday puts it in early-to-mid July. The clearance investigation is introduced immediately afterward with no stated gap, and is said to take seven months. Seven months from July lands in February, not autumn (Sept–Nov). To land the result in autumn, the seven-month clock would need to start around February–April — several months after the exam that apparently triggers it, and that gap is never mentioned. This is reported at lower confidence than 3.1 and 3.2 because the text does not explicitly say the investigation begins the day of the exam; there could be an unstated delay before it opens. But as written, with no gap stated, the arithmetic is off by a season, and it compounds the same June-birthday assumption already in question at 3.1.

### 3.4 The Chapter 15 / Chapter 27 "six years ago" checks out; the Chapter 20 / Chapter 34 "six years back" does not, on the same method

Included here for completeness, as a control: the book's other "N years ago" cross-reference is internally sound, which makes the failure at 20/34 (see 2.1) more notable rather than less.

Chapter 27 has Theo, "in his eighth month" at his intelligence job (i.e. roughly a year after the ch23 graduation, so ~18–19), read a file dating the Watch-drill intruder incident to "six years ago" and confirm he "was twelve that year":
`29_the_file.md:86, 49, 84`

12 + 6 = 18, which matches Theo's approximate age at the time he's reading the file. That checks out cleanly. The equivalent claim for the diner night (2.1 above) does not: 6 years back from Chloe's age at ch34 (~20–22, following directly from ch33's "four months into" the job that starts at 20) gives roughly age 14–16 for the diner night, not the 16–17 its chapter position implies.

## 4. Underdetermined

- **Chapters 13 ("Ten Pages") and 14 ("Sixty Degrees"):** ages 10 and 11 are inferred entirely from sequence position (between the confirmed "Nine" of ch12 and the confirmed "Twelve" of ch15). Neither chapter contains a line where Chloe or another character states her age directly the way ch4, ch9, ch12, ch15, and ch16 do. Nothing contradicts the inferred ages — this is a gap in evidence, not a found error — but a single confirming line in either chapter (as exists for every other age-titled chapter) would remove the last inference in the age-8-through-16 spine.
- **Chapter 20's actual date:** per 2.1, the internal evidence (Ruth's box, the ch34 dossier's "six years back") points toward the "Fourteen" year rather than the post-"Sixteen" slot the current numbering gives it. But this is not a clean fit there either — Sam's fighting ability in ch20 reads as considerably more advanced than his self-defense record at 14 (`17_fourteen.md`), and Nadia does not appear anywhere in the story before ch16. A firm re-slotting is not possible from what's on the page; what's clear is only that "right after ch19" is not securely supported. Fixing chapter 20's date would require the author to decide when it happens and let the rest follow, not a reshuffle by this report.
- **Chapters 24, 26, 27, 29, 32:** all sit in the loosely-ordered early-career cluster after graduation. None gives Chloe's or Theo's age directly; ages are triangulated from job tenure ("eighth month," "four months into a job," "month four" of Sam's training) and from the "nine years old" chat and "eleven months old" company references. The relative order of this cluster (24→25→26/27→28→29→30→…) is plausible but not independently pinned by any single chapter; it depends on ch30's "January… twenty-one in June" anchor holding, which is itself implicated in the birthday problem at 3.1.
- **Ch32 ("The Money") and ch34 ("The Files"):** no explicit statement of Chloe's age or the calendar date; both were dated in this report only by their position following ch30–ch31 and the "eleven weeks" / "following two months" durations they give for their own events. If a firm age for the "six years back" Waffle House dossier entry (2.1) is wanted, pinning Chloe's exact age in ch34 first would help.
