Four times in quick succession. This is the signature of a pass that standardized paragraph openings.

Then "Seven men come across the car park to rob them" is also direct and plain, but "come across" has a slightly more narrative, dramatic quality than pure documentation. It's written in present tense while the incident narrative around it is past tense. The sentence integrates reasonably well into the spare style but has a faint seam where it was fitted in.

The chat reads naturally and moves fast.

## 5. Is it any good

What works: The reveal is perfectly shaped. We see the file say "no known method" in the document, then watch Ruth casually reveal she built "that box" in a weekend at thirteen and "it wasnt even hard." The landing is exact. Sam's joke about losing a shoe, then Ruth's realization, then Eli saying "i have never once been at the top of a room in my life"—these are beats, and they land.

What doesn't work as well: The document section feels assembled rather than written. The repeated opening moves, the anaphora, the studied spareness—it reads like the output of a revision pass that optimized for tone at the cost of voice. The document is functional but not quite alive. And that sentence about the seven men, while it integrates, has a faint grain of having been rewritten. It fits, but you can see the seam.

Would I keep reading? Yes, immediately. The chat is where the chapter becomes human again, and the revelation is strong enough to carry the whole thing. The mystery of Ruth's power is vivid and the moment of her recognizing what she did is genuinely good.
