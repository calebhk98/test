# Rudeness pass, Chapter 33 ("The Other One")

Scope: how the seven treat each other while they argue, not what they are
arguing about. Source: `33_the_other_one.md` as supplied for this pass.
Every quotation below was copied from that file and checked against it.

## Counts

- DELIBERATE, KEEP: 3
- UNINTENDED, FLAG: 0
- UNCERTAIN: 1

All three real instances involve the same person (Eli) not answering a
question, and all three are marked by the text itself, either by the
narrator naming the dodge directly or by another character calling it out
in the same breath. I looked hard for an unmarked instance involving one of
the other six and did not find one strong enough to put in front of the
author; the near-misses are noted below so you can see what was checked
and rejected.

## DELIBERATE, KEEP

### 1. Line 5 — Eli answers around Theo's question, for days

> That first night he sends back one question of his own: what happens to
> the four of them who never held a badge if this goes wrong. Eli's answer
> comes back within the hour, three lines about compartmentalization that
> don't touch the actual question, and the no holds through everything
> that follows, unargued after that, for days.

Does it: Eli. Receives it: Theo. The narrator states outright that the
reply "don't touch the actual question." That is the book naming the
dodge, not a reader inferring one. Keep.

### 2. Lines 37–45 — Ruth's follow-up on the cameras goes unanswered

> ruth: and if youre wrong about the cameras
>
> eli: then i go away for a very long time, so im not going to be wrong

> Ruth reads that twice from the floor of her dorm room, closes her eyes
> for a second, and types back anyway.
>
> ruth: you dont get to just not be wrong. give me the actual number of
> cameras youve counted, not the number youre confident about

> Eli doesn't answer that part, and the thread goes quiet for the night
> without anyone else picking it up either.

Does it: Eli (with the rest of the thread going along by staying silent).
Receives it: Ruth. The narration states directly that "Eli doesn't answer
that part," and notes nobody else raises it either. Explicitly flagged.
Keep.

### 3. Lines 73–77 — Sam asks for a number, gets a non-number, says so

> sam: how long's this go for
>
> eli: as long as it takes to get in and out once
>
> sam: thats not a number

Does it: Eli. Receives it: Sam. Sam names the evasion himself in the very
next line, in the thread, so the book is not letting this slide unremarked
— a participant calls it out on the page. Keep.

Note for the record, not a defect: Sam does get an actual figure
eventually — "Sam signs the day he finally gets an actual number for how
long the test runs, because the number was what he'd been waiting on" /
"sam: five weeks. ok" (lines 143–145) — but no one is ever shown giving it
to him. The gap between the dodge at line 75 and the resolution at 145 is
consistent with the same pattern as findings 1 and 2: Eli under-answers
and the record simply moves on. Given the narrator later names this as a
trait of his ("because deciding costs something he'd rather spend
elsewhere," line 97), all three instances read as one deliberate,
consistent characterization rather than three separate accidents.

## UNCERTAIN

### Line 11 — Ruth's text to Theo

> In the chat he keeps to small talk, the weather outside his building, a
> joke Sam makes about the food at basic. Ruth texts him alone once, just
> his name.

Does it (if it is anything): Theo, by not being shown to reply. Receives
it: Ruth. This is not a chat-thread question so it does not fit the
pattern of the three findings above, but it is a direct, personal,
named-address reach-out during the days Theo is going silent on
everyone, and the text never says whether he answered it. Two readings
are both plausible: (a) it is deliberate — showing that even Ruth, reaching
past the group channel, gets nothing back, which would underline his
isolation during the decision; or (b) it is simply not the kind of gesture
that needs an on-page answer (a name, sent with no question attached, may
not be asking for one). What would settle it: whether the author intended
this beat to register as Theo going quiet on Ruth specifically, or purely
as color for his state of mind with no reply expected either way.

## Checked and set aside (not included as findings)

Noted here only so the author can see the sweep was done, not as
findings in their own right.

- **Line 19, "ruth: noted"** — a one-word reply to Theo's "i want it on
  record that this is the stupidest thing any of us has ever done" (line
  17). Read alone it could look dismissive, but Theo immediately presses
  ("noted where," line 21) and gets a straight answer ("in the chat,
  theo," line 23). Reads as banter between two people who know each
  other, not a point being rolled past. Not included.
- **Lines 109–127, the Ruth/Nadia stop-clause argument** — hard
  disagreement, but every one of Ruth's lines gets a direct Nadia reply,
  including her hardest question ("and if theyre wrong," line 121,
  answered at 123). This is people arguing, not one of them being
  disregarded. Not included.
- **Line 81, Chloe's message** — the last line of her vignette, and
  nobody in the thread replies to it on the page. But none of the other
  five vignettes (Nadia's, Kavi's, Sam's) get a reply past their own
  closing line either — it is the shape of the section, each person
  reading and reacting in their own scene, not a case of Chloe being
  singled out and ignored. Not included.
- **Line 99, Chloe's birthday "mentioned once and then not again"** —
  no addressee, no quoted exchange to check word for word, and no
  particular person shown declining to respond to another. Too
  unspecific to stand as an instance of one character disregarding
  another. Not included.

## What I would fix first

If the author wants only one of the three fixed and the other two left as
running characterization of Eli, fix **finding 2** (lines 37–45, the
cameras question). It is the sharpest of the three: Ruth names the
evasion explicitly in her own words ("you dont get to just not be wrong"),
which makes the non-answer that follows the most pointed one in the
chapter, and it is the one most likely to read to a reader as the group
simply letting a real safety question drop.
