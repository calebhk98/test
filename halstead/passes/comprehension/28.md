# Comprehension Review: Chapter 28 (Nadia)

## What I understood

Someone attempts industrial espionage against Nadia's company in May 2024. A cleaner hired through a cleaning contract carries a USB stick, apparently intended to steal data from Nadia's machines. The machines don't have USB ports, so the attempt fails. The person tries three more USB sticks and then just performs the cleaning work. Nadia fires him, then investigates who hired him—it takes a week. She discovers "four guys and an office above a tyre place" are responsible. She visits them and directly tells them they wasted their money and that she won't involve the police, essentially shaming them for an incompetent espionage attempt. One apologizes.

The chapter then shifts to Nadia's hiring challenges. Her company is small (40 people, 11 months old) and growing. She interviews 400 people that year and conducts all interviews herself. Most people fail a take-home test; she makes it easier twice. By November, the test is so easy she'd give it to a twelve-year-old, but fewer than one-third of applicants still pass. In interviews, she asks people to explain something they built, then asks two follow-up questions. Many cannot answer the second question—they can't extend beyond what they've already said. Despite this, she hires good people with excellent references who measure well on other metrics. She's troubled: she "can't find anyone who can finish a thought." Ruth suggests it's a hiring pool issue—the best candidates work elsewhere. Nadia agrees but says it doesn't feel right.

## Where I got lost

1. **"She's forty people and eleven months old"** (line 8)
   - This momentarily reads as Nadia personally being 40 years old and 11 months (nonsensical)
   - Clearly means her company is 40 people strong and 11 months old
   - The pronoun reference is unusual enough that a reader might pause

2. **"By November it's a version she'd have handed a twelve-year-old at Halstead"** (line 70)
   - "Halstead" is mentioned without context. Possible readings:
     - A school where Nadia worked or attended?
     - A company?
     - A person's name?
   - The reference is completely opaque to a reader unfamiliar with the larger context

3. **"I can't find anyone who can finish a thought"** (line 91)
   - Ambiguous what this means exactly:
     - Candidates can't articulate complex ideas?
     - Candidates memorize solutions but can't think beyond them?
     - Candidates lack depth of understanding?
   - The chapter context suggests option 2 (they can explain one thing but can't answer follow-up questions), but the phrase itself is vague

## Ninth grader

A ninth-grader could mostly follow this chapter. The structure is clear: industrial espionage attempt, Nadia's confident response, then a shift to hiring troubles. The dialogue is straightforward.

They would stop or struggle at:
- Understanding what a "take-home test" is (context clues suggest it's a programming/technical assessment, but this isn't explicit)
- The "Halstead" reference (no context given—this requires external knowledge)
- The exact meaning of "can't finish a thought" (vague enough that they might interpret it as something different than intended)
