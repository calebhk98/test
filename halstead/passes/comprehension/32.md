# Chapter 32: The Money — Comprehension Review

## What I understood

This chapter covers eleven weeks from November 2025 to February 2026. Eli builds a financial system designed to look like normal, boring reconciliation work. Kavi builds a monitoring system to watch it. They test it for a month against Nadia's systems before deployment.

Over the following two months, the system finds "a shape" — evidence of a single person making financial decisions from around 1998 or 2001 onward. There's no name or bank identified, just a pattern of decisions.

Ruth argues this is proof of one person and presents three pieces of evidence:
1. Gaps in the data (3–5 days with no pattern, no calendar correlation) that wouldn't occur in institutions
2. Every decision lands in the same seven-hour band over twenty years, shifting only forty minutes total over two decades (consistent with aging)
3. A consistent preference for an inferior instrument since 2003 with no operational reason

Ruth gives 99.99% confidence it's one man; the 0.01% is humility.

The conversation then shifts to who else might have discovered this. Theo reveals that the government has had a file on "the school" for nineteen years. They think it's probably a state or company and have been wrong twice. When the characters were eleven, the government placed a man on staff; they spotted him in a day. Six years ago, people "went over the fence at four in the morning" and "four of us put them on the floor." Chloe thought this was a drill, but Theo read the file and says it wasn't.

Ruth argues the government's nineteen years of being wrong is still valuable — they know things about the school that the characters don't. Eli proposes they go read the government file.

## Where I got lost

1. **"The financial one takes eleven weeks."** — Is this the build time, test time, or total time to completion? The next line says they test it for a month, which doesn't obviously add up to eleven weeks.

2. **"None of them have that kind of patience. The program does."** — What patience are we talking about? I inferred this means they lack patience for the slow, boring monitoring, so they need an automated program. But it's not explicit.

3. **What is "it" in "What it finds, over the following two months"?** — Is this the financial system Eli built, or Kavi's monitoring system? What data are they actually searching through? This feels like a time jump or scene change with no clear signal.

4. **"the school"** — This appears suddenly with no introduction. The government has been watching "the school," they placed a man on staff "when we were eleven," and six years ago "people went over the fence." Who runs the school? What is it? Are the characters still affiliated with it? This is the first mention of anything called "the school" in what I've read.

5. **"they sent people over the fence at four in the morning and four of us put them on the floor"** — Does "put them on the floor" mean knocked them down, detained them, something else? The phrasing is unclear.

6. **"theo that was a drill"** — Who decided it was a drill? Who was in charge of ordering it? This suggests the characters have some authority or connection to the school that isn't explained.

7. **The connection between the financial discovery and the school revelation** — Are these related? Is the man making financial decisions connected to the school? Or is Theo's revelation about government surveillance a separate but parallel discovery? The chapter doesn't make the connection explicit.

8. **Timeline of "six years ago"** — If the chapter is set in February 2026 and something happened "six years ago," that would be around 2020. But I don't know if this matters or how it fits into the larger narrative.

## Ninth grader assessment

A ninth-grader could follow the basic structure: people built a program, it found evidence of one person, and they discovered the government is also investigating something. They could understand Ruth's three pieces of evidence and the logic.

However, they would stop being able to follow the story when Theo starts talking about "the school." Without knowing what the school is, who runs it, why the government is watching it, or what the incident six years ago was really about, the significance would be lost. The chapter would feel like it's jumping into the middle of a conversation without enough context.

They might also struggle with the vague phrasing around what the program does, what "going anywhere" means, and what "put them on the floor" means.
