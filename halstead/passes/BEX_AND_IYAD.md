# Bex and Iyad: an antagonism audit

*Report only. No chapter has been edited. Read against `HOUSE_RULES.md`,
`DO_NOT_FLAG.md`, `characters/BEX.md`, `characters/IYAD.md`, and the two
scenes named as the standard: `chapters/12_nine.md` (the bridge) and
`chapters/18_fifteen.md` (the Aurel rumour).*

## Method, and one tension flagged up front

The brief asks for two different repairs and they pull in opposite
directions, so the resolution has to be said plainly rather than smoothed
over in the entries below.

The author wants Bex and Iyad to read as "trying to be mean," the way
Malfoy is trying to be mean. But `BEX.md` and `IYAD.md` are explicit and
repeated on the opposite point: Bex "does no such thing and never has,"
and "any line that shows her choosing the moment, checking whether the
other person is in the room, or enjoying the transfer has broken her."
Iyad's sheet is the same: giving him "any awareness at all of the
transaction" is listed as the first failure mode, and the signature move
is defined as something that happens "without ever having been asked to,"
delivered "without noticing he has arrived anywhere."

Both sheets are right that a scheming Bex or a knowing Iyad would be a
worse, smaller character than the ones written. So nothing below gives
either of them a chosen target, a checked room, or a private motive. What
both sheets already license, and what the existing manuscript already
uses well in its two standard scenes, is a mechanism that is not
scheming and still lands like a blow: the *content* of what Bex or Iyad
says is accurate and freely given, and the *staging* around it, who else
is in the room, what else just happened, whether Chloe is holding a tray
or sitting an exam result, is what does the damage. That is the version
of "deliberate" available here: not the character choosing the moment,
but the scene choosing it for them, the way the bridge scene and the
Aurel scene both already do. Every proposed line below stays inside each
sheet's YES / would-do list and touches none of the NO / would-never
clauses. Where I could not find a fix that stays inside the sheet, I have
said so and left the scene alone.

The second, larger gap is on Chloe's side, and it is more consistent: in
scene after scene she skips the reaction and goes straight to the audit.
`CHILD_VOICE_BRIEF.md`, already in this folder, names the general
mechanism and the fix: "a clever child who is hurt and then picks the
moment apart is human; one who picks it apart instead of being hurt is
not." Every rewrite below puts a body or a behaviour first and lets the
working-out, where it survives at all, come after it rather than instead
of it.

Every current-text quotation below is copied verbatim from the chapter
file and checked against it a second time before being printed here.

## ch. 12, age nine (`chapters/12_nine.md:121-123`) - the Mandarin call-out

**What happens now:** Iyad recites the year's news at dinner nightly; in
March what he has is a linguist's private assessment of Chloe's accent,
delivered at volume as she is still walking the length of the hall with
her tray. Chloe responds over the following weeks by changing which door
she enters dinner through and which seat she takes, permanently, without
telling anyone why.

**Trying to be mean, or merely tactless?** Tactless, and correctly so.
This is Iyad's sheet working exactly as designed: the read-out is
"triggered by his having a list and an audience, which is most
evenings," and the damage is "the item he arrives at without noticing he
has arrived anywhere." Nothing here should be rewritten to make him
choose her or the moment; that would break him. The timing (tray still
in hand, no way to duck it, the whole hall able to hear) already does the
staging the brief is asking for, without Iyad doing any staging himself.

**What Chloe does now:** Reroutes her own dinner habits, quietly and
completely, and says nothing to anyone about why.

**What a nine-year-old would do:** Reroute herself, yes, that part rings
true, but also tell somebody, badly, at the wrong moment, to the wrong
person, and get nothing useful back for it. Nine-year-olds complain to
whoever is nearest, not whoever could actually help.

**Proposed:** Insert after "Sam asks him something about the fish tank
and he answers that too."

> She tells Ruth on the stairs going up, going too fast to get it out in
> order, doing Iyad's volume badly to show her.
>
> "He said it while I still had the tray."
>
> "He says everything while people still have trays," Ruth says, not
> stopping on the landing. "Eat something."
>
> It is not the answer she wanted. She eats something anyway.

And extend the following paragraph, currently "Chloe works with Iyad in
chemistry for a term after that... takes the seat at the end where the
plates arrive last," with one added clause at the end:

> ...and takes the seat at the end where the plates arrive last, and does
> not say why the seat changed when her mother asks, on the phone, whether
> she likes the new spot.

**Strain check:** Neither sheet is touched. Iyad's lines are unchanged.
Ruth's line matches her established bluntness elsewhere in the book.

## ch. 13, age ten (`chapters/13_ten_pages.md:87`) - the paintball technique

**What happens now:** Chloe and Ruth work the barrel-reading technique
out themselves on a Thursday; on the Friday, people start asking how,
Ruth deflects, and Chloe shrugs. Bex overhears the shrug and takes the
whole explanation to the coach herself, unprompted, on the Monday, "at
the volume she says everything at." The technique goes on the board
under Bex's name. Chloe's only reaction shown: sitting with her knees up,
then privately reconstructing which parts of Thursday she said out loud.

**Trying to be mean, or merely tactless?** Tactless. This is the sheet's
central mechanic exactly as written: "she pushes anybody who is
stalling, and she pushes by starting without them." Bex did not check
who was in the room or wait to see whether the credit would land
somewhere else; she answered a stalled situation the way she answers
everything. No change proposed to Bex's action.

**What Chloe does now:** Silent audit only. No dialogue, no anger, no
grudge that goes anywhere.

**What a ten-year-old would do:** Be short with the person closest to it
for a day or two, even though that person (Ruth) isn't really the one at
fault, because Ruth is the one in reach.

**Proposed:** Add after "Ruth was the person she had been explaining it
to."

> She finds Ruth before lights out anyway.
>
> "You told people you'd been practising."
>
> Ruth doesn't look up from the book. "I have been practising."
>
> "You said it right before Bex heard you say it, and now it's got Bex's
> name on the board instead of ours."
>
> "It's got Bex's name on the board. Bex is the one who told Bell."
>
> Chloe goes to bed still going over who said what to whom, and she is
> short with Ruth for two more days about a thing she couldn't put a
> number on.

**Strain check:** Fine for Bex (untouched). Ruth's tone matches her
established manner (flat, slightly combative, unbothered) seen
throughout this same chapter.

## ch. 17, age fourteen (`chapters/17_fourteen.md:55`) - the self-defense retelling

## ch. 19, age sixteen (`chapters/19_sixteen.md:37-45`) - the corridor "fiftieth"

**This is the clearest instance of the exact failure named in the
brief, and it is the top priority for a rewrite.**

**What happens now:** Chloe has just failed the bar exam by four points
out of two hundred. Bex, who passed, crosses the length of the hall to
reach her before dinner and, meaning to reassure her, tells her the
margin is "a fiftieth," "at the volume she says everything at, over the

console the next person. Chloe's entire recorded response: she stays
where she is until the hall clears, then works out on the stairs
"whether she had it or whether she was still working it, because Bex had
all of it and had it fast," meaning: she re-litigates her own math
homework rather than reacting to having just failed a major exam and had
the margin of her failure broadcast down a public hallway.

**Trying to be mean, or merely tactless?** Tactless, and a strong example
of the sheet working as intended: this is Bex trying to help, using the
same volume and the same accuracy she uses for everything, in the one
place it does the most damage, "the harm is entirely in who else is
within earshot and cannot say anything." No line shows her checking the
room or choosing the moment; she is already past Chloe and on to the
next person before the words have landed. No change proposed to Bex's
dialogue or action, which stays exactly as written through "on to the
next of them."

**What Chloe does now:** Immediately reruns her own arithmetic. No
anger, no exit, no comeback, nothing carried to dinner or to bed. This is
the audit-instead-of-hurt pattern named directly in the brief.

**What a sixteen-year-old would do:** Leave the spot rather than stand in
it. Avoid the thing that reminds her of it for a while. Rehearse, later,
the short sentence that would have shut it down. Say nothing to Bex
about any of it.

**Proposed:** Replace the final paragraph of the scene.

> Chloe stays where she is until the hall has thinned out, Bex's voice
> still going somewhere down it, on to the next name.
>
> At dinner she sits with her back to the corkboard for the first time

> want the fiftieth or the margin or any part of the board tonight, and
> eats without looking up. Later, lights off, she runs it again anyway,
> not the fraction this time but the volume of it, Bex's voice going the
> length of the hall loud enough for people still reading their own names
> to hear it land on hers, and she gets as far as the one sentence that
> would have shut it down in a single line. By breakfast she still hasn't
> said it to anybody, least of all Bex.

**Strain check:** No change to Bex. Sam and the corkboard-avoidance are
