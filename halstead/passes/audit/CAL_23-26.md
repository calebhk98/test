## 1. Spock, Sheldon, AI, mini-adult

Nothing in these four chapters reads as any of the four. The specific tests:

**Sam is not an AI in 25 or 26, and the chapters work at it.** He cannot say whether he
broke the thumb on purpose: "the fourth gets a thumb, deliberately or not he genuinely
can't say afterward" (`26:25`). He does not know how long the culvert took: "he only
finds out how few when somebody who timed it tells him afterward" (`26:25`). He does not
remember the decision on night four: "Sam doesn't remember deciding anything about it"
(`26:41`). And he is wrong about something for four straight months and stays wrong
(`25:86`). That is four separate refusals of perfect recall and perfect execution inside
two short chapters, and it is the single best-defended thing in my range.

**Sam is not Sheldon.** He never once frames a result as a thing he is good at. The
closest the text comes is `25:62`, "He is aware, in the flat way he's aware of most
things about himself, that this comes easily," and it survives because what comes easily
is a task he has already dismissed as trivial ("that's a measurement of whether the rifle
works," `25:80`). It is one word from trouble and I would watch it in any future pass,
but as written it reports the task, not the man.

**Chloe is not a mini-adult in the Georgetown scene**, which was the place to check.
`23:194`-`23:206` has her laying out six stages and running the arithmetic forward, which
is adult work, but the scene keeps her a child by having her father supply the thing she
had not thought of: "that's a long two years to spend just waiting on one date. Find
something in it you'd actually want to do anyway" (`23:203`). She is being parented in
the middle of her own competence. That is the correct shape.

**Kavi at `24:359`** ("you tell people theyre exceptional early so they work harder and
dont leave. its in the same chapter as the four percent thing") is the one line in the
chat that could be read as brilliance worn as personality. It survives on two grounds:
the group treats it as ordinary shop talk and nobody remarks on it, and the line's
function is deflationary rather than displaying. No change wanted.

**No mini-adult register in 23's family material.** Chloe finds her mother's face before
anyone else's (`23:26`), her mother says "I'm so proud of you I could be sick" (`23:56`),
and the twelve-year-olds kick a water bottle down a row for twenty minutes (`23:14`).

## 2. A child knowing they are exceptional, comparing favourably, or being modest

### 2.1 The Sam chapters: no finding, and I pushed hard

Taking the three the brief named, in order.

**Ten out of ten, and the captain's praise** (`25:68`-`25:107`). Sam is told twice that
this is the best mark on the range all cycle. He never accepts the frame, never deflects
it, and never acknowledges it as praise. Asked "What's your accuracy?" he answers
"About forty" (`25:71`) — a literal answer about a different measurement, which is the
correct move, because a modest Sam would have heard the compliment and pushed it away.
He then supplies his own ranking and puts himself under it: "I'm at about forty percent
and that's poor, there are people I went to school with at ninety" (`25:89`). His sample
is Halstead and he places himself below the middle of it. This is the cleanest execution
of section 2 I found anywhere in my range.

**Four men in eleven seconds** (`26:25`-`26:27`). He does not know the number until
somebody tells him, and when he gets it he uses it to prosecute himself: "It's the
eleven seconds that stay with him longer than the thumb does... The fifteen seconds
before it, where he decided the culvert was worth the risk of not knowing what was in
it" (`26:27`). Note what is not there: he does not think the eleven seconds were good, he
thinks they were not the part worth reviewing. "Not what happened once he was inside; he
can't fault that" is a workmanlike audit of a routine result, not modesty and not pride.

**Beaten in two seconds by a man who waited six hours** (`26:39`-`26:41`). He gives the
win entirely to the other man and takes the fault entirely himself: "ground he'd spent
minutes reading his way across without once catching the man waiting on it" (`26:41`).
No trace of surprise that he could be beaten, which is the tell a character with a
private sense of standing would leave.

**Modesty specifically.** I checked every point in 25 and 26 where somebody hands Sam a
compliment. The grader (`25:28`), the buddy with the stopwatch (`25:34`), the man behind
him on the ruck march (`25:45`), Ruiz (`25:17`, `25:51`), the captain (`25:68`, `25:77`),
the major (`26:51`, `26:69`). In every one of them Sam either answers a factual question,
disputes a fact, or says nothing. There is no "it was nothing," no changing the subject,
no discomfort with being praised. He is not deflecting, because he does not register
that anything has been offered. Correct.

### 2.2 The one Sam line I would query: `25:28`

> "You done this before?" he asks. "No," Sam says. "I do other stuff." **He keeps the rest
> of it to himself.**

The bolded clause is the only sentence in either Sam chapter that implies he knows there
is something worth withholding, which is the definition of the modesty failure. Two
things make it a query rather than a finding. First, it may only mean he does not
elaborate about Halstead, which is a privacy habit rather than a competence habit.
Second, three lines later the same beat runs again and lands the opposite way: "'No,'
Sam says. 'Boarding school.' The table waits for the rest of it. **That's the whole of
it**, and the table goes back to eating" (`25:31`). At `25:31` there is nothing more to
say; at `25:28` there is a rest of it that he is holding. If the author wants both, the
`25:31` version is the calibrated one and `25:28` should follow it. Cutting the clause
outright costs nothing: "'No,' Sam says. 'I do other stuff.'" already ends the exchange.
Reported, not edited, because it turns on whether the author reads it as reticence about
the school rather than about himself.

`SAM.md:202` supports the query — "reads his own standing as middling and means it... is
physically exceptional and doesn't remark on the fact of it" — and `SAM.md:112`/`:208`
confirm the corrected reading the brief mentions: he is the cast's most willing teller of
stories against himself, which is not modesty and does not conflict with anything in
these chapters. Nothing in 23-26 contradicts the corrected sheet.

### 2.3 The one Sam clause with a whiff of looking down: `25:80`

> "Right, but that's not accuracy," Sam says, and he says it kindly, because he has worked
> out that this must be a trick question and **wants to let the man save face**.

The trick-question half is exactly right and is the load-bearing rationalisation. "Let
the man save face" is the part I would watch: it puts Sam in the position of granting
something to a superior, and it does not quite follow from the trick-question premise
(a man asking a deliberate trick question has no face at risk). It is one clause and it
is inside Sam's misreading rather than the narrator's voice, so I left it. If it ever
gets touched, "and he says it kindly, because he has worked out that this must be a trick
question" stops at the right place.

## 3. Contrary evidence that goes unrationalised

**No finding.** Every outside signal in these four chapters gets absorbed. The
mechanisms, so the author can see they are not all the same one:

| where | the evidence | how it is absorbed |
|---|---|---|
| `25:28` | grader reads the total twice | not registered; Sam answers the question asked |
| `25:31` | "You play college ball or something" | "Boarding school." The school explains it |
| `25:34` | "you already had the best number in the platoon" | Sam answers about the watch, not the claim |
| `25:45` | accused of cheating on the weigh-in | treated as a dispute about a scale reading |
| `25:68`, `25:77` | best mark on the range all cycle | a trick question (`25:80`) |
| `25:92` | "your school had you doing it before you turned eighteen" | "It's small, you wouldn't have heard of it" (`25:104`) — the outside case is defective |
| `25:86` | four months of everything being easy | basic training is a screening exercise and the real one has not started |
| `26:51` | the major reads his three times out to the room | "Was that the assessment, or was that still setup?" (`26:57`) |
| `26:39` | beaten in two seconds | the other man's patience, and Sam's own failure to read the ground |
| `24:72`, `24:92` | five vulnerabilities, one thought impossible | luck, and a cheat, and a joke |
| `24:359` | a performance review calling them exceptional | a retention tactic |
| `24:529` | a doctor who has never heard of the drug | a supply and paperwork problem, not a fact about Halstead |

Two I looked at twice and cleared. `25:34` and `24:529` are absorbed by **not
registering** rather than by an active explanation, which is a fourth mode the standard
does not list. In both cases the character's attention lands on a nearby concrete
question (is the watch right; what is actually in the pill) and never reaches the
comparison at all. I think that is stronger than an explanation would be, because an
explanation implies the character noticed something needed explaining. Flagging it only
so the author knows the audit saw the difference and let it stand.

The `25:86` master rationalisation deserves its own line, because it is doing four
months of work at once:

> he has become increasingly certain that basic training is some kind of extended
> screening exercise and that there is a real one somewhere and that he is being kept out
> of it by not having said the right thing yet. (`25:86`)

That belief is what makes every other absorption in chapter 25 cost him nothing, and it
pays off in the chapter 26 coda: "i genuinely thought there was a real one" (`26:89`).

## 4. Scale announced rather than buried

Three findings, all narration, none of them edited.

### 4.1 `23:101` — the narrator ranking Sam

> Sam joins the Army. **He's been the best fighter in his year since he was ten, by a
> margin wide enough that the field stopped contesting it by the time he was twelve**, and
> in July, at a recruiting office off the highway outside Pittsburgh, he signs the papers
> the way he takes a drill.

The first half is legitimate: `characters/_CALIBRATION.md` says the ranking "reads to
them as a number next to other numbers," and best-in-year at Halstead is exactly such a
number. The second half is the narrator admiring the margin, and "the field stopped
contesting it" is the narrator's judgment rather than a fact anyone in the book holds.
This is the closest thing to a section 4 defect in chapter 23. The minimal repair is to
stop at "since he was ten." Not edited: the clause carries real information about how
long the gap has existed, and removing it is an authorial call about what chapter 23 owes
the later Sam chapters.

### 4.2 `23:125` — the narrator confirming that Nadia's speed matters

> Nadia clears the register screen and does the whole thing herself in under fifteen
> minutes, upload and all. The woman thanks her like she's just fixed something enormous.
> **Which, done at that speed for that many people who need it, it apparently would be.**

"The woman thanks her like she's just fixed something enormous" is the buried version and
it is enough; the tally at `23:128` (eleven marks in a slow week, nineteen in a fast one)
then supplies the scale as an aside, which is the model working. The appended sentence
tells the reader the woman was right, and it puts "at that speed" into the value, which
makes Nadia's speed part of what the reader is being asked to price. "Apparently" hedges
it back toward Nadia's own view, which is why I did not edit. If it is ever cut, nothing
in the business thread is lost, because the tally is the thing that starts the company.

### 4.3 Echo glosses the narrator points at

Three places where the narration reaches across the book to connect two beats for the
reader, which House Rule 1 names directly ("the narrator glossing an echo the reader can
see"):

- `25:28` — "the way the captain will read a different sheet twice in four months' time."
  A forward wink at a scene eighty lines away. Also arithmetically off: this scene is week
  two, early August, and the captain scene is "month four" (`25:59`), i.e. November, so
  four months forward from here overshoots.
- `25:121` — "She reads the fourth paragraph twice, **the way the grader read Sam's sheet
  twice**," and later in the same sentence "which is its own kind of answer." The second
  of these is the purer instance: the reader can see that setting a report on top of a
  stack rather than into it is an answer, and being told so is the defect. Strike both
  clauses and the paragraph is unharmed.
- `23:98` — "She still remembers wanting the last names after camp and coming up short,
  and this time she gets the count while it's still in front of her." Chloe counting the
  heads in the photograph before she has decided to is the whole beat; the callback is
  then explained.

Two more House Rule 1 items in my range that are not scale, listed for completeness:

- `23:92` — "The frame is exactly where it's always been; she's grown well past having to
  reach for it." Her hand flat on the frame with her whole arm straight already said it.
  The semicolon clause is the narrator naming what the image means.
- `25:25` and `25:37` — the same gloss on Sam's relationship to numbers, twice, twelve
  lines apart: "because in Sam's head a number he's already hit is just the number that
  comes next," then "There's no version of a task anywhere in Sam's head where he reaches
  a number, decides that's enough, and stops there." The second is the dramatised version
  restated. The scene between them (`25:34`) proves it without either.

### 4.4 What is not a finding here

`26:51`, where the major reads Sam's three numbers out to the room, is a *character*
totalling in front of an audience whose reaction is the point, and the narration then
refuses to gloss it: "He reads both numbers out to the room like they're supposed to mean
something on their own" is inside Sam's uncomprehending POV. That is the right way to put
a total on the page. Same for `26:75`, "It goes upward anyway," which is four words and
explains nothing. `24:347` ("In the space of one evening the same names move from a
federal supervisor's notes to a fall-and-catch drill... to the exact gigabyte count on a
store laptop") is the narrator arranging a range, but it prices nothing as impressive and
I would leave it.

author wants one more beat of the age anywhere in chapter 23, this is the place with room

## What is working, briefly

Beyond the items already cited: the whole of chapter 26's debrief is the audit's ideal
case in miniature. A room of ordinary soldiers, a major who says out loud that a man went
four days untouched and that patience beats a tired soldier every time, three numbers
read to the room, and the only thing Sam takes from any of it is a self-criticism he had
already prepared and an opinion that the exercise was thin. The major then tells him, in
public, that a soldier who wants the next one made harder "has decided the exercise is
about him" (`26:69`) — a correction Sam gets for a reason that has nothing to do with
ability, which is the right way to punish a calibrated character. Nothing in the chapter
lets him, or the reader, total anything up.

Chapter 24's design also deserves the note. Putting the calibration into the *replies* —
Nadia's "luck, not skill," Sam's "youre gaming the count," Kavi's "not informative" —
means the friends do the rationalising for each other in real time, in four words each,
which is both faster and less visible than a character rationalising in their own head.

## Outside this audit's remit, reported because I found them

1. **A probable garble in the captain's dialogue, `25:74`-`25:77`.** As it stands the
   captain says "That's not great... forty puts you well under it, whatever the number on
   this sheet says you did out here today," and then, with no intervening beat and no
   attribution, "Son, you hit ten out of ten... that's the best mark I've seen on this
   range all cycle." The same speaker contradicts himself across two paragraphs. Commit
   `420749d` removed the line that used to sit between the setup and the first speech:
   "The captain looks at the sheet again. **It still says ten out of ten.**" That sentence
   was what made the captain's confusion legible, and without it the two speeches read as
   a mistake rather than a man arguing with his own sheet. This matters for calibration,
   because the captain's confusion is what licenses Sam's trick-question reading at
   `25:80`. Author's call: restore the struck sentence, or give Sam a line between the two
   speeches.

2. **Arithmetic error, `26:41`.** "He checks him, hand on the sternum same as the other
   **five**." The count is six: two at the fence (`26:19`, "He checks both of them, hand
   flat on each sternum") and four in the culvert (`26:25`, "checks that everyone behind
   him is still breathing"). Sam's own tally at `26:61` agrees: "a two, a four, and a one
   over four days." Should read "the other six." Not edited because it is not a
   calibration matter and I was asked not to push chapter 26 around, but it is
   unambiguous and one word.

3. **Two stale "nine years" in chapter 24.** The chat's age was corrected to five
   (`24:5`, per `chronology/CALENDAR.md` fix item 5) but two dependent figures were not:
   `24:14`, "It has for nine years," describing the chat's own answering habit nine lines
   after the chat is established as five years old; and `24:154`, "you have had nine years
   to learn it properly," where the cohort has known each other eleven years by chapter
   23's repeated count. The first is a flat contradiction inside one chapter; the second
   may be Ruth being loose and can probably stand.

4. **The brief's chapter 23 items.** The nine admissions offices that could not believe
   the transcripts are not in chapter 23. That beat lives at `21_the_applications.md:57`
   and `:59` and belongs to whoever holds chapters 21-22; chapter 23 contains no
   transcript material at all. Everything else the brief listed for 23 is present:
   graduation, Georgetown, Nadia's company, Eli's security job.

5. **A stale sheet line, `characters/ELI.md:179`**, quotes Sam as "thats cheating" at
   `24_the_chat.md:54`. The manuscript has Sam saying "youre gaming the count" (`24:69`)
   and Eli calling one of his own finds a cheat (`24:40`, `24:78`). The sheet's reading of
   the beat is right; the quotation and the line number are not. Prefer the manuscript.

6. **Chapter 25's date header.** The chapter is headed *November 2023* and opens with the
   bus arriving "the first week of August." The November date matches the range scene at
   `25:59` ("In month four"), so the header is describing the chapter's destination rather
   than its start. Consistent with `chronology/chapter_dates.json`; noted only because it
   reads as an error on a first pass.
