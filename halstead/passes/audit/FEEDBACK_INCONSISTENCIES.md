**A15 against A2, A12, A13 and A14 — four separate ways.**

- **A15 vs A14.** `30_cleared.md` says in terms that the twenty-first birthday is in August,

**A15 cannot be rescued by moving the birthday.** A2 (`"Six."` in July 2012) and A3

sentence, or to move chapter 33 — and chapter 33 is pinned to May 2026 by its own "four
months into that job."

# 3. The pairing-code arithmetic in chapter 15

**Claim:** "a six-digit pairing code … a few billion codes." Six digits is one million.

**VERDICT: STILL LIVE.**

**Current text, `15_twelve.md`, unchanged:**

> In under thirty seconds, Kavi is on their comms. "It's AES-256," he says. "But, it's a
> six-digit pairing code and they never changed it off default." He's already got the
> campus cluster chewing on it. "**You just do a few billion codes** and see which one
> gives you something that isn't noise. It's four seconds of compute. It's not even
> interesting."

Callback later in the same chapter, unchanged and unaffected by any fix: `"Four seconds of
compute."` / `"Four seconds," Sam says, like it offends him.`

Two problems, not one. The arithmetic is off by three orders of magnitude — and **"a few
billion" inverts the line's meaning.** Kavi's register here is contempt; the point is that
the search space is contemptibly small. "A few billion" is a boast. A million is the sneer
the scene wants.

**The deniability premise is already carried and needs nothing new.** Ruth, four lines
later: *"They're almost certainly actors, look at this. This is a consumer handset with a
default code. My dad's work has better than this and my dad sells insurance."* And Chloe's
paragraph at the end of the chapter already plants the real reading: *"All of it wrong in
the same direction, which is the direction a school would get it wrong in if a school were
building this, and also the direction four real people would get it wrong in if somebody
had handed them the wrong list."* Federal operators on unattributable civilian kit is
exactly what that describes. Do not add a line explaining it.

**Fix, in order of preference:**

| | Sentence | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **a** | "You just do the **whole keyspace** and see which one gives you something that isn't noise." | **Best.** No number to get wrong, and "keyspace" is the word the best cryptographer in the building would actually use. Keeps the rhythm and the contempt. |
| b | "You just do **all million** codes and see which one gives you something that isn't noise." | Correct, concrete, and "all million" carries the sneer. Use this if the author wants the figure said out loud. |
| c | Lengthen the code so "a few billion" is right | **Rejected.** Needs ten digits. Consumer radios use four to six, and the whole joke is that it is a factory default. |

**Optional half-sentence in the same line, if it is being touched anyway:** "never changed
it off default" and "do the whole keyspace" sit slightly oddly together — if he knows the
default, he does not sweep. *"But the key comes off a six-digit pairing code and they
never changed it off default"* removes the wobble by making the point that the AES-256 key
is only ever as large as the PIN behind it, which is the technically correct and more
damning version.

"Four seconds of compute" is fine under (a) or (b) and needs no change.

This narrows the earlier audit's A3, which recommended "all million"; (a) is better because
the line's job is contempt, not arithmetic, and a stated number invites the reader to check
it a second time.

# 10. Owen's departure  *(§III)*

**Claim:** Sam says Owen left because he wasn't having fun; Kavi says he wanted to stay and
was keeping up. The reviewer judges this intentional ambiguity.

**VERDICT: REVIEWER RIGHT on the reasons — it is deliberate, and better built than the

**On the reasons — deliberate, and the text marks it three ways.**

`05_behind.md`, Sam, secondhand and incurious:

> "He went home, his mom came and got him yesterday, before dinner," Sam says from the
> doorway, already moving through it.
>
> "He wasn't having fun, and that's the whole reason, they'll let you go home for that."
> … "He said so, straight out, that he wasn't having fun, so he left, and that's the whole
> story."

`10_april.md`, Kavi, flagged as eyewitness in the line itself:

> "Owen wanted to stay, and **before you ask, I am going off something I saw with my own
> eyes.** I know you're about to ask me how I know." … "And he was keeping up, if that's
> the next one, because he was doing exactly what the rest of us were doing, the water
> thing and the bridge and all of it, and doing all of it fine."

And `10_april.md` gives the institutional third version, from the writing teacher, which is
the frame that reconciles them: "People do go home sometimes, because their family decides
they want them home." Sam repeats the stated reason; Kavi saw the real one; the school has
a policy sentence for it. Chloe spending two chapters unable to make the accounts sit
together is the scene's actual subject. **No change. Do not resolve it.**

**The live problem the reviewer did not see: the two accounts give different days, and
different places, for the same events.**

- `05_behind.md`: "Owen leaves **in the second week**, and the way Chloe finds out is that
  he is in her hall on the **Monday** and by **Wednesday** his door stands open" → and
  Sam, on that Wednesday: "his mom came and got him **yesterday**" = **Tuesday**.
- `10_april.md`, Kavi: "He was crying by the kitchens on the **Thursday** … and then on the
  **Saturday** his mom's car was outside and he was in the back of it."

Tuesday against Saturday. And the crying is in two places: Ruth, in `05_behind.md`, "He was
crying **in the stairwell**, on Thursday"; Kavi, "crying **by the kitchens** on the
Thursday."

This is a different class from the reason-ambiguity. Interpretations can differ; the day a
car was outside cannot. And the departure day is not something either boy is characterised
as guessing at — Sam gives a flat "yesterday," Kavi is explicitly the eyewitness.

**Fix, cheapest first:**

| | Fix | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **a** | In `10_april.md`, drop Kavi's weekday: "…and then his mom's car was outside and he was in the back of it." | 1 clause, 1 chapter. Removes the collision, keeps the eyewitness force, and Kavi's "Thursday" for the crying still matches Ruth's. |
| b | In `05_behind.md`, change Sam's "yesterday" to something vaguer — "his mom came and got him" | 1 clause. Also works, but "yesterday, before dinner" is a good Sam detail (precise about the logistics, blank about the person). |
| c | Reconcile the stairwell and the kitchens too | Optional. Two sightings on the same Thursday is a legitimate reading and needs nothing; if the author wants them identical, make Kavi's "by the kitchens" into "in the stairwell," since Ruth says it first and to Chloe's face. |

**Recommendation: (a), and leave the stairwell/kitchens alone.** Supersedes the earlier
audit, which did not surface this.
