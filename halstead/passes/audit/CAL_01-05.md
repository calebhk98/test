## 1. Spock, Sheldon, AI, mini-adult

Nothing in the four named failure modes appears in any character's *behaviour*. What does
appear, in five places, is a narrating voice that reaches above a six-year-old's register
while reporting a six-year-old's interior. Chloe's own sheet already flags this class of
thing (Known problems #5, "Flagged for the author's judgment; no specific rewrite
proposed"), so I have not proposed rewrites either. Listed in descending order of how much
they read as the author thinking rather than the child.

**1.1 (the one real finding, and it sits in question 3 as well).** `02_march_4th.md:161-197`.
The narration establishes at :161 that "Only pieces of it reach her ... until a piece of it
comes through clean, because he has turned toward the door," and from :163 onward the
Prentice-and-Meg conversation is rendered in full, with Chloe's reactions interleaved at
:169, :177 and :197. That means she hears, in plain unhedged English, `:175`:

> "The talking-and-reasoning part of this, she's at the top of it, and the speed part,
> she's ordinary, so when you average those together you get a number that says bright kid,
> and bright kid undersells the room I sat in this morning."

She is given no beat that absorbs it, misreads it, or explains it away. Her next reaction,
`:177`, is body only: "Chloe shifts on the chair, her feet nowhere near the floor." This is
the standard's section 2 and its point 3 at the same time: a child who has just been told
she is at the top of something, in her hearing, and who neither knows it afterward nor
rationalises it.

Two things argue the other way and I want them on the record, because the scene is close to
working. First, what she demonstrably retains is the one word she did not understand,
"percentile," and when her mother defines it she immediately files it as an ordinary thing
everybody has: "So what was mine, then, if everybody gets one?" (`:223`). That is a
rationalisation, and a good one. Second, `:171` gives her the deficit half of the same
speech ("she's slower than most kids her age, and the biggest one is copying"), which she
does not react to either, so the non-reaction is at least even-handed.

The gap is specifically the clause "she's at the top of it." Everything else Prentice says
in that room is either technical (composite, averages, label) or about her slowness. That
one clause is six-year-old English and it lands unopposed. If the author wants it fixed,
the smallest available fix is to put the muffling where it does work: let :161's "words
bigger than her" cover the composite-score speech and bring the clean stretch back for
"there's nothing wrong with her" / "she's completely fine" at :165-167, which is the part
she would actually be listening for. I have not done this because it moves the parents'
key exposition and that is the author's call.

The technique the chapter needs already exists one chapter earlier, done correctly. See
"What is working," item A.

**1.2** `01_before.md:203`: "Chloe turns that one over while he waits, because the real
answer has two halves and the question was built to hold one." "The question was built to
hold one" is an adult's formulation of a child's hesitation. The line below it, `:205`,
then states both halves plainly and does the whole job, which means :203's causal clause is
also an HR1 case by the strike-and-read test: strike it and nothing is lost, because the
proof is the next sentence. Reported rather than cut per Known problems #5.

**1.3** `02_march_4th.md:47`: "Chloe looks at him hard, the way you look at somebody before
you decide whether to believe them, and files the last part with everything else adults say
when they want you to sit still." Chloe's sheet cites this beat in its old form ("Chloe
doesn't believe the last part. Everybody says that") as an age-6 line carrying age-16
polish. It has evidently been rewritten since; the polish is still there, now in the verb
"files" and in "everything else adults say when they want you to sit still," which is a
categorised generalisation about adults rather than a six-year-old's suspicion of one man.
The *content* is right for her (she sorts adults by whether they answer straight, and he has
not yet). Only the register is above her.

**1.4** `02_march_4th.md:147`: "Chloe opens her mouth to ask whether she did good, then
presses her lips together, because that question is off limits to children, so she asks the
one underneath it instead." Two narrator glosses stacked: a rule stated on her behalf ("off
limits to children") and then the subtext flagged ("the one underneath it"). The question
she actually asks, `:149` "The one with the symbols, does everybody finish it?", is one of
the best-calibrated lines in the five chapters and needs no introduction at all.

**1.5** `05_behind.md:97`: "the question turns over into a different one: dividing by an
eighth is asking how many eighths fit inside the thing you started with, which is the whole
of it and all it ever was." The insight is correctly hers; the cadence of "which is the
whole of it and all it ever was" is the author's. Note that the scene rescues itself
immediately at `:99` with "'Oh,' she says, out loud, in the dark, at eleven at night,"
which is exactly the private, un-triumphant register her sheet asks for. I would leave this
one alone even if I were rewriting.

**Not a finding, examined and cleared.** `02_march_4th.md:33`, the NO CELL PHONES joke. The
premise involves people who cannot read a sign, which is the nearest any Chloe line in these
chapters comes to a contemptuous frame. It clears because it is a closed loop she finds
funny rather than a judgment about anyone in the room, and because the beat that follows is
purely six ("It is still funny a minute later, when she is looking at something else").
Flagging only so the author knows it was looked at.

`01_before.md:199` ("if I already know how it works, doing it thirty more times just proves
the same thing again") is crisper than most six-year-olds, but it is her own lived
experience being described to an adult who has just waited for her, which the sheet says is
exactly when she gives the full version. Cleared.

## 2. A child knowing they are exceptional, comparing favourably, being modest, or treating a normal-for-them feat as a feat

**No instances.** This is the strongest part of the five chapters and the audit found
nothing to change. Searched for it directly: the words smart, clever, gifted, genius,
brilliant, advanced, talent, prodigy, smarter, faster than, better than appear nowhere in
Chloe's mouth or in narration about her across all five chapters. The only "gifted" in the
band is Prentice's, to Meg, at `02_march_4th.md:189`.

Two near-misses, both of which I judge to clear, quoted so the author can check my
reasoning:

**2.1** `05_behind.md:25`: "while Chloe sits with her six, which would have been the longest
answer in Mrs. Aldana's room by half a page." This is a favourable comparison on its face.
It clears because of where the sentence ends: "and that meant the whole class listening to
it read out on a Friday afternoon and then guessing, correctly, whose it was." The
comparison is not scored as standing, it is scored as exposure. The same page has already
put her fourth of four for output (`:13`: a page and a half from Ruth, most of a page from
Kavi, six sentences from her, two from Owen), so the local frame is that she is the slow
one. Working as designed.

**2.2** `01_before.md:227`, to her cousin Dana: "there's a boy in my class who's on chapter
four of the sled dog book, and I already finished the whole thing." A direct comparison, and
she draws nothing from it: the sentence's actual destination is the bathroom floor and
having to know if the dog lived, and two turns later the same conversational thread arrives
at a complaint about the library limit (`:235`, "so I run out fast"). She reports the fact
and wants the thing she cannot get. No conclusion about herself anywhere in it.

**Supporting cast.** Ruth, Sam and Kavi are clean. Ruth wants credit for having been
*right*, never for being able (`04_pluto.md:237` "I told you ... I said that on Friday";
`05_behind.md:49` "Triangles hold and squares fold, that's the whole of it"), and both times
the text scores the social cost against her, not the ability in her favour. Kavi's
`05_behind.md:69` "says he already knew it, out of a book" is flat report with no claim
attached. Sam talks his own record *down* at `05_behind.md:47` ("then that he would have
written a lower number if anybody had let him"), which is his sheet's at-length trigger
appearing correctly at seven and a half. His only boast in the band is about swimming two
lengths (`:105`, `:113`), which is ordinary child competitiveness inside the peer group and
not about ability.

**The adults hold the line too**, which `characters/_CALIBRATION.md` says is the mechanism.
Mr. Doyle takes a correction from a six-year-old with "Mass, and thank you for catching
that" and keeps going (`04_pluto.md:235`). Mr. Baptiste answers Chloe's "why do you flip
it" with "Good, that's the question" (`05_behind.md:65`) and Ruth's divide-by-zero with
"Good, watch" (`:87`), praising the question as the expected thing and never the child.
Best of all, `05_behind.md:69-71` gives the identical phrase to Kavi, who is ahead, and Owen,
who is lost: both are "told that this is fine." Nothing is remarked on as impressive
anywhere in the camp chapters.

## 3. Contrary evidence that goes unrationalised

**3.1** `02_march_4th.md:175`. The single finding, set out in full at 1.1 above.

**3.2, examined and cleared.** `05_behind.md:119-161`, Owen leaving. Chloe is handed direct
evidence that the thing she finds effortless and delightful is unbearable to somebody else,
and she does not explain it away. Under a strict reading that is unrationalised contrary
evidence. I do not think it is a defect, for two reasons. First, the evidence is about
*Owen*, not about her standing, so there is nothing about herself for her to rationalise.
Second, the not-resolving is the point and is dramatised as a failure of hers rather than an
insight: "the bridge was fun, and Owen must not have liked it, which sit crosswise, and
neither will move over for the other. She starts from the beginning and checks them a third
time ... trying to work out how a person could stand in front of a bridge and want to be
somewhere else" (`:161`). This is the sheet's "confused by them, never contemptuous," and
it is the best single execution of that instruction in the band. Note also that the scene
gives Owen his own visible difficulty earlier (`05_behind.md:13`, two sentences with one
crossed out; `:71`, losing the thread of the fractions) and Chloe never once reads it as
slowness.

**3.3, cleared.** `01_before.md:279`: "Probably not, because they're still on chapter four,
and if somebody else was ahead she'd have taken their book as well, but she only took mine."
She is handed the fact that she is the only one ahead and converts it, immediately and
entirely, into evidence about the teacher's fairness rather than about herself. That is
rationalisation working exactly as the standard describes.

## 4. Scale announced rather than buried

No totals of achievement anywhere in the band, and the two lists in it are correctly
buried. What I did find is three places where the *narrator* points at something the scene
already carried. None of them totals an achievement; all three are HR1-shaped rather than
calibration-shaped, so they are reported here at low weight.

**4.1** `02_march_4th.md:241`: "What she works out that same month is bigger than the pencil
trick: finish first, sit still, keep your answers short, and the room leaves you alone, the
teacher included." "Is bigger than the pencil trick" is the narrator ranking her own
discoveries for the reader's benefit. The strike test: remove the four words and the
sentence reads "What she works out that same month: finish first, sit still ..." and nothing
is lost, because the two following lines prove the size. This is the closest thing in the
band to an edit I would have made; I did not make it because it is a prose-repair call on a
sentence that is otherwise the load-bearing statement of her survival economy, and the brief
says report when in doubt.

**4.2** `02_march_4th.md:243`, immediately after: "and all she knows about it is that being
quiet is what makes her disappear." A second gloss stacked on the first, naming the meaning
of the thing just named. Same test, same result.

**4.3** `01_before.md:41`: "Reading works a different way entirely, and the difference shows
up wherever it is her own idea rather than an assignment." This introduces the reading list
at `:43` by explaining what the list is going to demonstrate. The list itself is a model
execution of the standard's section 3 (cereal box both sides, a contest that ended in a
different year, road signs, the church bulletin, four books at a time, and then `:61` "which
are gone by Wednesday") and needs no introduction; the sentence above it is the narrator
making sure the reader gets it.

**4.4, minor.** `01_before.md:271`: "She gets in trouble for reading, in reading." An
epigram restating the scene that just ran. It is a good line and I would expect the author
to keep it; noting it only because it is the one sentence in chapter one that exists to be
noticed.

**4.5, cleared.** `05_behind.md:209`, the chapter's last line: "and the sentence comes back
clean, as do the next nine after it." A counted total, and the only one in the band. It
clears because what is being counted is her own checking rather than her output, so the
number measures how bruised she still is, not how much she has done.

**Cleared, and worth the author knowing it was checked.** `05_behind.md:45-55`, the bridge
predictions. Chloe writes fourteen, the bridge holds eleven, and the narration says "wrong by
three pounds" (`:55`) without ever noting that this is the closest prediction stated in the
room. Sam's twenty-five against eleven is on the page for a reader who wants to do the
arithmetic. That is exactly the skim-undercounts, careful-reader-finds-it design and it is
handled without a word of pointing.

Similarly `02_march_4th.md:93`: she holds eight digits forward and six backward "without any
idea whether any of it is a lot, since this is the first time anybody has ever said a number
of numbers to her." The number is on the page, the assessment of it is refused, and the
refusal is her ignorance rather than the narrator's coyness. Model handling.

## What is working

Briefly, so the author can see the audit discriminates.

**A. `01_before.md:161-183` is the model for handling praise in Chloe's hearing.** Mrs.
Aldana's "one of the strongest readers she's had, and she said it twice" is read out to her
in the car, and it slides straight off: her mother pivots to the criticism inside one
sentence ("which is the part I want to talk about, since the reading part is already fine"),
and Chloe's own next thought is not the reading at all but a private confirmation that the
complaint is true: "The parking lot, the boxes, the question that had already happened
without her: all of it is true, and Chloe watches the streetlights come across the ceiling
instead of saying so." Praise arrives, is not registered, and the deficit is accepted
without argument. That is the standard's section 2 executed in three lines. It is also the
technique `02_march_4th.md:175` needs and does not use, which is what makes 1.1 a finding
rather than a matter of taste.

**B. The camp calibration switch is slow, and it is about safety rather than standing.**
This is the thing the brief asked me to watch and it is right. The arc, in order:
`04_pluto.md:243-251`, Ruth corrects a teacher, Chloe watches all day for the punishment she
knows must be coming, nothing comes, and she responds by getting *more* careful, not less
("she keeps both hands flat on the desk through the next class, and the one after that");
`05_behind.md:9`, "she cannot stop checking where everybody else has got to on the page";
`:25`, Mrs. Aldana's room still live in her head in week one; `:89`, ears hot for an hour in
week two; `:143-145`, the actual mechanism, where Sam's ordinary school turns out to be the
same as hers ("mine too, same paper till everybody's got it") so the difference is located
in the room and not in her; and only then `:207-209`, week three, where she runs the check
out of habit and it comes back clean. Four weeks, five graded steps, and at no point does
she conclude anything about her own ability. What relaxes is the flinch, not her self-
estimate. If anything the risk here is the opposite of the one the brief anticipated: the
switch is slower than it needed to be, and it works.

**C. She is at the bottom of the room and the text says so plainly.**
`05_behind.md:5`, "Everything here moves at the speed of whoever finishes first, and in the
first week that person is Ruth"; `:13`, fourth of four on output; `:41`, still working out
whether a diagonal is the whole argument by the time Sam has glued the first joint; `:105`,
arrived unable to swim and put in the shallow end; `:61`, eight problems right and the
reason "stays shut to her," with the gap "making her furious in a way she can feel along her
jaw." That last is her sheet's needing-the-mechanism trait, with the body reaction, in one
sentence.

**D. The compression is written as damage in progress, not as policy.** `01_before.md:131`
"Yeah," after "you talk weird"; `:133` her hand staying flat on the desk for the rest of the
morning; `:135` working out in the cafeteria "which part was the wrong part, because it is
either the Icarus or the saying it a second time louder," which is a child blaming her own
delivery; `:141` taking the clouds sentence down to one word while looking at the back of
Kayleigh's head; then `04_pluto.md:179`, "Chloe has a whole sentence about the lid ready,
four words, five, and she lets it go," which carries the whole injury into the camp without a
word of explanation. The cause is always a specific person in a specific room, never a
setting she switches on.

**E. Chapter three's Chloe beats are the best-aged writing in the band**, and chapter four's
first day is the best argument in the manuscript that she is not an alien: the noise, the
license plates, the kid who looks at her shoes too long, half a step in behind her dad, and
then the phone.

**F. The invented images are all hers, said once, and never explained.** Icarus
(`01_before.md:121-125`), clouds as the ocean going somewhere else (`01_before.md:141`,
`:227`), the one that isn't a planet anymore (`04_pluto.md:107`), and the moon as a liar
(`05_behind.md:167`). The "If you..." signature also appears intact at six, at
`05_behind.md:179`.
