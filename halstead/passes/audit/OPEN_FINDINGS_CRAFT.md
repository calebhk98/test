## Top ten, ordered by how much fixing each would improve the book

1. **Chloe's clearance generates zero friction (item 11).** The single most exposed unpaid spend in the book, in a book that otherwise makes every character pay for their exposure. One sentence in ch36 fixes it.
2. **Nobody comes back for Priya's two prisoners (item 13).** Same technique the book uses everywhere else (an absence, noticed, not explained) — one line, already in the character's idiom.
3. **The "forty targets" math error (item 1).** A one-word fix in a book whose readers are exactly the kind who will do the multiplication. Cheap, clean, and currently wrong three times in the same chapter including the title's own number.
4. **Ch30's Foreign Service job never named as something other than the appointment (item 9).** The one place in the book where a character's careful research turns out not to have mattered — unless one clause resolves it. Needs an author decision, not a mechanical fix.
5. **`both hands` at 37 uses, unaddressed while every sibling gesture was fixed (item 17).** The cheapest big-visibility fix left: the rule that solved this problem everywhere else in the book has simply not been applied here yet.
6. **Ch34's internal contradiction: "case number opened in 2013" and "nineteen years of updates" in the same chapter (item 4).** A fresh find, six lines apart, in a document the book otherwise treats as internally rigorous.
