## If the author only does five

**1) The clearance-friction fix (C1)** — one sentence, the single most corroborated finding in the whole round, and it fixes the book's least-believable moment at zero structural cost. **2) The chapter 34 same-chapter contradiction (A1)** and **3) the Forty Targets math error (A2)** — both one-word fixes that a careful reader will catch immediately, sitting right next to each other in cheapness and visibility. **4) The Foreign Service timeline ruling (B1)** — not because it's expensive, but because it's the one place in the book where research a character did on the page turns out not to have mattered unless the author says which reading is intended, and the fix is one clause once he decides. **5) Giving Ruth her own line back in chapter 18 (C2)** — the cheapest, most on-the-record craft fix on the list, and a repeated pattern (the narrator finishing a character's thought for her) worth breaking here before it recurs elsewhere. Together these five cost less than an afternoon and resolve the two things most likely to make a careful reader stop trusting the book: an unbelievable ending with no visible cost, and math that doesn't check out.
