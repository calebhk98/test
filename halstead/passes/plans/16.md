# Plan: chapters/16_thirteen.md

## Baseline measurement (python3 prose_grade.py chapters/16_thirteen.md)

maturity percentile: 41 (median of 16), lost to benchmark on 8 of 16, strongest
chapter in 11-20.

Three worst measures, confirmed by the grader's own "fix first, by size of the
gap" ranking (in corpus standard deviations):

1. sentence length variation (CV %) — 71.60 vs bench 82.54, 4th pct, 1.2 sd
2. commas per sentence — 0.68 vs bench 1.17, 17th pct, 1.1 sd
3. sentences of 20-35 words % — 12.57 vs bench 18.00, 17th pct, 0.9 sd
   (next-worst, subordinate clause %, is 0.8 sd — confirmed this is really
   third, not fourth)

These three are one finding, per the task: a narrow band of sentence lengths
and too few commas inside them. The chapter has almost no 20-35-word
sentences and needs a handful of long, properly-joined ones, not a general
lengthening.

## Measures already above the 50th percentile — protect these, do not chase anything that would move them backward

- sentences with a relative clause % — 23.04 vs 22.81, 78th pct (winning)
- sentences inside a run of 3+ short ones % — 13.61 vs 15.08, 70th pct (fewer
  short-sentence runs than the benchmark already — the highest-leverage lever
  in RAISING_THE_LEVEL.md is already applied here; do not go hunting for more
  runs to break, there aren't enough left to matter)
- sentences under 10 words % — 40.84 vs 42.67, 70th pct (lower is more
  mature for this measure; the chapter already beats the benchmark)
- mean word length (characters) — 4.10 vs 4.06, 57th pct
- words of 7+ characters % — 13.28 vs 12.74, 52nd pct

None of the planned edits below touch vocabulary (word length / long-word
share) or hunt for more short-sentence runs to break — there is no run left
worth breaking. The edits work entirely by joining existing narration
sentences that are already medium-length (8-19 words) into single long ones,
which pulls sentences *out* of the under-10 and run-of-3 buckets, so those
two winning measures should if anything improve slightly as a side effect,
not weaken.

## Also true from the full read

- "and" is already over its target share (3.94% vs 2.5-3.5%, style_report
  FAIL) and "but" is under its floor (0.23% vs 0.4-0.9%, style_report FAIL).
  So the joins below are built with subordinators (when, though, while),
  a semicolon once, or "but" — never a new "and". Two edits (M1, M2) actually
  remove an existing "and" by turning a coordinate clause into a participle
  or an appositive.
- Front-loading is capped at 3.3% of sentences and this chapter sits at
  0.52% (1 sentence), far under. One edit (M1) opens on "When," which is
  the only front-loaded construction added, well inside the cap.
- No sentence targeted below is a character's dialogue. Every one is
  narration, so none of the character-sheet "when they talk at length"
  limits apply. Dialogue in this chapter (Marek, Ruth, Kavi, Eli, Nadia,
  Amberg, Dave) is left untouched.
- Sentences deliberately not touched because they are doing work as short
  devices, per PROSE_RULES and RAISING_THE_LEVEL ("short sentences held
  back until the end of a paragraph, arriving after real setup"; "a short
  sentence after three long ones is a device"): "Nobody teaches the
  thirteens." / "Marek hands nothing in." / "Two more turn up, work,
  leave." / "Somebody stopped and never said so." / "She does the cuts."
  / "Then she goes back inside." / "Chloe." These stay exactly as written.

## The edits

Eight joins, all narration, spread across eight different paragraphs so no
single passage is over-worked. Each takes two existing, already-written
sentences (8-19 words each) and joins them into one 20-31 word sentence with
a real subordinator or "but", carrying more information per sentence, not
new information.

**M1 — Marek section.** Moves: 20-35 band, commas, CV; removes an "and".

> Now: "She asks him four questions off the sheet. He answers all four, and
> the fourth he answers by a route she had not considered."
>
> Becomes: "When she asks him four questions off the sheet, he answers all
> four, the fourth by a route she had not considered."

**M2 — the labs paragraph.** Moves: 20-35 band, commas (four in one
sentence), CV; removes an "and" (the coordinate "and hers does materials"
becomes the participle "hers doing materials").

> Now: "Eleven labs split the year between them and hers does materials.
> What the youngest person in a lab does is fetch, clean, and take on the
> jobs nobody senior wants."
>
> Becomes: "Eleven labs split the year between them, hers doing materials,
> where the youngest person fetches, cleans, and takes on the jobs nobody
> senior wants."

**M3 — the Sanders instrument-log paragraph.** Moves: 20-35 band, commas,
CV. Uses a semicolon (already used elsewhere in the manuscript, e.g.
chapters/13, 15) rather than a new "and"; keeps the existing "but".

> Now: "The logs hold four thousand runs, but the paper reports eleven
> hundred. Neither document says which eleven hundred, or which runs were
> dropped, or when, or by whom."
>
> Becomes: "The logs hold four thousand runs, but the paper reports eleven
> hundred; neither document says which eleven hundred, or which runs were
> dropped, or when, or by whom."

**M4 — the two bridge reports.** Moves: 20-35 band, commas, CV. "it" and
"him" replace repeated "the commission" / "the contractor" — same fact,
less repetition, not a new claim.

> Now: "The English one says the commission found a design fault in the
> bearings. The Russian one says the commission found overloading by a
> contractor, and names the contractor."
>
> Becomes: "The English one says the commission found a design fault in the
> bearings, though the Russian one says it found overloading by a
> contractor and names him."

**M5 — the dinner-table regulars.** Moves: 20-35 band, commas (up to seven
in one sentence), CV. No new "and" — the three "and"s already existed.

> Now: "Sam, Ruth, Kavi, Priya, Odile, Eli and Nadia are always there. Four
> or five others come and go, and Chloe knows all their names."
>
> Becomes: "Sam, Ruth, Kavi, Priya, Odile, Eli and Nadia are always there,
> while four or five others come and go, and Chloe knows all their names."

**M6 — Nadia paragraph closer.** Moves: 20-35 band, commas, CV.

> Now: "They work the November cipher set together and it goes well. Sam
> sits between them most nights, and neither of them has ever said a word
> to him about it."
>
> Becomes: "They work the November cipher set together and it goes well,
> though Sam sits between them most nights, and neither of them has ever
> said a word to him about it."

**M7 — Chloe and Marek at the door.** Moves: 20-35 band, commas, CV. Puts
her frustration and his patience in the same beat instead of two flat
statements next to each other.

> Now: "Chloe puts the chalk down on the ledge harder than she means to.
> He waits by the door until she says fine, then goes."
>
> Becomes: "Chloe puts the chalk down on the ledge harder than she means
> to, while he waits by the door until she says fine, then goes."

**M8 — the 1981 paper offer.** Moves: 20-35 band, commas, CV, "but" share
(currently under its floor).

> Now: "In February she offers to read the 1981 paper and mark him on
> that. He says no, politely, without pausing."
>
> Becomes: "In February she offers to read the 1981 paper and mark him on
> that, but he says no, politely, without pausing."

## What was cut from consideration, and why

- Merging the two "English one / Russian one" sentences with "and" instead
  of "though" — rejected, "and" is already over target.
- Combining the Ivy/Tomas and Beatriz sentences in the "six of the seven"
  paragraph — rejected, they are about two different students; joining them
  with a subordinator would imply a causal or contrasting relationship
  between the two that isn't there.
- Any edit inside Amberg's, Marek's, Ruth's, Kavi's, Eli's, or Nadia's
  dialogue — rejected outright. This is a sentence-level narration pass;
  dialogue length is governed by the character sheets, and none of these
  scenes are the licensed "talks at length" circumstance for the character
  sheet in question, so their lines are copied verbatim.
- A general pass lengthening every short narration sentence in the chapter —
  rejected per the brief's own warning: the chapter's short sentences are
  mostly load-bearing devices (see the "not touched" list above), and the
  run-of-3+-short-sentences measure is already winning, so hunting for more
  of them to break would cost a measure the chapter doesn't need help on.
- Chasing lexical diversity — not attempted. RAISING_THE_LEVEL.md is
  explicit that this is a drafting property a line edit won't move, and the
  chapter shouldn't spend budget on it.

## Expected effect

Eight merges reduce the total sentence count from 191 to 183 and add roughly
eight sentences into the 20-35 word band (24 → ~32, taking the share from
12.57% toward the high teens), each carrying at least one new comma from the
join (raising commas per sentence), and each replacing two mid-length
sentences with one long one next to the chapter's existing short ones,
which widens the spread of sentence lengths and should raise CV. None of the
eight touches vocabulary, dialogue, or a short sentence doing work as a
device, so the five already-winning measures should hold or improve
slightly rather than move backward.
