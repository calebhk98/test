# Reading-level plan: chapter 11 (`chapters/11_eight.md`)

## Measured baseline (prose_grade.py / style_report.py)

maturity percentile: 35th (median of 16). Three worst:

- sentence length variation (CV %): 66.17 vs benchmark 82.54, 4th percentile
- words from the commonest 100 %: 49.94 vs benchmark 49.35, 9th percentile
- sentences with a subordinate clause %: 18.31 vs benchmark 22.24, 26th percentile

Protected (already winning, must not cost):

- sentences inside a run of 3+ short ones %: 7.98, 83rd percentile
- sentences of 20-35 words %: 20.19, 78th percentile
- sentences under 10 words %: 38.03, 70th percentile

## Diagnosis

The chapter is not choppy. It has almost no short runs and a healthy share of
sentences under 10 words already. The CV problem is uniformity in the middle:
several places in the chapter carry three, four, even five adjacent narration
sentences all sitting in the 12-25 word band, doing separate small jobs each
in its own sentence, none of them short, none of them long. The fix is not to
touch the short sentences (which are doing real work — beats, punchlines,
scene turns) and not to touch dialogue (character sheets for Chloe, Dave,
Ruth, Sam, Kavi, Bell, Deb, and Dorothy do not license longer speech in this
chapter's circumstances — none of the licensed "talks at length" triggers
occur here: no camp-decision argument for Dave, no forced re-derivation for
Ruth, no wrongly-flattering assessment for Sam, no misstated mechanism for
Kavi, no changing-danger lesson for Bell). It is to find pairs of adjacent
*narration* sentences in those uniform runs that are doing two related jobs,
and join them with a subordinator into one properly long sentence, leaving
every sentence around them untouched.

Preferred subordinators are ones outside the commonest-100 list — `while`,
`although`, `since`, `until`, `before` — rather than `because`/`which`/`that`/
`when`, which are already in the top 100 and would not help the second
target. All joins land mid-sentence, never sentence-initial, so none of them
touch the front-loading ceiling (currently 0.94%, capped at 3.3%).

## Planned changes

1. **Para at line 11** (Chloe's talkative summer, five sentences at 20/16/12/15/20
   words — the most uniform run in the chapter).
   Now: `She talks the entire summer, and she comes downstairs already talking
   most mornings, about something she was thinking about upstairs. She has
   opinions about the news, which she gets off the paper on the kitchen
   table.`
   Becomes: `She talks the entire summer, and she comes downstairs already
   talking most mornings, about something she was thinking about upstairs,
   since she has opinions about the news, which she gets off the paper on
   the kitchen table.`
   Moves: CV (removes one of two identical-length-band sentences, builds one
   37-word sentence), subordinate clause % (+1 "since" clause), commonest-100
   (adds a non-top-100 connector without adding a top-100 word).

2. **Para at line 111** (Fen/quartz).

   corridors all year, and once in November Fen shows her a piece of quartz

   they say hello in corridors all year, once in November Fen shows her a
   piece of quartz the size of a fist.`

## What was cut from consideration

- Any merge that would touch a sentence under 10 words, or a run of 3+ short
  ones (there are almost none in this chapter — correctly, per the brief,
  nothing to hunt for).
- Any merge inside dialogue. No character sheet's "when they talk at length"
  circumstance is in play in this chapter, so no character's lines are
  lengthened.
- Merges that would force a causal/temporal relationship the two sentences
  don't actually have (e.g. the apple-throwing beat, the wood-shop beat) —
  several candidate pairs were rejected because the join would be synthetic,
  which the brief and PROSE_RULES both rule out as padding.
- Sentence-opening ("front-loaded") subordination as the mechanism — all nine
  joins land mid-sentence so the front-load rate (0.94%, cap 3.3%) is not
  touched.
- Chasing lexical diversity (sTTR) directly — RAISING_THE_LEVEL.md says this
  is a drafting property a line pass won't move, and it isn't one of the
  three worst measures here regardless.
- Paragraph-joining (lever 2). Sentences-per-paragraph (26th percentile) and
  words-per-paragraph (48th percentile) aren't among the three worst
  measures for this chapter, and none of the nine joins above cross a
  paragraph boundary, so paragraph structure is untouched by this pass.
