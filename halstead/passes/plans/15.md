## Method this round

Went through all 196 post-round-1 paragraphs in file order (full listing
generated with `paragraphs()`/`sents()`/`words()` from style_report.py) and
classified every one-sentence paragraph as device or habit:

- **Device, left alone:** every paragraph break between two *different*
  speakers' dialogue (standard turn-taking, not habit — Rule 14 says a
  paragraph break marking a real shift in speaker is correct), plus every
  paragraph explicitly protected below.
- **Habit, joined:** a one-sentence narration paragraph followed or
  preceded by dialogue from the *same* actor (a scene-opener into its own
  first line of dialogue, or a character's action into their own next
  line) — Rule 14's "an action and the dialogue it leads into are a single
  unit" — and a run of consecutive one-sentence narration paragraphs
  describing simultaneous parts of one moment (the post-fight restraint/
  equipment/costume beats).

Separately, re-scanned every paragraph in the file for a narration
sentence sitting between two quoted chunks (mid-dialogue action beat) that
had not yet been folded into its tag, using a regex sweep for `"..." X. "`
patterns, and folded every one that wasn't Sam's own protected speech.

## Why fewer than 80

45 changes, not 80-120. After the paragraph-join and tag-fold sweep, a
full-file check for remaining short-sentence runs and one-sentence
paragraphs showed the overwhelming majority of what's left is one of three
things this brief still bars from editing:

1. **Standard multi-speaker dialogue turn-taking.** A run like "They
   didn't go past the kitchens." / "They could have." / "They didn't." is
   three different speakers' turns, correctly one paragraph each — that
   is not habit paragraphing, it is the rule (Rule 14: a new paragraph
   marking a real shift in speaker is correct), and joining different
   speakers into one paragraph would blur attribution rather than fix a
   problem.
2. **Dialogue whose brevity is the character's own voice**, mainly Sam's
   ("one flat clause, said and done, with no subordinate clause") and
   Kavi's ("clipped by default, three words or fewer"). RAISING_THE_LEVEL
   says explicitly not to lengthen dialogue a character sheet doesn't
   license, and the spoken-sentence mean is already at 8.51 words,
   inside the 8-10 target — pushing it up further would fight the sheets
   for no benefit on a measure the chapter is no longer losing.
3. **The two Rule-7 fast-list documents** and Sam's two licensed
   at-length scenes, both explicitly out of scope.

Every remaining fold or join that didn't fall into one of those three
buckets is in the 45 above. Padding narration to manufacture more changes
(inventing new physical beats, restating facts) was avoidable and the
brief bars it directly ("Do not pad... Every added word must be a real
combination or a real subordination"), so the round stopped at the point
where the honest supply of that kind of change ran out rather than
continuing to a target count.

## Three targets

1. Break the runs of 3+ short sentences, prioritizing the ones that
   straddle narration and a quotation mark (per the brief, the
   highest-leverage lever in the book).
2. Join one-sentence narration paragraphs that belong to the same beat as
   the paragraph next to them (Rule 14), and fold short mid-dialogue
   action beats into the dialogue tag they belong to (Rule 7/18) instead
   of leaving them as their own sentence.
3. Add subordination (while, before, when, because, that/which) to
   single-clause sentences, mostly by merging two short sentences that
   are already adjacent rather than by inventing new content.
