## Method

The brief for this chapter is explicit: the combining is already done,
and everything landed at 14.7 words/sentence with too little spread. The
fix is not more merging in general, it is two or three of the sentences
that are *already* the biggest in the chapter (30-50 words) growing
further by absorbing one adjacent sentence that is outside the protected
20-35 band, so the merge costs nothing in the band we're protecting and
adds real spread. Everywhere else, the comma target is chased inside
existing sentences (fronted prepositional phrases, a moved parenthetical,
"and"-chains turned into subordinators) rather than by more merging, and
the word-length target is chased by naming a concrete noun where the
narration currently uses a filler one (a man / the thing / an adult /
of them), never by swapping a plain word for a longer synonym of itself.

All edits are narration. No line spoken by Bell, Kavi, Ruth, Sam, Odile,
Hearn, Sinclair, Meg, Dave, or the grandmother is touched, so no
character-sheet dial is at stake. Bell's one licensed long turn (When
they talk at length, BELL.md) and Odile's one licensed long turn (same
section, ODILE.md) are both quoted verbatim in this chapter and are left
completely alone, word for word.

## Considered and cut

- Renaming "the man" who runs wood shop. Chapter 11 already establishes
  "the man who runs it" as this character's fixed epithet across the
  book; changing it in this chapter alone would break consistency with
  a chapter outside this pass's scope, which is exactly the kind of
  invented fact the brief rules out. E15/E9 touch the sentence around
  him without touching the epithet itself.
- Reaching for "personnel" in place of "adults" in "Forty adults have to
  come through doors." Longer and would import a bureaucratic register
  the narrator doesn't use anywhere else. Cut; the brief's own warning
  against a fancier synonym for a plain word applies directly.
- Any word-level change inside a quoted line. Every character sheet
  read for this chapter (Bell, Kavi, Ruth, Sam, Odile, Hearn, Sinclair,
  Meg, Dave, grandmother) gates longer or different turns behind a named
  circumstance; none of the dialogue in this chapter needed to move for
  the three target measures, so none of it was touched.
- Merging any sentence inside one of the chapter's four existing runs of
  3+ short sentences ("You don't know you're doing German." / "Nobody's
  told you that." / "Nobody's told me I'm not, either."; "I took the
  inflation out." / "It's still nine times what it was." / "She has both
  elbows on the table."; "Not stupid." / "Everyone wants it." / "Kavi
  says." / "I'd swap you for juries."; "October." / "Her grandmother
  pats her on the arm." / "Well, you'll get there, dear."). These are
  exactly what the chapter is winning on at the 91st percentile; the
  brief says protect them and none is touched.
- A broader sweep of additional merges to push commas/sentence further.
  The brief is explicit that this chapter has already done the
  combining and the CV problem is under-spread, not under-merged;
  piling on more merges after E1-E3 and E7-E8 would start moving mass
  out of the 20-35 band and back toward the middle, which is the one
  thing the brief says not to do here.
