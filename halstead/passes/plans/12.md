# Reading-level plan: chapter 12 (`chapters/12_nine.md`)

## Measured baseline (prose_grade.py / style_report.py)

maturity percentile: 35th (median of 16). Three worst:

- sentence length variation (CV %): 68.77 vs benchmark 82.54, 4th percentile — worst by a distance
- words from the commonest 100 %: 49.77 vs benchmark 49.35, 9th percentile (lower is better)
- commas per sentence: 0.65 vs benchmark 1.17, 13th percentile

Protected (already winning, must not cost):

- sentences with a relative clause %: 24.10, 78th percentile
- sentences of 20-35 words %: 22.05, 78th percentile
- sentences inside a run of 3+ short ones %: 11.28, 74th percentile (this chapter
  is not choppy — breaking short runs is a weaker lever here than combining

## Diagnosis

This chapter's problem is not choppiness. It runs a recurring "subject is
Tuesdays" pattern for each school subject and a run of short scene-setting
beats for Sam's overhang and Priya's riding, and those short/medium
sentences are doing real work — they should stay untouched.

The actual defect, confirmed by walking every sentence of 14+ words: a large
number of them are single clauses stretched to 18-30 words with zero or one
comma — coordinate "and" chains standing in for structure ("Ruth starts
sitting with her on Sundays in December and is blunt about it..."), or plain
run-on facts with no internal break at all ("The sheet in her box on the
first morning has one more line on it than last year's did."). These read
flat at any length, which is exactly what is dragging CV down: the chapter's
long sentences are not much more complex than its short ones, so the
distribution is bunched instead of spread.

The fix used throughout: convert an "and"-joined compound predicate into a
comma + participial phrase (cuts "and," which is a top-100 word, and adds a
comma without adding words), front a prepositional or participial phrase
instead of opening on the subject every time (adds a comma, breaks the
subject-first drumbeat, stays out of the front-loading cap because it is not
a subordinator), and in the two places where two adjacent sentences describe
one continuous beat, merge them with a subordinator placed mid-sentence
(never sentence-initial, so front-loading stays at 0%, matching the chapter
11 agent's approach).

Cut from consideration: a second sentence-merge in the vendor paragraph
("On the Thursday he asks the vendor... / Two of them come back with
dates...") would have produced a 45-word sentence, pulling two sentences out
of the 20-35 word band the chapter already wins on for a single gain in the
over-35 band. Dropped — it would cost a protected measure to help an
unprotected one. Also cut: merging "Her father starts to answer and stops
with the page half turned." into the next sentence — the period after
"stops" is doing real work, enacting his hesitation typographically; folding
it into a longer sentence removes the beat the content is describing.
Also cut: breaking any of the 3+ short-sentence runs (Sam's overhang list,
the escalating first-aid paragraph) — the brief flags this as the chapter's
best-performing measure already, and these runs read as deliberate list/
escalation devices (Rule 7, Rule 13), not defects.

No dialogue is lengthened. Every dialogue edit below is a missing comma
before a coordinating "and" joining two independent clauses — a Rule 35
mechanical fix, zero words added, and it doesn't touch any character's
"When they talk at length" circumstance (none of those triggers occur in
this chapter: no wrong assessment for Sam to correct, no forced
re-derivation for Ruth, no misstated mechanism for Kavi, no camp-decision
argument for Dave, no complete-account moment for Meg).

## Planned changes

   than last year's did.`

   word count, same facts.
