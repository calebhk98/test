# Bex: the consequences pass

The behaviour (Bex takes credit from girls, gives boys their name back) is
being placed in chapters 12, 13, 14 and 16 by another pass and is taken here
as given, not re-implemented. This pass covers everything downstream of it in
the five chapters this pass owns: `chapters/19_sixteen.md`,
`chapters/21_the_applications.md`, `chapters/22_the_offer.md`,
`chapters/23_the_first_one.md`, `chapters/30_cleared.md`. Two of those,
23 and 30, were read in full against the brief and left untouched; the
reasons are under "What was not touched" below.

Three of the four required beats needed new material. The fourth, Ruth,
was already fully built by the prior pass (`BEX_NO_UPSIDE.md`) inside
chapter 21 and is only described here, not re-done.

## Beat 1 — Chloe, `chapters/21_the_applications.md`

**Whose:** Chloe's. **Costs her:** the one chance in these chapters where she
tries to say the thing plainly, on the phone, to the one person who would
believe her without being shown the receipts, and what comes out is smaller
than what she meant.

The site is the phone call home after the Caltech-rep breakfast, immediately
following the confrontation that was already in the chapter (Bex: "I did
tell him that, and it was true by the time I said it"). Before this pass,
the call ended in silence:

> "Then what's the part?"
>
> Chloe doesn't have a short answer for that one, and the line stays quiet
> long enough that her mother asks if she's still there.

After:

> "Then what's the part?"
>
> "She's done this before," Chloe says. "She's fast, that's all. She's
> always been fast." Then the line goes quiet long enough that her mother
> asks if she's still there.

"She's done this before" is the closest the chapter lets her get to naming a
pattern rather than an instance, and the next breath takes it back down to a
compliment. Nobody articulates the rule; Chloe doesn't either, even here.
The silence that follows is the same silence that was already in the
chapter, now sitting under a sentence that undersold itself rather than
under nothing.

## Beat 2 — Ruth, `chapters/21_the_applications.md` (pre-existing, not added to)

**Whose:** Ruth's. **Costs Bex:** a habitual courtesy, the kept seat.

Already in the chapter before this pass, from `BEX_NO_UPSIDE.md`: Ruth hears
Bex give the geometry to a second boy the same morning, says "That's the
second time this morning," gets "It's a good story, and it's true," and by
lunch the seat Ruth keeps for Bex has somebody else's bag on it. This is
exactly the shape the brief asks for — flat, timed wrong (mid-breakfast, not
a confrontation), a withdrawal rather than a speech — and it was verified
against the current file rather than re-written. No second Ruth beat was
added; a repeat would have been the over-egging the brief and the house
rules both warn against ("an overused technique is cut back, not to zero,"
and in reverse, a technique added a few places, not everywhere).

## Beat 3 — Sam, `chapters/22_the_offer.md`

**Whose:** Sam's, and it costs Ruth something too, in a smaller way — the
"I know" that doesn't get anywhere.

New scene, inserted after the Kavi network-room scene and before Iyad's
dinner rundown:

> Ruth catches Sam in the stairwell with a stack of admissions mail under
> one arm, hers this time, MIT's return address on top.
>
> "Bex do your exit interview yet?"
>
> "Tomorrow." Sam shifts the stack to his other arm. "Why?"
>
> "No reason." Ruth starts past him, then stops on the landing. "She still
> owes you for the range numbers, or did she ever pay that back?"
>
> "Owes me?" Sam says. "She didn't take anything, she gave me the whole
> breakdown better than I had it myself. My name was on every line of it."
>
> "I know," Ruth says, and goes on up the stairs.
>
> Sam stands on the landing with the mail still under one arm, then shrugs
> and goes to find out what's for dinner.

Sam's line is true and said in Bex's favor, because from where he stands it
is a fact in her favor: she gave him the numbers back with his name attached,
which is the boy's half of the same behaviour the girls in the year are
living the other half of. He isn't being obtuse or defensive; he has no
data that would make him otherwise. Ruth's "I know" closes the exchange
rather than pushing on it, because there's nothing in his answer to
correct — he's reporting his own experience accurately. Sam is left turning
over that something in it didn't land, without landing on what, which is
the blind spot as written in `characters/SAM.md` ("he will accept the first
available answer about it") rather than a stated one.

## Beat 4 — the year, `chapters/19_sixteen.md`

**Whose:** nobody named; ordinary traffic. **Costs Bex:** one habitual
audience, quietly, without a scene.

New paragraph, inserted into the practice-paper stretch (May-to-September,
the run that already has Kavi and Nadia marking Chloe's papers):

> In April she leaves a spare copy of each paper at the corner of the
> library table where Bex works some evenings, in case anybody there wants
> to check theirs against it. In May she starts leaving the spare with
> Kavi. Marek still takes his to Bex some weeks, when she's the one still
> up when he finishes, and she marks his in red, in the margin, no comment
> underneath, the way she marks anyone's.

This is deliberately small and deniable: a change of desk, not a boycott.
Chloe's copy moves; Marek's doesn't. Nothing says why, and Bex still marks
fairly and well when the paper does reach her, which is the point — the
cost is a smaller audience, not a worse Bex. It reads as one girl's changed
habit on a skim, and as the shape of "which girls stop putting things in
front of her" against "who still goes to her" on a second look, which
matches how the paintball and breakfast beats in `BEX_NO_UPSIDE.md` were
built to work.

## What was not touched, and why

- **`chapters/23_the_first_one.md`.** Read in full. Bex does not appear in
  it at all; the chapter is graduation and the whole cast's next moves, and
  inserting a Bex beat into a chapter that currently has none would have
  meant building a fifth scene rather than extending one of the four, which
  the brief scopes as "small and concrete," not exhaustive. The chapter
  also sits at 4,581 words already, and the four beats above did not need
  a fifth site.
- **`chapters/30_cleared.md`.** Read in full. The two Bex-relevant lines
  specified in the brief as ALREADY THERE ("That'll be Bex," Chloe says.
  "Alcantar." and the Whitaker exchange ending "I did," Chloe says. "She
  was there for the run in April, but the geometry's mine, from January.")
  are exactly as specified and were left untouched. This is the chapter's
  existing payoff for the ch21 theft, five years on, in a federal file
  rather than to Bex's face, and it already does the job cheaply; adding a
  second device here would risk exactly what `BEX_NO_UPSIDE.md` flagged
  about doubling a mechanism in a short span: it stops reading as ordinary
  and starts reading as a device.
- **A second Chloe beat, or a second Ruth beat.** The brief asks for one
  moment each; the house rules' "cut back, not to zero, and not driven past
  the level that would be good either" cuts both ways, and Chloe already
  has a full public confrontation (ch21) that fails, and Ruth already has
  the seat and the "second time" line. One more of either would be the
  over-corrected version of the ratio.

## Honest estimate: which beat is weakest

The Sam beat (3) is the weakest of the four. It does real work — his answer
is genuinely true and genuinely his, and Ruth getting nothing to push
against out of it is the honest version of "his honest answer makes it
worse" — but the mechanism (Bex "gave him the whole breakdown" on the range
numbers) is invented for this scene rather than a payoff of something the
reader has already watched happen to Sam on the page, the way the geometry
theft in ch21 is a payoff of the long-field work the reader watched in
earlier chapters. A reader who hasn't independently inferred that Bex does
this for boys too will read Sam's line as a nice thing a nice girl did for
him, full stop, and Ruth's "I know" will read as a non sequitur rather than
as the closest a Ruth-shaped person gets to giving up on a point. The scene
depends on the reader already having the rule from chapters 12-16 in hand
when they reach chapter 22; it does not build that rule itself, and per
the brief it isn't supposed to. If chapters 12-16 land the pattern clearly
by the time a reader gets here, this scene should complete it. If they
don't, this is the beat most likely to read as filler rather than as the
asymmetry finally showing its other half.
