## Changed, one sentence

### chapters/35_nine_minutes.md, line 11

**Before:** "Three logs come open instead of one: the process's own record,
the host underneath it, and the outbound trace Kavi built to sit beneath
both, each blind to the other's existence."

**After:** "Three logs are opened instead of one: the process's own record,
the host underneath it, and the outbound trace Kavi built to sit beneath
both, each blind to the other's existence."

**Why:** This is the call-out held back in `passes/VOICE_35_36.md`, re-judged
with both directions as asked.

The defect is real and survives the rule change: "logs come open" is the
same construction as "the phone comes back up" earlier in this same
chapter (fixed in the prior pass, correctly, and left alone here), an
inanimate subject taking a verb of motion that only the missing agent could
actually perform. That is house rule 8's third category, the book's own
recurring fault, and it is not a passive question at all in its broken form.

Both directions were tried:

- **Active, naming Eli:** "Eli opens three logs instead of one..." This is
  what the prior pass considered and declined, because the very next
  paragraph opens "Eli pulls the logs." Naming him at the head of two
  consecutive paragraphs, both about logs, reads as flat repetition of the
  same beat rather than the general-methodology-then-specific-instance split
  the chapter appears to be going for. That concern holds up under a second
  look; it was not an artifact of the old rule.
- **Active, with a pronoun instead of the name:** "Instead of one, he opens
  three logs..." Solves the repetition problem, but the fix this pass
  actually wanted was a sentence that did not start with "He" or "She",
  since that construction is sitting at 118.9-119.7 per 100,000 against a
  target of 120 book-wide, close enough to its ceiling that voice2's own
  instruction is to reach for the name instead, not the pronoun-fronted
  workaround, when a straight "He/She + verb" opener is the only other
  option. This option also just moves the awkwardness rather than solving
  it: "Instead of one" as a sentence-opener is not natural for this voice.
- **Passive:** "Three logs are opened instead of one..." Chosen. Under house
  rule 8's actual test, the agent (Eli) is obvious from context, there is
  nobody else in the room and no one else it could be, and the sentence
  itself is doing something other than crediting an action to a person: the
  paragraph's whole point, finished off in the next, separate sentence
  ("Because a single log is a story somebody wrote, and logs that agree
  read closer to a fact") is about the logs as evidence, not about Eli
  doing the checking. The receiver is what the sentence is about, which is
  Gopen and Swan's own case for the passive. This also leaves "Eli pulls the
  logs" as the paragraph break's first and only naming of him doing
  something with logs that night, instead of the second.

Referent check: no pronoun was touched, nothing in the sentence points
anywhere new, and the list after the colon is unchanged word for word.

Measured before and after with `measures/style_report.py chapters/35_nine_minutes.md`:
word count 2101 both times, sentence count 121 both times, section breaks 3
both times, no new tic. `measures/tics.py` book-wide: "sentence opens She/He
+ verb" unaffected by this edit, since neither version added or removed one
(119.7/100k against target 120, pass, same as before the edit within
rounding). Scorecard measure not moved in either direction.

"It's still warm enough at midnight that Chloe has the window cracked..."
at 101, "Chloe reads that at one in the morning..." at 103, and "her badge
scans..." at 105. Confirmed against `19d60e7`'s diff that the repair was a

at 105 has exactly one available antecedent. Nothing here needed a second
pass.

## Both directions, more generally

Read chapter 35 and chapter 36 fully, narration and dialogue, checking each
sentence against all three of house rule 8's tests in both directions
(passive that drops a present, relevant person; active that buries an
established topic behind an unimportant or invented subject; inanimate
subject with an active verb of motion or intent). Outside the one sentence
above, neither chapter has an instance of any of the three. Both chapters
have already been through the agency and passivity passes and the two
research-based rewrites already applied by `19d60e7`, and it shows: the
remaining passives in both chapters (Ruth's phone getting picked up at 2am
in chapter 35, the badge and the file entry being read and closed in
chapter 36's closing paragraph) are already the correct kind, object as
topic, agent obvious, and were left alone rather than reported, since they
are not defects.

Dialogue in both chapters was also checked for wrong voice, per the "dialogue
above all" instruction, since a protected bad sentence is exactly what this
pass wants to see. None found. Priya's account of the extraction in chapter
36 uses some dissociated, matter-of-fact phrasing about what she did with
her own hands ("the two i took off the grass went into the engine blocks"),
which reads at first like the fake-agency fault dressed as speech, but it is
consistent with how she talks about the rest of that same event (minimizing,
technical, present-tense-flat throughout the whole exchange) and is not
reported as a defect; it is characterization, not an accident.
