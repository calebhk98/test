# Ruled deliberate: do not report these as defects

Every item here has been reported by at least one reviewing agent as a mistake,
and every one has been ruled deliberate by the author. Read this before writing
any review of the manuscript. Reporting one of these again wastes a slot that a
real finding could have used.

Nothing here is beyond criticism. If you think one of these fails **for a reason
nobody has raised before**, say so in one line and give the new reason. Do not
restate the objection that has already been overruled.

---

## Names

**Ruth's surname is Aymar, and a boy in the year is also called Aymar.** Three
separate agents have called this a continuity break. It is not one. The author:
*"It is not a Continuity break, it is because people IRL share names."* Chapter
17 now has one line acknowledging it, because enough readers tripped on it, and
that line is all the acknowledgement it gets.

## Characters

**Meg and Dave sound similar.** They are a married couple in the same argument.
The author: *"they are both her parents. They are going to be a bit similar."*

**Chloe calls the muggers retarded after the parking-lot fight.** This is the
calibration, not contempt. A 100 IQ man reads to her the way a 40 IQ man would
read to us, and she can only explain a fight that went that way by assuming the
other side was handicapped. The author: *"Chloe literally sees them as retarded.
They quite literally experience the fight, and can only rationalize the bad
fight as them being handicapped."* This is the clearest instance in the book of
the standing rule that these people rationalise evidence of their own ability
rather than accept it.

## Repetition that is doing work

**"Finishes first, checks it, waits."** Partly deliberate: it is her repetition,
and its return in chapter 7 is the point, because she is back inside it. The
author notes the chapter 5 instance may be a misreading, since chapter 5 is at
camp, where the shape should be her NOT waiting.

**Chloe asking about Halstead in chapters 3, 7 and 8.** Deliberate, and the
three are not the same. In chapter 3 she asks a few times and is curious; she
would take it or leave it. In 7 and 8 it is the only thing in her head, and the
repetition is there to show how much she asks and what it is doing to her
parents.

**The lists.** Accumulating "and X, and Y, and Z" runs are a device aimed at the
reader on purpose. Never flag them.

**Contradictory accounts of why Owen left.** The rumour mill working. Nobody in
the book corrects it and nothing signposts it.

## Prose

**"She is relieved to find it still works like it worked in first grade."**
Chapter 7. The author's own sentence, dictated almost verbatim after an agent
deleted it once. Two agents have now come for it. It stays.

**The ending does not resolve the plot.** Known and intended. The author: *"I
agree it's not a good ending, but it is the intended ending."* Do not propose
resolving the founder, the seventy-five, or the prisoners.

## Unreliable speakers

**Ruth has Owen crying in the stairwell on the Thursday, Kavi has him crying by
the kitchens on the same Thursday, and both claim to have seen it themselves.**
An agent found this during the passivity pass, and it is correct that the two
accounts cannot both be true. It was reported as a continuity break, and it was
"fixed" by moving Kavi to the stairwell before the author stopped it. The fix
is reverted and the disagreement is the point.

Owen left because he could not keep up with the work. He was overwhelmed, he
called his mother, and she came and got him. Nobody in the school knows that.
The work is so easy to the children around him that it is obvious to them he
could do it, so the reason for his leaving has to be invented, and the rumour
mill invents several. Ruth's version and Kavi's version are two of them, and
Kavi's carries the additional false claim that Owen was "doing all of it fine,
the water thing and the bridge and all of it."

The evidence that he was not fine is on the page, in the narration, where it
cannot lie: his sheet comes back two sentences long with a line through one of
them, his table is still short of the span when the bridge session ends, and he
says out loud that he has lost the thread of it. The narrator never lies. The
speakers do, and are wrong, and gaslight, and repeat what they heard from
somebody who was also wrong.

The author, on the design: *"This is to show readers that the text is
unreliable speakers. The narrator never lies, but it puts red herrings
everywhere, and speakers can and will lie (or be wrong, gaslight, manipulate,
etc)."*

So: two speakers contradicting each other is not a defect in this book, and a
speaker contradicted by the narration is not a defect either. Before reporting
any disagreement between characters as a continuity break, check whether the
narration settles it. If the narration is silent or on the other side, the
disagreement is doing its job. Owen is the worked example and the list is the
thing he is evidence against.
