# Chapters 1-12: four-job pass report

Scope: `chapters/01_before.md` through `chapters/12_nine.md` only. House rules
(`passes/HOUSE_RULES.md`) read first; no character-sheet or reference-doc
files touched.

## Job 1: the word "but"

Went through every "and" in each chapter and converted the ones joining
genuinely contrasting clauses to "but" (or added a "But" across two adjacent
sentences already in contrast). Left mechanical conversions alone where no
real contrast existed — several chapters land a little under the 0.4% floor
as a result rather than being padded to hit it.

## Job 2: letting people say more than one sentence

Fixed the single most common pattern: a person's continuous thought split
across two `"..."` spans by a dialogue tag or an action beat, which the
measuring script counts as two separate one-sentence quotations instead of
one multi-sentence one. Moved the tag to the front or the end so the full
thought sits inside one pair of quotation marks. Left in place the beats
that are doing real work (a demonstration mid-explanation, a deliberate
pause in the birthday-begging scene in ch9, the "Fen." / Silence. / "Fen,
are you asleep" pacing in ch10).

Constraint honored throughout: children's **turns** got longer, not their
**sentences**. E.g. ch4's Sam turn became "Mine's got a pool you can't use.
It's in the building and it's for the high school. So they just have it
there. And it's got a lid on it." — four short sentences, one turn.

## Job 3: dialing back literary polish

This span (camp/school chapters, present tense, close third on a six-to-
nine-year-old) had already been through a flattening pass, so it was
mostly plain. Found and fixed the clearest instances of a fancier word
standing in for an ordinary one, or a crafted/periodic sentence shape:

| chapter | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | "somebody is **invariably** still going" | "somebody is **always** still going" |
| 01 | "so the explaining starts with the station, **whose size is what everything else depends on**" | "so the explaining starts with the station, **because everything else in the story depends on how big it is**" |
| 02 | "Chloe had held his sleeve a second longer than the door needed, and **not knowing whether** this is the kind of appointment you bring a dad to, she reads the waiting room instead." | "Chloe had held his sleeve a second longer than the door needed. **She doesn't know whether** this is the kind of appointment you bring a dad to, so she reads the waiting room instead." |
| 03 | "answered straight away, **immediate and complete**" | "answered straight away, **quick and complete**" |
| 03 | "the invitation showed up **unsolicited**" | "**nobody asked for the invitation, it just showed up**" |
| 05 | "Chloe can manage a length **provided** she stops once in the middle" | "Chloe can manage a length **as long as** she stops once in the middle" |
| 06 | "her sneaker keeps catching the chair leg, **a small percussion swallowed by the adults talking over her head**" | "her sneaker keeps catching the chair leg, **a small noise nobody at the table notices**" |
| 10 | "Swimming is the first afternoon hour and **compulsory for everybody**" | "Swimming is the first afternoon hour and **everybody has to do it**" |

Chapters 4, 7, 8, 9, 11, 12 were read closely for the same problem and left
alone — they're already action- and dialogue-driven and didn't have a
periodic sentence or a $10 word to plain down. Sentence length itself
(including the long comma-joined compounds) was left untouched everywhere;
only word choice and a couple of fronted/inverted clause shapes changed.

## Job 4: chapter 2's reading grade

Chapter 2 was at Flesch-Kincaid 5.4 against a 5.5 floor — the book's only
band failure. Fixed entirely in narration (dialogue untouched, including
the child's dialogue) by combining short adjacent narrative sentences at
five spots during the testing-room scene, e.g.:

- "Her hands go after it. The top row goes down flat and true, fast." →
  "Her hands go after it, and the top row goes down flat and true, fast."
- "Ben watches her. The watch keeps going. / She goes back to doing it
  neat." → "Ben watches her while the watch keeps going, and she goes back
  to doing it neat."

Result: Flesch-Kincaid 5.6, ARI 5.5 — both now clear the floor. Nothing
above chapter 2's neighbors was raised; the other nine chapters in the
1-10 band were left where they were per house rule 6.
