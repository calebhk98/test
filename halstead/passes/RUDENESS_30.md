# Manners pass, Chapter 30 ("Cleared")

Scope: `30_cleared.md` only, checked against `HOUSE_RULES.md` and
`DO_NOT_FLAG.md`. Every quotation below was copied from the file and
re-verified with grep before this was written.

## Summary

This chapter is almost entirely a formal background-investigation interview
between Chloe and Whitaker, and both parties are written as scrupulously
correct with each other throughout: he answers her one direct question in
full on the first try (line 41), lets her finish every long answer before he
turns the page (line 61), and she gives him "more than the minimum" (line
43) because of it. That politeness is the point of the scene, and it left
little to flag. Counts:

- **Deliberate, keep:** 2
- **Unintended, flag:** 0
- **Uncertain:** 1
- **Unanswered questions:** 0 explicit; 1 implicit (see below)

## Deliberate, keep

**1. Line 111-113. Whitaker declines to confirm or deny "Bex."**

> "That'll be Bex," Chloe says. "Alcantar."
>
> He doesn't confirm it, but he doesn't deny it either, and he writes
> something down before he goes on.

Chloe names a name; Whitaker gives her nothing back, in either direction,
and moves on without acknowledging she was right or wrong. This is the
investigator-stonewalling case named in the brief: the whole scene is built
on his professional reticence (he tells her up front what gets recorded and
what stays sealed, line 37 and 41), so a source he won't confirm is the
character working as designed, not the book failing to notice. This is also
the nearest thing in the chapter to a question that never gets answered:
Chloe's naming amounts to a request for confirmation, and none ever comes,
anywhere in the chapter.

**2. Line 125-127. Whitaker's parting line, addressed to the folder, and his exit before she can respond.**

> At the door, coat half on, he taps the folder once against his palm. "Now
> I get to write it up in triplicate for a form that only ever needed one
> copy." He says it to the folder, not to her, and he's already down the
> first flight of stairs before she's worked out it was the joke.

Read cold, this is a person cracking a joke and leaving before the other
person can react, which is on the list of things to flag. But it lands as
a character beat rather than a slight: Whitaker has been established across
both visits as plain, undemonstrative, and fair (declining her coffee for
his own thermos so as not to impose, thanking her "as plainly as he thanked
her the first time," waiting out her silences), and the payoff here is
Chloe working out the joke after the fact, not being hurt by the exit. Keep,
but it is close enough to the pattern that it is worth the author's eyes: if
he ever wants Whitaker's exit to read as ruder or warmer, this is the line.

## Uncertain

**Line 61. Whitaker leaves the table mid-answer to refill his thermos.**

> The classes come after that, all of them, mathematics through politics
> through the forge, and it takes a full twenty minutes to get through the
> list; his coffee cools enough in the open thermos cap that he crosses to
> her counter partway through and tops it up, standing there through one
> whole answer before he sits back down, and he lets her finish every year
> of it before he turns the page.

This is the one place a character does something else while being spoken
to, which is squarely on the list. But the same sentence says he "lets her
finish every year of it before he turns the page," which reads as him still
tracking the answer despite being on his feet, so I can't tell whether this
is inattention the text doesn't notice or just staging (a man on his fourth
hour of note-taking, stretching his legs, still listening). What would
settle it: whether his notes anywhere show a gap that lines up with the
part of the list he'd have missed, or whether the author intends the coffee
break as a small discourtesy at all. I would not spend a fix on this without
that answer.

## Unintended, flag

None found with confidence. I looked specifically for Chloe doing something
a real person would find rude, per the note that her own passivity matters
as much as rudeness done to her, and did not find an instance in this
chapter: she answers every question Whitaker asks (including declining
carefully rather than silently, lines 83-99), and nothing shows her reading,
writing, or looking elsewhere while spoken to. The blog-inbox material
(lines 7-13, strangers whose threads "go quiet" after Chloe says she works
alone) is unresponsive correspondence rather than a live scene, so I did not
force it into this category.

## Questions that never get answered

None. Every question with a question mark in the chapter (lines 39, 45, 49,
67, 75, 93, 97, 115) gets a direct answer within a line or two, and the two
question-shaped statements without a mark (line 63 "You taught as well,"
line 85 "Asked by whom.") are answered too. The only unresolved query in the
chapter is the implicit one at line 111, listed above under Deliberate,
since it is Whitaker withholding rather than Chloe or anyone else leaving a
question hanging.

## What I would fix first

Nothing in this chapter needs a fix. If forced to rank, the coffee-refill
beat at line 61 is the only candidate worth a second look, and only because
it is the sole moment that technically matches the pattern; everything else
here is either clean or working as the scene intends.
