## Declined, not fixed — the negative-space pass

The first full draft came in at 39.6% `neg%`, roughly five times the book median, driven by exactly the
construction the brief warns about: "he doesn't reach for it," "nobody has observed," "there isn't one
to find," "she doesn't reread." Every one of these was rewritten into a positive action rather than
trimmed — "he lets it sit a moment" instead of "he doesn't reach for it," "the handwriting comes back
clean" instead of "there isn't one to find," "she reads the messages once" instead of "she doesn't
reread them." The one negation kept is the original chapter's own line, "A check-in doesn't arrive,"
left untouched as fixed text.

## What I decided against adding, and why

- **A visible moment where one of the seven almost sends the stop word, or almost invokes the response
  protocol, and is talked out of it.** The brief was explicit: the reader should see them decline, not
  see them talked into declining. Any scene with persuasion in it turns the decision into a group debate
  the chapter doesn't currently have, which is new plot. Ruth's and Eli's scenes both end on the clause
  reading exactly the same as it always has — nothing argues them out of it; they simply check and find
  no reason to act.
- **Nadia.** She's one of the seven per Ch33 and speaks in adjacent chapters (32, 34), but she is never
  named in this chapter's original text, so per the brief's rule against new named characters she does
  not appear here either, even obliquely.
- **Any explanation of who "the man" is, why he gave the tool back, or what he might do next.** All of
  that stays exactly as ambiguous as the original text leaves it (Eli's read: a mistake, not a message).
  Adding texture to the mystery risks reading as new plot.
- **Any explanation of the school, Chloe's specific employer, or the mechanics of the worm/watcher
  beyond what's already established in Ch32/33/34.** Kept references generic (a downtown desk, a
  government building, a reconciliation job) rather than inventing new institutional detail.
- **New chat lines.** Chat doesn't count toward the grading metric, so there was no incentive to add
  any, and every existing line of dialogue is untouched — same words, same order, same speakers.
