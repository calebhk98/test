## What I decided against

- **New dialogue for Theo.** He's named in the intro (the note that sends him outside) but never speaks in the chat. His character sheet and chapter 29 both establish that he processes privately for one to two days before he'll post anything, so I kept him out of the transcript and gave him physical prose beats (closing the laptop, going outside, the closing-file bookend) instead of forcing a line that would contradict his established pace.
- **Explaining Theo's note.** I dramatized the reaction (closes the laptop with both hands, twenty minutes outside) without ever saying what the note contains. Same restraint applied to the "blank on what the four looked like" and the "collection against the channel comes back empty" lines — I added reading reactions, never resolutions.
- **A fourth witness statement in the Waffle House report** describing being "asked what he'd have scored it." I drafted this initially by analogy to chapter 29's loading-dock report, then cut it — that detail belongs to a different incident (four trained operators, chapter 15/29) and would have conflated two separate documented events in the same book.
- **Pushing the word count further past ~2,075.** It's within the requested 2,000–3,000 band; I stopped once the additions had covered staging, the document's texture, and all seven characters' presence rather than continuing to pad a chapter whose emotional center is meant to be compact and quiet.
