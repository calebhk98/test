# Military reference: US Army entry training, 2023

Research note for chapters 25 and 26. Sam enlists straight out of school and
ships in the first week of August 2023. Everything below is what the Army
actually did that year, with sources at the bottom. Where a source conflicts
with another, both figures are given.

This document is a fact sheet, not a style guide. House Rule 1 still governs
anything that goes on the page: none of this may be explained to the reader.

---

## 1. What Sam is actually in

Two different things get called "basic training" and the chapter has to pick one.

**Basic Combat Training (BCT)** is ten weeks. Everyone who is not combat arms
does BCT, then travels to a separate school for Advanced Individual Training.
Ten weeks from the first week of August graduates in the second week of
October. It cannot cover four months.

**One Station Unit Training (OSUT)** is BCT and AIT run back to back in one
place, by one cadre, for combat arms. Infantry OSUT at Fort Moore, Georgia, is
**twenty-two weeks**, extended from fourteen in 2018 to 2019 and standard since.
Armor and cavalry OSUT run to similar lengths. Twenty-two weeks from the first
week of August runs to early January.

**Decision for the book: Sam is in Infantry OSUT at Fort Moore.** It is the
only version of entry training where "four months in and still in training" is
true, it is where a soldier of his description would actually be assigned, and
it puts him at a unit doing a field exercise by February 2024, which is what
chapter 26 needs. Fort Benning was renamed Fort Moore in May 2023, so in
August 2023 the name on the sign is Fort Moore.

Note for anyone writing this: OSUT keeps the BCT phase structure for the first
ten weeks and then continues. The phases at Fort Moore are Red, White, Blue,
Black and Gold. In the ten-week BCT-only version they are Red, White and Blue.

### The ten-week BCT phase structure

| weeks | phase | content |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Reception | not counted as a training week. Paperwork, medical, shots, haircuts, issue of kit, initial fitness screen. Typically three to seven days. |
| 1-3 | Red | drill and ceremony, Army values, first aid, land navigation classroom work, gas chamber, obstacle course, baseline fitness test. First contact with the rifle is here: familiarisation, mechanical training, dry fire. |
| 4-5 | White | Basic Rifle Marksmanship proper. Grouping and zeroing, then live fire out to the qualification distances. Two solid weeks of ranges. |
| 6-9 | Blue | advanced marksmanship, night fire, machine guns, grenades, convoy and urban work, the culminating field exercise, tactical foot marches. Record fitness test. |
| 10 | graduation | inspections, family day, ceremony. |

**Marksmanship starts in week three or four and is finished as a qualification
event by week eight or nine.** It is not a month-four event. A trainee who has
not touched a rifle by month four has not been in the Army.

## 2. Rifle qualification, as it actually is

The current standard is **TC 3-20.40, Individual Weapons Qualification**,
which replaced the old FM 3-22.9 course of fire and was fully in force by 2023.

**The qualification table:**

- **40 rounds, 40 target exposures.** One round per exposure. Hits are counted,
  not scored by ring.
- **Pop-up silhouette targets at 50 to 300 metres**, in fifty-metre increments,
  scaled so that a 300m silhouette subtends what a man would at 300m.
- **Five firing positions in sequence**, ten exposures each: standing
  unsupported at 50m, prone unsupported, prone supported, kneeling supported,
  standing supported. The supported positions use a barricade.
- **Magazine changes and position changes are part of the table.** The soldier
  moves through the sequence on a clock: 3 minutes 58 seconds for the
  qualification table, with ten seconds allowed per transition. The practice
  table is tighter, 3:10 with eight-second transitions, and includes a dummy
  round that induces a stoppage the soldier has to clear.
- Grouping and zeroing happen separately, at 25 metres, on an A8 target with a
  4 MOA diamond, three five-round strings.

**The bands, unchanged from the old course:**

| hits out of 40 | band |
|---|---|
| 36-40 | Expert |
| 30-35 | Sharpshooter |
| 23-29 | Marksman |
| 22 or fewer | unqualified, retrain and refire |

So: **Expert is the top band and it starts at 36.** Forty out of forty is a
real possible score and a rare one. It is the thing that makes a cadre member
walk over. It is not "ten out of ten", and there is no version of this range
with ten static targets on it.

**Vocabulary that is right:** record fire, qual, qualification table, going
down range, the tower, lane, firing point, scorer, zero, grouping, hits,
"he shot expert", "forty out of forty". **Vocabulary that is wrong:** score
out of ten, bullseye, dead centre by the look of the holes (pop-ups fall when
hit; nobody walks downrange counting holes on qualification day).

## 3. The ACFT

In 2023 the test is the **Army Combat Fitness Test**, six events. The five-event
Army Fitness Test that drops the power throw arrives later and is not this book.

Events: three-repetition maximum deadlift (MDL), standing power throw (SPT),
hand-release push-up (HRP), sprint-drag-carry (SDC), plank (PLK), two-mile run
(2MR).

**Scoring: 0 to 100 points per event, 60 required in each to pass, 600
maximum.** Age and gender normed since the April 2022 revision.

100-point standards, male 17 to 21 (sources vary slightly on two of these):

| event | 100 points |
|---|---|
| deadlift | 340 lb |
| power throw | 12.5 m |
| hand-release push-ups | 60 reps |
| sprint-drag-carry | 1:29 to 1:33 |
| plank | 3:20 to 4:20 |
| two-mile run | 13:22 to 13:30 |

**The load-bearing fact: the scale caps.** A 600 is the top of the instrument.
Beating a 600 is not a thing that can happen; the sheet has nowhere to put it.
A soldier who wants a better number than 600 has to go and beat his own raw
figures off a stopwatch, and no record anywhere will show it.

**Plausibility of maxing.** Trainees are tested more than once in a cycle:
a baseline or diagnostic in Red Phase and a record test late in the cycle.
Practice is permitted and there is no fixed date. A 600 in training is
extraordinary but documented: the first BCT trainee to record one, at Fort
Jackson in October 2019, was the third soldier in the entire Army to do it, and
it made the Army news wire. So an exceptional eighteen-year-old maxing it early
is publishable-news rare rather than impossible. What it produces is a company
that talks about it, not a scale that goes higher.

For the book, note that SAM.md already fixes this: "maxes the fitness
assessment twice." Two 600s, the diagnostic and the record test. Not a 600 and
then something better.

## 4. Communications

The 2023 position, and it is nothing like the 1980s one.

- **Phones exist and are in the building.** Personal phones are collected and
  held by the drill sergeant, and handed back for a window, usually Sunday, if
  the platoon has earned it. Calls of ten to thirty minutes are typical. Many
  cadre allow voice calls only and restrict texting, photographs and social
  media. Phone privileges are given and taken as a routine lever.
- **Letters are normal, encouraged, and public.** Mail call is a scheduled
  event, run by the cadre, and mail is handed out in front of everybody, often
  with push-ups charged per letter as a running joke that everyone pays
  willingly. Personal time in the evening, roughly 2000 to lights out at 2100,
  is explicitly for laundry, boots, hygiene and **writing letters**.
- **Email** is not part of the trainee's day. There is no personal internet
  access for trainees.

**Consequences for chapter 25.** A soldier writing a weekly letter home is the
single most ordinary thing in the barracks. There is nothing to conceal, nobody
to hide it from, and it is not possible to conceal it anyway: he is never alone,
the mail goes through the cadre, and everyone in the bay is doing the same thing
at the same hour. Writing it "in secret" for four months without being caught is
the wrong idea twice over. What is actually private about it is the content and
the recipient, not the act.

## 5. Food

Bad chow at BCT in 2023 is a cliché, and it is the wrong one.

- Three meals a day, at a dining facility (DFAC), or MREs in the field. The DFAC
  food is by most accounts genuinely decent: a line, a salad bar, real
  variety.
- **The real complaint is time.** Trainees are supposed to get twenty minutes
  and often get ten by the time the line has cleared. What the experience
  actually produces is described as panic eating: getting the calories in
  before being told to move.
- **The other real complaint is supervision.** Cadre stand over the line and
  police what goes on the tray. Desserts, sodas, condiments and second helpings
  get restricted, particularly for anyone on a weight programme.

So a soldier who complains about the food is repeating something he heard.
A soldier who is happy is likely to be happy about volume and unhappy about
minutes. Sam, who eats standing, fast, and keeps ordering after everyone else
has stopped, has a specific and accurate grievance available: not the taste,
the clock.

## 6. Ranks, and who talks to whom

A BCT or OSUT **company** is around 200 trainees in four platoons.

| who | rank | what they are to a trainee |
|---|---|---|
| drill sergeant | SSG or SFC | all day, every day. This is the relationship. |
| senior drill sergeant | SFC | the drill sergeants' drill sergeant. |
| first sergeant | 1SG | the company's senior NCO. Addressed as First Sergeant. |
| company commander | **CPT** | commands the company. Signs everything. |
| platoon leader (where one exists) | 2LT/1LT | often a lieutenant learning the job. |
| battalion commander | LTC | a voice at a ceremony. |

**How often does a private get spoken to by his company commander?** Rarely,
and it is an event when it happens. The commander addresses the company at
formations and ceremonies, and speaks to individuals for a reason: a serious
deficiency, a decision the drill sergeants have pushed up, a disciplinary
action, an open-door request, or a result he has to sign for. Qualification day
is one of the days a commander does circulate on a range, because company
qualification rates are his statistic and he reports them.

**Form.** Trainees go to **attention** for a commissioned officer and address
him as **sir**. For an NCO they go to **parade rest** and use the rank:
"Drill Sergeant," "First Sergeant." A captain addresses a trainee by rank and
surname, or surname alone: "Private Marsh," "Marsh." He does not call him son.
He does not deliver a paragraph of praise. If he is pleased he says something
short and moves on, and the compliment that matters arrives as a line in a
record rather than out loud.

The range itself is run by an officer in charge (OIC) and a range safety
officer, with drill sergeants as scorers on the lanes and a tower controlling
the exposures. A captain reading a scoresheet on that range is plausible. A
captain interviewing a trainee at length on that range, in front of the lanes,
is not; he takes it somewhere else or takes it to the drill sergeant.

## 7. What actually happens when a recruit looks anomalous

This is the item that matters most, and the author's instinct is correct.

The Army's reaction to a trainee who is inexplicably good and who volunteers an
account of advanced training from a school that cannot be found is **not
praise**. Three separate machines start moving, and none of them is a
commendation:

**a. Counterintelligence.** Under **AR 381-12, the Threat Awareness and
Reporting Program** (TARP, formerly SAEDA), every soldier is required to report
to counterintelligence any information touching on espionage, foreign
intelligence involvement, unauthorised specialised training, or an unexplained
relationship with an unidentified source. This is annual mandatory training that
every leader in the building has sat through. A private describing live-fire
techniques taught to him before the age of eighteen, at an institution the
officer cannot find, is squarely inside what a leader has been told to report,
and the reporting is not discretionary. It goes to the supporting Army
Counterintelligence element.

**b. Personnel security.** Anything the soldier declared on his security
questionnaire (the SF-86, submitted through e-QIP) can now be checked against
what he just said out loud. Education is a listed field on that form. A school
that does not resolve is a discrepancy, and discrepancies in the education,
foreign contacts and foreign influence fields are adjudicative concerns under
the National Security Adjudicative Guidelines. This is the route that treats him
as a possible liar, and it is entirely routine and entirely paper.

**c. Behavioural health.** A trainee making claims his cadre judge implausible
gets a referral, and in an entry training environment that referral is fast and
low-threshold. The chain does not have to believe he is unwell to send him; it
has to be unable to rule it out.

The captain does not have to choose between these three. He is not required to
form a view. **What a competent commander does is write it down accurately,
route it, and stop owning it.** The instruments are a memorandum for record, a
counselling entry on DA Form 4856, and referrals through the S-2 and the
supporting CI office. The phrase the author wants, *appears to have received
specialised instruction from an unidentified source*, is the correct register
for that memorandum: it describes without asserting, and it is the kind of
sentence that survives being read by strangers for twenty years.

**What that produces at Sam's end: nothing.** He is not told. He is not
punished. Nobody argues with him. The absence of a reaction is the reaction,
and a soldier who believes an honest test always looks like a test will read the
silence as the subject having been dropped.

## 8. Small true things worth having

- Brass is picked up. Leaving brass on the ground is one of the first things
  anybody is taught not to do, and there is a police call for it.
- Ruck marches are graded on the clock and the load, and packs get weighed.
- Land navigation is taught, then run day and night, on a course with numbered
  points and a scorecard.
- "Trainee" and "private" are both correct. Cadre often say "trainee."
- Trainees do not say "yes sir" to a drill sergeant. That is a punishable joke.
- Sunday is the day with religious services, phone privileges, laundry and
  letters in it.
- The end-of-cycle field exercise is a named event and everyone knows the name.
- Nobody is issued an M16 in 2023. It is an M4 carbine.
- Sergeant Ruiz, per SAM.md, is a drill sergeant. Trainees address him as
  Drill Sergeant Ruiz or Drill Sergeant, never as Sergeant Ruiz.
