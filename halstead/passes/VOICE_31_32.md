# Voice pass: chapters 31-32

Scope: `chapters/31_ruth.md` and `chapters/32_the_money.md` only. Measured
against `passes/HOUSE_RULES.md` and the voice brief. Checked with
`measures/style_report.py` on each file before and after; no regression on
either file's numbers (word counts moved by four and thirteen words
respectively from the edits below; sentence-shape, conjunction, and
quote-length figures are unchanged within rounding).

## Changed

### chapters/31_ruth.md, line 5

**Before:** "Ruth spends her first year at MIT believing she has been put in a remedial track."

**After:** "Ruth spends her first year at MIT believing the school has put her in a remedial track."

**Why:** Ruth is the subject of a passive ("she has been put") — the
highest-priority fault named in the brief. The doer is obvious and faceless
(the institution that placed her), so it goes in as the active subject
instead of leaving a person as the one things get done to.

### chapters/31_ruth.md, line 7

**Before:** "Her assumption is that a real sequence exists somewhere she hasn't been given, a bureaucratic problem, and she spends most of October trying to find the right office."

**After:** "Her assumption is that a real sequence exists somewhere the school hasn't placed her in, a bureaucratic problem, and she spends most of October trying to find the right office."

**Why:** Same fault, two sentences later: "she hasn't been given" puts Ruth
in the subject slot of a passive again. "The school" is a reasonable,
faceless institutional doer already established by the surrounding
paragraph (registrar, advising, the department), so naming it invents
nothing.

### chapters/31_ruth.md, line 11

**Before:** "A folder now goes everywhere with her, problem sets clipped in date order, because a written claim is harder to wave off than something merely said, and easier, in her experience, to make somebody actually answer."

**After:** "Ruth carries a folder everywhere now, problem sets clipped in date order, because a written claim is harder to wave off than something merely said, and easier, in her experience, to make somebody actually answer."

**Why:** Fake agency of the book's specific type — an inanimate folder
"goes" on its own, in the same shape as the house-rules example "the sheet
goes up on the wall." Ruth is right there in the sentence already (the
prepositional "with her"); she becomes the one doing it. Used "Ruth" rather
than "she" so the fix does not add another sentence-opening pronoun+verb,
which is already at its target rate for the book.

### chapters/31_ruth.md, line 43

**Before:** "Then the notebook closes and she starts the reading due Monday."

**After:** "Then she closes the notebook and starts the reading due Monday."

**Why:** The notebook is grammatically the one doing the closing, with
nobody in the sentence acting — the book's specific fault. Ruth is the
obvious doer one clause later ("she starts the reading"); folding the two
actions onto her removes the fake agency without changing pacing.

### chapters/31_ruth.md, line 49

**Before:** "Problem sets go in early, hand-delivered to the box outside the office."

**After:** "Problem sets get turned in early, hand-delivered to the box outside the office."

**Why:** "Problem sets go in" is the same fake-agency shape (inanimate
subject, active verb, nobody acting). This sentence sits inside a flat list
of facts about the shape of Ruth's year ("Class happens... Dinner is in the
hall...") where the paragraph's own rhythm keeps the objects as subjects
rather than swerving to "she" each time, so the second-best fix applies
here: a passive that still implies the doer, rather than a person-first
rewrite that would break the list's cadence.

### chapters/31_ruth.md, line 71

**Before:** "At the first he nods, but at the second the nod does not arrive, and he sits back off the page with the pen still up in his hand."

**After:** "At the first he nods, but at the second he doesn't, and he sits back off the page with the pen still up in his hand."

**Why:** "The nod does not arrive" turns the professor's own action into an
object that shows up or fails to, the same nominalize-then-move pattern as
"the name of it goes across the table." He is the one who does or doesn't
nod; the fix says so directly and reads faster, which suits the beat.

### chapters/32_the_money.md, line 41

**Before:** "Some of Kavi's timeline cards come home in her bag without either of them discussing it; she copies the gaps onto a strip of paper..."

**After:** "Ruth brings some of Kavi's timeline cards home in her bag without either of them discussing it; she copies the gaps onto a strip of paper..."

**Why:** The cards "come home" on their own — fake agency, and Ruth is
sitting in the very next clause as the one who copies the gaps. She is
named as the one carrying them too. Used "Ruth" rather than "she" to avoid
adding a sentence-opening pronoun+verb.

## Called out, not changed

### chapters/32_the_money.md, line 37

**Current:** "The shape resists being more than a single person no matter how they turn it, and by the time the call ends, it has stopped being a shared problem and become Ruth's."

**Why the voice is wrong:** "shape" is an abstraction (the pattern in the
data) given an active verb of intent, "resists" — the book's fake-agency
fault, an inanimate thing doing what only a person can really do.

**Why I did not change it:** this is the chapter's hinge sentence, the
moment the investigation stops being sharable and becomes Ruth's alone. The
sentence is making a claim about the evidence itself, not about a choice
Eli, Kavi, or Ruth made, and I was not confident a person-as-subject rewrite
would carry that same claim rather than quietly changing it to a claim
about the three of them. It reads as more likely a deliberate compression
than an accident, and I did not want to guess wrong on a sentence this
load-bearing.

**Replacement 1:** "No matter how the three of them turn it, they cannot make it more than a single person, and by the time the call ends, it has stopped being a shared problem and become Ruth's."

**Replacement 2:** "It will not come apart into more than a single person, however they turn it, and by the time the call ends, it has stopped being a shared problem and become Ruth's."

### chapters/32_the_money.md, line 101

**Current:** "Those minutes go where anything with real weight goes with him: laptop closed, out onto the back steps of his apartment building, phone still in his pocket."

**Why the voice is wrong:** "minutes" is a span of time given an active
verb of motion, "go" — again the fake-agency shape, an abstraction moving
on its own instead of Theo moving.

**Why I did not change it:** the sentence is built as a deliberate parallel
("where anything with real weight goes with him"), naming a standing habit
of Theo's rather than describing one object moving by itself the way the
folder or the notebook did elsewhere in these two chapters. I could not
tell whether that parallelism is the intended device the sentence is built
on or the same fault wearing a more literary shape, so I left it rather
than risk flattening a rhythm that looked purposeful.

**Replacement 1:** "Theo takes those minutes where he takes anything with real weight: laptop closed, out onto the back steps of his apartment building, phone still in his pocket."

**Replacement 2:** "He carries those minutes the way he carries anything with real weight, laptop closed, out onto the back steps of his apartment building, phone still in his pocket."

## Report

Sentences changed: 7 total in this pass. Six in chapter 31 (four fake-agency
sentences moved to a person doing it, one fake-agency sentence moved to a
passive that keeps its implied doer, and two person-as-subject passives
moved to active with the institution as doer), one in chapter 32
(fake-agency moved to a person doing it). Zero sentences moved from active
to passive; the direction of every fix in the two chapters is out of the
book's own fault, not into it.

Called out, not changed: 2, both in chapter 32.

Strongest call-out: chapter 32, line 37, "The shape resists being more than
a single person no matter how they turn it." It is the chapter's turn, the
line where the investigation stops being a shared project and becomes
Ruth's alone, and it is written in exactly the fake-agency shape the book
is being edited to remove. I left it because I could not tell whether the
personification is doing real work there — making a claim about the
evidence rather than about the three people reading it — or is just the
fault in a more literary coat, and a sentence carrying that much of the
chapter's weight is not one to guess on.
