# Rudeness pass, chapters 35-36

Scope: how the seven (eight, counting Priya) friends treat each other while
talking, not the content of what they discuss. Every quotation below was
copied from the source files and checked against them line by line. Line
numbers refer to the files as read from:

- `35_nine_minutes.md`
- `36_seventy_five.md`

Checked against `HOUSE_RULES.md` and `DO_NOT_FLAG.md` first. Nothing below
repeats an item already ruled deliberate (the "retarded" line in ch36 line 41
is the one that comes closest, and it is not reported here, per that ruling).

## Summary

- DELIBERATE, KEEP: 3
- UNCERTAIN: 3
- UNINTENDED, FLAG: 0

The two chapters are unusually careful with each other on the page: direct
questions are mostly answered by the person they were put to, disagreements
(Eli refusing Chloe's theory, Ruth and Priya arguing about percentiles) are
argued out in the open rather than run over, and the caretaking gestures
(Eli's list of door fixes, Sam's second flight offer, Nadia asking if Priya
wants company, Theo sending the form Priya actually needed) all land and get
acknowledged. I did not find a clean case of a sympathetic character
disregarding another that the text does not already seem aware of. What I
found instead were the chapters' existing silence-and-waiting beats, which
the brief already flags as deliberate, plus a few genuinely ambiguous spots
that are worth the author's eyes but that I would not sign my name to as
defects.

## DELIBERATE, KEEP

**1. `35_nine_minutes.md`, lines 65-67.**
> `ruth: why hasnt anybody already used this`
>
> `Nobody answers it. A minute later she tries the other thing she actually came to say.`

Ruth to the group; nobody. The non-answer is stated outright by the
narration in the same breath, which is the book marking its own silence
rather than a slip. It reads as "there is no good answer," not as anyone
snubbing her, and she visibly moves past it herself.

Theo to the group; nobody. Same device as #1: the scene answers the

## UNCERTAIN

**4. `36_seventy_five.md`, lines 69-71.**
> `ruth: priya`
>
> `priya: i got out. i have a hand that will not close all the way and that is the whole list`

Ruth's one-word line lands right after Priya mentions having time to think
about what a slowing dart meant, and reads as an alarmed interjection, a
"Priya" meant to make her stop. Priya does not acknowledge it in any way;
she continues her factual account as if it were not sent. This fits Priya's
established flatness elsewhere in the same chapter (she answers concern
with a photo of a gate and the word "sorted," and her warmest line all
chapter is a dry "thank you for the heads up, genuinely"), so it may well be
the character rather than an unmarked defect. What would settle it: whether
the author intends Priya's deflection of concern to register as her own
trait (in which case it is working as designed) or whether Ruth's "priya"
was meant to actually land and got lost in the edit.

**5. `36_seventy_five.md`, lines 95-99.**
> `priya: read it how`
>
> `kavi: long story and you have been in a field`
>
> `nadia: the short version is that it was not a normal school, and somebody has been keeping paper on what came out of it since before any of us started`

Priya asks a direct, narrow question (how the file was read/obtained).
Kavi's reply declines to answer it at all, and Nadia's answers a different
question (what the file says, not how they got it). "How" is never actually
answered anywhere later in the chapter either. This could be the group
protecting a friend who has just escaped captivity from a long explanation
she does not have bandwidth for right now, which would be considerate
rather than rude, or it could be a real dodge of a fair question from
somebody it directly concerns. What would settle it: whether "how" is meant
to stay a live thread (the book's ending is explicitly allowed to leave the
seventy-five unresolved per `DO_NOT_FLAG.md`, and this may be part of that)
or whether it was simply supposed to get answered off-page and did not.

**6. `36_seventy_five.md`, line 43.**
> `priya: these were fine. all of them. that is the part i want somebody to explain to me`

Priya's most pointed ask in the whole exchange, addressed to the group, and
no one ever takes it up; the conversation moves straight into blow-by-blow
detail instead. Softening this toward DELIBERATE: Priya does not pause for
an answer, she keeps talking in the next line, which reads more like
thinking out loud mid-monologue than a question left hanging in real time.
I could not tell from the text alone whether that is intentional (the
question resolves itself later through what the group reveals about the
school) or an unmarked drop. Flagging it because it is the one direct
appeal in the chapter that never gets picked up by anyone, in either
direction.

## What I would look at first

If the author wants to spend attention on this pass, items 5 and 6
(`read it how`, and `that is the part i want somebody to explain to me`) are
the two worth a second look, because they are the only spots where a
specific, answerable question from a sympathetic character goes past
without any narrative signal that the silence was noticed by anyone,
including the narrator. Everything else here is either already marked on
the page as intentional quiet (items 1-3, matched pairs across the two
chapters) or protected by the ending's deliberate non-resolution
(item 4). Item 4 (Ruth's unacknowledged "priya") is the softest of the
three UNCERTAIN entries and probably needs no action; it reads as Priya's
character rather than an oversight.
