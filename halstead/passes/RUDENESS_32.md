## DELIBERATE, KEEP

### 1. Theo does not answer "which four" — his own silence

- Line 135: `chloe: theo which four`
- Line 137: `He doesn't answer it that night, and the question sits there, unread, while the rest of the thread keeps going without it.`
- Who does it: Theo. Who receives it: Chloe.
- **Category: DELIBERATE, KEEP.**
- Reasoning: this is the chapter's one explicit, camera-level statement that a
  named question goes unanswered, so the book is visibly aware of it, and it
  lands on a character the same passage has already built as someone who
  filters what he shares under real stakes. Two lines earlier he has said
  "nobody repeats any of this. i mean it. this is so far above me that i
  shouldnt know it" (line 105), and the whole disclosure runs on his
  authority to decide how much comes out. Not naming which four of the group
  fought off intruders is consistent with that established caution, not a
  slip in it. Noted so the author sees it was seen, not offered as a defect.

## UNINTENDED, FLAG

### 2. Six old friends let Chloe's direct question drop and never raise it again

- Line 135: `chloe: theo which four`
- Line 137: `He doesn't answer it that night, and the question sits there, unread, while the rest of the thread keeps going without it.`
- Lines 141-153: the thread continues past midnight into a completely different
  subject (what the government's dossier is worth) and ends the chapter there;
  nobody — Ruth, Sam, Kavi, Nadia, or Eli — ever says a version of "which
  four" themselves, or tells Theo he still owes Chloe an answer.
- Who does it: the other five participants in the thread (Ruth, Sam, Kavi,
  Nadia, Eli), by omission. Who receives it: Chloe.
- **Category: UNINTENDED, FLAG.**
- Reasoning: Theo's own silence has a plausible in-character reason (see #1
  above). The rest of the group's silence does not have one supplied on the
  page. This is a group of seven who have just been told that, eight years
  earlier, four of their own friends physically fought off people coming over
  a fence at 4 a.m. — a fact with obvious personal stakes for whoever was
  standing there, which is exactly what Chloe is asking after. A close friend
  group that finds out something like that and lets one member's question
  about who was in danger simply scroll away, with nobody echoing it or
  pressing Theo on her behalf, reads as callous in a way nothing in the
  passage marks or accounts for. Chloe is not written as someone the group is
  cold to elsewhere in the chapter (Sam's unfiltered "WHAT" gets an answer;
  Ruth reassures Sam by name at line 121), which makes the one addressed,
  named question that nobody in seven people ever revisits stand out as an
  oversight rather than a chosen slight. This is the strongest candidate in
  the chapter and the one to fix first, either by having someone else in the
  thread pick the question back up (even to get the same non-answer) or by
  giving Theo's silence a beat that makes it a choice rather than an absence.

### 3. Kavi corrects Eli twice reading currency codes on the board call

- Line 37: `The three of them get on a call to look at the board properly in the second week of January, Ruth taking notes for once, Eli reading currency codes off Kavi's screen faster than the others can follow, Kavi correcting him twice on a code before he starts reading them right.`
- Who does it: Eli (reading too fast to follow, requiring the same correction
  twice). Who receives it: Kavi, and by extension the rest of the call who
  can't keep up either.
- **Category: UNCERTAIN.**
- Reasoning: this matches the author's "same question multiple times" shape,
  inverted (same correction given twice because the first didn't land), but
  it happens inside a working technical call rather than a social exchange,
  and nothing suggests Eli brushes the correction off on purpose. Ordinary
  haste under pressure reads differently from rudeness. What would settle it:
  whether Eli's habit of moving faster than the room can follow shows up
  elsewhere as a trait others react to, or whether this is a one-off born of
  the moment.

  after Sam's `sam: WHAT` (line 115) and Theo's revelation that people were

- **Category: UNCERTAIN.**
- Reasoning: `tighten the polling interval again` could be read as tone-deaf,

  a concrete response to the news that the group has been closer to being
  caught than they knew. What would settle it: whether Nadia is elsewhere
  characterized as someone who retreats into practical tasks under stress
  (which would make this a consistent trait, not a slight) or whether the
  book means this as her simply not registering the moment.

## What to fix first

Finding #2 (the group's collective silence on Chloe's question) is the one
worth an author decision. It costs nothing to keep Theo's own non-answer — that
reads as in-character — but the fact that none of the other five ever echoes
the question, presses him, or even acknowledges Chloe asked it is what makes
seven long-time friends read as less attentive to one another than the rest of
the chapter shows them being. A single added line from any other person in the
thread (even one that also goes unanswered) would be enough to make the
silence look like Theo's choice rather than the group's.
