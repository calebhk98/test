# Voice pass: chapters/07_the_same_room.md, chapters/08_the_asking.md

Scope: active/passive voice only, per the brief. House-rule issues (trailing
clauses, banned phrases, named states, etc.) were left alone even where
noticed, since they belong to a different pass.

## Changed

### chapters/07_the_same_room.md, line 66

**Before:** "in the chair that puts the phone in her sightline"

**After:** "in the chair that faces the phone"

**Why:** Fake agency. The chair isn't doing anything; it's just positioned so
Chloe can see the phone from it. "Faces" reports the chair's position the way
a camera would, instead of giving it a false action.

### chapters/07_the_same_room.md, line 168

**Before:** "she asks over her mom's shoulder while she is on the phone, until
she gets waved off, and again the moment the call ends"

**After:** "she asks over her mom's shoulder while she is on the phone, until
her mom waves her off, and again the moment the call ends"

**Why:** Rule 2.1, highest priority: a person (Chloe) was the subject of a
passive. Her mom is right there in the sentence doing the waving; the fix
just gives the action back to her.

### chapters/07_the_same_room.md, line 208

**Before:** "That brings Ms. Vance down the row to crouch all the way down
beside the desk, in front of everybody, and put a hand between Chloe's
shoulder blades and leave it there."

**After:** "Ms. Vance comes down the row to crouch all the way down beside
the desk, in front of everybody, and put a hand between Chloe's shoulder
blades and leave it there."

**Why:** Fake agency. "That" (the crying) was grammatically doing the moving;
Ms. Vance, a named person standing right there, becomes the subject instead.
The rest of the sentence's infinitive chain (to crouch... and put... and
leave...) needed no other change.

### chapters/07_the_same_room.md, line 212

**Before:** "The answer, whatever it is, refuses to come out of her."

**After:** "Whatever it is, she can't get the answer out."

**Why:** Fake agency: "the answer" was personified with a will of its own
("refuses"), when the person failing to speak is Chloe. Rewritten as an
opener other than "She/He + verb," since that measure sits at its target and
this pass is not allowed to add another hit.

### chapters/08_the_asking.md, line 45

**Before:** "Every question goes to her mother, and Chloe sits through it."

**After:** "Dr. Ammons asks every question of her mother, and Chloe sits
through it."

**Why:** Fake agency: "question" was the grammatical actor. Dr. Ammons, named
and present in the room, is the one actually directing every question at the
mother. "Chloe sits through it" is untouched: that clause already carries
the chapter's point (she is sidelined in her own evaluation); naming who is
asking doesn't cost it anything.

### chapters/08_the_asking.md, line 185

**Before:** "but the rest of the sentence stays stuck behind that one"

**After:** "but she can't get the rest of it past that one"

**Why:** Fake agency, the same pattern as the chapter 7 fix above: "the
sentence" was personified as stuck, when it's Chloe who can't make herself
say the rest of it.

**Tally: 6 sentences changed.** 1 was a person in the subject of a passive
(the top-priority rule). 5 were fake agency, all fixed the same way: replace
the inanimate/abstract actor with the real person already in the scene. None
required turning an active into a passive or vice versa in the ordinary
sense: no defensible passive-should-be-active or active-should-be-passive
sentences turned up in either chapter outside of these.

## Called out, not changed

### chapters/07_the_same_room.md, line 116

**Current:** "The first math sheet arrives midmorning, and she squints at it
before she can tell that it's adding with carrying, which she learned last
year and can still picture: the little box that you write the one in, and
Mrs. Aldana drawing that box in green marker, thirty of them on the page."

**Why the voice is wrong:** "Arrives" gives the sheet its own motion; someone
(presumably Ms. Vance) hands it out.

**Why I did not change it:** School worksheets showing up at a set point in
the morning read close to the book's own "systems on a clock" exception,
this is routine, scheduled classroom motion, not a transaction like "it goes
to Deb." I was not confident this is the fault the rule means to catch rather
than the timer exception, and naming Ms. Vance as the one handing it out
invents a specific fact the sentence doesn't give me.

**Replacement 1:** "The first math sheet is handed out midmorning, and she
squints at it before she can tell that it's adding with carrying, which she
learned last year and can still picture: the little box that you write the
one in, and Mrs. Aldana drawing that box in green marker, thirty of them on
the page."

**Replacement 2:** "Ms. Vance hands out the first math sheet midmorning, and
she squints at it before she can tell that it's adding with carrying, which
she learned last year and can still picture: the little box that you write
the one in, and Mrs. Aldana drawing that box in green marker, thirty of them
on the page."

### chapters/07_the_same_room.md, line 164

**Current:** "The library card ends up in the drawer in the kitchen where the
batteries are, and weeks later, when her mom asks whether she wants to go on
Saturday, Chloe says no thank you but asks her to renew them please."

**Why the voice is wrong:** "Ends up" hides whoever actually put the card in
that drawer, an inanimate object doing something no object does on its own.

**Why I did not change it:** This is one of the chapter's own passives-with-a
-point. The library habit is dying out from under her without anyone,
including Chloe, deciding it: that's the same drift the rest of the chapter
is tracking (she stops finishing books, stops going). Naming a hand that put
the card away (hers, her mom's) would turn a drift into a decision and I
could not tell which the scene wants. This is the kind of sentence the brief
told me to flag rather than guess at.

**Replacement 1:** "Chloe puts the library card in the drawer in the kitchen
where the batteries are, and weeks later, when her mom asks whether she wants
to go on Saturday, Chloe says no thank you but asks her to renew them
please."

**Replacement 2:** "The library card sits in the drawer in the kitchen where
the batteries are now, and weeks later, when her mom asks whether she wants
to go on Saturday, Chloe says no thank you but asks her to renew them
please."

### chapters/08_the_asking.md, line 55

**Current:** "The questions keep coming in that voice, working down through
who ran it, how they found her, whether Chloe came back different, in what
way, and starting when."

**Why the voice is wrong:** "The questions" is the grammatical actor, and
Dr. Ammons, sitting right there asking them, is not in the sentence at all,
the same fake-agency pattern I fixed nine lines earlier at line 45 in this
chapter.

**Why I did not change it:** I already made this exact fix once in this
scene (line 45). A second fix ten lines later would drive the technique to
zero in a single interview, and the house rules are explicit that an
overused technique gets cut back, not eliminated: plus there's a real case
that the impersonal, procedural feel here (a clinical interview experienced
as a sequence of questions rather than a person asking them) is doing
something for the scene rather than just hiding an actor. I was not
confident enough to take that away.

**Replacement 1:** "Dr. Ammons keeps asking, in that same voice, working
down through who ran it, how they found her, whether Chloe came back
different, in what way, and starting when."

**Replacement 2:** "She keeps the questions coming in that same voice,
working down through who ran it, how they found her, whether Chloe came
back different, in what way, and starting when."

### chapters/08_the_asking.md, line 203

**Current:** "Her mom waits, sitting very still on the end of the bed; that
is all of it: a hallway with a brass plate on the last door that says PLUTO,
a boy honking with laughter about a room-shaped object, standing there
afterward with the hallway tile cold enough through her socks to notice, and
being allowed to be wrong for a whole afternoon."

**Why the voice is wrong:** "Being allowed" puts Chloe (understood, not
named, as the last item in the list) into the receiving end of a passive,
close to the book's flat rule against a person in the subject of a passive,
though the sentence never actually names her as the grammatical subject; the
whole clause is a floating gerund in a list of images.

**Why I did not change it:** This is the sentence that answers Dr. Ammons's
question about what was actually different at Halstead, and the answer this
pass is built around is "nobody was mean": being allowed to get something
wrong without consequence is the positive shape of that same idea. An active
version ("nobody corrected her," "no one told her she was wrong") reads
colder and more clinical, and I could not find a rewrite that kept the warmth
of "allowed" without a passive doing the work. I'd rather flag it than guess
wrong on a sentence carrying that much of the chapter's point.

**Replacement 1:** "Her mom waits, sitting very still on the end of the bed;
that is all of it: a hallway with a brass plate on the last door that says
PLUTO, a boy honking with laughter about a room-shaped object, standing there
afterward with the hallway tile cold enough through her socks to notice, and
a whole afternoon where nobody corrected her."

**Replacement 2:** "Her mom waits, sitting very still on the end of the bed;
that is all of it: a hallway with a brass plate on the last door that says
PLUTO, a boy honking with laughter about a room-shaped object, standing there
afterward with the hallway tile cold enough through her socks to notice, and
a whole afternoon of being allowed to be wrong."

### chapters/08_the_asking.md, line 241: strongest call-out

**Current:** "It happens without her, somewhere in the last week of January,
so that standing at the sink brushing her teeth she works out that she
hasn't asked in two weeks, and the strawberry magnet on the fridge is holding
up a grocery list now instead of the note."

**Why the voice is wrong:** "It happens without her" is active voice with
nobody in it in its purest form in these two chapters: no person, no
institution, not even a body part standing in for one. Just "it," happening.

**Why I did not change it:** This is the sentence the whole chapter has been
walking toward. Chloe spent months asking, in every way a nine-year-old has
available (a chore list on the pillow, a mark for every day without a yes,
skipping her mom to try her dad), and then she just... stops, without ever
choosing to. Giving that an actor, "she stops asking" or "she gives up",
would turn an erosion into a decision, and the entire point, right down to
finding out about it after the fact while brushing her teeth, is that it
wasn't one. This is exactly the kind of sentence the brief warned me about:
a passive (or here, agentless active) that is the chapter, not a mistake in
it. I'm flagging it anyway because it is, on its face, the single clearest
instance of the book's own fault in either chapter, and the author should
see it and rule on it rather than have it fixed or missed silently.

**Replacement 1:** "Somewhere in the last week of January she stops asking
without ever deciding to, so that standing at the sink brushing her teeth she
works out that she hasn't asked in two weeks, and the strawberry magnet on
the fridge is holding up a grocery list now instead of the note."

**Replacement 2 (no change; argument for leaving it):** "It happens without
her, somewhere in the last week of January, so that standing at the sink
brushing her teeth she works out that she hasn't asked in two weeks, and the
strawberry magnet on the fridge is holding up a grocery list now instead of
the note."
