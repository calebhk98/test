# Chloe and Nadia

The author's description of the relationship: they get along enough to work
together and are not unkind to each other, but they do not mesh, and they do
not seek each other out. That does not change until well after graduation,
around chapter 30 and after. This pass exists because a reader took the
chapter 16 line about Sam sitting between them as possible romantic tension,
and that ambiguity is what the author asked to have removed.

Chapters 1 and 2 are locked and contain no Nadia material. Chapter 27 is
Nadia's own chapter and was read but not touched or reassigned.

## Full interaction list

Found by grepping every chapter for "Nadia" and reading each hit in context.
Nadia has scenes without Chloe present (her own applications, her own shop,
her chapter 27) that are not listed here because they are not Chloe-Nadia
interactions; the list below is every scene where the two of them are
actually in the room, or in the same chat thread, together.

1. **Chapter 13, "Ten Pages," line 105.** Background sentence only: Nadia gets
   a code "off her own back" the same month Kavi and Priya get theirs. Chloe
   is not in the scene. Not counted as an interaction.

2. **Chapter 16, "Thirteen," lines 147-193.** The dinner-table cipher scene.
   Twelve of them eat together most nights; Eli puts a napkin cipher on the
   table, Nadia turns it toward Chloe, and the two of them go back and forth
   testing each other's read of it ("You always go quiet like this... you've
   got it and you'd rather keep it off the table") until Chloe cracks it and
   Nadia concedes, flatly, "She's right. It's both." The chapter then
   summarizes the pattern for the rest of the year: Nadia is there most
   nights, blunt on principle so that half of what she says lands as an
   accusation, and they work a whole cipher set together and it goes well.
   **This is the flagged scene.**
   - **Read:** the sparring itself (lines 165-189) is correctly neutral: two
     sharp people testing a claim, no warmth, no hostility, resolved on the
     facts. The summary sentence that follows it (**"though Sam sits between
     them most nights and neither of them has ever said a word to him about
     it"**) is the problem. "Neither of them has ever said a word to him
     about it" implies there is an "it," something unspoken between the two
     of them that Sam is a buffer against. A reader has no way to resolve
     that "it" toward neutral, and fills it in as attraction (why else would
     two people need a third person between them and never mention it) or
     as suppressed rivalry. Read as **romantic-ambiguous** as written.
   - **Changed.** Cut the "neither of them has ever said a word to him about
     it" clause, which is what was doing the damage, and replaced it with a
     plain fact about their working relationship that matches the author's
     brief directly: they work well together and do not extend it. New text:
     "They work the November cipher set together and it goes well, Sam
     between them most nights the same as always, and once the set is done
     neither of them starts another one." Sam's seat is kept (it is a real,
     camera-visible habit and it pays off again in chapter 34), but the
     insinuation of an unspoken "it" is gone. What replaces it is a fact
     about scope: the collaboration is real and it is bounded, which is
     exactly the texture asked for.

3. **Chapter 17, "Fourteen," lines 115-175.** The wireless-sound-array
   project. Nadia takes the practical, labor-costed view of the engineering
   question ("A wire is people... every hand out there is a hand that isn't
   somewhere else"), gets overruled, is right, and is deferred to when the
   group goes back to wire. In April, Chloe redraws the placement on her own
   and brings it to Nadia at breakfast; Nadia's whole response is "That's
   better," and she files it.
   - **Read:** correctly neutral-but-not-close. This is two competent people
     doing a joint project competently, with the minimum words the work
     needs. Chloe seeks Nadia out here, but for a folder, not for company,
     and gets two words back.
   - **Left alone.** This already is the target texture: useful to each
     other, no more than the work requires.

4. **Chapter 18, "Fifteen," line 43.** Chloe has the range cards spread over
   the dinner table, working out why her scores plateaued. Nadia walks past
   with a tray, delivers one accurate, slightly needling read of what
   Chloe's doing ("You're looking for people who stopped where you stopped,
   so you can find out whether it comes back"), and sits down at the far end
   of the table to eat, alone.
   - **Read:** correctly neutral-but-not-close, and one of the cleanest
     examples in the book of it. She engages for one line, is right, and
     then physically removes herself to the far end rather than staying at
     Chloe's end of the table.
   - **Left alone.** This is doing the author's job for him already.

5. **Chapter 19, "Sixteen," lines 93-95.** Chloe writes practice law papers
   over the summer; Kavi marks some, Nadia marks some. "Kavi's marking fills
   the margin. Nadia marks by ruling a line down the side of a paragraph and
   writing *said nothing* beside it; there is nothing else anywhere on the
   page."
   - **Read:** correctly neutral-but-not-close, bordering on too blank, but
     it reads as Nadia's established voice (see her sheet: short, exact,
     scaled to the offence, no decoration) rather than as coldness aimed at
     Chloe specifically, since the same paragraph shows Kavi doing the
     opposite with the same papers. It is contrast between two markers, not
     a verdict on the relationship.
   - **Left alone.** Reads as characterization, not as a relationship
     signal, and re-reading it as one would be reaching.

6. **Chapter 19, line 179.** Chloe tells her father about Nadia's
   negotiation-board score while recapping her year. Nadia is not present.
   Not counted as an interaction.

7. **Chapter 20, "The Parking Lot," lines 7-163.** The overnight heist
   chapter. Contains several beats:
   - Line 51-57: "You take the thing you're worst at and you stay in it,"
     Nadia says, to the table rather than to Chloe, about the forge. Chloe
     pushes back hard ("Pick something you've actually watched me do before
     you hand me a pattern"). Nadia repeats the point, sharper and more
     accurate ("You're just still the only one who's there every morning
     nobody's making you be"), and goes back to dividing the bill without
     waiting to see how it lands. Chloe goes quiet over her food.
   - Lines 91-109: the fence vault, the fight, and the aftermath. Nadia,
     Chloe, and Ruth work as a drilled unit with no negotiation needed; this
     is competence, not warmth.
   - Lines 149-159: group banter about sending Sam to fight the waitress.
     Nadia takes part in this the same way Ruth does; nothing in it singles
     out Chloe.
   - **Read:** correctly neutral-but-not-close throughout, and the "worst at
     it" exchange is one of the clearest pieces of legitimate friction in
     the whole set: an accurate, unwelcome read, delivered flatly, that
     costs Chloe something and that Nadia doesn't chase or soften. It is not
     bullying (it's true, and Nadia isn't gloating) and it is not warm.
   - **Left alone.** This is close to a model instance of the target and is
     one of the "two or three signals" the chapter set is already carrying.
     No changes needed, and adding anything here would be piling a signal
     onto a chapter that already has one doing its job.

8. **Chapter 21, "The Applications," lines 61, 151, 199-219.** Nadia fills
   out job applications in the same room as Chloe (background presence, no
   exchange); Chloe later borrows a drawing out of "Nadia's folder"
   (transactional). The real interaction is lines 207-219: Ruth suggests
   Chloe read one of Nadia's applications, Nadia gets a dig in first
   ("Chloe writes four pages when they ask for six hundred"), Chloe goes
   quiet, then defends herself, and Nadia, after "a second longer than she
   has looked at her all evening," pushes the notebook across and asks for
   the real help anyway. Chloe reads it, Ruth checks it too, and "both of
   them keep their seats."
   - **Read:** correctly neutral-but-not-close, with a genuine
     useful-to-each-other beat underneath the needling. Considered the
     "looks at her a second longer than she has looked at her all evening"
     clause specifically, since a lingering look is a common shorthand for
     attraction elsewhere in fiction. In context it reads as Nadia deciding
     whether asking is worth the cost (which matches her sheet: "asking for
     help costs her, and the cost shows as a pause"), not as noticing Chloe
     in particular, and the scene right after it is entirely transactional.
   - **Left alone.** The signal here is deliberately weak, and the author
     asked for two or three clear signals, not a pass over every borderline
     clause in the book. Flagging it here in case a future reader reads it
     the other way, but it did not meet the bar for a change on this pass.

9. **Chapter 24, "The Chat," medication thread, lines 457-511.** The group
   chat tries to identify a discontinued medication; when the group's
   channel dead-ends on "internal," Chloe goes around it by photographing an
   old box label and sending it to Nadia alone, not the group. Nadia comes
   back within the hour with three chemical names off a supplier's safety
   sheet. The original closing line read: "The chat moves on within minutes.
   Chloe's tab stays open on her desk for the rest of the evening, the group
   thread gone still while the other one, the one with Nadia, keeps lighting
   up."
   - **Read:** the DM itself (Chloe goes to Nadia specifically because Nadia
     can actually get the answer, and gets it) is exactly the
     useful-to-each-other texture the author wants, and needed no change.
     The closing sentence was the problem: "keeps lighting up" describes an
     ongoing, active private exchange, set directly against the group
     thread "gone still," which is a familiar shorthand in fiction for
     where a character's real attention has gone. Nothing later in the
     chapter or book cashes that image in, so it reads as **warmer than
     intended**, an unclosed loop of intimacy rather than a single
     transaction that ended when the information arrived.
   - **Changed.** Replaced the tail clause so both threads go quiet at the
     same time: "The chat moves on within minutes. Chloe's tab stays open on
     her desk for the rest of the evening, the group thread gone still and
     the one with Nadia gone still right behind it." The favor is still
     exactly as useful and exactly as private (there was a real reason for
     it to be private: it is Chloe's own body, not group business), but the
     private line does not linger open while the group one closes.

10. **Chapter 27, "Nadia."** Nadia's own chapter. Checked; Chloe does not
    appear in it at all. Not reassigned, not touched.

11. **Chapter 34, "The Files," lines 91-93. AFTER CHAPTER 29, NOT EDITED.**
    The group is reading a leaked government file on all of them at once.
    "Chloe reads yes twice before anyone types again... Nadia, back at the
    counter with the shop's lights off around her, reads that silence and
    stays exactly where she's standing... On the page about who is in
    contact with whom, Chloe and Nadia sit in the same list under the same
    date. In the chat it is Sam between them, as it has been at every table
    since they were children, and neither of them has ever said a word to
    him about it."
    - **This is a near-verbatim echo of the original, unfixed chapter 16
      line** ("Sam between them... neither of them has ever said a word to
      him about it"), and it carries the identical ambiguity: standing alone,
      it still reads as if there is an unspoken "it." Since it comes after
      chapter 29, it is outside this pass's edit window and was left exactly
      as written. Flagging it for the author because the fix made in
      chapter 16 does not carry forward to this line automatically, and by
      chapter 34 the surrounding scene (a leaked file, real danger, "at
      every table since they were children") may be doing enough work to
      recolor the line as long, quiet constancy rather than mystery, in
      which case it is fine where it is; that is a call for the author, not
      for this pass.

12. **Chapters 32, 33, 36.** Checked for Chloe-Nadia scenes. None found; both

## What changed and why (summary)

Two chapters edited, two clauses changed, nothing else:

- **Chapter 16** (`chapters/16_thirteen.md`): cut the implication that there
  is an unspoken "it" between Chloe and Nadia that Sam's seat exists to avoid,
  and replaced it with a plain statement that the collaboration is real and
  bounded. This is the fix the reader's flag was actually about.
- **Chapter 24** (`chapters/24_the_chat.md`): cut the image of the private
  thread staying lit while the group thread goes dark, which read as
  emotional intimacy the author did not ask for at this point in the book.

Both edits are clause-level, exact-string replacements, verified against the
files after editing. Word counts: chapter 16 is 4,096 words (was 4,095);
chapter 24 is 3,782 words (was 3,783). Both remain inside the 2,000-5,000
band. `measures/style_report.py` was run on both chapters before and after;
the conjunction, sentence-length, and section-break numbers are unchanged
within rounding, and the tic scan returns no hits on either chapter, both
before and after.

## What was deliberately left alone

Chapters 17, 18, 19, and 20 already carry the texture the author described,
in each case with a different one of the required signals: chapter 17 is
transactional collaboration, chapter 18 is Nadia physically choosing not to
sit with Chloe after saying her piece, chapter 19 is a difference in marking
style rather than a comment on the relationship, and chapter 20 carries the
one piece of real, legitimate friction ("you're just still the only one
who's there every morning nobody's making you be") that does the most work
in the whole set. The "looks at her a second longer" clause in chapter 21 was
considered and left alone as a weak, explainable signal rather than a clear
one; the author asked for two or three clear signals, not a sweep of every
line that could theoretically be misread.

## Open item for the author

Chapter 34's echo of the chapter 16 line (see item 11 above) was not edited
because it falls after chapter 29. It carries the same ambiguity the chapter
16 line did before this pass. Worth a read in the context of wherever the
turn actually lands, to decide whether the echo should change to match, or
whether the surrounding scene already reads it differently by that point in
