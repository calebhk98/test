## Job 1: the word "but"

Read every "and" in each chapter and converted the ones joining a genuine
contrast to "but" — a success next to a discounted reward, a claim next to
its reversal, an effort next to its outcome. Left mechanical, non-contrastive
"and"s alone; a few chapters (17, 19, 20, 22, 24) land under the 0.4% corpus
floor as a result rather than being padded to hit it. Chat transcripts
(ch24) were left alone entirely, as instructed.

## Job 2: letting people say more than one sentence

The dominant pattern found across the span was a character's continuous
thought split into two `"..."` spans by a dialogue tag or an action beat —
the measuring script counts each span separately, so a single three-sentence
answer was scoring as two one-sentence quotations. Fixed by moving the tag
or beat to the front (or occasionally the end) of the turn so the whole
thought sits inside one pair of quotation marks. Also collapsed a handful of
real "one-word prompt, then the rest" exchanges (ch16's recorder-device Q&A,
ch20's police-report Q&A) by folding the prompt into the answering turn.

Left clipped throughout, as instructed: every Sam line, the interrogation
scenes in ch15 and ch17, and all chat-transcript exchanges (ch24, plus the
lowercase turns embedded in ch16/ch20). Chapters 15 and 17 are within a few
dozen words of the 5,000 ceiling, so their merges were chosen specifically
because removing a tag or a repeated "X says" costs words rather than adding
them — both chapters came out at or under their pre-pass count.

## Job 3: what reads as advanced

This span had already been through a prior reading-grade and trailing-clause
pass (see git history), and it shows: a close read of all twelve chapters
turned up very little actual literary-fiction vocabulary or crafted
rhetorical balance. Grepped for the standard tells (nevertheless, myriad,
ineffable, palpable, thence, semicolon overuse, "however" as a formal
conjunction) and found none — the three "however"s in ch22 are all the
colloquial "however long it takes" sense, not the literary one. What did
turn up:

| chapter | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| 13 | "the other side appears nowhere on the page **in a form** its own supporters would recognise" | "...**in a way** its own supporters would believe" |
| 21 | "**Since the middle of a year gets what the middle of any year gets,** the state school is what she is expecting." (periodic sentence, verb held to the end, rhetorical echo) | "The middle of a year always ends up at the state school, so that's what she is expecting." |
| 24 | "This is the first September **that finds** all of them somewhere else." (personification) | "This is the first September **when** all of them are somewhere else." |

Reported honestly rather than padded: the span's remaining long sentences
(ch17's engineering brainstorm, ch19's bar-exam opening, ch23's graduation
paragraphs) are long because the book's house style is long compound
sentences joined by commas, not because they're dressed up — per the brief,
that voice was left alone.

## Job 4: chapter 22's reading grade

Baseline: Flesch-Kincaid 6.80, band floor 7.0 (chapters 16-22, ages 13-19).
Combined six pairs of adjacent short narration sentences into single
sentences — no dialogue touched, no word added without one being folded in
elsewhere:

- "At fifteen minutes exactly he stands. Chloe is already up." -> "...he
  stands, and Chloe is already up."
- "...the figure she gave him. A second count gets there too." -> joined
  with "and".
- "Nadia spends the wait in the corridor finishing an application on her
  phone, the ninth of that particular week. She is in the chair..." -> one
  sentence.
- "...gets the voice the rest of it gets. The boy across from her wants to
  know whether she is sure." -> one sentence.
- "Then he goes round the ones who have already been in... told anybody at
  home yet. In the morning the roster goes up..." -> one sentence.
- "...all of their names go up inside the first few days. The last breaks
  it: he's a boy from the language block..." -> joined with "except for the
  last, which breaks it".
- One dialogue-tag beat merged: "already moving. He's past her..." -> "...
  already moving, then past her..."

Result: FK 6.998 -> after one more merge, 7.031, verified with
`grade.py --one chapters/22_the_offer.md` showing `pass`. Word count moved
from 4,135 to 4,142 (+7, still mid-band). The 16-22 band average rose from
7.25 to 7.28; chapter 22 no longer appears in `grade.py`'s under-floor list.

**Note for the author:** chapters 15 (grade 5.0, floor 6.0) and 20 (grade
6.0, floor 7.0) are also individually under their band floors and were not
touched, because JOB 4 named only chapter 22. Both band averages still clear
their floors (11-15 at 6.68 vs floor 6.0; 16-22 at 7.28 vs floor 7.0), so no
house rule is violated, but flagging them in case a future pass is meant to
reach them too. Neither number moved during this pass — the Job 1/Job 2
edits made to those two chapters were conjunction swaps and dialogue-tag
repositioning, which don't materially change sentence length or word
difficulty.
