# Voice pass: chapters/05_behind.md, chapters/06_the_list.md

Sentence-by-sentence pass for passive-where-should-be-active, active-where-should-be-passive,
and fake agency (active voice with nobody in it), per the house rules and the voice brief.
Dialogue and chat lines were not touched. Nine sentences changed, all in the
active direction (six removing a person from the subject of a passive, three
removing fake agency); no case called for the reverse (active made passive).
Two borderline instances were left alone and are written up below.

## Changed

### chapters/05_behind.md, line 27

**Before:** "Chloe is put with Sam and Kavi, but Ruth, assigned to the next table, comes across anyway before the glue has finished heating."

**After:** "Mr. Doyle puts Chloe with Sam and Kavi, then sends Ruth to the next table, but she comes across anyway before the glue has finished heating."

**Why:** "Chloe is put" is a person in the subject of a passive, the highest-priority fix in the brief. Mr. Doyle is the teacher running the activity (he delivers the glue-gun rule two sentences earlier), so naming him as the one doing the assigning is not an invented fact.

### chapters/05_behind.md, line 51

**Before:** "Sam wants a hundred, is told the number has to be one he believes, but comes down a long way;"

**After:** "Sam wants a hundred, but Mr. Doyle tells him the number has to be one he believes, so he comes down a long way;"

**Why:** "is told" puts Sam in the subject of a passive, again matching the "he was asked" example directly. Mr. Doyle is running the bridge exercise in the same scene.

### chapters/05_behind.md, line 77

**Before:** "A rectangle appears, half of it shaded, then the shaded half cut into pieces while he talks through the question underneath the question; Chloe follows him as far as the shading, but loses track of what the pieces are pieces of, and is left looking at a rectangle with lines across it."

**After:** "Mr. Baptiste draws a rectangle, shades half of it, then cuts the shaded half into pieces while he talks through the question underneath the question; Chloe follows him as far as the shading, but loses track of what the pieces are pieces of, and ends up looking at a rectangle with lines across it."

**Why:** Textbook fake agency: "a rectangle appears" and "the shaded half cut into pieces" give an inanimate subject the action while the actual doer, Mr. Baptiste, is named two sentences earlier and is still the "he" in the same sentence. Also changed "is left looking at" to "ends up looking at" while already in the sentence, since "is left" is a passive form with Chloe as its subject.

### chapters/05_behind.md, line 79

**Before:** "Kavi says he already knew it, out of a book, and is told that this is fine but he should listen anyway;"

**After:** "Kavi says he already knew it, out of a book, and Mr. Baptiste tells him that this is fine but he should listen anyway;"

**Why:** Same pattern as line 51: Kavi, a person, is the subject of "is told." Mr. Baptiste is running the lesson in the same scene.

### chapters/05_behind.md, line 125

**Before:** "Swimming is the last thing every afternoon, and because Chloe arrived unable to swim she is put in the group at the shallow end of the pool with an instructor called Deb, who has them blow bubbles with their faces in the water for two days before anybody moves on."

**After:** "Swimming is the last thing every afternoon, and because Chloe arrived unable to swim, an instructor called Deb puts her in the group at the shallow end of the pool, where they blow bubbles with their faces in the water for two days before anybody moves on."

**Why:** "she is put" is a person in the subject of a passive, and Deb, the doer, is named in the very same sentence. Rewritten so Deb does the putting; kept the second "and" out of the sentence by using "where" instead, so the conjunction count did not go up.

### chapters/06_the_list.md, line 41

**Before:** "Others take that up; by the time somebody's kid has come over needing something and been sorted out, half the table has gone."

**After:** "Others take that up; by the time somebody's kid has come over needing something and a parent has sorted it out, half the table has gone."

**Why:** "been sorted out" puts a child in the subject of a passive. The scene is a parents' breakfast, so "a parent" is the doer already implied by the setting, not an invented character.

### chapters/06_the_list.md, line 55

**Before:** "Then dividing fractions, which she does on a napkin with a pen taken off the next table, drawing the rectangles the way Mr. Baptiste drew them and running all the way through to why you turn the second fraction over, so that the working reaches the edge of the napkin and comes back, while her dad leans in over the napkin."

**After:** "Then dividing fractions, which she does on a napkin with a pen taken off the next table, drawing the rectangles the way Mr. Baptiste drew them, running all the way through to why you turn the second fraction over until she reaches the edge of the napkin and keeps writing on the back, while her dad leans in over the napkin."

**Why:** "the working reaches the edge of the napkin and comes back" is the fake-agency fault: an abstraction (the working) given the active verb while Chloe, sitting right there as the sentence's real subject, is the one actually writing. Made her the subject of that clause instead. This also dropped the sentence from two "and"s to one, which helps rather than hurts a chapter already over the "and" ceiling.

### chapters/06_the_list.md, line 57

**Before:** "A few tables over, another father is sitting through a version of it, a pen going on a different napkin while his own daughter talks him through her month."

**After:** "A few tables over, another father is sitting through a version of it, his own daughter running a pen across a different napkin while she talks him through her month."

**Why:** "a pen going on a different napkin" is fake agency, an inanimate subject with an active verb, when the doer, "his own daughter," is named in the same clause.

### chapters/06_the_list.md, line 71

**Before:** "...and then the paintings they were taken to look at, one of which cost more than a house, which baffles her, because it is a painting."

**After:** "...and then the paintings they went to look at, one of which cost more than a house, which baffles her, because it is a painting."

**Why:** "they were taken" puts the children in the subject of a passive. No specific adult is named anywhere in the text as the one who took them, so naming a person would invent a fact; making the children the ones who "went" removes the passive without inventing a doer.

## Called out, not changed

### chapters/05_behind.md, line 7

**Current:** "The next thing arrives the moment the last is done, all morning and all afternoon, so that Tuesday passes without any of the empty stretches she had budgeted for, the kind she used to spend with a paperback wedged into her desk, its corner against her palm."

**Why the voice is wrong:** "The next thing arrives" gives an inanimate subject ("the next thing," meaning the next worksheet or assignment) an active verb, with no teacher or student named as the one handing it over.

**Why I did not change it:** This reads as a summary of the whole camp's pace across the opening paragraph, not one classroom moment with a teacher standing in it, and I could not find a specific doer anywhere nearby to put in the subject without inventing one. It may also be the kind of "system on a clock" the rules exempt, since the point of the sentence is that work arrives continuously rather than that any one person hands it over. I was not confident enough either way to touch it.

**Replacement 1:** "The next thing is on her desk the moment the last is done, all morning and all afternoon, so that Tuesday passes without any of the empty stretches she had budgeted for, the kind she used to spend with a paperback wedged into her desk, its corner against her palm."

**Replacement 2:** "She picks up the next thing the moment the last is done, all morning and all afternoon, so that Tuesday passes without any of the empty stretches she had budgeted for, the kind she used to spend with a paperback wedged into her desk, its corner against her palm."

### chapters/05_behind.md, line 113

**Current:** "A second try gets her no further, so Chloe draws it a third time with her own numbers, because his numbers have been sitting in her head since the lesson and taking her nowhere: three quarters divided by an eighth."

**Why the voice is wrong:** "A second try gets her no further" hands the active verb to an abstraction (the try) instead of to Chloe, who is doing the trying and sitting right there in the surrounding sentences.

**Why I did not change it:** This one is milder than the flagged examples in the brief, which are about a physical object like a sheet or a name moving on its own; here the "actor" is an event noun standing in for an attempt she made, which is a common and fairly settled English construction. I was not confident this is the fault the brief means rather than ordinary phrasing, so I left it rather than guess.

**Replacement 1:** "She tries a second time and gets no further, so Chloe draws it a third time with her own numbers, because his numbers have been sitting in her head since the lesson and taking her nowhere: three quarters divided by an eighth."

**Replacement 2:** "Trying it a second time gets her no further, so Chloe draws it a third time with her own numbers, because his numbers have been sitting in her head since the lesson and taking her nowhere: three quarters divided by an eighth."
