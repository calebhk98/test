# Variant B — Sit in the Chair: Chapter 21, "The Applications"

Method: walked the chapter in order. At every point someone acts or is spoken to, I
occupied them for three lines (who I am / what I've just been given or asked / what I'd
actually do), then checked that against what the text has them do. Chloe is walked the
same way at every point she's given, told, or asked something. All quotes verified
against `chapters/21_the_applications.md` with line numbers from that file. After the
walk I cross-checked against `SYNOPSIS_CHARACTERS_TIMELINE.md`, `chronology/BOOK.md`, and
the character sheet for every named person in the chapter (CHLOE, RUTH, SAM, KAVI,
ODILE, HEARN, FEN, MEG — Léo has no standalone sheet; he's covered under ODILE.md).

## The walk

### Ruth, opening the MIT number (lines 11–38)

I am Ruth, a Halstead senior, best mathematician in a year of ninety, sitting with a
laptop open in the common room. I've just read a number off MIT's own site. What I'd
actually do: state it flat, then follow it wherever the evidence goes, including against
myself.

Text: *"'MIT says four percent,' Ruth says."* (11) → *"'Neither.' Ruth's eyes stay on the
laptop. 'You'd think the sign was doing a job that had nothing to do with the store.'"*
(32) → *"Ruth is quiet for a second. 'That's probably it,' she says, and closes the
laptop."* (38). Match: this is exactly her signature move — accept the better argument
without defending the position she opened with, go quiet rather than contest it out loud.
No finding.

### Meg (mother), accepting "fine" (lines 44)

essay, same applicant, two different page counts within one chapter.
