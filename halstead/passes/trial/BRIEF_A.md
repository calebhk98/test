# Variant A: predict, then read

You are testing a chapter by predicting it. Do not edit anything. Do not
rewrite. Your output is one report.

The method is simple and you must not shortcut it, because the shortcut produces
nothing.

## How to work

Go through the chapter **in order, in small steps of one to three paragraphs**.
At each step, before you read the next step:

1. Write one line saying where the story now stands.
2. **Write your prediction.** What happens next, and more importantly, what the
   people in this scene do next. If somebody has just been handed something,
   said something, or asked something, write what a real person in that role
   would do about it. Be specific: name the action.
3. Read the next step.
4. Compare. If the text does something different from your prediction, ask
   which of you is right. Sometimes the text will surprise you correctly, and
   that is good writing. Sometimes the text will do something a person in that
   position would not do, and that is a finding.

Write every divergence down, whether or not you think it is a fault, with the
quote and one line on what you expected instead.

## The two questions that matter most

**What would a real one do?** For every professional in the chapter, ask what a
real person doing that job would actually do in that moment: what they would
say, what they would check, what would worry them, what they would refuse. If
the text has them do something a real one would not, that is a finding, and it
is the most valuable kind.

**What is missing?** A person receives news and does nothing about it. Somebody
asks a question and nobody answers it. A thing is handed over and never used.
These are findings even though there is no sentence to quote, and you should
quote the sentence on either side of the gap instead.

## After the walk

Read `SYNOPSIS_CHARACTERS_TIMELINE.md`, `chronology/BOOK.md`, and the sheet in
`characters/` for every named person in the chapter. Then go back over your
divergence list and add any where a character does something their own sheet
says they would not, or knows something they could not know, or works at a rate
that contradicts what the book establishes elsewhere.

## Rules

- Quote verbatim, with line numbers, and check the quote against the file.
- One line per finding. No essays.
- Include the ones you are unsure about, marked *unsure*.
- Do not report prose style, rhythm, word repetition, or quality.
- Do not rewrite. Do not edit the chapter.

Write to the report path you were given and stop.
