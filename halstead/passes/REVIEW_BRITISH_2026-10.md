# British spelling and idiom: items for the author to decide

Compiled 2026-10-23. Nothing in the chapters has been changed for this list, with one exception noted below. Every line number and sentence was re-read from the current files in `halstead/chapters/`, not copied from the fix-agent reports, because several chapters were rewritten after those reports were written. ASCII only.

**Totals:** 67 distinct terms in the main list (clear British forms) with 253 instances, plus 15 terms in the borderline list (forms that are also normal or acceptable American English, or were only mildly flagged) with 53 instances. Combined: 82 terms, 306 instances (320 individual word occurrences; an instance is one sentence, so a sentence with two hits counts once). Of the 306 instances, 225 are narration and 81 are dialogue or chat.

## 0. Read this first

- **Already changed (one item):** Ch 14, "it's worth thirty pounds" became "it's worth thirty dollars" (current text, Ch 14 line 97). It was treated as a fact fix because the currency is wrong for the US, not as a spelling fix. You can revert it if you want it back; "pounds" here would only make sense as British currency, so it is the one item in this list that is wrong on the facts, not just in flavor.
- **Characters:** I checked every sheet in `halstead/characters/`. No character is British or from the Commonwealth. Speakers are American throughout: Ruth (eastern Iowa), Sam (Georgia), Kavi (Mid-Atlantic university town), Odile (Maine, French-speaking household), Fen (Detroit), and the remaining students and staff, none of whose sheets give a non-American origin; Meg and Dave are Chloe's Pennsylvania parents. The nearest cases are Nadia, who is the US-raised child of immigrants from Gujarat (her sheet gives no British tie, and she says "fortnight", "rung me up", "rang the shop" and "till" in this book), and Baptiste, whose family is French-Caribbean (he does not speak any line in this list). So **no line below is excused by the speaker.** Nobody in the book is a British character whose voice you would be flattening. The one non-American background I found among staff is Mrs. Sun (Mandarin teacher, grew up in Harbin and taught in Vancouver, Ch 12); she speaks no line on this list. Lines spoken by named staff with no sheet (Mrs. Okonkwo, Amberg, Whitaker, Sandoval and the unnamed teachers) are also treated as American.
- **How to read the counts:** "R" means a reviewer source named that term in that chapter (or, for marks, mark scheme and "sat an exam", named it as "throughout"). "F" means a fix agent noticed it in its own chapter report and no reviewer named it. "S" means neither: I found it when re-scanning the chapters for the same term. S items are the least certain candidates.
- **Kept off the list as normal American English:** "till" meaning "until" (Ch 5, 11, 16, 17), "around" already used correctly, "round" meaning a round of ammunition, a fight round, a round of a game, "rounding" a number, or "year round"; "kitchen cupboard", "dishcloth"; "I've got a lot going on"; "a column of marks" (tally marks); "wall bars"; "pounds" as a weight; "queue" in the computing sense; "post" meaning a job; "revises" meaning edits; "backwards" (American too). Details are in section 4.
