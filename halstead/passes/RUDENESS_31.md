# Rudeness pass, Chapter 31 (Ruth)

Scope per the author's brief: places people are not answered, are talked over,
are read/written past, get a question back instead of an answer, or a question
asked and never answered. Checked against `HOUSE_RULES.md` and `DO_NOT_FLAG.md`
first; nothing below duplicates an item on that list. All quotations copied
and verified word for word from `31_ruth.md`.

## DELIBERATE, KEEP

**Line 15-19, department office woman to Ruth.** Ruth asks directly: "I sat a
diagnostic in August and I would like to know what it was actually for."
(line 17). The answer she gets: "The woman repeats that it's the placement
anyway, and offers her a pamphlet about tutoring resources that stays on the
counter after Ruth leaves." (line 19) — a real question about the diagnostic's
purpose is met with a repeated non-answer and a leaflet. This reads as rude,
but it is the chapter's opening engine: a whole section (lines 7-19) is built
on institutional brush-off, the woman is otherwise written sympathetically
("I'm sorry, because I can see how much of this you did," line 15), and the
narration explicitly frames it as Ruth hitting a wall rather than a person
being unkind. Keep.

**Line 111, Sam to Ruth.** Ruth: "im going to say a lot of things and i need
everyone to not make a joke for ten minutes" (line 109). Sam immediately:
"thats a big ask" (line 111) — a joke inside the ten seconds she asked not to
have one. This is caught in the room: "ruth: sam" (113), "sam: sorry" (115).
Text knows it is rude and has Ruth call it out on the spot. Keep.

**Lines 97-103 and 125, the group chat generally.** "she watches somebody
make a joke inside a minute and move on" (97); "Every message gets read, but
barely any get answered." (103); "The chat stays empty until Kavi answers."
(125). People not answering Ruth's posts is stated outright as the pattern of
the whole spring, and the isolation it produces is the point of the section
that follows (the numbers, the confession). Keep.

## UNINTENDED, FLAG

**Line 83, the professor to Ruth.** After the full, warm compliment — "But
you went off and found the sentence." — the same sentence continues: "He is
already reading down the next question on his list, with the pen back up in
his hand for it." He has moved to the next item before her thanks lands; the
next line is "Ruth thanks him, packs the loose pages into her folder in the
wrong order, and leaves" (85), with no reply from him recorded to the thanks
at all. This is the professor the text goes out of its way to make likeable
("she likes him, genuinely," line 59; "the only person who has ever pushed
back on a proof of hers," line 61) doing the exact thing on the list — moving
his eyes to other work while she is still there — and nothing in the scene
marks it. It reads, unremarked, as the compliment being cashed and the
conversation being over on his clock, not hers.

**Line 49, Ruth to her mother, on the phone.** "Her mother asks once, in
March, whether the classes have gotten any harder, and Ruth says they're fine
and asks what her father wants for his birthday." A direct, caring question
gets a two-word dismissal and a different question thrown back in the same
breath — the exact "question gets a different question back" pattern named
in the brief. The same sentence generalizes it: "Twice she gets as far as 'so
the placement thing' and then asks about the dog," and the paragraph closes
on "Neither of them mentions, after that, that she still hasn't finished a
sentence about the placement in months of Sunday calls." Ruth is written
sympathetically throughout this passage (dutiful, answers on the second
ring, gives her mother "the actual week") and the deflection toward her own
worried parent is never treated as anything other than Ruth managing her own
privacy. From the mother's side of the line, three months of a question
being swapped for a birthday present or a dog is the thing a reader would
notice. Flag.

## UNCERTAIN

**Line 21, the preceptor at the section-room door.** Ruth "asks him straight
whether a class this easy is normal for the room or just for her. He says he
hasn't graded enough of these to know yet, and goes back to his stack." He
does answer, after a fashion, before returning to his own work, so this falls
short of the "answers nothing" bar. Whether "goes back to his stack" reads as
a brush-off or just as a minor character with nothing more to say is a
judgment call. Would be settled by whether any other scene treats this
preceptor as someone Ruth is close to or indifferent to — the chapter never
names him again, so there's no second data point.

**Line 39, the February study group.** Ruth's real question (see below) gets
silence, a beat of held laughter from Devansh, and then "somebody changes the
subject back to the actual problem set." No single person is shown doing
something callous; the room's discomfort could be genuine confusion at an
odd question rather than a decision to freeze her out, and this is a group of
friends the book treats warmly elsewhere. Would be settled by knowing whether
the author intends this beat as the same "adults keep not truly answering
Ruth" pattern as the registrar/department scenes, in which case it belongs
with DELIBERATE, or as an unmarked slight from peers, in which case it belongs
with FLAG.

## Fix first

The professor scene (line 83) and the mother phone call (line 49) are the two
I would take to the author first. Both involve a character the text is
actively building sympathy for — a professor Ruth admires, a mother she is
reassuring — doing something a reader would clock as a brush-off, with zero
narrative signal that anyone in the room noticed. The study-group question
(line 39) is a real gap but weaker: it may simply be the same "nobody can
give Ruth a straight answer" machinery the registrar scenes already run
deliberately, just transplanted to peers instead of administrators.
