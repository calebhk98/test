## Changes made: 1

### chapters/13_ten_pages.md, line 27

**Before:** "The last page gets written first, then the middle, and the rest
splits across a few sittings."

**After:** "Chloe writes the last page first, then the middle, and splits
the rest across a few sittings."

**Why:** Passive that should have been active. Chloe is the established
topic of the entire paragraph (she decides, she builds, she gets the pages
out) and this sentence is specifically about her distinctive, deliberate
process, writing out of order. The passive drops her as the doer of her own
described habit for no reason tied to any of the three tests: the agent is
not unknown, irrelevant, or obvious, it is the person the whole paragraph is
about, and the sentence loses the thing the paragraph exists to show, that
she does this on purpose. "The rest splits across a few sittings" compounded
it, an abstract subject ("the rest") given an active verb of its own
motion, when what actually happens is that Chloe splits her writing across
sittings. Fixed to active with her name, not "She," because `sentence opens
She/He + verb` is already over target for this chapter's run of
`style_report.py` and a new "She" opener would have made that worse; "Chloe"
reads clean here after several pronoun-only sentences in a row and does not
strain the paragraph.

## Call-outs: 2

### chapters/13_ten_pages.md, line 91 (inside the protected paintball
passage)

**Current:** "...that the pump gives you a count you can time, not one you
wait to see, which Kavi had off the wall bars days before anybody asked
him."

**Why the current voice is wrong:** This is the end-focus finding already on
record for this line: Kavi's discovery, the newest and most important
information in the sentence (that he worked this out on his own, unprompted,
before Bex ever spoke to him), is buried inside a trailing relative clause
modifying "a count" instead of landing in its own clause with its own
end-weight. The sentence structure gives the emphasis to the mechanism
Bex is reciting, not to the fact of Kavi's independent discovery, which is
the actual point of naming him at all.

**Why it was not auto changed:** Line 91 sits inside the paintball
floor-and-corridor passage (lines 65-111), a passage `BEX_SELECTS.md` built
on purpose to establish the boy/girl credit-taking asymmetry, and this exact
line was already flagged as a known end-focus finding with instructions not
to touch it. Writing it up again, with replacements, per that instruction.

**Active:** "...that the pump gives you a count you can time, not one you
wait to see. Kavi had worked that out off the wall bars, on his own, days
before anybody asked him."

**Active:** "...that the pump gives you a count you can time, not one you
wait to see, the same count Kavi had already worked out off the wall bars,
on his own, days before anybody asked him."

**Passive:** "...that the pump gives you a count you can time, not one you
wait to see. That count had already been worked out, off the wall bars, by
Kavi, days before anybody asked him."

The two active versions split Kavi's discovery into its own clause so the
sentence ends on "days before anybody asked him," the fact that matters. The
passive keeps the count as the grammatical subject (matching the sentence's
run of parallel "that..." clauses about the mechanism) while still moving
Kavi's name and the timing to the end rather than tucking them into a
modifier. All three keep Kavi as the actual doer; none of them changes what
Bex says or how she says it, only how the missing weight is written up here,
which is not something an edit inside this passage is allowed to do.

### chapters/14_sixty_degrees.md, line 223

**Current:** "...and she stands in front of that one with her arms crossed
until somebody behind her needs the space, because she spent those hours on
a landing while the number of teachers upstairs went uncounted."

**Why the current voice is wrong:** This is the third category, active
voice with nobody in it. "The number of teachers upstairs went uncounted" is
an abstract noun taking a verb of motion ("went") for an action nobody
performs; nothing goes anywhere. What actually happened is that Chloe and
the others on her landing never counted the teachers, which the sentence
right before it already states in a different, correctly vague, form ("at
no point in the two hours did anybody establish..."). The evasive
construction lets the sentence close on an inanimate near-motion verb
instead of on the person standing in the scene right now, reading the
finding about herself.

**Why it was not auto changed:** I was not sure who the sentence should name.
Chloe was one of five others on that specific landing, and the failure to
establish troop numbers was a whole-building problem, not only hers; naming
her alone as the one who "never counted" risks overstating her personal
blame past what the scene supports, but naming the group flattens the
personal weight the paragraph is visibly building toward (she is the one
standing in front of the sheet with her arms crossed). That is an author
judgment about how much individual guilt this beat is meant to carry, not a
mechanical fix.

**Active:** "...because she spent those hours on a landing where nobody,
herself included, ever counted the teachers upstairs."

**Active:** "...because she spent those hours on a landing, and no one on
it, her counted among them, ever worked out how many teachers were still
upstairs."

**Passive:** "...because she spent those hours on a landing while the
number of teachers upstairs was never counted."

**Passive:** "...because she spent those hours on a landing, and the
teachers still upstairs were never counted by anyone on it."
