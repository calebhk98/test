## The eight instances found (narration only; dialogue lines were left alone
per the brief)

7 were rewritten to a positive action, in order of the brief's preference
(what the character does > what the next person does > cut). 1 was kept
deliberately — see below.

**1.**
Before: "Getting past it and leaving nothing behind to say anyone was ever
inside is the harder one, and the twelve pages are mostly about the second
problem, not the first."
After: "Getting past it and leaving the file looking exactly as it did
before, with every trace of them erased, is the harder one, and the twelve
pages are mostly about the second problem, not the first."
Why: "leaving nothing behind" restated as what the tool actually does —
erases the trace and restores the file's prior state.

**2.**
Before: "It isn't the arithmetic that finally moves him."
After: "The reasoning moves him in the end, not the arithmetic."
Why: same contrast, led with the positive subject (what does move him)
instead of opening on a negated one.

**3.**
Before: "...in small pieces that don't look like much on their own."
After: "...in small pieces that look unremarkable on their own."
Why: one-word swap, "don't look like much" to "look unremarkable" — same
fact, positive verb.

**4.**
Before: "For most of them the tool sits somewhere only Eli and Kavi can see,
doing nothing but watch itself work: how long a query takes, whether
anything logs it, whether the shape of its own traffic looks like the
ordinary noise around it."
After: "For most of them the tool sits somewhere only Eli and Kavi can see,
watching itself work: how long a query takes, whether anything logs it,
whether the shape of its own traffic looks like the ordinary noise around
it."
Why: "doing nothing but watch" carried no information "watching itself work"
doesn't already carry on its own; cut the negative frame around it.

**5.**
Before: "The version where nothing visibly happens to anybody, where the
only evidence is a gap that closes again before anyone can point to it, sits
outside all nine pages."
After: "The version that leaves only a gap closing again before anyone can
point to it, quiet and invisible, over before it registers, sits outside all
nine pages."
Why: this is a fact about the document's scope (a real gap in the plan, kept
per the brief's "negative that is the actual fact"), but it doesn't need
"nothing... to anybody" to say so — "quiet and invisible" states the same
absence of visible harm without the negation.

**6.**
Before: "Eli signs first, hours after he sends the document, because for him
it was never really a question."
After: "Eli signs first, hours after he sends the document, because for him
the answer was already settled."
Why: "was never really a question" and "was already settled" are the same
fact about his certainty; the second doesn't announce itself as an absence.

**7.**
Before: "A clearance is a piece of paper that says the government has
already decided to trust her with things most people never get near, and
she's about to spend that trust on a file it never agreed to show her."
After: "A clearance is a piece of paper that says the government has already
decided to trust her with things it keeps far from most people, and she's
about to spend that trust on a file it always meant to keep sealed from her
too."
Why: both halves restated as something the government actively does
(withholds, seals) rather than something it never did.

**8. Kept — the one deliberate instance.**
"Chloe is four months into that job when she writes the nine pages, her hand
steady through all nine of them, and she does not stop."
This is the chapter's last line. It's doing real work: the sentence before it
names exactly what she's risking, and "she does not stop" is the beat that
answers it, landing on the negation on purpose as the close of the chapter.
Rewriting it to "she keeps going" was tried and reads flatter in that
position — kept the original.
