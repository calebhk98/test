## 2. Ch15 — the bolt cutters. The chapter does not have her let it go.

**Author:** *"Ch 15 seems to have her let it go?"*

**Answer: it does not.** The synopsis is half right and half stale, and the finding is
correct on the half that matters. **No change made to the chapter**, for two separate reasons
given at the end.

### Every mention, quoted

There are two, plus a third beat that finishes the thread.

**L201**, Kavi inventorying the kit, one clause inside a list:

> Kavi is going through the equipment: two bags, a laptop, a lockpick set, a pair of bolt
> cutters that are the wrong bolt cutters for the fence they came over.

**L259**, the decisive passage:

> Chloe sits against the wall with her arms around her knees while Sam talks, **working at the
> wrongness of it instead of listening.** The bolt cutters wrong for that fence. The boots
> wrong. All of it wrong in the same direction, which is the direction a school would get it
> wrong in if a school were building this, and also the direction four real people would get
> it wrong in if somebody had handed them the wrong list. **She turns it over the whole way
> through and it comes out the same both ways**, and the second man's chest going up and down
> a few feet away sits on the same list as the bolt cutters.

**L269**, after Sinclair sends them to bed:

> Back in bed she lies on top of the covers with her shoes on and runs the list from the top,
> and gets to the end of it, and starts over.

### What the evidence settles

The synopsis says she "cannot make the wrongness mean anything and lets it go." Those are two
claims and they come apart:

- *Cannot make the wrongness mean anything* — **accurate.** "It comes out the same both ways"
  is exactly that: the wrongness is consistent with a school-built exercise and with four real
  people working off a bad list, and she cannot get it to discriminate.
- *Lets it go* — **contradicted, twice, in eleven lines.** "Working at the wrongness of it
  instead of listening" is her working at it while a conversation happens around her. "Turns
  it over the whole way through" is her working at it to the end. And L269 has her in bed,
  shoes still on, running the same list from the top and starting it again. A character who
  lets a thing go does not restart it lying down.

So: she works at it, gets nothing, and keeps working at it. The finding's "does the opposite
at length" is right; it just needs the qualifier that the *conclusion* she reaches is the
synopsis's blank. The synopsis line should be fixed to something like "cannot make the
wrongness mean anything, and cannot stop working at it," which also happens to be the more
useful sentence about her.

### Why nothing was changed here

1. The chapter is not ambiguous, so the third branch of the brief does not fire. This is a
   stale synopsis, and the synopsis is fixed elsewhere.
2. All three lines sit **inside the protected intrusion scene.** `passes/fix/D14-18.md:113`
   defines it: "the incident itself (alarm at 4am through Sinclair's 'Grading's in the
   morning, go to bed') runs from roughly line 121 to line 279 and per the assignment is not
   a target." L201, L259 and L269 are all within 121-279, and Sinclair's line is at L267.
   Nothing in the span was touched.

No physical description of the bolt cutters was added, per the author's earlier ruling that
this is answered in a later chapter.

Ch15 word count unchanged at 4,671 (already over band before this pass, and nothing was added).

## 3. Ch30 — "She tells him everything."

The line is currently cut. **Recommendation: leave it cut.** The paragraph works without it,
and what looks like a deliberate one-line punch became a restatement partway through the
book's own revision history. The evidence is checkable in one command, below. Both restore
options are written out at the end so the author can overrule this in ten seconds.

### The passage as it now stands, L45 to L47

> That's the whole answer, first try, the real one instead of a softer one built to sound
> reassuring. Chloe likes him for it. He's thorough, and direct about what each question is
> actually for, and she gives him more than the minimum from the first page onward, because
> he's already shown her what happens to it.
>
> "Residential the whole time?" he asks, pen already moving.

### Why the line stopped earning its place

`git show c215ee0 -- chapters/30_cleared.md`. Before that expansion pass, the paragraph was
two short sentences:

> Chloe likes him. He's thorough, and direct about what each question is actually for.
>
> She tells him everything.

There, the one-liner was the only statement of her decision to be forthcoming, and it was
doing real work with a real punch. The expansion rewrote the paragraph and appended "**and she
gives him more than the minimum from the first page onward, because he's already shown her
what happens to it**" — which states the same decision *and* gives it a cause. From that
commit onward "She tells him everything" sat directly under a sentence that had just said so,
which is House Rule 1's first bullet: a sentence that restates what the scene just dramatized.
The third agent's cut was removing a redundancy the expansion created, not overruling the
first report. Both reports were right about the draft in front of them.

### What was lost, and what was not

- **Information: nothing.** It is in the sentence above.
- **The setup for the refusal at L75: nothing.** The narrator's "everything" is not what makes
  the one boundary legible; Whitaker does that himself, on the page, at L85: "Anything else in
  that category?" and her "Two others." The scene names the exception and its size out loud,
  which is better than a narrator claim that the scene then falsifies.
- **Tempo: a little.** A long reflective paragraph, a four-word paragraph, then the Q&A opens.
  That beat is gone. But the chapter makes the same move without a hinge two paragraphs
  earlier, at L39 into L41: narration, then straight into "Does any of it get released?" The
  transition is consistent with the chapter's own habits.

### On the absolute itself

`python3 absolutes.py --list chapters/30_cleared.md` returns **5** narration absolutes as the
chapter stands. Restoring the line makes it **6** — the tool counts it. It is the same
flat-absolute-then-exception shape as ch3's "Nobody has ever sent Chloe anything," which is a
real error elsewhere. Worth saying plainly, though: the objection is to the sentence and not
to the scene. Chloe declining to name the subject of the third paper, while naming that it
exists, who ran it and when, is the favourable answer in a clearance interview, and the scene
is right as written.
