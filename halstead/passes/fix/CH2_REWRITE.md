# Chapter 2, the testing scene: finishing pass

Second agent on this file. The first one rewrote Block Design and converted the
mother's questionnaire to a Record Form, and stopped there. This pass finished
the battery, moved the cost of the under-answering, and paid the word budget.

## What was already in place when I opened the file

More than the handover credited. Reading the chapter against the brief line by
line, the first agent had already put in **Matrix Reasoning** and **Picture
Concepts**, already fixed **Coding** to Form A, already added **Comprehension**
with the two-reasons query, already added **Letter-Number Sequencing** and
**Symbol Search** and **Cancellation**, and already removed **Mazes** and the
**story retelling**. Items 1 through 5 of the brief were done. I verified each
rather than take it on trust:

- **Matrix Reasoning.** "a square of pictures with a piece cut out of it and a
  row of pieces along the bottom." Untimed, no watch on the page, answered by
  putting a finger on the piece. She goes through to the end of the pages.
- **Picture Concepts.** Rows above and below, one from each, answered by
  pointing. "There is no watch for this one, and his thumb stays where it is."
  She goes through at the speed he can turn the page.
- **Coding A.** The key is a star, a circle, a triangle, a cross and a shape
  like a house, each holding a mark: a line standing up, a line lying down, a
  ring, two dots. **No numerals anywhere in the chapter** except the chapter
  title, the date line, and "March 5th". Grepped to confirm.
- **Cancellation beats Coding.** One stroke per animal, and she reaches the
  bottom of the page with "time left over, which hasn't happened yet today."
  Coding is a hundred controlled novel strokes and she cannot finish a page of
  it. The gap between the two is the whole motor argument and it is visible.
- **Mazes and story retelling.** Absent. Grepped.

An accident worth recording: the subtest order on the page is now exactly the
WISC-IV standard administration order. Block Design, Similarities, Digit Span,
Picture Concepts, Coding, Vocabulary, Letter-Number Sequencing, Matrix
Reasoning, Comprehension, Symbol Search, Cancellation. A clinician reading it
will recognise the sequence, which is worth more than any line of description.

## What I added

Nothing. The two missing subtests were in. Adding a third nonverbal beat on top
of Matrix Reasoning and Picture Concepts would have been noise, and the chapter
had no budget for it.

## What I cut, and why

**486 words, from 4,485 to 3,999.** The brief predicted roughly 600 needed for
two new subtests; since those were already on the page, the requirement was the
485 over the ceiling.

**One whole beat went: Picture Completion.** "Then pictures come with a piece
missing and she has to say what's missing, including a picture where the
missing piece is a shadow." It is supplementary rather than core, the brief
flags it as such, and once Matrix Reasoning and Picture Concepts are both on
the page it is the third consecutive demonstration that she is fine at
nonverbal reasoning when her hands are not involved. Two of those is evidence;
three is the author making a point. Cutting it also removed the chapter's
eleventh subtest, which was starting to make the afternoon read long.

**Two beats went as redundancy the earlier rewrite created:**

- Ben's speech to the mother said twice that the composite will read as fine.
  "that number is going to be lower than it should be, low enough that a school
  skimming for a label could miss her entirely" was doing the same work as "it's
  bad numbers that move a system, and this one is going to read as good on the
  page" forty lines later, and the second one is sharper because the mother has
  asked for it. The first is gone. 40 words.
- The corridor had her stomach making a noise, and so did the end of the test
  session eight paragraphs earlier. The corridor one is gone.
- "and all she knows about it is that being quiet is what makes her disappear"
  restated the sentence directly above it, which already lists the rule she has
  worked out. House rule 1. Gone.
- "then puts her chair up on the desk as told" duplicated "she does what she's
  told" in the paragraph above. Gone.

**Everything else is compression, not deletion.** Roughly 300 words came off in
five- and ten-word shavings across the waiting room, the corridor and the car,
with paragraph merges where two short paragraphs were one thought. Nothing that
happens in the chapter stopped happening.

**Not touched:** Block Design entire, the digit span paragraph, the Coding
execution paragraph and the neat-not-fast stop, the hour-and-year answer, the
Similarities booklet out of the drawer, Cancellation.

## Where the under-answering costs her

Comprehension, as instructed, and it was already correctly placed. Two items
get "Tell me another reason", which is the examiner's query for a second
general concept, and both times she answers by **elaborating the reason she has
already given** rather than producing a second one: the hand-washing answer
comes back with doorknobs and the ground and the underneath of the table, which
is one concept with more instances, and the bank answer comes back with a
person climbing in a window, which is the same concept with a picture on it.
Under WISC-IV scoring that is 1 point on a 2-point item, twice, after query.

The mechanism is on the page and not explained: "every answer she gives seems
to be the right answer, so she stops trusting the questions and watches the pen
instead, and the pen moves the same amount whatever she says." She never finds
out that she is losing points, because the pen is not a scoreboard.

The stubborn beat stays on Vocabulary, which is already at ceiling and pays
nothing for it.

## What Prentice can see

- She answers the hardest verbal items completely and the easier ones briefly.
- She holds eight digits forward and six back, and he goes off-protocol for one
  more after she fails a length because he can tell the form is not catching
  something.
- She runs out of Similarities on the record form and he fetches a thicker book
  from a drawer, and none of what she does out of that book can be scored.
- Every block design is correct. All of them are late.
- She cannot make a pencil do what a six-year-old's pencil is asked to do, and
  she is fresh when she takes it, twenty-five minutes in.
- She is markedly better at Cancellation than at Coding, which tells him the
  problem is the stroke and not the search.

From that he concludes: bright child, real and non-trivial graphomotor
slowness, composite dragged down by processing speed, the school is the only
lever and it has nothing for two years. **That is the correct reading of the
available evidence.** He states the motor finding accurately, predicts it will
show in her handwriting for years, and refuses to give the mother a number he
cannot defend. He is the best clinician the scene could contain.

## What Prentice cannot see

- **A short answer given on purpose is a short answer.** She is not hedging.
  She produces a clean, confident, sufficient answer and stops. There is no tell.
- **The under-answering only exists as a pattern across items, and the pattern
  is inverted by the fact that she answers the hardest items in full.** A child
  stronger on hard items than easy ones reads as engaged by difficulty.
- **The ceiling.** At six, normed in three-month bands, the raw score for a
  scaled 19 is low, and her genuine performance and a much weaker one produce
  the same number. He can tell there is more above the ceiling, and says so
  ("the form ran out before she did"), and the instrument gives him no way to
  say how much more.

**His one human error, self-corrected:** he writes something down about her
stopping mid-row on Coding, and when she tells him at the break that she was
checking whether it had to be neat, he turns back a sheet, strikes it, and
writes it again. The score is identical either way. Nothing else in the room
goes wrong.

Two notes on the table.

**Reading grade fell 7.8 to 6.8.** The chapter's band is 5.5 to 7 and grade.py's
own note says past 7 it stops being worth pushing, so the chapter has moved from
above its band to the top of it. House rule 6 forbids lowering a figure to fit a
lower bar; this was not that. Every word cut was cut for the word ceiling, and
the grade came off with the length. A chapter 2 sitting below chapter 19 is also
what rule 5 asks for.

**Mean spoken sentence fell 14.5 to 13.5** and was already failing. This is the
one number I made worse. It is dialogue compression: Ben's report speech went
from 109 words to 91 and the mother's percentile answer lost a clause. I
recovered part of it by rejoining three speeches I had split, which cost nothing
in words, and stopped there rather than pad dialogue back up to move a metric.

**Negative space.** Eight negative sentences in 200, and seven of the eight are
dialogue, which the negative-space brief puts out of scope. The single narration
instance is the one I kept deliberately: "because there is nothing else to do
with her hands now", inside Block Design, which is protected and is the best one
in the chapter. Two narration negatives were removed on the way past: "in rows
with nothing in them" became "in empty rows", and "it still isn't fast" became
"it is still slow".
