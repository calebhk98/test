## The ruling on note 13

**The flatness note is the odd one out, and the reason is that flatness is not
a failure of her social reading. It is what she does with the reading.**

She calibrates four things: whether a thing gets said at all, who is present
when it is, when, and through which channel. That is where the whole of her
enormous social competence is spent, and it accounts for every scene the
reviewer listed. She lets somebody talk for twenty minutes because talking is
the thing they need. She hands a man his change instead of the answer because
he was not owed the answer. She takes a question to one person days later and
out of earshot, framed as logistics, because in a group the same question would
be a cost rather than an offer. She holds forty seconds of weather with a
stranger. She reads a stranger's odd behaviour in the dullest available way and
means it.

What she does not calibrate is the wording, once she has decided the thing gets
said. She spends the entire read on the decision and none of it on the
delivery, because softening the words would misrepresent what she saw, and she
thinks that is a small lie. So the read gets set down whole, in front of the
person, at its actual size.

That is the mechanism, and it makes the two halves the same person: **a plain
sentence from her carries the information that she has already seen through
you, and being seen through is the part that smarts.** It is content, not tone.
Anybody can produce a flat sentence; hers land because they are correct.

Two riders, both on the sheet:

1. **It is selective, and the selection is the opposite of what a reader
   expects.** With strangers and people she has no claim on she spends almost
   nothing: generous reading, few words, lets them be who they are. She presses
   the people she has chosen. Closeness is the condition under which the true
   thing becomes affordable, so the hardest version of her goes to the people
   she likes most, and she would be sincerely surprised to hear that called
   unkind. She is doing to them the thing she most wants done to herself, which
   is also, exactly, the deal she offers: come at me directly.
2. **She reads the moment and never runs the total.** Each correction is
   individually accurate and correctly sized, so as far as she is concerned
   nothing accumulates. A hundred of them over four years is a relationship,
   and she has no count of it. This is the blind spot that generates nearly all
   her friction, and it is the piece a writer needs in order to write friction
   that is nobody's fault.

So: deliberate, selective, and unaware only of the accumulation. Not flat
affect, not tone-deafness, and the chapter 16 line describing her as landing as
an accusation *because* she is flat has the causation backwards. The sheet now
says so in a section of its own, `Reading people, and why a plain statement
from her can land as an accusation`, written so a chapter agent can replace
that line without inventing anything.

## What the Chloe friction is made of

Not dislike. They pick the same table for eleven years and the work between
them is genuinely good, which has to survive.

**Chloe's method requires privacy until the thing is finished. Nadia reads
privacy about a result as a small performance.** Nadia does not want the
answer. She wants the other person to stop pretending not to have one, and she
keeps a finger on the evidence until it is admitted. To Nadia that is the
highest compliment she has: I will not insult you by pretending you are less
than you are. To Chloe it is exposure, in public, of the precise thing she has
built her entire way of working to keep covered until it is ready.

The engine is that **each of them is offering the other the form of respect she
most wants for herself, and each receives it as its opposite.** Nadia wants to
be come at directly and finds a soft approach insulting. Chloe wants to be left
with it and finds being called out humiliating. Nadia sees a small dishonesty;
Chloe sees a hand pulling the cover off. Neither is wrong, and neither can
concede without becoming someone else, which is why it does not resolve and why
they keep sitting together.

Why the book never paid it: neither of them will say any of it out loud, Nadia
does not run the total so nothing has visibly accumulated, and they let a third
person sit in the middle of it for years without either of them naming the
arrangement to him or to each other. That last is the portable shape of it: a
friendship with a buffer nobody discusses.

Written on the sheet as a type (`the one she works in parallel with`) with
Chloe named inside it, plus a `how to write it` instruction: never a row. A
tightening at the table, a hand taken off something, a subject that closes
early, and both of them back the next night in the same seats doing excellent
work together.

## What I added

**New sections**

- `One register, every content` (top of `the thing to get right first`, above
  the labour material). The unlock for the whole reviewer problem: she has one
  delivery and puts appetite, praise, jokes, apology, fear and confession
  through it, so a reader decodes the flat register as negative by default and
  hears only the refusals. The instruction is explicit: **do not widen the
  register, add the contents.** She should be exactly as flat at the end of a
  scene as at the start, and want things in half of them.
- `The feeling arrives in her body and in her schedule`. A fixed vocabulary of
  physical tells, since the book is third limited and rarely from her POV.
  Moved: she goes still, which for a person always already moving is the
  largest signal she has. Afraid: she recounts a figure she already has right.
  Pleased: she accelerates. Ashamed: she keeps the record and shows nobody.
  Angry: she narrows. Laughing: her face gives out all at once with no build.
- `What she wants, out loud`. Six concrete appetites (anything scored in
  public and she says she means to win it; physical speed; taking the repair
  off somebody slower because she wants to do it; being in the room rather than
  told after; food and firm ordinary preferences; and the one want she will not
  say). Plus a licence for an enthusiasm that goes nowhere and produces
  nothing.
- `Fear, shame, and being eighteen`. Fear attaches to obligations taken on
  behalf of other people, never to her own exposure, and presents as
  rechecking. The doubt exists and arrives *after* the decision and *alone*,
  which preserves the no-deliberation-in-the-room trait while admitting the
  night. Shame presents as a kept written record and a total she will not read
  out, continuing at a failed approach longer than she would let anyone else,
  and giving a smaller true number when asked how it is going. Explicitly: a
  stretch where she cannot tell whether the fault is the system or her, which
  she ends by starting to build rather than by working it out. And her physical
  fearlessness is filed as naivety, not courage: she has not got as far as the
  question of whether she could handle it, per note 6.
- `Humour`. Deadpan literal extension of somebody else's premise one step past
  where they meant to stop, delivered in her fact voice so people check her
  face. She may start one when the target is an idea or an institution. What
  makes her laugh: confidence outrunning competence, and physical stupidity
  that damages equipment.
- `Reading people, and why a plain statement from her can land as an
  accusation`. The note 13 ruling above.
- `Relationships`. Written as reproducible types with the book's names inside
  them: the friend who plays the fool (genuine, repeated annoyance, aimed
  precisely at a capable person acting incapable, which is the same offence as
  laziness to her; affection real and unstated; Sam); the friend who is
  excellent and will not be seen needing anything (she goes still, presses
  nothing, then does something disproportionate and practical and tells nobody;
  Ruth); the one she asks privately (Priya); the one she works in parallel with
  (Chloe, above); her parents; a younger sibling.
- `The friction with the one she works in parallel with`. Note 14.

**Added inside existing sections**

- `How she talks`: three new paragraphs. Admitting confusion out loud (always
  about a person, never a mechanism; she would rather be the one who said the
  clumsy version than the one who said nothing). Asking for help (a pause, then
  a very short sentence; names the need not the situation; one person, off to
  the side; never repeated if declined; overpays afterwards). Warmth (arrives
  as a fragment, buried under a task in the same breath, not repeated if asked,
  and she can regret it, which shows as extra brusqueness aimed at nobody).
- `Under pressure`: four new states. Frightened. Socially wrong (registers it a
  beat late off somebody's face, does not apologise in words because words
  caused it, does the thing the person needed instead, is slightly too helpful
  for the rest of the day, and agrees at once if named). Moved. And a rider on
  targeted: a lazy attempt insults her more than a serious one frightens her.
- `Home`: an argument-with-a-parent mechanism, since note 8 puts negotiation
  there. Being told what she is allowed to do is the one argument she stays in
  past the first refusal; she does not raise her voice and does not concede;
  she returns with a smaller version and a number attached on a later day. Also
  that she is sharper with a younger sibling than with anyone outside the
  family, which is where her proportionality slips.
- `Body and physical business`: two hands tells, both usable anywhere. A finger
  left on the object under discussion for the length of an argument, which is
  how she declines to let a subject be taken off the table. And doing something
  with her hands while being told a hard thing, which looks like inattention
  and is the opposite.
- `Interior life`: the eighteen-year-old fear layered under the existing one
  (somebody arranged their life around a promise of hers and the arithmetic
  will not come out); bad nights as one number over and over, which she answers
  by getting up and working; two new blind spots, the missing running total and
  the assumption that people will not do the outrageous thing.
- `Personality off the page`: she prefers company for hands-on work and would
  never put it that way.
- `Would do`: seven new entries covering want, speed, clumsy confusion, asking,
  keeping the count, checking a frightening number twice, and laughing
  completely at something with no wit in it.
- `Would never`: two additions, both protecting the temperament rather than
  softening it. She will not say what she is feeling in the words for it or
  accept somebody else's word for it, and she will not perform an emotion she
  is not having in either direction to make a room easier.
- `Likes`: six positives added, to get the sheet near the five-to-one ratio
  `_DIFFERENTIATION.md` asks for. It was heavily negative.
- `Age and change`: rebuilt into four bands. Fifteen has the humour and nothing
  that has frightened her yet. Seventeen-eighteen is flagged as the band where
  the emotional range must be widest, carrying the upset, the ground-down
  count, the not knowing whether the fault is hers, the first obligation to
  other people's money, and, in the same months, the best time of her life on
  any evening with a scoreboard in it, neither commented on.
- `Do not confuse with`: added Sam, since both are short and both are funny and
  the two humours are now distinct enough to collide.
- `Dials`: rows added or rewritten for register, laughter, appetite, fear and
  apology.

## What I removed or reversed

- **She essentially does not ask.** Reversed. She asks constantly; the
  questions are narrow, answerable and acted on within the hour. What she does
  not do is ask openly for an opinion, ask rhetorically, or ask to be
  reassured. The statement-wearing-a-question-mark move is kept but demoted to
  a separate, less frequent thing, and identified as her telling somebody she
  has already seen it, which makes it the move most likely to land badly.
- **Jokiness: dry and closing, she finishes somebody else's joke rather than
  starting one**, and the matching `would never open a joke` warning. Both
  removed. She starts them. The constraint that replaces it is about target and
  form, not about who goes first.
- **Emotional range: narrow in words, wide in speed.** Replaced with: full;
  narrow in vocabulary, wide in speed, legible in the body. The old phrasing
  was being read as a licence for a character with a small feeling.
- **Three ways a writer gets this wrong: giving her doubt or self-justification
  before a decision.** Narrowed to putting the doubt *in the room* instead of
  in the night. The trait survives; the reading that she has no doubt does not.
- **Forty staff, three counties of employers, the product description, every
  employer signed in person.** All removed from the continuity block. Replaced
  with the bare facts that survive the pass (founds a company in hiring and job
  matching at eighteen in the June she graduates, still running it at
  twenty-one, the first version fails to attract users and she rebuilds it into
  something else) plus an explicit deferral to `passes/nadia/REVISION_NOTES.md`
  for team size, product shape and timeline, so nothing on this sheet can
  contradict what the chapter agents are writing.
- Added to continuity: she does not go to college, and the post-graduation job
  hunt is long, unsuccessful, and written down. Appearance range extended to
  chapter 36.

  not believe it would touch her, and take the second run at the thing that
