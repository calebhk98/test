# Reading-grade pass — chapters 14-18

Owner: 14_sixty_degrees, 15_twelve, 16_thirteen, 17_fourteen, 18_fifteen.

Technique per `passes/FK_BRIEF.md` and `passes/WHY_HIGHER.md`: cataloguing (name
the members, in series, inside one sentence) and spelling out the logical joint
(because / so / which meant / even though). No comma-splicing: every merge either
carries the connective that was implied or turns two adjacent clauses into one
that says more than the pair did.

Rules the pass was run against changed mid-flight. Final state: reading grade is
banded by Chloe's age (chs 11-15 floor 6.0, chs 16-22 floor 7.0, judged on the
band average), word ceiling 4,000, and no narration that exists to make sure the
reader has understood something.

## Chapter 16 (thirteen) — 7.3 → 9.4, 3,409 words

The easiest of the five: no protected scene, and the chapter was already the
book's second-worst on negative space (11.3%), which meant the two jobs were the
same job. Almost every "nobody / nothing / doesn't / never" in it was a logical
joint left unstated, and writing the joint in removed the negation and lengthened
the sentence at the same stroke.

> *before* — Teaching starts at thirteen and you take the year below. Her cohort
> is the oldest in the building, so her cohort is the only one doing it, and the
> twelves are the only year in the school being taught by another student instead
> of a teacher. Nobody teaches the thirteens.

> *after* — Teaching starts at thirteen and you take the year below, so her
> cohort, being the oldest in the building, is the only cohort doing it, which
> makes the twelves the one year taught by another student and the thirteens the
> one year with no teacher of their own.

Cataloguing, on the seven students, four short sentences into one:

> *before* — Six of the seven are straightforward. Ivy and Tomas want the answer
> handed over, but they can be talked out of that inside a month. Beatriz arrives
> already knowing most of it, so she takes eleven minutes of the hour and uses
> them well. Two more turn up, work, leave.

> *after* — Six of the seven are straightforward: Ivy and Tomas want the answer
> handed over, though they can be talked out of it inside a month, Beatriz
> arrives already knowing most of the material and so takes eleven minutes of the
> hour and uses them well, two more turn up, work, leave.

Marek's refusal ("It isn't work if I can already do it, it's just a receipt")
became "Copying it out afterwards is a receipt for something I've already done,
and a receipt is a different object from work" — same claim, positive
construction, one clause longer. His "Nothing would" was left exactly as it is:
it is the chapter's hinge and it is already answered in the next breath.

The author-added Saturday-bus passage kept every beat (the duty teacher reading
four messages over a shoulder, Kavi's service that looks like every other
service, Ruth writing the encryption out of the term's material, Chloe breaking
it on traffic analysis rather than on the cipher, the December bus). What changed
there is sentence joinery only.

## Chapter 17 (fourteen) — 6.2 → 8.6, 3,326 words

Protected and untouched: the winter-break confrontation, "Winter break, second
night" through "as much of the sentence as the room can hold", including both
narratorial sentences in the aftermath.

That scene is **575 words over 53 sentences at F-K 3.70** — 17% of the chapter's
words but 27% of its sentences. Everything else was worked, and the remainder now
reads **F-K 11.3 at 29.5 words per sentence**. Because the protected block is a
quarter of the sentence count at 10.8 words each, the whole chapter lands 8.6,
which is 1.6 above its band floor.

Flagging rather than fixing: 29.5 words per sentence in the worked material is
above anything in the corpus, and it is high because it is carrying the
confrontation. Two sentences that had run past eighty words were split back
apart; if the confrontation is ever unprotected, the right move in this chapter
is to shorten the remainder rather than lengthen it further.

> *before* — Self defense is three rounds an hour. One person stands in the
> middle, the other ten come at them, and there is a minute on the clock, before
> the middle changes and it goes round until all eleven of them have taken it
> three times.

> *after* — Self defense is three rounds an hour, with one person standing in the
> middle while the other ten come at them and a minute runs on the clock, after
> which the middle changes and it goes round until all eleven of them have taken
> it three times.

Negative space fell 7.3 → 3.4 by the same method: "There's nothing on here about
what it's holding" became "There's a page and a half here about how hard it
squeezes and a blank where the thing being squeezed goes"; "without breaking
stride" became "still walking"; "without lifting her cheek off the stock" became
"with her cheek still down on the stock". The remaining negatives are all inside
the protected confrontation, where they are the scene.

## Chapter 18 (fifteen) — 6.0 → 9.0, 3,394 words, negative space 0.0%

No protected scene, so the whole chapter was open, and it is the cleanest result
of the five: every one of the fourteen negative-space constructions was replaced
by a positive statement of the same fact, usually longer.

> *before* — "When you were ten you were fighting ten children who had never been
> hit by anybody. They had no experience, so lasting in that was like taking candy
> from a baby." ... "You're not getting worse, they're just coming up off nothing,
> and you are having an actual challenge now."

> *after* — "When you were ten you were fighting ten children who had yet to be
> hit by anybody, so lasting in that was like taking candy from a baby." ...
> "You're getting better and they're coming up off a standing start, which is the
> first actual challenge you've had."

The turret opening is the clearest case of a catalogue paying for itself: "A
machine ... fires a live round down the lane, on a cycle that never varies. The
students stand off to the side of it, behind glass, so the round crosses in front
of them left to right. They shoot across its path on a count that they take off
the machine." — three sentences, one negative, became one sentence with the
causal chain written in and the negation gone.

The author-added phone-hardening passage kept every beat and, after the
mid-flight instruction, kept its original sentence forms as well (see the
last section).

## Chapter 14 (sixty_degrees) — 6.3 → 8.8, 3,535 words, negative space 0.6%

No protected scene named. Bell's instruction and Sinclair's intercom were left in
their command register. The Latin-is-cheating argument that `characters/CHLOE.md`
names was kept turn for turn; what changed inside it is that Ruth's closing point
now states its consequence instead of a negation:

> *before* — "Everybody who could has been dead fifteen hundred years and none of
> them are going to sit up and say you've done the ending wrong."

> *after* — "Everybody who could has been dead fifteen hundred years, so every
> ending you get wrong stays wrong in private, for ever."

The astronomy paragraph was the single biggest arithmetic win in the five
chapters: a four-item catalogue written as four sentence fragments ("How you get a
mass off an orbit. Why the rocket equation punishes you the way it does. ...")
became one catalogued sentence, taking six sentences to two with no content lost
and no words added.

Word count came down 252, from 3,787 to 3,535, by compression rather than by
cutting beats. It is inside the 4,000 ceiling.

## Chapter 15 (twelve) — 4.4 → 5.1, 4,626 words. The ceiling is real; here is the number.

The earlier run's "hard ceiling" claim is correct, and it is worse than that run
stated. Measured directly rather than inherited:

    the intrusion incident (protected)   1,920 words   191 sentences   10.1 w/sent   F-K 3.52
    everything else (worked)             2,706 words   131 sentences   20.6 w/sent   F-K 7.5

The incident is 41% of the chapter's words but **59% of its sentences**. Whole-
chapter F-K is a ratio of total words to total sentences, so those 191 short
sentences are a fixed weight on the denominator that no amount of work outside
them can lift.

**The arithmetic, three ways.** Holding the incident fixed at 1,920 words / 191
sentences / 1.287 syllables per word:

| scenario | whole chapter reads |
|---|---|
| remainder at F-K 9.0 (22 w/sent, 1.357 syl/word), chapter at 4,000 words | **5.2** |
| remainder at F-K 9.0 (23.5 w/sent, 1.307 syl/word), chapter at 4,000 words | **5.1** |
| remainder at F-K 9.0, chapter left at its current 4,626 words | **5.7** |

So chapter 15's ceiling is about **5.2 at the word ceiling and 5.7 if the length
is allowed to stand**, and reaching either requires the non-incident material to
be running at F-K 9.0 throughout — which is what the mid-pass draft did before
the "no talking to the reader" instruction (the remainder measured 9.75 at 25.5
words per sentence). Restoring the live exchanges that draft had flattened into
reported narration is why the remainder now measures 7.5 and the chapter 5.1: the
restored dialogue is short, and it should be.

**Negative space in chapter 15 is capped the same way.** The protected incident
contains 22 negative-space sentences of its own ("Nobody said anything." / "They
do not enter a building." / "They didn't go past the kitchens."). For the whole
chapter to reach 5%, it would need 440 sentences — a 4,626-word chapter at 10.5
words per sentence, which is the opposite of this pass. Every negative outside
the incident was removed (the remainder measures **0.8%**); the 7.1% that remains
is 22 sentences of protected scene divided by the chapter.

**What was done, since the number is capped.** The forge, the metronome
handover, the term summary, the growth-spurt doorway, the bread test, the stock
count, the grading aftermath and the whole Thanksgiving table were rewritten on
the joint-and-catalogue method; the incident, Pruitt's two lines and their
surrounding narration were not touched except to change one attribution
("without stopping" → "and keeps going") that was costing negative space and is
narration rather than his speech.

**Word count.** 4,838 → 4,626, still 626 over the 4,000 ceiling. It cannot get
there by compression: 1,920 of those words are the protected incident, leaving
2,080 for the forge apprenticeship, the counting handover, a term of new
subjects, the growth beat, a three-location bread experiment, the stock count,
the grading aftermath and a Thanksgiving dinner with both parents. That is eight
scenes in 2,080 words. Reaching 4,000 means deleting one of them or trimming
inside the incident, and both are the author's call, not this pass's. My
nomination if one has to go: the stock count (about 190 words), which is the only
scene whose work — Ruth reorganising a count — the chapter states again elsewhere.

Left short on purpose inside the worked material: Priya's "Do it how?", Mrs.
Adeyemi's "Is that oven on?" and "Names", Meg's "Signed off by who?", Dave's
"Come apart how? Come apart like what, exactly?", and Chloe's "You mostly can't"
— each is a character sheet signature or a place where something specific and
nameable stops her talking.

## Removed under "no talking to the reader"

Spelling out a logical joint is one keystroke from spelling out the point, and
the pass produced both. What went back out, after the instruction:

**Narration that explained a beat the scene had already carried**

- ch17: "Chloe fumbles the bolt release for two weeks, until in the third week her
  hand finds it on its own while she is looking at the far wall, **which is the
  whole reason for the cloth.**" — the trailing clause told the reader what the
  cloth was for. Cut.
- ch17: "**Because it is the one subject on the sheet where the evidence runs out
  before the argument does,** she sits through comparative religion mostly
  listening, and comes out of it with about four opinions and no way to test any
  of them." — the opener said what the closing clause already says. Cut.
- ch17: "**Because the writing system does half the work of the vocabulary,**
  Japanese moves faster at the front than she expects" — the next sentence is
  "The kanji arrive with most of their meaning already on them". Cut.
- ch16: "Amberg could take it apart in an afternoon, **which is why they leave him
  out of it**" — the original left it implied and was better; restored to "and not
  one of them asks him to".
- ch16: "he says no, politely, at once, **which is fair given that** she is a year
  older and has yet to open it" — the narrator ruling on whether a refusal was
  fair. Now "since".
- ch16: "Chloe breaks it in nine days, and **what breaks is the traffic rather than
  the encryption, which holds**" — labelled the answer before showing it. Now "the
  encryption holds the whole way: what she gets instead is that ...".
- ch15: "she walks down in the first week of September for a chisel and stays the
  whole afternoon **because watching a man called Pruitt make one turns out to be
  better than owning it**" — an authorial verdict on why she stayed. Back to
  "stays the whole afternoon watching a man called Pruitt make it".
- ch15: "to say what the small things are she has to start with the release, and
  the release needs the count ... **so the honest answer is four winters long**" —
  the sentence had just dramatised exactly that. Cut.

**Aphorisms bought in dialogue that the original did without**

- ch15: Chloe on the hook — "an inch where somebody stopped paying attention" was
  a flourish the scene had not earned; back to "the same curve from one end to the
  other".
- ch14: Sam's devil's-advocate line had gained "**and a blank is a worse essay
  than a wrong one**". Cut; the line now stops where his point stops.
- ch14: "Right, **so the whole year runs on a point staying away from a person**"
  reads as a thesis statement; now "so a point stays away from a person".
- ch18: Chloe's reply to Dr. Sandoval had drifted into "there's a rule that says I
  have every right to talk", which is both a speech and wrong; now "when there's
  no rule anywhere that obliges me to".

**Live exchanges the pass had flattened into reported narration**

Converting dialogue to narration raises words-per-sentence cheaply, and it is the
same defect wearing a different hat: the reader is told about a conversation
instead of being in the room for it. Restored to dialogue:

- ch15 — the blind-test argument (Kavi's "Then do it blind" and Priya's "Do it
  how?"), Sam's "You can taste it though", "We are not doing thirty slices of
  toast at midnight, Kavi", Kavi's oven-temperature exchange, the whole of Mrs.
  Adeyemi's arrival, the stairs exchange about whether the first round counts, the
  stock-count exchange ending in "They're by your foot", her father's "What were
  the four of you doing in a kitchen at half eleven?" (whose loss had left the
  next line asking about bread that nobody had mentioned), and "Okay, I'll stay in
  my room" / "Say it like you mean it, please".
- ch16 — the two Nadia/Chloe turns over the napkin; "Marek hands nothing in" as
  its own one-line paragraph (rephrased as "Marek's sheet comes back blank, week
  after week" so it costs nothing on negative space); "Sam eats a second dinner
  through the whole of it and contributes twice", cut only for the old 3,400
  ceiling.
- ch17 — Sam's "How long till this bit's boring?", "They might leave it at clays",
  Halide's "Does the test count?" and the reply behind her.
- ch14 — the chisel exchange at the front of the wood shop, and her father's "You
  still doing the archery, every day like you said?"
- ch18 — the phone-hardening passage's imperative pair, which the pass had chained
  into a subordinate clause: "Close the window and there is a clean pane of glass
  in your pocket with a login sitting on it. Take the phone off somebody and what
  you are holding is a phone."

Also split back apart: four sentences that had run past eighty words in ch14,
ch17 and ch18 — long enough to read as accumulation rather than as a catalogue.
