## Method

1. **Sentence-count reduction, not word addition.** At ch19's starting shape, adding a
   word buys 0.002 grades and removing a sentence boundary buys 0.037.
2. **Spelling out the logical joint** (because / so / whereas / since / once), which is
   what makes a merge legitimate rather than a splice.
3. **Cataloguing** where the draft named a category and left the members implicit.
4. **Precise nouns**: proctoring, ignition, sabotage, sodium lamp, numerical marks,
   corridor, photograph, duplicated, interval, accreditation.

## Relative clauses

The metric counts any sentence containing who / whom / whose / which / that, so it does
rise with sentence length; I said as much in an earlier draft of this report and
under-weighted it. Measured directly against the corpus, the excuse does not hold:

    Wharton, Age of Innocence   20.1 w/sent   29.6%   (corpus maximum)
    black_beauty                22.8 w/sent   27.4%
    little_women                19.8 w/sent   25.1%
    treasure_island             18.3 w/sent   24.8%
    wind_in_willows             18.8 w/sent   19.5%
    SunAlsoRises                 8.5 w/sent    7.8%

Length explains the slope. It does not explain 33-34%: at 22.8 words a sentence Black
Beauty sits at 27.4, and no book in the 23-book corpus reaches 31. Roughly six to seven
points of what my pass produced was genuine over-reliance on "which is" as the default
joint, and the ten "which is" clauses I found in ch19 round one were the visible edge of
it. Final rates, all inside the 14-26 band:

| | before pass | after merge rounds | after this fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 19 | 22.5 | 33.1 | **21.4** |
| 20 | 24.8 | 32.6 | **22.7** |
| 21 | 28.8 | 34.4 | **20.9** |
| 22 | 21.0 | 33.6 | **17.0** |

The fix was to keep the length and change the joint: appositives ("the column everybody
actually reads" from "which is the column..."), participials ("a page restating what the
shed already made obvious"), plain coordination, "so" for "so that", "for X to do Y" for
"enough that X did Y", and, in about a third of cases, cutting the clause outright because
it was a House Rule 1 gloss with a relative pronoun on the front. The two fixes really
were largely one pass, as predicted.

  against HEAD shows those six substitutions and nothing else. Flagging it for the author.
- ch22, the two interruption dashes at "Dave—" and "That's not what I—", left in place per
  instruction, since a speaker cut off mid-word is the one case the house has no
  alternative for. `check_edits` therefore still reports 1 problem on this chapter, and
  that is the expected state until the author rules.

**`check_edits.py --chapters 19,20,21,22` is a false pass.** The flag is `nargs="*"` and
matches on `f.name[:2]`, so a comma-joined argument matches no files at all and the script
prints an empty table and "0 problem(s)". The correct form is
`--chapters 19 20 21 22`, which is what surfaced the ch22 dashes. The FK brief specifies
the comma form; other agents running it as written are getting the same false pass.
