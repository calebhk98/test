# H30 — chapters/30_cleared.md

## Verdict: no edit made

The author's worry was that the chapter "reads like a children's book." I read it cold,
then checked the metrics against the two things flagged as protected (the clipped
Whitaker register, and the one refusal). It doesn't read that way, and the flat numbers
are explained by the chapter's shape, not by weak prose. Changing nothing.

### Why the numbers are low without the prose being flat

**1. The chapter is ~50% interview, and the interview is dialogue-heavy by design.**
Of 51 paragraphs, 13 are eight words or fewer. Nine of those thirteen are Whitaker/Chloe
exchange lines from the clearance interview: `"Yes."` / `"Seven."` / `"Yeah."` (x2) /
`"From when?"` / `"Understood." He writes it down anyway.` / `"You're recording something
with no legal standing."` / `Whitaker doesn't look up. "Asked by whom."` / `"And you
agreed to that," he says.` — plain question, plain answer, one line each its own
paragraph, which is standard dialogue formatting, not compression of thought. `CHLOE.md`
("The thing to get right first") plus the brief's own note that a "compliant, clipped
register with Whitaker is correct characterisation" both point the same way: this is
exactly the register the scene calls for. Sam-style flat clauses would be wrong here;
this isn't that — it's an adult giving a government interviewer short, complete,
cooperative answers, which is what clearance interviews sound like.

**2. The other four short paragraphs are a deliberate authorial beat, not a lapse.**
`Chloe's blog gets big in about fourteen months.` (opener), `She tells him everything.`
(mid-scene turn), `He writes that down too.` (closer to the refusal beat), and
`She starts in January. She's twenty-one in August.` (chapter closer) are one-line
punches used to mark a turn or close a unit — the same device the chapter's own longer
paragraphs are built to set off. They read as intentional understatement next to the
elaborated sentences around them, not as simplicity.

**3. The narration itself is not simple.** Outside the interview exchanges, sentences
carry real subordination and layered clauses, e.g.:

> He knocks once, at the exact minute he said he would, and stands in the doorway a beat
> too long for it to be nerves, more the habit of a man taking a room in fully before he
> steps into it.

> The clearance comes through in the autumn, on an ordinary Tuesday, thin enough that she
> almost sets it on the counter with the takeout menus before the seal above her own name
> catches the light.

These are not children's-book sentences by any reasonable reading — they're of a piece
with the complex, clause-stacked sentences in the neighbouring chapters.

**4. Lexile 1060 is not a children's-book number.** It's below the 1122–1311 range of
its neighbours, but 1060 is still solidly adult-literary; children's prose runs several
hundred points lower. The gap reads as "this chapter's register is plainer than its
neighbours' because Whitaker's scene calls for plain speech," not as "this chapter is
babyish."

### What I checked and didn't touch

- **CHLOE.md, "The thing to get right first"** — confirms adult Chloe here is not the
  six/seven-year-old the section is mainly warning about; nothing in the chapter reads
  young-Chloe's voice, so no risk there either.
- **The refusal** (lines 77–93: the third, unnamed project, told to Whitaker rather than
  concealed) — present and untouched.
- Paragraph convention: `grep -c '  $' chapters/30_cleared.md` → 0, confirming this is a
  blank-line-paragraph chapter (7+), consistent with how I read it.

No sentences were combined, no paragraphs merged, no vocabulary changed. `sync_chapter.py`
was not run because the file in `chapters/` was not edited.

### Notes for the author, not fixed (outside scope of this task)

Nothing else stood out as broken. If the flat numbers still bother you on a second look,
the highest-leverage place to look is the interview block (lines 41–95) — that's where
essentially all of the sub-8-word paragraphs live — but I'd only touch it if you want the
interview itself to feel less clipped, which would change Whitaker's and Chloe's manner,
not just the metrics.
