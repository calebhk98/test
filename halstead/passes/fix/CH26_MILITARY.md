## What changed structurally, before the item list

The four days now run on force-on-force equipment, and the encounters are
resolved by the equipment rather than by hands. That single decision fixes
items 1, 2 and 3 together, and it is what the rest of the chapter is built
back around.

The exercise is a named battalion field problem, **Winter Anvil**, on the board
outside the orderly room for three weeks before it starts. Sam draws his rifle
from the arms room truck, a blank adapter goes on the muzzle in front of an
armorer, and he is fitted with a laser harness: sensors round the chest, a band
over the helmet, a transmitter clamped to the barrel. A specialist keys it with
a controller gun until it answers. He signs for the harness, the transmitter, a
compass, a red-lens flashlight and thirty rounds of blank ammunition, and turns
twenty-eight of them back in four days later.

The safety brief is read out at the issue table off a laminated card, and it
plants the three rules the rest of the chapter turns on: minimum engagement
distance five metres, blanks prohibited inside it, and **no hand-to-hand at any
range**; a harness in a steady tone stops the soldier wearing it where he
stands until a controller reaches him.

The contact ledger is now 2, 4, 1 as before, but the outcomes are different:

- **Night one, the fence.** Sam hears them, withdraws fifteen metres, waits
  eleven minutes for the near man's weight shift to become a rhythm, and takes
  both at forty metres. Two harnesses go to a steady tone. Nobody is touched.
- **Night two, the culvert.** Four men, both ends blocked, a blank discharged
  at two metres inside a concrete pipe, five harnesses in a steady tone at once
  and no way to tell whose is whose. Sam puts hands on two of them and clears
  the far end. This is the safety incident.
- **Night three.** Eleven miles, no contact. Unchanged in substance.
- **Night four.** The patient man wins. Sam's rifle is coming round when his
  own tone starts, and he stands up in the open at four in the morning and gets
  beaten clean. A controller keys him quiet, keys him live, and sends him on to
  finish the leg.

Sam's four days are therefore two kills, one death, one voided engagement and
one written safety violation, which is a set of numbers a real lane produces
and which gives the AAR something to be about.

## Part 1: the four failures

### 1. No weapon and no force-on-force equipment

Fixed as above. Weapon, blank adapter, blank ammunition, laser harness,
helmet band, barrel transmitter, controller gun, lane controller, cold lane,
round count at turn-in. Engagements are scored by the harness tone, which is
also what makes the culvert insoluble in the dark and what gives the major his
correction. The kit is never explained to the reader; it is only ever used,
signed for, or turned in.

### 2. Seven OPFOR put down and checked for breathing

Gone entirely. There is no casualty check anywhere in the chapter and no
unconscious soldier. Six of the seven contacts are resolved by the equipment.
The one physical encounter has a consequence and the exercise absorbs it:

- The lane goes cold for forty minutes.
- Staff Sergeant Ives puts Sam on the road with his weapon cleared and his
  harness off and starts writing before he has finished his first question.
- A medic works the pipe: the man Sam put into the wall is sitting on the lip
  with a glove under his nose, and he is on light duty the next day.
- Ives voids the engagement, makes Sam recite the contact rule, and writes.
- The incident goes onto a form that evening with Ives's statement stapled
  behind it, and it is the item the major spends the most time on at the AAR.

Nobody is hurt beyond a nosebleed and a bad night, which is what a training
exercise can survive, and the violation is Sam's rather than ambiguous.

### 3. The thumb

Removed. He does not break anything, which is the author's point: he is far too
practised to injure his own hand throwing somebody. What is left is a split
knuckle across two fingers, and it is **routed through the medic on the spot**,
inside the forty-minute halt:

> "Open the hand." ... "Anything in there that objects to bending."

The medic bends every finger, tapes two of them together, and enters Sam's name
on a card underneath the man with the nose. The tape then carries the injury
through the rest of the chapter at the right size: it makes the compass harder
than the cold does on night three, and it is sitting on his knee at the AAR.
The major's joke about a still-swollen thumb is gone.

### 4. Sam says sir

Ten instances, all of them to the major, plus "Sergeant" to the NCO at the
issue table and to the medic. His opening word at the AAR is now "Yes, sir,"
where it was "Yeah." The major addresses him as **Private Marsh** twice, in the
two places where the register is coldest, and as Marsh otherwise, while calling
the other two soldiers nothing at all.

## Part 1b: the five smaller notes from CH25_MILITARY.md

**One grid versus two points.** The card now carries two grids of six figures
each and lists four checkpoints between them, in the order he is required to
take them. The sergeant's "these two points" is now "these," pointing at a card
that holds both.

**The unnamed exercise.** Named Winter Anvil, and posted on the board for three
weeks first, which also does character work: Sam checks the board.

**Corporal.** Now a specialist, in both places an E-4 does a job in this
chapter (the harness keying at issue, the time check at turn-in).

**Debrief.** The word is gone. The event is the AAR, once in narration, and it
is run as one: the opposing element briefs first, because they were on the
ground longer than anybody sitting down, and each lane is walked through
against the overlay with the soldier asked to account for his own decisions.

**Four days solo with no rest plan, no radio, no buddy.** Softened rather than
solved. It is now an individual movement lane inside a named battalion
exercise, with numbered checkpoints, a controller who owns the lane, a
controller on night four who can key a harness live again, a medic on a truck,
and a turn-in table with a clipboard. The soldier is alone on the ground but
the exercise is visibly around him at all times.

## Part 2: the author's notes

### 5. Future reference

Every instance swept. What was removed:

- "They come at him three times." (proleptic summary of the whole chapter)
- "and this is the one that changes what he's doing with the rest of the
  exercise"
- "he only finds out how few when somebody who timed it tells him afterward"
- "deliberately or not he genuinely can't say afterward"
- "The thumb swells to twice its size by the following evening and stays that
  way for a week"
- "It's the eleven seconds that stay with him longer than the thumb does"
- "The fourth night is the one that goes in the report."
- "What he has yet to learn, and what only becomes clear at the debrief the
  next afternoon, is that a man has been in position ... since a little after
  nine" (the worst one: an entire paragraph narrated from the AAR's knowledge)
- "like the number surprises him as much as it's about to surprise the room"
- "February does both at once, every year, on this range" (not prolepsis, but
  narrator omniscience of the same family)

The six-hours fact was the hard one, because the chapter needed it and it was
only available from the future. It is now delivered in the present, by the man
himself, in two lines of dialogue at four in the morning:

> "How long were you in there."
>
> "Since nine."

Sam does the arithmetic and lets the subject go, and the major puts the same
fact on the record the next day, which is where it belongs.

### 6. The debrief is far too nice to Sam

Rewritten. The major now behaves like a major who has read paperwork about this
soldier.

What is gone: the praise, the reading of Sam's numbers out to the room as
though they meant something on their own, the instruction to say it again
slower to everybody, and **"Everybody write that down."**

What is there instead:

- He leaves Sam in his seat rather than calling him to the front the way he
  called the other two, and starts without looking up from the clipboard.
- Night one gets one flat sentence of fact and no adjective.
- Night two is announced as a safety incident and done as one, read off Ives's
  statement in the voice a form gets read in, ending on the other man's light
  duty.
- He makes Sam recite the contact rule and the tone rule out loud, which is a
  correction, not a lesson for the room.
- The correction itself is the chapter's best line and it lands on Sam's
  actual blind spot: *"That leaves you precisely one option, Marsh, and it is
  the opposite of the one you took, because the option is to be dead. That is
  what the tone is for."* Sam has no model for an honest test in which being
  killed is the available answer.
- When Sam volunteers the failure nobody assigned him, the major writes it
  down and turns the page. The room gets the sound of the pen and no comment.
- Night four is used to praise the man who beat him.
- The "difficulty is set above you" answer is kept intact, because the audit
  was right that it is correct.

The wariness travels as behaviour rather than as narration. He opens the
clipboard at a page he has had shut all afternoon and asks one question that is
not about the exercise, gets "School, sir," writes it on the same sheet as the
culvert, and leaves which school alone. Sam waits for a second question. There
is none, which is the same non-reaction chapter 25 ends on and which he has no
way of reading.

The chapter then sends its material upward through paper, not praise: the
safety form with Ives's statement and the medic's card behind it, signed by
three people Sam has never heard of, and the major's own sheet, which travels
separately. "It goes upward anyway" is unchanged and now sits on top of both.

### 7. The chat

Rewritten against CALIBRATION_AUDIT section 2. The three defects and the fix:

**"They have 0 idea what the real one is."** Sam now quotes his own question in
full, so everyone in the chat has the same information he had, and the chat is
about a schedule question rather than about a phrase nobody can parse.

**"Nadia has no reason to think anything is wrong with asking."** The "nadia:
sam" reprimand is gone, and nothing replaced it. Every person in the thread
treats the question as obviously reasonable and says so: *thats a schedule
question*, *thats fine*, *thats fine*, *i would have asked that*, *everyone
would have asked that*, and Priya three days late agreeing as well. Nadia's
contribution is a theory about the room, not a correction of Sam: nobody asked
in the first ten minutes, so by the time he said it out loud it had been
sitting there an hour and had stopped being the same question. Theo lands on
the group's conclusion, that the major was managing the room.

**"Sam was told there was a real one."** He now says so, accurately, from
chapter 25: *a drill sergeant told me in august there was a real one. he said
it was tuesday.* Ruth is the one who reads Ruiz correctly, and she reads Ruiz
rather than Sam: *thats not an answer thats a man getting rid of you.* Sam does
what the sheet says he does, restates unchanged and louder, *he said it twice*,
and gets *he said it twice to get rid of you twice*.

The scene ends with the mystery unsolved and the group split three to one on
whether he should go and ask the major again, which is Eli's vote.

Nobody in the exchange knows better than Sam about anything except Ruiz's tone.

## Constraints

**House Rule 1.** No sentence anywhere explains the gap between Sam and the
Army, names what the major is doing, or tells the reader what the silence
means. Two narrator glosses that survived the previous draft were cut in this
pass as well: "the question he takes off it is one he has put to no other

**Sam does not learn he is exceptional.** He is corrected, voided, beaten clean

not his to set. He reads none of it as evidence about himself: he asks whether

lot of walking for very little, and asks for more of them. The chapter 25
screening theory is what he is still standing on and nothing here disturbs it.
His only self-assessment is a downward correction he volunteers unprompted.

No hard breaks, no em dashes, no curly quotes. `check_edits.py --chapters 26`:
0 problems. `build_manuscript.py`: clean, 35 chapters, 109,318 words.

## Things the audit did not flag

**The chapter had no rank discipline of any kind, not just the missing sir.**
Nobody in the old draft was addressed by rank at any point. The soldiers on the
opposing element, the controller, the medic and the E-4 at the turn-in table
were all anonymous. Ives is now named and ranked, the major uses Private Marsh
where he is coldest, and Sam answers NCOs with Sergeant.

**Sam initiated no contact anywhere in the old chapter.** Every one of the
three encounters was somebody jumping him and Sam reacting, which is a strange
shape for an infantryman on a movement lane and part of why it read as a
martial arts scene rather than a military one. The fence line now has him
finding them, withdrawing, waiting eleven minutes, and choosing the moment,
which is also what gives the major something to ask him about at the end.

**The old chapter had no loss in it.** Sam went four days and seven contacts
without being touched once. The single largest cause of the fan-fiction reading
was not the unarmed fighting but the absence of any outcome that cost him
anything. He is now killed on night four by a man who did nothing but lie still
for seven hours.

**Blanks in a confined space.** The opposing element discharging a blank at two
metres inside a concrete pipe is itself outside the safety rule, and the major
says so and says it has been dealt with over there. This is deliberate: real
incidents have fault on both sides of them, and it keeps the exercise
continuing without pretending Sam did nothing.
