## Job 1 — why ch26 and ch30 sat below the band

**They sat low for opposite reasons, and neither reason was "short chapter."** Length is
not the mechanism: F-K is a per-sentence and per-word average, so a 2,100-word chapter and
a 3,700-word chapter are measured identically. ch24 is 1,422 words and reads 9.5.

Splitting F-K into its two terms across the band:

|---|---|---|---|
| 23 | 8.5 | 20.4 | 1.367 |
| 24 | 9.5 | 21.8 | 1.402 |
| 25 | 8.1 | 20.3 | 1.337 |
| **26** | **7.9** | **20.7** | **1.307**  ← lowest in the band |
| 27 | 8.7 | 21.7 | 1.377 |
| 28 | 8.2 | 18.6 | 1.428 |
| 29 | 8.7 | 20.1 | 1.394 |
| **30** | **7.7** | **17.8**  ← lowest in the band | **1.385** |
| 31 | 8.9 | 20.1 | 1.411 |
| 32 | 11.9 | 29.1 | 1.322 |

**ch26 was a vocabulary problem, not a sentence-length problem.** Its sentences were
already at the band's median length. Its words were the shortest in the band. Per-paragraph
the chapter runs 1.18 to 1.31 syllables per word almost everywhere, and the floor of the
distribution is exactly where you would want it to be: the three contacts. The fence line
sits at 1.183, the casualty checks at 1.226, the culvert at 1.311. A man being taken in the
dark is written in wire, post, grass, boot, thumb, pipe, ground. Buying the missing 0.01
syllables per word out of those paragraphs would mean Latinising a hand-to-hand chapter,
which is the exact failure the FK brief's addendum names ("longer synonyms for short ones").
**So the vocabulary was never the place to fix ch26, and I did not touch it.**

**ch30 was a sentence-length problem, and the short sentences are the interview.** Its
vocabulary is fine, above the band median. It is 17.8 words per sentence because half the
chapter is `"From when?"` / `"Understood."` / `"Asked by whom."`. 42% of its sentences run
under ten words and 12.7% sit in runs of three or more short ones, both band-highs.
Inflating clearance-interview Q&A to reach 16 words a line is the second failure the
addendum names, and `passes/fix/H30.md` already refused this chapter once on exactly that
ground and was right to. **So the dialogue was never the place to fix ch30, and I did not
touch it.**

### What actually moved the numbers

Neither chapter was raised by a reading-grade technique. Both were raised by work that had
an independent reason to happen.

**ch26 rose to 8.1 as a side effect of Job 2.4.** The chapter's low point was not its
vocabulary but its ending: a fully staged, fully populated debrief that stopped writing
itself two paragraphs before the end. Writing the major's actual response added 22 sentences
of instructional speech, which is naturally longer-clause than the action narration around
it. Word count 2,344 to 2,561, still mid-band. Two precise nouns went in as well, both
because they are what the things are called rather than for syllables: `signs for the
compass on a hand receipt` (you sign a hand receipt, not a clipboard) and `exactly the
shape of the casualty check he's been drilled to give` (the chapter does that check three
times and never names it). Together those two are worth about +0.02; the debrief is the
rest.

The chapter's dialogue measures also came off the floor as a consequence, without a word of
existing dialogue being changed: quoted share 12.8% to 17.9% (pass), mean spoken sentence
13.1 to 15.8, spoken lines of 1-3 words 8.7% to 6.9% (pass).

**ch30 rose to 8.0 on five edits, three of which are prose repair.**

1. Cut `She tells him everything.` — D_FINDINGS flags this sentence (not the scene) as the
   same flat-absolute-then-exception construction as ch3's "Nobody has ever sent Chloe
   anything," and it is contradicted thirty lines later by the refusal. It also restates
   the sentence directly above it: "she gives him more than the minimum from the first page
   onward." **See the caveat below — a previous pass defended this line.**
2. Cut `She says it anyway. It's true, and it's the whole of what she has.` — House Rule 1.
   It sits after Chloe has already said it and glosses an answer the dialogue carried, and
   "She says it anyway" appears to be a fossil from a draft where the narration preceded the
   quote. "He writes down the reasoning along with the answer" already does its job.
3. `an online form` became `the electronic questionnaire for national security positions`.
   The form has a real name and Chloe would know it. This is the single largest contributor.
4. `a government ID` became `a federal investigator's credentials`, per `WHITAKER.md`.
5. `Most of it is easy. A few fields make her stop` became `Most of it is easy, but a few
   fields make her stop` — the logical joint written in.

Plus one neutral precision change: `The clearance investigation` became `The background
investigation`, which is the correct name for the process and removes a clearance/clearance
echo with the paragraph that closes the chapter.

ch30 now sits at 8.0 exactly (8.05 unrounded). That is thin, and I stopped there on purpose:
everything past this point in that chapter is either interview dialogue or already dense
narration, and the next move would have been padding.

## Job 3 — should ch32 place Chloe? Yes. Applied, in the narrower of the two forms.

I drafted two placed versions and read all three against each other. The number is not a
factor here: ch32 reads 11.9 as-is and all three variants land between 12.1 and 12.3.

**A, as it stood:**

> chloe: theo that was a drill
>
> theo: it wasnt a drill. i read the file.
>
> Chloe reads that one twice and sets the phone face down on the desk before she picks it
> back up.

**B, Chloe added to the ensemble roster** that opens the last chat block, alongside Sam at
the gym and Nadia at the till:

> ...Nadia closing out a Tuesday's till a second time to be sure the number holds, Chloe at
> her own desk six weeks into the job the clearance was for, reading the thread in the gap
> between one document and the next.

**C, placement carried entirely on the beat she already has** (this is what I committed):

> Chloe reads that one twice at her own desk, six weeks into the job, and sets the phone face
> down next to the badge before she picks it back up.

**Recommendation: C. B is worse than doing nothing.**

Reasoning, in the order it decided me:

1. **"the desk" in version A has no antecedent.** It is the one definite article in the
   chapter pointing at something the reader has never been shown. Every other body in ch32
   is placed, most of them twice: Eli at the window onto the parking structure and later
   standing at his kitchen counter, Kavi in the converted classroom, Nadia at her counter,
   Ruth on her apartment floor, Theo on the back steps. Chloe alone got a definite article
   and no room. That is not a format decision, it is the one gap in an otherwise complete
   pattern.

2. **What she is placed next to does the chapter's work without a word of commentary.**
   Theo has just said the government has held a file on their school for nineteen years.
   The badge on the desk beside her phone puts her inside that government, holding its
   credential, at the moment she is the only one of them who has it wrong. Nothing in the
   sentence says so. This is the cheapest available version of the chapter's own irony and it
   costs one clause.

3. **B flattens her into the group at the exact moment she needs to be outside it.** The
   roster sentence promises participation, and Chloe does not participate — she posts one
   line, near the end, and it is wrong. Putting her in the opening roll-call alongside two
   people who are actively working makes her one of the crowd, and it overloads a sentence
   that already carries two placements and is followed by two more. Her thinness in this
   chapter is the effect; C keeps it and B spends it.

4. **"six weeks into the job" earns its place separately** by anchoring the chapter's own
   Nov 2025 - Feb 2026 dateline at the far end, and it pre-echoes ch34's "five months into a
   job whose clearance she is stretching" without stealing it.

I deliberately did **not** write "the job the clearance was for," which was in my first
draft. It is a backward nudge at ch30 and the badge already carries it.
