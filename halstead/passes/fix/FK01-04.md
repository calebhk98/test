## Chapter 1 — Before

Arrived already rewritten by the interrupted run, at 8.5 but 5,051 words. Two
jobs: bring the length down, and undo the addendum's inflation defects.

**Inflation found and undone.** The earlier pass had swapped short words for
longer ones that were not what the thing is called, and had appended clauses
that named a feeling the action had already carried:

- `telephone` → `phone` (three places), `the remainder of the morning` → `the
  rest of the morning`, `the examination room` → cut, `in the cafeteria` kept
  only where lunch is actually in the cafeteria.
- *"her thumb keeping a place she has already abandoned, because turning pages
  at the right speed is what somebody reading a book looks like from the
  kitchen"* → the clause is gone. The reader can see why she turns the page.
- *"and she decides the room simply failed to hear her, which is the most
  generous explanation available"* → *"so they must not have heard her."*
- *"stamping cards with her eyes on the cards rather than on Chloe, which is its
  own kind of answer"* → the tag is gone.
- The dinner speech had been coordination-spliced into one long run
  (*"...with the batteries and it got there on its own"*); the original's
  *"Nobody put it there"* beat is restored as *"neither of them put it there."*

**Compression.** 1,300 words came out of narration without losing a scene:
the worksheet block went from eleven sentences to five, the reading catalogue
(cereal box, road signs, church bulletin, four books at a time) went from five
sentences to one, and the checkup and conference scenes lost their throat-
clearing. Representative:

> *before* — Reading works a different way entirely. In the morning she reads
> the cereal box, both sides... At her grandmother's house she reads the church
> bulletin. At the library her card is good for four books at a time, which she
> has now argued about with the same librarian more than once. **(5 sentences)**

> *after* — Reading works a different way entirely, and the difference shows up
> wherever it is her own idea rather than an assignment. In one week that is the
> cereal box at breakfast, both sides, including a contest that ended in a
> different year, the road signs read aloud from the back seat until her mom
> asks her to stop, her grandmother's church bulletin, and the four books at a
> time her library card is good for, argued about with the same librarian more
> than once. **(2 sentences)**

**Restored when the ceiling moved to 4,000.** Four things had been cut only to
fit 3,400 and are back, because none of the cuts was an improvement on its own
merits:

1. the recess tennis-ball argument, restored as dialogue (*"You're out." /
   "No I'm not, because I caught it clean..."*) instead of the narrated summary;
2. the librarian's *"How old are you?" / "Six." / "Four."*;
3. the doctor's *"Do you finish your work?"* and Chloe's answer;
4. cousin Dana's whole conversation and the car home afterwards (*"She was quiet
   tonight." / "She was fine." / "I didn't say she wasn't fine."*).

Ch1 is 963 words shorter than the version this pass received and 963 shorter
than the original draft's 4,712; the chapter is now the third longest in the
book rather than the longest.

## Chapter 2 — March 4th

Untouched at the start, 5.1 and 3,366 words. There was almost no room, so this
was strictly same-content-denser-sentences plus the dialogue reserve.

**Sentence combining with real joints.** 215 sentences down to 136 with the word
count unchanged. The Ben exchange is the clearest case:

> *before* — "So. We're going to do some stuff that's kind of like games. Some
> of it's easy, some of it's really hard, and the hard ones are supposed to be
> hard. If you don't get one, that's fine, because that's how it's built. Deal?"
> / "Deal. Is it a test? Like a real one, with a grade at the end?" / "Sort of."
> / "Can you fail it? What happens if I get all of them wrong?"

> *after* — "We're going to do some stuff that's kind of like games, and some of
> it is easy while some of it is really hard, and the hard ones are supposed to
> be hard, so missing one is part of the design." / "Okay, but is it a test, like
> a real one with a grade at the end, and can you fail it, because what happens
> if I get all of them wrong?"

**The dialogue reserve.** Spoken mean 9.1 → 16.6 and one-to-three-word lines
23.5% → 1.7%, with no line lengthened past its natural size. It was done by
removing filler turns rather than inflating kept ones: *"Sort of."*, *"Go,"*,
*"Stop."*, *"Juice?"*, *"Okay."*, *"Deal."*, *"Six,"*, *"Four,"* either became
narration (`Ben says go and starts the timer with his thumb`) or were folded
into the adjacent turn. Kavi's warning in the addendum was the test applied
throughout: any line already over sixteen words was left exactly as written.

**Vocabulary, precise rather than longer.** `the questionnaire on her mom's
clipboard`, `Then it's vocabulary`, `Then the questions turn into similarities`,
`cross out the duplicates`, `a set of judgement questions`, `the parking
structure`, `the corridor`, `handwashing`, `the pediatrician`. Nothing was
swapped for a longer synonym of itself.

## Chapter 3 — The Letter

Untouched at the start, 5.2 and 3,408 words, and the hardest of the four because
70% of it is two adults arguing in short turns.

**Arguing is where reasoned speech belongs**, so the parents' exchanges took
subordination rather than compression:

> *before* — "We didn't apply to this." / "No, this is the first we've heard of
> anything like it." / "So how do they have her name." / "I don't know, I've
> never given anybody her name for something like this, not a school, not a
> program, nobody."

> *after* — "Did you see this, because I have no memory of applying to anything
> of the kind." / "See what," her dad says, taking it and reading it one-handed,
> still eating, then putting the fork down to hold it with both, "and no, this is
> the first I've heard of it." / "So how do they have her name, when I keep it
> off every form I am handed, school, program, all of it."

**Negative space** fell 4.9 → 2.2 in the process, mostly by rewriting the ones

something addressed to her parents"*; *"turn up nothing beyond the school's own
website"* → *"turn up the school's own website and then stop"*; *"a building I
have never seen"* → *"a building I have yet to see."*

## What resisted

**7+ character words: 10.1-12.3% against a 13.2% median, in all four.** This one
is not going to move without breaking the addendum's rule. These chapters are
set in a first-grade classroom, a hotel room, a psychologist's testing room, a
kitchen with a letter on the table, and a summer dormitory. The objects have
short names: blocks, cards, symbols, juice, a folder, a stamp, a sticky note, a
tray. Everything with a real long name was already used or has been put in
(`questionnaire`, `parking structure`, `viscosity`, `percentile`, `composite`,
`transportation`, `unsolicited`, `evaporation and condensation and
precipitation`, `similarities`, `vocabulary`, `handwashing`, `duplicates`,
`pediatrician`). Past that point the only remaining moves are longer synonyms
for short words, which is the exact defect the addendum names, and I stopped.
Chapter 1 reaches 12.3% and the other three sit near 10.5%; the difference is
that chapter 1 has a school, a library and a doctor's office in it and the other
three have a testing room, a phone and a dormitory.

**Sentence-length variation CV: 61-73 against an 85.3 median.** This is the real
cost of the technique and it is worth being explicit about. Combining short
sentences into one long one with its joint spelled out pulls the whole
distribution toward the middle: chapter 1 went 72.7 → 50.6 at its densest before
I deliberately re-broke five sentences into short ones and re-merged two others
into long ones, which brought it back to 61. Chapters 2-4 landed 68-73 the same
way. Getting to 85 would need genuinely two-word sentences sitting next to
sixty-word ones, which is a different prose style from this book's, and is not
what any part of the brief asked for. I stopped at the point where the reading
grade was met and the prose had audible variety.

**Chapter 4's negative space, 4.0 → 4.6.** It went up rather than down, and the
cause is arithmetic: the chapter has a fixed set of negatives it cannot lose
(*"a pony isn't a baby horse"*, *"they put us in the one that isn't a planet
anymore"*, *"It isn't a punishment"*, *"Nothing comes, all day"*), and cutting
the sentence count by a third raises their share of it. Three were removed to
hold it under 5%; the remaining six are all load-bearing.
