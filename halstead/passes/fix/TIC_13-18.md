## Per construction

### "that's not X, that's Y" — 1 found, 1 changed

Only one lowercase instance in the span (the pattern is case-sensitive, so sentence-initial
"That's not..." does not count). No character sheet in `characters/` records this as a
signature, so nothing was held back for one owner.

  of an argument."* → **"No, hold on, you've got most of an argument there."** The "No"

### The word 'same' — 74 found, 44 changed, 30 kept

The largest job. The rule I used: keep it where the sameness is the claim being made, cut
it where it is a connective.

Best substitutions:

- ch13, Chloe working out the paintball problem: *"It's the same barrel, Ruth, pointed at
  where the ball's going... and everyone's shooting at the same speed"* → **"The barrel is
  the hose, Ruth, pointed at where the ball's going... and every marker on that floor
  shoots at one speed."** Two "same"s out, and the metaphor she is actually making
  (barrel = hose) gets stated instead of gestured at.
- ch13, Chloe on Hearn: *"Same length, same week, same handwriting at the bottom."* →
  **"Ten pages, every week, and the same handwriting at the bottom."** The anaphora
  survives with one instance instead of three, and "ten pages" is the chapter's title.
- ch15, Ruth on the bread: *"It's the same bread, Sam, same flour, same ovens, same
  people."* → **"It's the same bread, Sam, and the same flour and ovens and people behind
  it."** Four down to two; the list still runs.
- ch17: *"all seven get the same question wrong on the same test in the same way"* → drops
  the middle one, which the next paragraph ("the seven papers") makes redundant anyway.
- ch17: *"all seven arrived at the same wrong place"* → **"all seven arrived exactly where
  she had pointed them"**, which is more damning and drops the word.
- ch16, Eli: *"the same message going out every Tuesday at the same time"* → **"every
  Tuesday, always at that hour"**. The repetition is the cryptographic weakness, so one
  "same" had to stay; the second was decoration.
- Straight deletions with no replacement, per the brief: "the same thing they have been
  doing with darts" → "what they have been doing with darts"; "with the same man teaching
  both" → "with one man teaching both"; "in the same corridor" → "in one corridor"; "That
  same week" → "That week"; "In the same month" → "That month".

Kept, thirty of them, all cases where the sameness is the assertion: Hearn saying
everything at the same volume (his introduction); "everyone's shooting at the same speed"
in its new form; "the same picture in both of those numbers" (the art-history argument);
"the same ethics class as you since we were ten"; "Both are describing the same report"
(the bridge); "you're both running off the same count" (the arrow); "playing the same six
seconds"; "the same twenty seconds and no new reason for them"; "the same proof since
Easter"; "You have shot the same number four different ways" (Voss, scored); "It's the same
bread" as the payoff at the Thanksgiving table; and the seven inside the protected scene.

### The numbers

Handled as one job across four patterns, because the same sentence often carried two.
Total: 197 found, 130 changed, 67 kept.

**Method.** Three moves, in order of preference. First, kill a cluster at its root where
one figure was being repeated by the scene rather than by the author: ch17's clay trap
threw *every nine seconds* and the number then recurred in Aymar's stopwatch line, Voss's
card line, Aymar's rebuttal and Kavi's summary. Changing the machine's cycle to seven
seconds removed five "nine"s at a stroke with nothing lost. Second, drop the precision
where it was never load-bearing: *nine minutes later* → "a few minutes later", *about four
days* → "a few days", *running the eleven numbers again* → "running the numbers again".
Third, substitute a non-tic figure, keeping internal arithmetic consistent.

**Consistency checks made.** The ch13 drill: intruders went four → five in both the scene
and the failure sheet that scores it. The ch14 Watch sheet: item four → three and item nine
→ seven, both in the sheet and in the two later references, with the count of failures left
at eleven so the item numbers still fit inside it. The ch18 ledger: motors went nine → a
dozen at eleven → eight dollars apiece, with "four of which are turning over" and "nearly
sixty in the barn" unchanged, and the loan sums (120 + 40 + 30 against 200, closing at 217
to Rustem's 940) left exactly as they were. The ch16 author list went sixth of nine → sixth
of ten, with "the other eight people" following it to nine.

**Kept, and why.** Everything scored kept its figure. The 10v1 progression in ch13 (best in
the room nine seconds, Chloe four then six, Sam fourteen, Chloe eleven by June) and its
continuation in ch15 (twenty-four seconds, thirtieth of ninety) and ch18 (average forty-one
against forty-five at fourteen, thirty-eight then forty then thirty-nine on the bad
Tuesday). The marksmanship plateau in ch18 (sixty-one, fifty-nine, sixty; twenty then
forty-one then sixty; eleven people stopped, five at sixty, three at fifty-eight, two at
fifty-five, one at sixty-three, which has to add to eleven). Voss's "the same number four
different ways". Hearn's opening arithmetic in ch13, which is the chapter's premise: an
essay every four days, two to four thousand words. The four axiom lines at the top of a
proof page in ch16. Sandoval's four signatories in ch18. Ruth's four hundred million Arabic
speakers. The four teachers left standing at half past three in ch14, and "eighteen minutes
to four" against the board's "three forty-two".

**What is still over, and why.** "Number: four" finishes at 47 against a scaled target of
31. Eighteen of those 47 are inside the protected scene, where the number four is the
scene's spine: four men over the fence, four kids, four seconds of compute, four in the
morning, four buildings away. Outside that block the span holds 26, which is under target.
"Number: forty" finishes at 8 against 6, and all eight are scored self-defence averages,
the marksmanship progression, or the investing ledger — the case the assignment names.
Where a scored number and an incidental one sat in the same sentence I cut the incidental
one, which is how the remaining figures ended up almost entirely load-bearing.
