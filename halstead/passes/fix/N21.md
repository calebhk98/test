## The two reorderings

**Papers before reps.** The "three research papers / read-in-a-locked-room" section
used to sit after the reps had already arrived and toured the school. It now sits
right after the essay-discovery section (the Penn officer, the thirty flagged
files) and closes on "Within the month, offices that had only ever exchanged
letters with the school start asking about a visit," which now feeds directly into
"Eleven of the flagged schools send somebody." The papers are what gets them there.

**Four percent after acceptances.** Deleted the early common-room scene where Ruth,
Sam and Chloe dissect the 4% figure before anyone has applied — at that point they
have no reason to find it strange, only reason to be anxious about getting in, so I
replaced it with a short scene of that anxiety instead (Ruth compulsively
refreshing a portal, Sam turning a card over without playing it, Chloe crossing out
a line on her deadline sheet for no reason she can name). The store-window
analogy, the "you'd want your money back for the trip in" line, and the "that's
probably it" beat are all moved into the new closing scene, after Chloe, Sam and
Ruth compare notes on how many of them got in. Chloe now raises the strangeness of
the number herself, in dialogue with the others, rather than the narration doing it
for her.

## The cut and replace

Deleted, verbatim, both lines the author flagged:
- "Either the board that has been placing her since she was seven is measuring
  something these fourteen offices cannot see, or it has been measuring her
  against the wrong ninety people all along, and both of those are questions about
  the school rather than about her."
- "So either every one of those places is lying on its website, or the number
  means something that isn't us."

In their place, the relocated four-percent scene (above) does the work: Chloe asks
the store-window question, Ruth answers it, Sam supplies "or you'd want your money
back for the trip in," and Ruth lands on "It's on their site, and it isn't real.
Both of those can be true at once." Nobody states a conclusion about the school
itself; they land on "marketing" and stop there, same as the author's note
describes.

## The sports building

Rebuilt per the note. Cut the "two rooms that stay locked whether or not anyone is
visiting" (which would have blocked ordinary students from class) and the
unmotivated crate. In its place: the fighting hour and the range are simply the two
class-visit requests reps never get approved, on the same 48-hours-notice request
system that governs every other class visit — nothing is physically locked, staff
just decline those two slots every time, with a different stated reason each time
(the "Sport" / "Athletics" / "Recreation" beat is kept, now attached to this
mechanism instead of a locked door). The one physical clue is now a Duke rep
hearing something "a beat too regular for a ball game" through the wall and passing
two students carrying an unmarked canvas case, gone before he can place either. The
reader has what they need to connect this to the ten-on-one and the range,
established earlier via Sam's own board and rifle lines in the same section; nobody
states the connection.
