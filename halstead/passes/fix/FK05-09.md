## Chapter 5, Behind

| Flesch-Kincaid | 5.1 | 9.0 | **9.3** |
| words / sentence | 14.9 | 24.1 | 24.8 |

| word count | 3,064 | 3,543 | **3,398** |

The job here was 145 words over the ceiling without losing the 9.0, plus a check
of the inherited rewrite. It came out at 3,398 and 9.3, so the grade went up
while the chapter got shorter.

**How the words came out.** Not by shaving adjectives: shaving alone drops
words-per-sentence and takes the grade with it. Ten pairs of sentences were
merged with their joint written in, which removes a sentence and a few words at
the same time and holds the mean up. Example:

> He draws a rectangle, shades half of it, then cuts the shaded half into
> pieces, talking the whole way through about the question underneath the
> question. Chloe follows him as far as the shading, loses track in the middle
> of what the pieces are pieces of, and is left looking at a rectangle with a
> number of lines across it.

became one sentence joined on `and`, four words shorter and with the same
content. Same move on the Owen-in-maths paragraph (three sentences to two,
eleven words), the bridge-load paragraph, the packet paragraph, and the last
line of the chapter.

**Two defects found in the inherited rewrite and fixed.**

1. *A joke lost its antecedent.* The teacher's line had been expanded from
   "You can keep going if you're mid-sentence... Not mid-paragraph" to "if
   you're in the middle of a sentence... but not if you're in the middle of a
   paragraph." Sam's next line is "How mid is mid," which no longer had a "mid"
   to refer to. Reverted; that also gave back six words.
2. *A comma splice.* "There is a pool, swimming is at four every afternoon, and
   because Chloe arrived unable to swim..." is two short independent clauses
   welded with a comma, which is the failure the brief names in rule 1. Rewritten
   as "Swimming is at four every afternoon, and because Chloe arrived unable to
   swim she is put in the group at the shallow end of the pool..."

**Negative space.** The one narration hit went: "and Chloe, without running the
sentence past herself even once, says" became "and Chloe answers him a half
second later with". The remaining four hits are all dialogue in the Owen scene,
where "he wasn't having fun" is the thing being said and cannot be said any
other way.

Hard line breaks: 102, unchanged.

## Chapter 6, The List

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| Flesch-Kincaid | 4.8 | **9.0** |
| words / sentence | 14.2 | 24.6 |
| 7+ char words % | 10.3 | 11.0 |
| spoken mean (tool) | 12.9 | 23.1 |
| spoken mean (per quoted sentence, see note) | 10.6 | 15.0 |
| negative space % | **12.1** | **1.4** |
| word count | 3,048 | 3,399 |
| quoted share % | 49.6 | 48.2 |

The hardest of the five: the worst negative space in the book, the talkiest
chapter in the book, and only 352 words of headroom under the ceiling.

**What went wrong first, and the correction.** My first draft of this chapter
hit 9.2 and came in at **3,910 words** - the exact defect the addendum names.
The cause is worth recording, because it is a trap in the method: spelling out
a logical joint costs four to six words a sentence, and this chapter has 215
sentences, so treating every sentence adds 900 words before you look up. The
lever that actually works is **merging**, not adding. Merging two sentences with
their connective costs about two words and removes a sentence, so it raises
words-per-sentence from both ends at once. The finished chapter has 215
sentences down to 138 and only 351 words added.

**Negative space, 12.1% to 1.4%.** Twenty-six negative constructions down to
three. Most were curable by saying the positive thing, which is usually the more
specific thing:

> "Nobody put theirs on the bus." -> "Every one of us drove, and the letter offered a bus."
> "It doesn't mean anything here." -> "Here it buys you a paper plate and a seat at this table."
> "I don't know what that means here." -> "...which is a sentence that means something there and lands in this room as weather."
> "Sam never got it, and he said the click was broken, but it's not broken" -> "Sam gave up before his and decided the click was broken, but the click works fine"
> "You weren't even on the phone call" -> "You were on the wrong side of a door for that call"

Three were kept deliberately, and they are the chapter's spine: the mother's
"- I still don't know what it is", and her dad's "I don't know what that is,
Meg" four hundred words from the end. The chapter is about adults who cannot
name the thing; those two sentences are the naming failing, and rewriting them
positive would delete the point.

**Cataloguing.** The recitation was already a list; the change was moving the
computer-room block out of quotation and into one narrated sentence, which took
115 quoted words down to 95 narrated ones and dropped three sentences:

> Then the computers, which she says are actually two entirely different things,
> counting them off on her fingers: the first is typing the words in yourself
> instead of dragging coloured blocks around like it's a game, and she made it
> count to a hundred and skip every seven on the way past; the second is a room
> they were taken into, enormous and kept freezing cold on purpose, because the
> processors throw off enough heat to cook themselves, where a woman who works
> in there took them through the whole arrangement, down to the water pipes
> under the floor.

The chapter already alternated quoted and narrated items in the recitation
(fractions, wood shop and chess were narrated before this pass), so this is the
chapter's own pattern, not an imported one.

**Note on the spoken mean, 23.1.** This number is not what it looks like, and
the author should know before reading it as the addendum's chapter-10 defect
happening again. `dialogue_study` concatenates all quoted spans and then splits
on terminal punctuation, so a line that ends `...both times," the woman says.`
runs straight into the next speaker's line, because neither quote contains a
full stop. Four of the "spoken sentences" it counts in this chapter are three
consecutive speakers glued together, the longest at 83 words. Measured per
quoted sentence instead, this chapter is at **15.0**, up from 10.6, against
chapter 10 - the chapter the addendum called inflated - at **17.3** on the same
measure. So the dialogue here is less lifted than the line that was flagged.
The longest single spoken sentences are Chloe's recitation (37 to 51 words),
which is the chapter's premise, and the originals in that passage were already
28 to 44.

Hard line breaks: 125, unchanged. Line count unchanged at 130.
