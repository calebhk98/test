## Chapter 10 — the two named defects

**1. Kavi's line.** Restored to the original wording, exactly:

> "That's worse than not knowing. If you don't know then it's nobody's fault,
> but he knew and he forgot, and that's a decision about how much it mattered."

The rewrite ("ignorance belongs to everybody equally… forgetting is a decision
about how much a thing mattered") bought four words and an aphorism and cost an
eight-year-old his voice. His tag went back from "I'm allowed to *consider*
that worse" to "I'm allowed to *think* that's worse" for the same reason.

**2. "whose name is a mystery to me"** → back to **"whose name I don't even
know."** A longer synonym for a short phrase, not the name of anything. The
same paragraph got its gesture back as its own beat: *He points down the
corridor at nobody.*

**Ten further dialogue lines deflated.** The spoken mean was 22.8, the highest
in the book. It is now 18.7. Every one of these was the FK pass fusing two
sentences with a connective and producing a line nobody would say:

- "…which makes it a February opinion, and February is over" → "…and February is allowed to change its mind."
- Ruth's arrival speech: one 45-word sentence split back into two at "Come on".
- "What's your last name, spelled out, **because** I want to get it right…" → back to a question and a statement.
- "You got it," Ruth says, "**so** which way did you go…" → back to a full stop.
- "so mine is shorter only in the cases where it happens to be right" → "so mine isn't shorter, it's just wrong most of the time."
- Kavi's stairwell speech un-fused; "You've already got all of it" back toward "There isn't anything to get, Chloe."
- Chloe: "Then I'm still missing whatever actually happened to him" → "Then I don't understand what actually happened to him."
- Her mother's eating question back to "Are you eating? I don't mean are they giving you food, I mean are you actually eating it."
- Ruth's cooking speech got its second half back: "That's why he keeps saying it. He's said it to me six times and he'll say it to you six times."
- Fen: "which is the only rule there is, and I'm the only person permitted to touch them" → "That's the only rule there is, and nobody touches them but me."

**Sixteen narration stiffenings undone**, all of them longer synonyms rather
than the names of things: retrieves→picks it up, surrenders→just gives her,
timetable→day, academic subjects→subjects, demonstrate→show her what he means,
interior staircase / gallery of shelves→a staircase inside it / a second level
of shelves, circumference / a single revolution→the rim / while the wheel goes
round one time, three notices→three sheets of paper, kitchen access / permitted
→the kitchens / allowed in them, cartography→drawing the world, arrives /
departs→comes in / goes out, "at speed, with all five republics in the right
order"→"in one go", "the Orkneys and the Shetlands"→"the small islands off the
top of Scotland".

Two corrections kept, because they were errors rather than register: "both
storeys" → "both floors" and "all the way round" → "all the way around". The
chapter is otherwise American and those two came in with the FK pass.

**Word count.** 3,738 → 3,400 → 3,685. Everything cut only to reach the old
3,400 ceiling is back: the boy asking about the bridge at camp, Kavi's corridor
gesture, the breakfast tunnel joke, "When it finishes, she finishes", the

**Still short.** `spoken lines of 1-3 words` 8.6% against 8%. The six are

- Six sentences into two: "Mathematics carries over, along with writing, literature, political history. French is new. Logic is new. ### Where chapter 13 fails a rule, and why

`negative-space sentences` is **7.0% against the 5% ceiling**. It came down to
4.9% and went back up when the rules changed. Eleven sentences carry a hit and
I judge all eleven right:

- **Two are the Watch scoring sheet** — "They do not enter a building." / "They do not learn anything about you." That is a posted institutional document, not prose, and its rules are written the way rules are written.
- **Two are protected lines** — "That's Madison," he says, and starts the hour *without adding anything to it*; and the closing "I don't know," Chloe says.
- **Seven are dialogue where the negative is the content**: "you can't react to it, there isn't time to react to it, you have to already be there" (the discovery the whole paintball scene turns on); "you don't watch the water" (the two-beat that makes the analogy land); "nobody is waiting on you to walk it to a printer on Saturday morning" (Hearn's whole comparison); "I don't know what we were supposed to be doing either" (Ruth, from a prior pass); "it doesn't sort them"; "that's not nothing"; "having the answer doesn't help you at all, does it".

Getting under 5% here needs either a paraphrase that stiffens the line or a
narrator who explains the point instead — and item 3 of the new rules forbids
the second. I have taken the rule as the lower priority of the two and am
flagging it rather than meeting it quietly. If the ceiling is meant to bind
regardless, say so and I will take another four out of the seven.
