## Chapter 10 — april

This is the chapter the interrupted run overshot. The job here was to come
**down** to a defensible number with natural dialogue and get under the ceiling.

| metric | before (overshot) | after | note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flesch-Kincaid | 9.8 | **9.1** | brought down into the 9.0-9.5 band asked for |
| words per sentence | 24.7 | 23.0 | |
| 7+ char words % | 13.5 | 13.4 | still above the 13.2 corpus median |
| mean spoken sentence | **22.8** | **19.6** | highest in the book, now well inside range |
| negative-space % | 0.0 | 4.1 | 0.0% was scrubbed, not clean; under the 5% ceiling |
| word count | 3,738 | **3,400** | at the ceiling |

Goals at goal: 15/22 → 16/22.

### What was reverted, and why

**1. Kavi's line (named in the addendum).** Restored to the original wording:

> "That's worse than not knowing. If you don't know then it's nobody's fault,
> but he knew and he forgot, and that's a decision about how much it mattered."

The rewrite ("ignorance belongs to everybody equally... forgetting is a decision
about how much a thing mattered") bought four words and an aphorism and cost an
eight-year-old his voice. The original was already two sentences and thirty
words and needed nothing. Kavi's tag went back from "I'm allowed to *consider*
that worse" to "I'm allowed to *think* that's worse" for the same reason.

**2. "whose name is a mystery to me" (named in the addendum)** → back to
**"whose name I don't even know."** A longer synonym for a short phrase, not the
name of anything.

**3. Eight further dialogue lines deflated** where the FK pass had jammed two
sentences together with a connective and produced a line no one would say:

- "I said probably not once, in February, which makes it a February opinion, and February is over." → "…and February is allowed to change its mind."
- Ruth's arrival speech: the 45-word single sentence split back into two at "Come on".
- "What's your last name, spelled out, **because** I want to get it right…" → back to a question and a statement.
- "You got it," Ruth says, "**so** which way did you go…" → back to a full stop.
- Ruth's proof: "so mine is shorter only in the cases where it happens to be right" → "so mine isn't shorter, it's just wrong most of the time."
- Kavi's stairwell speech un-fused into two sentences.
- Chloe: "Then I'm still missing whatever actually happened to him" → "Then I don't understand what actually happened to him."
- Her mother: "Are you eating, and I mean actually eating it, rather than whether they are putting food in front of you at mealtimes?" → "Are you eating? I don't mean are they giving you food, I mean are you actually eating it."

**4. Negative space put back where it was natural.** 0.0% is not a clean
chapter, it is a chapter where every "didn't" has been paraphrased out. Six
sentences of ordinary negation are back ("I didn't know what to tell her",
"I didn't say anything", "Nothing."→"Silence." was kept as narration), landing
at 4.1% against the 5% ceiling.

### Where the grade was kept without the dialogue

The 0.7 the dialogue gave back was recovered in the narration, per the brief:
precise names rather than longer synonyms.

- "her day is seven hours: five subjects" → "her **timetable** is seven hours: five **academic** subjects"
- "off a list on a wall" → "off the **elective** list on the wall" (the word ch11 already uses)
- "one point marked on the rim… while the wheel goes round once" → "one point marked on its **circumference**… in a single **revolution**"
- "the small islands off the top of Scotland" → "the **Orkneys and the Shetlands** off the top of Scotland"
- "three sheets of paper" → "three **notices**"; "the kitchens, who is allowed in them" → "**kitchen access**, who is **permitted** in"
- "drawing the world from memory on blank paper" → "**cartography**, meaning the whole world drawn from memory on blank paper"
- Also corrected: "both storeys" → "both floors" and "all the way round" → "all the way around" (the FK pass had put two Britishisms into an otherwise American chapter).

338 words came out to reach the ceiling: one whole sentence (the boy asking
about the bridge at camp), Kavi's corridor gesture, the breakfast tunnel joke,
"When it finishes, she finishes", and roughly sixty small trims. Nothing was
added to the chapter.

### Still short

`sentence-length variation CV` 54.5 against a 85.3 median, and `relative
clause %` 34.5 against a 14-26 band. Both are inherited from the overshoot:
the chapter is now a run of 20-30 word sentences with very few short ones
(`sentences under 10 words` is 12.8%). Restoring "Silence." and the reverted
two-sentence speeches moved it in the right direction but did not fix it. The
honest fix is a further pass that puts short sentences back in, which would
cost about 0.4 of F-K.

`spoken lines of 1-3 words` is 9.4% against 8%. The six offenders are
"Aymar." (spelling her name), "Fen." (calling into a dark room), "What."
(Kavi's beat), "Are you eating?", "Yes." (the chapter's closing answer to her
mother) and "September," / "September, then,". Every one is doing its job.

## Chapter 11 — eight

| metric | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| Flesch-Kincaid | 6.2 | **9.1** |
| words per sentence | 17.1 | 24.3 |
| 7+ char words % | 10.5 | 11.1 |
| mean spoken sentence | 14.2 | 18.3 |
| negative-space % | 7.6 | **4.3** |
| word count | 3,593 | **3,400** |

Goals at goal: 11/22 → 13/22.

Narration went 18.6 → 28.4 words per sentence, which is where the whole gain
came from; spoken went 14.2 → 18.3 and is now above the floor without being
anywhere near ch10's old 22.8. Almost all of it is **spelling out the logical
joint**, not splicing:

- Six sentences into two, curriculum paragraph: "Mathematics carries over, along with writing, literature, political history. French is new. Logic is new. Logic is an hour of…" → "…with mathematics, writing, literature and political history carrying over and French and logic new, **logic being** an hour of being shown a sentence and asked what the sentence is actually asserting, **which** she can do from the first week."
- "The afternoon block is a different thing on each day of the week. Nerf on Mondays, … The school picks all five…" → one sentence with the catalogue inside it and "**with** the school picking all five, everybody doing all five, and no arguing with the sheet."
- "With drawing she found out at the end of term. With dance she can feel it while it is happening…" → "**because** with drawing she found out at the end of term, **whereas** with dance she can feel it while it is happening…"

Vocabulary, in the narration only, and only where the thing has a name:
the **gymnasium**, a **carpenter's mallet** whose handle comes off the
**spokeshave** oval, forty hands drawn in **graphite** on **cartridge paper**,
the **ranking sheet**, the back row of the **altos** and going flat on the
**descending line**, the whole job being **unison**, the **instructor**,
**stationary** paper targets, asking whether she can **transfer**, the
**language shelves**. "on the pitch" → "on the field", which is what the rest
of the book says.

Negative space 7.6 → 4.3 by rewriting nine sentences and keeping five: the
mother's "so it isn't fine, and I'm not having this conversation again in a
hospital", Chloe's "I don't think there's a way out of that", her father's
"Nobody's ever put the hinge to me", the grandmother's "I didn't want to say
anything to you at the time", and "says she isn't hungry". Those are the ones
where the negative is the meaning.

193 words came out: the apple-scene staging compressed, Ruth's redundant
question in it, two stage directions that had become redundant once the two
speeches either side of them were merged into one turn, "She asks again in
November and gets it in four words", "Dance is Fridays" and "Drawing is
Thursdays" (both already in the five-day catalogue three paragraphs earlier),
and about forty small trims.

### Still short

`7+ char words` 11.1% against the 13.2 median. This chapter is 41.5% dialogue
and most of it is a family arguing about a hot dog, a bicycle and a curfew in
their own kitchen; there is no honest place to put long words in that. The
grade was made on sentence length instead, which is what `WHY_HIGHER.md`'s
correction says is the whole of the gap anyway.

`sentences per paragraph` fell 2.1 → 1.4 and now fails its band. That is the
direct cost of the merging: a lot of paragraphs are now one long sentence.
It is worth flagging rather than fixing, because unmerging is exactly the
0.5 of F-K that was just bought.
