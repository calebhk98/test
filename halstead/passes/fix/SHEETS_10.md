## 1. BELL.md — the demonstration-object signature. Removed.

**Ruling:** *"Remove that signature."*

**Was:** the whole of `## Signature, theirs alone` read: *"Holds the demonstration object
while he talks and doesn't let go until the point lands"*, evidenced by three stage
directions from two chapters, and closing on "Three instances across two chapters." A
count of a draft, presented as the one construction nobody else may use.

**Now:** the section names the construction that actually is his and that nothing else in
the book does: **he measures the thing out loud, then rules on it.** He gives a size, a
speed, or a distance as a plain figure, and the instruction falls out of the figure rather
than out of his authority, so a student who accepts the measurement has already accepted
the rule. Two real quotations, both verified against the current `14_sixty_degrees.md:11`.
A closing sentence keeps the prop-handling as a habit and explicitly denies it the status
of a rule, citing the counter-instance the author found: at `15_twelve.md:13` he pockets
the metronome and buttons the pocket *before* he has finished making the point, and the
point lands anyway.

**Other places that leaned on it, all changed:**

- **Voice paragraph.** "he holds the dart, the arrow, his own flat hand, up where the line
  can see it" → "he gets the dart, the arrow, or his own flat hand into the line's eyeline
  and says what it is doing before he says what to do about it." The prop survives as
  behaviour; the holding-until-it-lands rule does not.
- **Would they say this?** Deleted "YES if the whole of the sentence is doing something
  with his hands while he talks," which was not a testable property of a line anyway.
  Replaced with "YES if it says what will not work before it says what will," which is
  testable and is what he actually does.
- **Would do.** "Hold a demonstration object steady through a full explanation rather than
  rush it. (three instances)" → "Give the measurement that makes a rule obvious, and let
  the rule arrive as the obvious thing."
- **Likes, dislikes, habits.** Dropped "(three instances)" from "Physically demonstrates
  before he explains."
- **When they talk at length.** Added that the same man makes a rule stick with no object
  in his hands at all, which is the whole of the metronome handover.

**Incidental, while in the file:** three quotations in this sheet had drifted off the
current text and were re-quoted from it, including the "most characteristic line" and the
long apogee speech. `verify_citations.py characters/BELL.md` is now clean.

## 3. SAM.md — what he will not discuss. Rewritten; he tells it himself.

**Ruling:** *"Change Sam.md, Sam will laugh. Sam.md hyperfocused on 1 event."*

**Was**, under Embarrassment: *"He won't discuss what he's been laughed at for, but the
silence isn't empty... It reads as him being fine. It isn't quite that."* The whole trait
was generalised from one narrated sentence in `20_the_parking_lot.md`.

**Now:** "He gets to it first and tells it louder than anyone else would." The bullet is
built on the counter-evidence: out of a school-wide exercise inside ninety seconds, over a
bannister onto three teachers, held down by a knee until he stopped moving, and he talks
about it from the bench all afternoon, again at dinner, and is still telling it in June, to
two people who were standing underneath him at the time (`14_sixty_degrees.md:203`, `:213`).
Being the joke and being humiliated are different things to him and the difference is who
is telling it. What survives from the old bullet is the deflection, re-hung on a trigger
that is true of him: it is not the laugh that closes him, it is being asked how he *felt*
about it, and then he picks up whatever is nearest and talks about that instead.

**The same over-fit, found and changed in two more places:**

- **Interior life.** "What he's afraid of, **more than being laughed at**:" → "What he's

- **Known problems #3** was a critique of a draft sentence that no longer exists

## 4. SAM.md — subordinate qualifying clauses. Rule removed, dials re-measured.

**Ruling:** *"Remove that rule."*

**Deleted:** "- NO if it contains a subordinate clause built to qualify or explain
something. That belongs to Theo."

`14_sixty_degrees.md:125` gives him exactly that shape and it is one of his best lines:
*"If you can't say why the man signed it, what you've got is a feeling that it was wrong
and a blank where his reason goes."*

**Replaced with** a NO that is true of him and still separates him from Theo: "NO if a
clause is hung on the end to soften what he just said. When he builds a longer sentence it
is to set a condition in front of a consequence, not to take the weight out of one." The
distinction is load-bearing subordination versus cushioning subordination, which is the
real difference between the two of them.

## 6. RUTH.md — the "0% questions" rule. Counted, and it was wrong.

**Ruling:** *"RUTH.md's 0% questions should probably be questioned if not removed. She
would ask questions right?"*

She does. Here is the count.

### Method

Every Ruth turn in the manuscript, both channels. Chat is exact (`^ruth:`). Spoken prose
was read scene by scene rather than grepped, so untagged turns inside an established
two-hander are included; that is where most of her questions live and it is why the
automated measure missed them. Each turn classified as a question **in substance** —
does it put something to somebody that wants an answer back — independent of punctuation.

### The figures

| | turns | questions in substance | rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| group chat | 98 | 18 | 18% |
| spoken prose | ~93 | 23 | 25% |
| **total** | **~191** | **41** | **~21%** |

**And she uses question marks.** The sheet said "never a question mark." Sixteen of her
spoken turns end in one, i.e. about two thirds of her spoken questions are punctuated as
questions:

`07:7` "so you have to, okay?" · `10:21` "Why bother, when everyone at camp already said it
wrong and I let them?" · `11:153` "since when do you draw like that?" · `11:205` "who even
made that rule up?" · `13:75` "What, what are you talking about?" · `14:33` "Is that the
release you're doing?" · `14:69` "Who corrects you?" · `15:131` "Anything about the
objective?" · `15:141` "Why would they take the stairs?" · `15:233` "What were you here
for?" · `15:235` "Second floor lab or the server room?" · `17:125` "do you know how many
things there are to do here?" · `20:103` "Should we report them to the police?" · `20:107`
"What if they report it?" · `21:33` "Then why is it four pages?" · `21:137` "Hardest by
what measure, and compared to what?"

(Two of the sixteen, `15:131` and `11:205`, sit in untagged exchanges; the surrounding
lines make Ruth much the likeliest speaker but they are the only two I would not defend
to the last.)

**Where the "0%" came from.** `voice_separation.py` attributes prose only where an
explicit "<name> says" tag is present, and the book drops the tag once a two-hander is
running — which is exactly where a question gets asked. It reports Ruth at 3% today. Zero
was never a fact about Ruth; it was a fact about the measuring instrument, and it then got
written into the sheet as a law.

### What the sheet says now

The rule was not narrower than "0%", it was about a **different axis**. The interesting
thing is not how often she asks, it is that **every question she asks is built so that one
answer will close it.**

- **Dial:** "about one turn in five is a question in substance: 41 of 191 counted, 23 of
  roughly 93 spoken and 18 of 98 in chat. Sixteen of the spoken ones carry a question mark,
  so a question mark is ordinary for her and not a defect. The move that is hers is the
  other third, a flat declarative that works as a question and gets answered as one."
- **Voice paragraph:** she asks a great many questions and punctuates most of them as
  questions; what is hers is the build. She hands over the two options and asks which
  (`15:235`), or refuses the question until the measure is named and names the measures she
  would accept (`21:137`), or drops the mark and puts the inference up flat for somebody to
  knock down ("So what you're telling me is he's right," `14:149`, which Chloe answers with
  "Yeah").
- **NO bullet.** "NO if the line ends in a question mark rather than a flat declarative
  demand" is gone. In its place: "NO if a question of hers leaves the answer open, or
  exists to fill a silence, to be sociable, or to take the edge off a claim she has already
  made. She asks constantly; what she does not do is hand somebody an open field."
- **Two YES bullets** added with real evidence for the two closing shapes (`15:235`,
  `21:137`).

All of it is stated as something a writer can apply to a scene that does not exist yet,
not as a percentage of the current draft.

**Note on an internal contradiction the count exposed:** the sheet already contained
"YES if the line collapses an open question into two concrete options and asks which one
applies" three bullets above "NO if the line ends in a question mark." The one line in the
book that best satisfies the YES bullet, "Second floor lab or the server room?", ends in a
question mark. The sheet was failing its own test.

## 8. School enrolment. Recorded as a curve.

**Ruling:** the school takes roughly 100 children a year, Chloe's cohort is the oldest, so
the school grows with her — about 200 when she is in third grade, closer to 1000 in her
last year.

**Where I put it, and why.** There is no worldbuilding or setting reference file in this
repo. The two candidates were `CURRICULUM_GRID.md`, which already carries the mechanism
("Her cohort is the oldest in the building"), and `characters/_CALIBRATION.md`. **I chose
`characters/_CALIBRATION.md`**, in a new `## How big the school is` section, because it is
the one file that opens "Read this before touching a sheet" and is therefore the file that
actually gets read before somebody writes a number into a sheet. `CURRICULUM_GRID.md` is
scoped to itself as "Working reference for the ch10–20 rewrite" and would only cover part
of the span. If you would rather the curve lived alongside the curriculum, moving the
section is a copy-paste.

The section states the intake, the cohort fact, the table, and then reconciles the three
figures the manuscript actually prints so the "inconsistency" cannot be re-found:
`14_sixty_degrees.md:199` "two hundred students out of eleven rooms" and `15_twelve.md:31`
"two hundred of us" from the lower half of the ramp, against
`23_the_first_one.md:14-17`'s "eight hundred children who are not graduating today" behind
ninety-one who are. **The school is a different size in chapter 14 than in chapter 23
because it is a different size.**
