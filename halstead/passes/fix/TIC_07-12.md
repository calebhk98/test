# Repetition pass, chapters 7-12

Span: Chloe's return to public school, the depression, the asking, and her
first three years at Halstead. 19,747 words, 17% of the book.

Worked construction by construction across all six chapters rather than
chapter by chapter, so every instance of a habit was visible at once and the
replacements could be varied against each other.

## Announced withholding, 3 of 3

All three deleted rather than replaced, per the brief, because in each case
the beat underneath already proves it.

- Ch10, Chloe wanting to know how long the older students' subject list is:
  *"...but every question she tries out sounds like she is measuring herself
  against them."* The clause "so she keeps it to herself" went; she never
  asks, and the reader can see that.
- Ch10, Fen's rocks: the withholding became an event. *"Fen has to check,
  running a finger along the sill before she answers, because another came in
  during March and moved the whole row along."*
- Ch12, Sam's sheet: *"Something on Sam's own sheet is different from
  everyone else's, and by Thursday there are three theories about what."* The
  theories are the proof that he did not say.

## Hands flat, 11 of 13

The book's default gesture, and in chapters 7 and 8 it was on the mother, the
teacher, the doctor, the librarian and Chloe. Two survive, deliberately
paired: the mother's at the counter on the phone (the one HOUSE_RULES uses as
its worked example) and Chloe's on the dining table in chapter 12, up on her
toes about dance being off the sheet. Same shape, four years and one whole
mood apart.

Best of the substitutions, all moving the beat to a different part of the body:

- Ruth at the flagpole: *"says it from six inches away with a fistful of
  Chloe's sleeve in each hand."*
- The mother listening to the school on the phone: *"settles into a long
  stretch of listening, her weight going onto one foot and then the other."*
- Ms. Vance crouching by the desk: *"put a hand between Chloe's shoulder
  blades and leave it there."*
- The mother during the jacket fight: *"Her mom's hand closes on the edge of
  the counter."* (grip, not palm, and it escalates where flat did not)
- Ch9, on the floor in the gap before the crying: *"sitting in the gap with a
  fistful of carpet in either hand."*
- Ms. Vance and the time capsule: *"reaching up to knock a knuckle against
  the cabinet."*

Six other "flat"s went with them (hugged flat, the pen down flat, feet settle
flat, the sheet lying flat, saying flatly). Kept: the foam disc thrown flat
and fast, the pattern laid flat on brown paper, and the choir run in ch11
("she goes flat on the way down... at least flat somewhere useful"), which is
the word for the thing and where the repetition is the joke. One redundant
"flat" was trimmed out of that run.

## Eyes, 5 of 7

"Keeps his/her eyes on X" was being used by the father, Ruth, the office
woman and the first-aid teacher: a gesture belonging to everybody. Ruth keeps
one (ch12, not looking up from her book while she tells Sam the truth about
dance) and the first-aid teacher keeps one, where it is literally a stopwatch.

- Ch7, the father in the car: *"her dad's hands come up to the top of the
  wheel"*, which sets up the mother's line about him putting both hands into
  the conversation instead of the steering wheel.
- Ch7, the library, the strongest of the eyes-down defaults: *"Chloe carries
  the two finished ones up to the desk by herself, walking fast past the
  shelves."* The avoidance is now in the pace.
- Ch8, Chloe lying in the dark: *"Chloe says to the ceiling."* The wall is
  saved for four lines later, where she lies about the bullying.
- Ch10, Ruth in cooking: *"Ruth carries on cutting."*

## "That's not X, that's Y", 2 of 3

Kept, and flagged: Chloe's *"that's not a sample, that's a coincidence with a
p-value attached to it"* in ch12. The correction is the entire content of the
argument, which is the brief's own test. ELI.md records the construction as
his signature, so this is a second mouth using it; if that is one too many,
this is the one to take.

- The mother in the car: *"it's the kind of question we sit down at a table
  for."*
- The literature teacher: *"There's no list at all, and I know that sounds
  like me being clever with you, but it's the answer."*

## "The whole / rest of it", 5 of 7

Kept the two doing work: *"that is already the whole of it"* in ch8, which is
the point of the scene, and Kavi's *"his mom came and that was the whole of
it"* in ch10. The removal that mattered most was ch10's closer, which was the
narrator explaining Chloe's own restraint: *"September," Chloe says, with Sam
and somebody's grandmother ten feet away on the grass.*

## Hedged exact, 16 of 19

Mostly by dropping one half. Three kept, all genuinely approximate: three
weeks working out what gets you sent home, two weeks of paper targets, a lip
that sticks out about two feet.

The best of them get funnier without the hedge, because the precision stops
apologising for itself: *"She asked me in the parking lot outside this
building, twenty minutes ago."* Kavi's *"It's been the rule for ninety
seconds"* beats "about four minutes" on both counts. Others dropped the
number instead: *"There are more of them than will go in a line"*, *"in under
an hour"*, *"an arm's length away"*, *"you said Kavi a hundred times in that
car"*.

## The numbers

Four went 70 to 27, nine 26 to 8, eleven 27 to 10, forty 11 to 5. Two moves,
as briefed: replace, or delete and let the precision live where it earns its
place.

Kept because they are load-bearing or are the joke:
- four weeks of camp, the librarian's four-at-a-time and the four books due
  on the Thursday (chapter 10 pays that off: *"let you take four books and
  not five"*), the four subjects she is behind in and the notebook that
  counts them, the four tones of Mandarin, four days off in July, "fifteen
  two, fifteen four", the dad's "four pages of the same trick"
- twenty past nine on the math sheet, twice, chapters apart, which is the
  sameness of that year; nine of them in the room instead of twenty-six; one
  word in nine and the other eight; eight hours going to nine after
  Christmas; "It's what nine gets"; the nine o'clock curfew argument
- eleven pounds on the bridge, eleven hours a night in ch8 and again in ch12
  (the same number twice, meaning opposite things), eleven at night in the
  library and the same phrase thrown back in the curfew fight, the eleven
  people in Kavi's study, the eleven minutes to the stables that add up to
  the twenty-two she walks alone
- forty a side at Nerf, the forty drawings and the fortieth one, forty
  minutes of the hour still to go, forty thousand dollars

Curriculum-list numbers, per the brief, are where the clustering was worst,
and the lists themselves are untouched: only the figures inside them moved.
Newspapers from six countries, five classrooms, seven names on the climbing
line, seven months to tunnel out, the whole elective wall instead of four
sheets of it.

Deletions rather than substitutions where the figure was doing nothing:
*"Chloe talks for the whole drive"*, *"After that she stops sitting in the
kitchen"*, *"which came second in the room"*, *"and it's twenty-two minutes
of walking for an hour of being bored"* (two elevens for one twenty-two that
was already on the page).

One continuity fix fell out of it: Dave's *"instead of nine weeks a year"* is
now ten weeks, which is what chapters 10 and 12 both say she is home for.

## "Same", 28 of 52

Chapter 7 is called The Same Room and the word is the depression motif, so
the ones that carry it stayed: the same room the whole time, the same
sentence twice on the same page of the book she cannot read, the same sheet
as Monday's with different numbers in it, the same voice Dr. Ammons asks
every question in, a different activity wearing the same word.

Cut where it was filler or where it was doubled inside one sentence: three
"same"s in one line of ch8 went to one, and the doubles in ch11 (same slot /
same hour, same angle / same cheap paper, same wall / same week, same hour /
same place) each lost one. Elsewhere a plain deletion did it: *"She was just
Ruth to everybody, the entire time."*

## She/He + verb openings, 28 of 51

Varied the opening, not the sentence. No single replacement pattern used more
than a few times, and participles were avoided because chapters 7 and 8
already lean on them.

- Object or subject first: *"The crying is loud enough that chairs scrape all
  along the row"*; *"It comes to her in the shower on the Wednesday"*; *"A
  novel about a lighthouse takes her six days"*; *"Her name goes on the choir
  line"*; *"There is singing in the shower now"*; *"South America is done by
  the end of May"*.
- Fronted time or place: *"Twice she gets it on the second ring"*; *"On a
  Sunday, in the car coming back from her grandmother's, she asks"*;
  *"Upstairs, on the back of a spelling worksheet under her pillow"*; *"From
  the stairs on a Sunday she hears her mom saying"*; *"For most of an hour
  now she has been braced for that point"*; *"Two weeks later she asks Kavi
  about it"*; *"Home from school, she goes upstairs with her coat still on"*;
  *"Home for ten weeks, she sleeps eleven hours a night"*.
- Cleft: *"What she is waiting for is the turn"*.
- Named the actual subject where "She" was ambiguous anyway: *"Sam is seven
  and a half"*, *"Her mother starts on the school in October"*, *"Her mom
  says thank you at the end of it"*.

Kept the openings that are doing something: the anaphora paragraphs (*"She
asks at breakfast... she asks in the car..."*, *"She says she'll be good...
She'll do her dad's jobs..."*, *"She talks about the room she'd have. She
talks about the woman who walked them around"*), and the short hard ones,
*"She goes upstairs and slams the door so hard the frame jumps."*, *"She
stops asking in January."*, *"She runs back up."*, *"She screams."*

One rewrite took a told feeling out with it. Ch7 ended a paragraph *"She is
relieved to find it still works like it worked in first grade"*, which named
the feeling the scene had just carried; it is now *"It still works exactly
like it worked in first grade."*

## Left alone

- **"The way you/she would", 4 instances, 0 changed.** The span is already at
  the target rate and all four are doing characterisation: Bryce delivering
  cruelty *"the way you would tell somebody their shoe was untied"*, Chloe on
  the days *"the way she is"* kept by her mother, the tap reached *"the way
  she has been told not to"*, and *"somebody picked ten the way somebody
  picked nine"* in the curfew argument. Nothing here is texture.
- **Turning an object, 1 instance.** Kavi turning his sheet over to get to
  objection two. Under target.
