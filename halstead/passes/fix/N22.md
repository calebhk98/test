Lexile and F-K dropped rather than held, which cuts against EXPAND_BRIEF's
"should not come out flatter." I pushed narrative sentences (the corridor
paragraphs, the interview's weighing beat, the phone call's stage directions)
toward more subordination in a second pass and recovered most of the gap

dialogue exchanges — two full scenes, one of them a phone call — where the
original was mostly narrated summary, and dialogue-heavy scene work reads
shorter per-sentence than summary prose almost by definition. `neg%` fell
from 9.0 to 2.1, which is the one metric this pass was unambiguously supposed
to move, and it moved a lot.

## The four things asked for

**1. The interview as a scene, from the top.** Rebuilt entirely. It now opens
with Chloe walking down, a beat in the corridor, then a real two-line
exchange before the offer: Amberg's "Eleven years... Anything for the file,"
her answer, then "Four things, then," into the enumerated offer. The salary
is stated in plain language exactly once — "a hundred and thirty five
thousand dollars a year, to start" — not derived by anyone doing multiplication.

**2. Chronological order.** The original jumped: Chloe's interview, then a
zoomed-out description of the whole nine-day corridor (including reactions
that happened *before* her own turn, positioned in the text after it), then
the tally, then Kavi, then the call. I moved everything that precedes her
interview in real time — the dinner-table reactions from other students, the
corridor wait, the boy with the laptop bag — to before her scene, and moved
the full-cohort tally (91 names, 11 yeses) to after it, since by her slot
(Wednesday of the second week of a nine-day run) nearly everyone else has
already gone. The chapter now moves forward once: buildup → her interview →
result → Kavi that evening → the call that night.

**3. The refusal, argued.** Cut "It's my school. Nobody stays at their
school," which was the line the brief singled out as not good enough, and
replaced it with a built parallel scenario in Chloe's own established
sentence shape (the "If you..." construction, `characters/CHLOE.md`'s
signature — the closest analogue on the page is her line in
chapters/21_the_applications.md about the store with the four-percent sign
in the window): "If you sat the same exam every year, in the same room,
against the same ninety people, and you kept coming out near the top of it,
would you ever actually find out if you were good at the exam, or just good
against that particular room?" It also ties back into the "wrong ninety
people" anxiety chapters/21 already establishes for her, so it isn't a new
idea, just the first time she says it out loud. I gave her a beat of visibly
deciding before it — turning the folder's edge, the "hands flat" tic already
established for her — rather than the line just arriving.

**4. The parents, redone from scratch.** The whole phone call is new prose.
Both parents now state directly that they think this is a mistake — Dave:
"That's not a decision. That's a mistake," followed by his own
cost-accounting ("what a hundred and thirty five thousand dollars a year
actually is... before you've paid a dollar of rent anywhere else in your
life"), which is the register `characters/DAVE.md` gives him (disagrees by
asking what something costs). Chloe answers with the argued reason, restated
for them in her own words, not a repeat of the Amberg line verbatim. Meg
pushes back with her own signature move — turning Chloe's logic back on her
as a question ("So you're saying no to guaranteed money for the chance of
finding out you're not as good as you think you are") — rather than simply
worrying. Every exchange the author flagged as summarized is now played:
Dave asking whether the offer would still be there later, Meg's "the state
school isn't free" line (previously narrated as something she'll "go on
saying every summer for the next two years" — cut the future-tense telling
and put an actual version of it in the scene, present tense, with Chloe
answering it directly instead of the exchange being reported after the
fact), and the Ruth/Sam question, which no longer needs the "the only two
names in that building already fixed in his memory" line (impossible
interiority — Chloe can't see inside her father's memory) because Dave's own
sheet already establishes he tracks her friends by name unprompted; he just
asks.
