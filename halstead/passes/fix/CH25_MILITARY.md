## Part 1: the research, in short

Full document with citations is `passes/MILITARY_REFERENCE.md`. The headlines
that changed the chapter:

**Length.** Basic Combat Training is ten weeks, which cannot hold a four-month
chapter. Infantry **One Station Unit Training at Fort Moore is twenty-two
weeks**, keeps the same Red/White/Blue phase structure for the first ten, and
is where a soldier of Sam's description actually goes. Shipping in the first
week of August, twenty-two weeks runs to early January, which also puts him at
a unit in time for chapter 26's February exercise. The chapter now uses OSUT.

**When the rifle appears.** Week three, not month four. Familiarisation and dry
drill in Red Phase, first live rounds at 25 metres in week four, two solid weeks
of ranges in White Phase, **qualification around week eight or nine**. A recruit
who has not touched a rifle by month four has not been in the Army.

**Qualification.** The author's research was right and the chapter was wrong.
Under TC 3-20.40 it is **forty rounds at forty pop-up silhouettes from 50 to
300 metres**, ten exposures from each of five positions (standing, prone
unsupported, prone supported, kneeling supported, standing supported), with a
magazine change built in and 3:58 on the clock. Bands: **Expert 36-40**,
Sharpshooter 30-35, Marksman 23-29, unqualified below 23. There is no ten-target
version of this range anywhere.

**ACFT.** Six events in 2023 (the five-event AFT is later). 0-100 per event,
60 to pass each, **600 is the ceiling**. The scale caps: beating a 600 is not a
thing the instrument can record. Maxing in training is publishable-news rare
rather than impossible; the first BCT trainee to do it, in 2019, was the third
soldier in the entire Army.

**Comms.** Phones are held by the drill sergeant and returned for a Sunday
window. **Letters are the single most ordinary thing in the barracks**: mail
call is a scheduled event run out loud by the cadre, and evening personal time
is explicitly for boots, laundry and letter writing. There is nothing to
conceal and no way to conceal it.

**Food.** Bad chow is a cliche and the wrong one. The DFAC food is decent. The
real grievance is the clock, ten to twenty minutes by the time the line clears,
and cadre policing what goes on the tray.

**Ranks.** A captain is the company commander of roughly 200 trainees. A
private gets addressed by him rarely and for a reason. Trainees go to attention
for an officer and say sir. Qualification day is one of the few days a
commander does circulate on a range, because company qualification percentages
are his statistic. He does not gush, and he does not call anybody son.

**The anomalous recruit.** Verified, and the author's position is correct.
Three machines start, none of them a commendation: a **counterintelligence
referral** under AR 381-12 (TARP, formerly SAEDA), which makes reporting
unexplained specialised training mandatory rather than discretionary; a
**personnel security check** against the education block of the soldier's SF-86,
because a school that will not resolve is an adjudicative discrepancy; and a
**behavioural health referral**, which in an entry-training environment is fast
and low-threshold. The commander's job is to write it down accurately, route
it, and stop owning it. The instrument is a memorandum for record, and
*appears to have received specialised instruction from an unidentified source*
is exactly the register such a memorandum uses.

## Part 2: the eighteen items

### The military

**1. Timeline, range timing, qualification, ACFT, comms, food.**
All six corrected. Sam is in twenty-two-week Infantry OSUT. The rifle arrives in
week three and the first live rounds at 25 metres in week four, on the A8
diamond. Qualification is the second Thursday of October, in the ninth week, and
it is the real table: forty pop-ups, 50 to 300 metres, five positions including
the barricade, one magazine change, 3:58 on the clock. The fitness test is the
six-event ACFT with its named events and its 600 ceiling. Letters are written in
the open during personal time, with mail call on the printed schedule and
push-ups charged by the letter. The food complaint is the nine minutes, and it is
the only complaint he lodges in four months.

**2. "In month four they put him on a range with ten targets."**
Gone. Both the timing and the format were wrong and both are replaced.

**Title: changed to "Forty Targets."** The old title named a course of fire that
does not exist. Forty is the real number of targets, and it is also the number
Sam gives the captain about himself, so the title now carries the collision the
scene is built on without anybody in the prose pointing at it. Nothing else in
the manuscript refers to the chapter by title.

### The captain

**3. Too much praise, and reading the accuracy off the sheet.**
Rewritten from the ground up. He now says nine words of approval, dry, and passes
the card sideways to the drill sergeant rather than handing it to Sam. **He never
asks what Sam's accuracy is.** Sam volunteers it, unprompted, because talking the
record down is what Sam does. The captain's question, "Forty percent of what,
Private," is a real question with no answer available to him on any sheet.

**4. The self-contradiction.**
Resolved by making the two fortys different quantities. Forty out of forty is
hits on the qualification table. Forty percent is Sam's intercept figure from
school. The captain is looking at a card that says forty out of forty while
being told forty percent, and that mismatch is now what drives the scene instead
of being an error inside it.

**5. His reaction: written up, not decorated.**
Rewritten as the author asked. He folds the card into his jacket instead of into
the stack on the table, writes it longhand that evening, and sends four
documents up: a memorandum for record quoting the soldier's own figure in
quotation marks and using the phrase *appears to have received specialised
instruction from an unidentified source*; a counterintelligence referral under
the standing reporting requirement; a request to the battalion S-2 for the
education block of the security questionnaire; and, signed last, a behavioural
health referral. **The three hypotheses are the three routes, and nothing in the
prose names them.** The reader gets asset, liar and psych case from the
addressees.

This feeds forward correctly. Chapter 34 says Sam's file is "three years of an
Army file stacked on top of everything Halstead ever generated on him," and that
"an officer's assessment quotes a number Sam gave honestly and flags the honesty
itself as the interesting part." The memorandum now literally quotes forty
percent inside quotation marks. Chapter 29's Halstead file, nineteen years old
and already open, is what the woman three floors up pulls out from underneath
the stack. Chapter 26's closing line, "It goes upward anyway," now lands on
something already moving.

**6. The captain has never heard of the intercept drill and will not believe it.**
That is now the scene. His answer is eleven years of service, no school in the
country runs this on children, barely a unit runs it on adults, and the men who
do have been through a selection course you could not have spelled a year ago,
followed by the question put once more with an instruction to take his time over
the answer. Sam gives him "Halstead," "It's small," "You wouldn't have heard of
it," and hears nothing back.

### Sam

**7. Unearned competence cut.**
He is now first off the bus and spends eleven minutes standing where nobody
wanted him. He wakes before the lights and it is worth nothing, because he is
slow for the first ten minutes of any morning and the bay is a footrace. **The
bed is now a ten-day failure**: wrong every morning, wrong in a different place
each time, until a private named Okoro who grew up around this does his own rack
at half speed alongside so Sam can copy the sequence. After that Sam is in the
back half of the platoon at it and stays there. Four of the six ACFT events are
new to him. The physical and the shooting are untouched.

**8. He must be puzzled and must rationalise outward.**
This is now the spine of the middle section. A short paragraph of him being
unable to reconcile any of it with what he knows about himself: forty percent at
school, Odile level with him at fourteen and ahead by the spring, Ruth on
ninety-one and bored, Kavi beating him at everything with a board in it for six
years. Then three rationalisations, in order, each of them pointed away from
himself: everyone here is tired in a way school could only approximate; eleven
years against nine days is a fact about calendars; and the one he keeps, that
this is a screen, that the men around him are being measured on something he has
yet to be shown and are holding a good deal back for it. **He never arrives at
being unusual.** No sentence anywhere tells the reader what he has got wrong.

**9. "He does that with everything. There's no version of a task..."**
Cut. The behaviour survives as action rather than as a claim about him: he runs
four of the six events again on a Sunday with a borrowed stopwatch, two figures
move, and when Okoro asks what he is going to do with them he says "Keep them.
They're just better." He takes the record test in week nine and posts the same
six hundred, because six hundred is the only score the sheet has room for.

**10. "The table waits for the rest of it... and the table goes back to eating."**
Cut entirely. He now answers properly and at length, which is what the sheet
says he does: he corrects the record downward, volunteers his range number,
tells a story against himself about Odile taking him apart on a mat in front of
thirty people, and another about losing something out of his own boot and
costing somebody eleven minutes. Two of them end up laughing at him. It is warm,
it is long, and the disbelief lands on a third man rather than on Sam being rude.

**11. Accuracy described as it was practised in chapters 17 and 18.**
The old version, "opposite lanes, you both fire on the same beat," was wrong.
Corrected to chapter 18: a machine at one end of the lane putting a live round
across your front, left to right, sixty metres of open ground, on a cycle you
can set a watch by, the shooters behind glass taking the count off the mechanism.
"The target is the round. You're shooting the bullet." Voss's own framing.

**12. Sincere, not arrogant.**
The line about the rifle stays, because it is his deflating literalism and it is
the point of the scene, but the framing that made it condescending is gone. He no
longer decides it is a trick question or tries to let the captain save face; he
reads the silence as room to be more useful and fills it. The arrogance is
entirely in the reception, which includes one laugh two lanes down that stops.

### Craft

**13. "exactly."** Four instances, all gone. Zero in the file.

**14. Future POV.** All swept. Gone: "the way the captain will read a different
sheet twice in four months' time," "What gets written up that evening and
forwarded twice by Friday" as a prolepsis, "this is the moment he has been
waiting four months for," "the way the grader read Sam's sheet twice," "That one
stays off the sheet," "None of it reaches Sam." The captain's paperwork section
is now narrated in the present as it happens rather than announced in advance.

**15. "He keeps the rest of it to himself."** Cut. Negative-space overall went
from 1.8% to 0.6%.

**16. The three-count.** The gloss is gone. It appears twice, each time attached
to something concrete and physically costly and with no explanation: before he
pulls the deadlift bar off the ground, and before the first exposure on
qualification day. Nothing tells the reader what it is for.

**17. "block capitals pressed hard enough to dent the page underneath."**
Simplified to "in block capitals, because he gave up on cursive somewhere around
the age of nine," which is the same fact from the character sheet in a form that
explains itself.

**18. "some kind of extended screening exercise."**
Planted rather than cut, and planted three times before it is used. Sam asks four
people how long the programme runs in week one, and asks two of them the question
that stops them. He puts it to Ruiz at a formation and gets "the real one is
Tuesday, it is also Wednesday" and a dismissal. Then the screening theory is
where his rationalising lands in the middle section. By the time he treats the
captain's silence as an opening it is the fourth appearance, and the chapter now
ends on him asking Okoro whether the sorting is finished, which hands straight to
chapter 26's "i genuinely thought there was a real one."

## Part 3: chapter 26 read against the reference

Read only. Nothing edited. Line numbers are `chapters/26_the_exercise.md` as it
stands. The chapter's dating now works: OSUT graduation in early January 2024
puts him at a unit for a February exercise.

### Serious

**L9, L15-19, L23-25, L39-41. There is no weapon anywhere in four days, and no
force-on-force equipment.** He signs for a compass, a red-lens flashlight and a
grid card, and that is his entire issue. A soldier moving tactically against a
briefed opposing element carries a rifle, and a real opposing-force lane runs on
MILES laser gear or blanks with a controller scoring engagements. Every one of
the seven contacts in this chapter resolves as unarmed hand-to-hand, which reads
as a martial arts exercise rather than an Army one. This is the largest gap in
the chapter.

**L17, L19, L25, L41. Seven OPFOR soldiers are physically put down and checked
for breathing, and there is no safety consequence at all.** In a real exercise,
an OPFOR member rendered unconscious is a training mishap: the lane stops, a
medic comes, and a report gets written. Sam checking sternums himself, seven
times over four days, with the exercise continuing each time, is not survivable
as written. Either the contacts need to be scored engagements rather than
casualties, or the chapter needs to acknowledge that something has gone wrong
with the exercise.

**L25, L27, L47, L51. The thumb.** He injures it badly enough to feel it go,
it swells to twice its size and stays that way for a week, and he never goes to
sick call. At L51 a major reads it out at an AAR as a joke. In a unit, a visibly
swollen hand at a formation gets a soldier sent to the aid station by the
nearest NCO the same morning, and it is documented.

**L57, L61, L67. Sam says "sir" zero times in the chapter**, including three
times when he is speaking directly to a major in a room full of people, two
months out of initial training. `grep -c sir` returns nothing. This is the
cheapest fix in the chapter and the one a reader with any service will catch
first. His opening word at L57 is "Yeah."

### Worth looking at

**L9. "a card with a six-digit grid on it" and "these two points."** One grid is
one point. Two points needs two grids, or the card needs to be described as
holding both. Six-digit is 100-metre precision, which is standard for a land nav
point, so that part is right.

**L9, L31, L37. Four days and nights of solo movement with no rest plan, no
radio, no buddy and no check-in.** This is the shape of a SERE evasion lane, not
of a unit field exercise. If it is meant to be a selection or evasion lane, one
line naming it would carry the whole thing. As written it is an exercise with no
name, which is unlike the Army: exercises are named and sit on a published
training calendar.

**L43. "a corporal checks the time on the card."** Corporal is a rare rank in a
line unit; the E-4 doing this job is almost always a specialist. Not wrong, but
"specialist" is the higher-probability word.

**L47-51. The debrief.** A major running the room off a projector is plausible
for a lane OIC. The Army word for the event is an **After Action Review**, and
"the AAR" is what soldiers in the room would call it. "Debrief" is used four
times and reads slightly civilian.

**L51. "Marsh comes out of it with a thumb that's apparently still swollen."**
Good line, but see above: the major is the one person in the room whose job is to
notice the injury rather than narrate it.

### Working well, against the reference

- **L69.** "Difficulty is set above you, Marsh, and it's set a good way above me
  as well" is exactly right on how authority over training is distributed, and
  it is the correct correction to give a soldier who has asked for a harder one.
- **L49.** The soldier who went four days untouched being told to hold the
  compliment is a real AAR move and a good one.
- **L7, L9.** The hand receipt gone soft with damp, the pen skipping twice, the
  second flashlight issued when the first will not come on. All correct texture.
- **L75.** "It goes upward anyway" now sits directly on top of the chapter 25
  paperwork and needs no change.
