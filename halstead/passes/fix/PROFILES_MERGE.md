# Merging the outside profiles into the character sheets

Scope: `characters/` only. The nine cohort members, plus the two shared files
in that directory that had to move with them. No chapter was touched.

## What the merge actually took

The outside profiles are thin and their labels are crude, and none of the labels
went into a sheet. Three things in them are better than what we had, and all
three are structural rather than cosmetic:

1. **Externalised behaviour.** Their strongest section says that in close third
   on one point of view, the other eight are bodies in rooms, and that if the
   narration describes all nine with the same vocabulary they converge no matter
   how carefully the dialogue is written. That is correct and it is the actual
   diagnosis. Our sheets were deep on interior and thin on what a person sitting
   across the room would see.

2. **Eight axes that are free.** Stuck, bored, publicly wrong, group work,
   responsibility for somebody younger, deadlines, where they sit and what they
   keep, and what the room thinks of them. Every one of those happens in scenes
   already being written, none of them needs a school to be true, and no two of
   the nine give the same answer to any of them.

3. **Permission to be warm, loud, touched, funny and wrong.** Ours had drifted
   into one temperature. Theirs is right that these are ten different kinds of
   person, and the corrective is almost entirely additive: the depth stays and
   the angle goes on top of it.

Every cohort sheet now carries a section called **Stuck, bored, and wrong in
public**, with the same eight axes and completely different content. The nine
can be read side by side on any of them, which is the test the author asked for.

## Per character

### Chloe
**Took:** the externalised sequence when she is stuck, which she already does
and which was only ever described from inside; the deadline split legible from
outside; the group-work behaviour, thinking out loud while somebody else holds
the pen; her manner teaching a younger student, over-explaining and uneven
results; and two physical facts, the chair she never stays in at six and eight,
and stillness as her reverse tell.
**Kept:** all of it. Hers was already the second-best sheet in the set and
nothing in it was contradicted. The additions are one section and one bullet
group in the body section.
**What distinguishes her:** she is the only one who turns an argument into a
physical scenario and argues the scenario, and the only one who has to have the
mechanism before the rule will sit still.

### Ruth
**Took:** volume, which was entirely missing, and the direction it runs, louder
when frightened and quiet when genuinely furious. Physical affection: the hug
hard enough to hurt, from the side, without warning, said from six inches away
while still holding on. Knocking things over. Lining objects up and knocking
them out of line. Then the whole eight-axis set: arguing that the question is
ill-formed when stuck, announcing at volume that there is nothing to do,
conceding privately hours later in four words, taking a group over in thirty
seconds, teaching strictly and knowing half of them dislike her, days-early
deadlines that she mentions.
**Kept:** the reasoning engine, the number habit, the private reworking of
finished work, the long arc where she cannot solve a wrongness about herself
alone in a room. One reconciliation added: the everyday concession and the large
public reversal are both hers and the sheet now says what separates them.
**Verified:** hugging hard enough to hurt is in the manuscript twice; so is her
screaming in a crowd. The sheet had neither.
**What distinguishes her:** she is the loudest and the most physically
affectionate person in the cast, and the only one who cannot let a wrong
statement stand in the air regardless of what it costs her.

### Sam
**Took:** the kinetic engine stated as a rule rather than as a list of angry
moments. He cannot process anything sitting still; the body burns it down and
then the talking is the second half and he is not finished without both.
Sadness as the one state he has no procedure for, where he goes quiet, which is
unlike enough to be noticed, and then overcorrects louder. Saying out loud on
day one, cheerfully, in front of everybody, that he has got nothing. Borrowing a
pen every day and never experiencing it as a pattern. No notes ever. The night
before, every time, for eleven years.
**Kept:** everything. The self-mockery section, the praise-correction, the
letters, the board he reads instead of deferring.
**What distinguishes him:** he is the only person here who can be publicly
incapable at zero cost, and the only one whose feelings have to be run through
his body before they reach language.

### Kavi
**Took:** the single best idea in their file for him, that his emotion arrives
as an absence rather than an expression, and a whole section built on it: the
plate left, the chair empty, the question not asked, the object left off its
mark, the days gone. That he does not know he is upset until told, accepts the
diagnosis flatly and still cannot say what it is about. That coming back is slow
and unnarrated and should not be given an explaining scene. Plus building
something under the desk when bored, disappearing physically when stuck,
handing in a perfect unintegrated part late, and deadlines that are three weeks
early or eleven days late with nothing in between.
**Kept:** everything, including our teaching entry, which is better than theirs
and contradicts it. Theirs has him incomprehensible to younger students; ours
has him good at it because they have not yet learned to pretend they followed.
Ours is right and stays.
**What distinguishes him:** he is the only one whose feelings are legible only
as subtraction, and the only one who plays the person across a board and the
mechanism in an argument and does not notice the two are different skills.

### Nadia
**Assessment first: this sheet already solved the problem and it is the standard
in the set.** The one-register section, the appetites, the fear that arrives
after the decision, the humour, the proportionality: none of it needed changing
and none of it was changed.
**Took:** one thing, so that the nine can be compared on the same axis, and it
is theirs and nobody else's: when stuck she writes down the exact sentence at
which her understanding stopped and works backward from it, and she is the only
one of the nine who can tell you precisely where her own boundary is.
**Verified:** their Nadia is written off an older draft. There is no tire shop
and no confrontation with four men; the pass notes and the manuscript govern her
arc and the sheet already defers to them correctly. None of that came in.
**What distinguishes her:** one delivery for every content, so the reader has to
be given the warm contents on the page or she reads as empty.

### Priya
**Took:** the warmth channel, which was the real gap. She touches people
constantly, arm and shoulder and back, including strangers inside a week, and
turns her whole body toward whoever is speaking. Nobody else in this cast
initiates contact at all. Laughing at herself out loud before she gives the
accurate account, and telling the story of being wrong at dinner as an anecdote
she is pleased to own. Asking three classmates before it occurs to her to ask
the teacher. Being the social glue of a group of four who cannot stand each
other. Moving seats constantly. Late, apologetic, forgiven. And their best line
about her teaching: her students adore her, her results are middling, she would
rather they liked it, and one of them tells her something serious they have not
told an adult, which lands exactly on the fear her own sheet already names.
**Kept:** the run-on sentence shape, the indifference to rank as an absence
rather than a stance, the horses, the writing to somebody who left.
**Fixed:** the sheet said she handled wrong, praised and losing the same unshowy
way with no visible reaction. That was the convergent flat register showing
through on the one character who should not have it. The account is still exact;
the laugh now comes first.
**What distinguishes her:** she is the only warm body in the room, the only
run-on, and the only one who arrives at what she knows socially rather than
alone.

### Odile
Kept deliberately small; she is a minor character and her sheet was already long
and already differentiated.
**Took:** four things. Leaving the room when bored, actually getting up and
going. One nod and no words when publicly wrong, the shortest response in the
cast. Being comfortable in a twenty-second silence and not experiencing it as a
pause, which is how she ends up being told things. And the scar as the one
subject that reliably gets more than a sentence out of her.
**Kept:** everything, including our teaching entry, where her patience lives in
her language teaching rather than in physical demonstration, which is more
surprising than theirs and is hers.
**What distinguishes her:** she answers a question about herself with a bare
number and no sentence around it, and she puts everything she feels into what
her body is doing.

### Eli
**This sheet needed the most work and it is the one place the outside file was
plainly right and ours was plainly wrong.** Ours had him as a third quiet
technical boy alongside Kavi and Theo, asking almost no questions, banned from
figurative language, with one comic register. The manuscript does not support
any of that.
**Verified against the manuscript:** he asks a great many questions, in
narrowing runs of two and three, aimed at what somebody is allowed to do and
what they actually get paid; he keeps going after being told to stop; he states
the unwelcome total in the middle of everybody else being encouraging and then
says plainly that he did not mean it as blame; and he uses concrete hazard
images about other people's exposure.
**Took and rewrote:** the joke as his channel rather than a decoration on a flat
register, with its four components named, the needle, the count, the stake as a
game, and himself as the material. The refusal to repair, which is the expensive
thing about him: told a remark landed badly he defends its accuracy in good
faith because to him those are two questions. Being frightened making the joke
bigger rather than smaller. Interrupting, apologising sincerely, interrupting
again inside a minute. Derailing entertainingly and being right a fifth of the
time. Undercutting himself first when publicly wrong, which works and is exactly
how he never sits in it. Two hours before the deadline, at speed, and fine.
**Kept:** the calibration problem, the security engine, the patience with
somebody genuinely behind, which is the one place the needling switches off
completely, the contraction-removal signature, the private collection, the
home. MBTI aid changed to match the rewrite.
**Also changed:** `_DIFFERENTIATION.md` Axis 3 and Axis 5, which had him at no
questions and no figurative language. Both are now correct and narrow: his
questions never seek information, and his one permitted image is the drop under
somebody else's position.
**What distinguishes him:** he is the only one who says the true unwelcome thing
for the pleasure of watching it land, and the only one who cannot see a
difference between a remark being accurate and a remark being worth making.

### Theo
**Took:** working longer and telling nobody when stuck, falling behind rather
than ask, and the falling behind being invisible because everything he hands in
is complete, which lands on the fear his sheet already runs on. Apologising out
of all proportion when publicly wrong, doing the work three times over, and
still turning it over a week later. Doing the parts of a group's work other
people dropped and refusing to name them under direct questioning. Lending
immaculate notes to four people who never return them and never being asked.
Waiting for a gap that does not come and going home with the useful thing
unsaid. Getting quieter as things get worse, so the quietest he has ever been is
the worst it has ever been. Being the best teacher of the nine and worrying
about one specific student on his own time. Underrated by everyone including
himself.
**Kept:** everything. The section on what he does with a thing he cannot say is
the strongest single section on any sheet in the set and was not touched.
**What distinguishes him:** he is the cast's only hedger, its longest sentences,
and the only one who would rather carry a thing wrongly than hand any part of it
to somebody else.

## Shared files

**`_DIFFERENTIATION.md`.** Two corrections to Eli's rows, as above. Two new
axes. Axis 6 is the nine-reactions proof: one problem, a week, no dialogue, and
nine different paragraphs, which is the fastest revision tool now in the file
and directly implements the covered-names test. Axis 7 is the externalisation
argument, the pointer to the new section on all nine sheets, and the two
free mechanics, latency before an answer and what each person does while
somebody else is talking. Em dashes in this file were removed while I was in it.

**`_ALLOCATIONS.md`.** Ledger rows brought back into line with the sheets:
Ruth's and Priya's new physical signatures recorded so nothing collides later,
Priya's home corrected to what her own sheet says, the Odile private-thing row
replaced because the one it carried had been cut from her sheet, and the "argued
for and won" claim on her language year removed for the same reason.

## Hard rules

- `python3 verify_citations.py` over the whole directory: **0 quotations
  checked, 0 missing.** Nothing I wrote quotes the manuscript.
- No line references, no draft statistics, no trait built on a single scene.
- No em dashes and no curly quotes in any file I touched.
- Nothing I added would be meaningless on a lunar colony. The eight axes are a
  task, a group and somebody in charge; the emotional channels are bodies,
  volume, touch, absence and jokes. Delete every book-specific section from any
  of these sheets and they still describe nine different people.
- One thing I left alone and am flagging rather than fixing: the last bullet of
  Odile's continuity section names two cut proposals so they are not
  reinstated. It is a reference to earlier versions and it predates this pass.
  Removing the guard risks reintroducing what it guards against, so it stays
  until the author says otherwise.

## Does the covered-names test pass?

**On the new material, yes, and cleanly.** Cover the names on the nine Stuck,
bored, and wrong in public sections and every single axis separates them. The
one who says out loud on day one that he has got nothing; the one who argues
that the question is ill-formed; the one who disappears for six days and misses
meals; the one who writes down the exact sentence where she stopped
understanding; the one who asks three classmates before it occurs to her to ask
the teacher; the one who takes the unsanctioned route; the one who works longer
and tells nobody; the one who does two hundred repetitions and never mentions
the count; the one who redraws the problem with her own numbers and arrives the
next morning wanting to explain it before breakfast. Nine, no overlap.

**On the sheets as wholes, mostly, with two honest reservations.**

The first is that the convergence was never in the sections these sheets are
longest in. It was in the "Personality off the page" and "Under pressure"
paragraphs, where six of the nine had some version of *bored, she finds
something to do with her hands; praised, nothing much happens; wrong, an
accurate account and no production made of it*. I fixed that where it was
actively wrong, on Priya, and where it was hiding a whole emotional register, on
Kavi, Sam and Eli. It is still faintly present on Odile, which I judged
acceptable because she genuinely is the taciturn one and because inflating her
was ruled out. If a second pass wants one more thing, it is that paragraph on
the remaining sheets.

The second is Chloe, Ruth and Kavi, whose "Do not confuse with" sections still
warn against each other on sentence shape. That is correct and it should stay,
because the shared baseline is the institution showing through and stripping it
would cost more than it bought. What has changed is that the baseline is no
longer the only thing on the page: Ruth is now loud and physically warm, Kavi
now has an emotional vocabulary made of things going missing, and Chloe now has
a body that will not stay in a chair. Those three could not previously be told
apart by a reader who was not tracking clause structure. They can now.

Rating the set against the author's own numbers: it was a three, theirs is a
five, and this is a seven. The gap between seven and higher is not more sheet.
It is the manuscript, where Eli in particular has been written for years as one
character and described on his sheet as another, and where the sheet, not the
book, was the thing that was wrong.
