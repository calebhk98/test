# Delinking the adult sheets

Eight sheets rewritten to `characters/_SHEET_RULES.md`: PRUITT, WHITAKER,
SANDOVAL, AMMONS, PRENTICE, GRANDMOTHER, DAVE, MEG.

`python3 verify_citations.py` on all eight now reports **0 quotations checked, 0
not found**. Before the pass it reported 66 checked and 41 stale across the same
eight. There is not a single double quotation mark left in any of the eight
files, so there is nothing left that can go stale when the manuscript moves
again.

Every sheet now ends with one clearly separated `Book-specific: navigation and
continuity` section carrying chapter lists, names, relationships and hard
continuity facts, and nothing else. Each of those sections opens with the line
that it can be deleted and the sheet still stands. That is the test the rules
document sets and every one of the eight passes it.

Common surgery across all eight, not repeated per sheet below:

- **Dials tables deleted.** Every one of them was rule 3 in a table: words per
  line, percentage of lines three words or fewer, percentage of questions,
  percentage of hedges. All measurements of a draft that has since moved. Where
  a figure pointed at something real it was converted into the tendency it was
  measuring, in words a writer can apply to a scene that does not exist yet.
- **Signature sections deleted as written.** Each was a quotation with a citation
  under a heading. Where the signature was real it survives as a described
  property in `How they talk`.
- **The kindly-and-inadequate test and the Halstead-staff test blocks removed.**
  Both were built out of quotations and both described the book rather than the
  person. The portable content in them was folded into `How they treat people`
  and `Blind spots`.
- **`Speaks: N lines` headers removed.** A line count is a fact about a draft.
- **New sections added, per the rules document:** what they want including the
  unsaid thing, what they are afraid of and what they do instead of admitting it,
  how they treat people, under pressure, blind spots, and age and change.

## PRUITT

The hardest case and the reason to lead with it. Two quoted lines in the entire
book, both stale, and his near-silence is load-bearing: chapters have been
written around not giving him a speech. Under the new rules neither line can
appear, and the old sheet's method was to quote both lines four separate times
and then reason from them, which meant that with the quotations gone there was
almost nothing left.

**Removed:** both quoted corrections, all four places they appeared. The
`Voice in one paragraph`, `Signature`, `Best at` and `Likes` entries that were
nothing but the two lines with commentary. The line-count framing throughout
(*2 quoted lines*, *two lines total, 9 and 8 words*, *four years and three
chapters of it*), which is rule 2 and rule 3 at once. The stale Known-problems
note quoting a summary phrase about which end to hold, which no longer exists in
the manuscript at all.

**Rewritten as:** two new sections that carry the whole character.

*What his silence is made of* breaks it into four parts, none of which needs a
forge. The work is legible, so a fault named once needs no argument. Being told
is worse than working it out, because the skill he is actually teaching is
finding the next fault alone. He refuses to be an occasion, because a correction
delivered standing still becomes a moment and a moment is a thing to dread.
Praise has nothing to name, because a piece that is right has already told its
maker so.

*When he does speak* gives a writer five properties that reproduce the effect in
any setting, with the note that the fifth does most of the work: after a long
silence, a flat sentence about a temperature is enormous, and the words
themselves do not have to carry anything. Followed by the warning that makes the
sheet usable, which is that the temptation is to give him a good line, and a good
line breaks him. The line should be plain to the point of dullness. The silence
around it is the instrument.

This is the clearest evidence in the set that a delinked sheet can be more useful
than the linked one. The old sheet could tell you what he said. The new one tells
you how to write him a line that has never existed.

**Believed wrong about the character, not merely hard-linked:** the old sheet
said his total spoken output in four years is two sentences and built the whole
of him on that. Chapter 17 shows him at the far bench keeping up a conversation
with two other adults while a student ten feet away gets nothing. He is not a
silent man. The silence is aimed at learners specifically, and it is temporary,
and that is a much better character. The new sheet says so explicitly and asks
that anyone writing a scene where a student gets nothing also show him talking
freely to the adults.

Also corrected: the old sheet said he was *working* at the far bench during the
scrap-bin scene. He is talking. And the old `Are they acting like themselves`
built a failure mode out of that single scene, which is rule 4; it is gone.

## WHITAKER

**Removed:** the quoted hypothetical, used three times. The
`Speaks: 4 quoted lines` header and every trait derived from a four-line sample
(*all 4 lines under 12 words*, *1 of 4 lines, 25%*, *3 of 4 lines are direct
questions*), all of which are rule 3 statistics computed off a sample too small
to mean anything. The Known-problems note about a blended paraphrase, which
described a repair to a previous version of this sheet rather than the character.

**Facts corrected, as instructed.** The sheet said the investigation ran seven
months across two visits. The manuscript says ten weeks, October and November.
The seven-month figure is gone from `Facts a rewriter needs`, from
`Are they acting like themselves` and from the sheet's sense of pace, and the
navigation section states the correction explicitly so it does not get
reintroduced.

**The explanation dropped.** The old `When they talk at length` had him laying
out in full what necessary would compel and what it would not, before asking her
to decide. He does no such thing on the page: he asks the hypothetical cold, gets
an answer, and writes it down. The manuscript version is the better-calibrated
character and the new sheet says so as a positive property, under `How they
talk`: he asks a direct hypothetical that puts a real choice in front of somebody
and then waits, and does not explain it first, because explaining it would be
doing the deciding for them and would also let them see the shape of the
interview.

That left him with no length condition at all, which the template forbids, so the
opening-up was relocated to where the manuscript actually puts it: he tells a
subject what the interview is for and what happens to their answers, before
asking anything, and answers a procedural question with the real answer on the
first try. That is his one expansive move and it is the thing that buys him
everything after it.

**Kept and generalised:** the flat uniform note-taking after every answer,
mundane or not, now with the reason attached (a subject who cannot tell which
answers interested him cannot start steering). The dry joke aimed at his own
paperwork, said to the folder rather than to the person, on the way out, which is
exactly what the manuscript does and is fully portable.

## SANDOVAL

**Removed:** all five quoted turns, which between them made up most of four
sections. The `Speaks: 3 quoted turns` header and the turn-count reasoning built
on it.

**The pronoun note is gone**, per the audit. The old sheet carried a long
subsection, a Known-problems bullet and a `Facts` block litigating a claimed
he/she conflict in a reference document, complete with two quoted manuscript
sentences as evidence. She is she throughout and there was never anything to
resolve. What survives is one line in the navigation section recording that an
older reference document made the claim, that it does not check out, and that the
note has been removed, so nobody re-opens it.

**Rewritten as:** her whole character reduces to one portable move, which the new
sheet states plainly and then applies. She names the structural truth about her
own position out loud, before asking, even when naming it weakens the ask.
`How she treats people` gives the mechanism: she considers a request with the
reasoning withheld to be an instruction wearing better clothes, she will not
simplify a real situation for somebody young, and she refuses the coercive
instrument when a non-coercive one exists and says that is what she is doing.
`Under pressure` carries the second-best thing about her, that she corrects her
own imprecise phrasing mid-argument when the imprecision favours her, and hands
the ground back. None of that needs a laboratory.

**Kept:** the blind spot, which is the sharpest thing on the old sheet and needed
no quotation to survive: she believes that stating a risk clearly always leaves
the other person freer to choose, and for somebody without the context to feel
what the stated consequence means, clarity and freedom are not the same thing.
Sharpened with a second clause: naming the power difference makes her honest, it
does not make it smaller.

## AMMONS

**Removed:** all seven quoted lines, appearing across `Voice`,
`When they talk at length`, `Signature` and the kindly-and-inadequate block. The
statistics (*6.9 words/line*, *0 of 7 lines are three words or fewer*,
*4 of 7 lines, 57%*, *the highest question rate in this batch*), every one of
them computed off a single hour of dialogue.

**Rule 4 applied hard.** She appears in one scene. Almost everything the old
sheet stated as a habit was over-fitted by construction, so the sheet was
rewritten as disposition rather than as observed behaviour, and shortened. The
navigation section now says outright that one scene is her entire evidence base
and that everything above it is a disposition consistent with that scene rather
than a documented habit, so a later writer knows what they are holding.

**Rewritten as:** the uniformity is the character. Not the length of her
questions but the fact that a routine question and a loaded one come out in
exactly the same voice, at the same pace, in the order they would have come in
anyway, so nothing in her delivery tells you which answer she is waiting for.
Her length condition, which was a quoted exchange, is now a condition a future
scene can meet: an account that sounds unambiguously good makes her more thorough
rather than less, because a glowing account is the exact shape of the thing she is
most afraid of missing, and she will say plainly that she is asking anyway.

Her signature, which was two quotations, is now described: she marks significance
with the pen and not with her face, writes through the routine answers, stops
writing and sets the pen down when something matters, and underlines a short
honest answer and then asks nothing else.

**Kept, and it is the best thing on the sheet:** the blind spot that her chart
has one place to put a true answer, so a real finding arrives as confirmation of
the frame she was already in. Added to it, unquoted, the observation the old
sheet buried in Known problems: a complete, careful, neutrally delivered protocol
collects exactly the same information a person with bad intentions would want,
and it is not obvious from the outside which one is being run, and she has never
had cause to think about that.

## PRENTICE

**Removed:** the three quoted lines and the `Speaks: roughly 28 quoted lines`
header. The terseness percentage and the words-per-line ranges. The Known-problems
bullets about a previous sheet's misquotation and mis-citation, which documented
a repair rather than the man.

**Rule 4 applied.** One scene, so the same treatment as Ammons: disposition
rather than habit, and a navigation-section line saying so.

**Rewritten as:** the register split, which is genuinely his and fully portable.
Short, plain and procedurally transparent with the person being assessed; long,
dense and unsparing with the person who can act. Length is reserved for whoever
can use it, and somebody being measured has no power in the room and so gets
clarity instead. His refusal to rate somebody to their face, and the exact figure
handed to the adult twenty minutes later, are now stated as properties rather than
quoted.

**One correction to the record.** The old sheet's jokiness entry, its `Signature`
and its `Do not confuse with` all leaned on lines that no longer read the way the
sheet has them, and one of the sheet's assertions, that he never varies his
delivery, turned out to be a property he shares with Ammons rather than a
separator. The `Do not confuse with` was rewritten around what actually
distinguishes them: he explains his reasoning as he goes and states the rules
first; she explains nothing during the hour and marks what matters with the pen.

**Kept:** the blind spot, restated as the real thing. He believes translation and
rescue are cleanly separable and that being good at the first discharges him of
the second, which is defensible and is also what lets him name a lever, know the
lever is two years out of reach, say so, and go home.

## GRANDMOTHER

Every one of her six quotations was stale, so there was effectively nothing left
to build on and she was rebuilt from what she does.

**Removed:** all six quotations. The words-per-sentence and terseness-percentage
entries. The kindly-and-inadequate block, which was three quotations and a rule.

**Rewritten from action.** Her whole method is physical and none of it needed a
word of dialogue: she takes a hand at the table and keeps hold of it after
whatever was being passed round has moved on, turns it over in both of hers, runs
a thumb over the marks on it for as long as she takes while a different
conversation carries on above her, then puts it down, pats it twice, and asks
about something else. Asks about what she found once, and lets a brush-off stand,
because she got what she needed with her hands before she opened her mouth. Takes
a physical observation to another adult, quietly, framed so nobody has to defend
themselves, says it once, and never follows it up. Hands out a job with a time
attached instead of asking whether somebody is all right.

Her miscalibration, the best thing about her, is now stated as a mechanism rather
than as an archery anecdote: anything reaching her secondhand about a world she
has not stood in gets measured against an ordinary life, and when corrected she
does not revise the estimate, she finds a gentler sentence that keeps the same
shape. The frame does not change, the wording does. That is a rule a writer can
apply to any report in any setting.

**Added, because the old sheet had the wrong reason for it:** her composure is a
professional trait, not serenity. Three decades on a hospital floor built somebody
who does not startle at a body doing something alarming and whose hands know what
to do first. In a genuine emergency she would be the most useful person in the
room and the least visibly moved.

**Believed wrong about the character, not merely hard-linked:** the old sheet's
Known problems said no scene after chapter 18 carries a quoted line and that her
final appearance stays in summary. She has a full run of dialogue at the
graduation, working a receiving line from a folding chair, asking each person who
passes exactly the same opening question and then one practical follow-up measured
against a domestic yardstick. That is one of her best scenes and the sheet was
telling rewriters it did not exist. The behaviour is now in `How she treats
people` and the stale note is corrected in the navigation section.

## DAVE

The most quotation-dependent sheet in the set: eleven of seventeen stale, and the
quotations were carrying `Voice`, `When they talk at length`, `Signature`,
`Would they say this`, `Subjects`, `Likes` and `Known problems`.

**Removed:** all seventeen. The measured words-per-line, terseness percentage,
hedging percentage and question percentage. The `Speaks: roughly 90 lines,
front-loaded` header. The repeat-count claim about a construction appearing again
five chapters later, which is rule 2 exactly.

**What survived, in the author's terms.** What Dave does with a problem he cannot
fix is now the spine of the sheet and has its own section. He takes apart whatever
adjacent thing has an edge on it. Given a child who has gone flat and a school
prospectus, he reads the prospectus four times, finds the one number that does not
add up, and presents that, and the prospectus is not the problem, it is the part
of the problem he can hold.

Above that, the deepest thing about him, which the old sheet had scattered across
three sections and hung on quotations: he wins his own argument and goes the other
way. He builds the strongest case in the room, patient and cumulative and correct,
and then sets it down for a fact about somebody he loves that he cannot document,
without announcing it and without experiencing it as a contradiction. Written as
a standalone section, `The thing to understand about him`, because it is the one
thing that transplants anywhere.

**What he does when Meg is wrong** is now stated: once, short, uncushioned, and
then he stops, and if she stays wrong he lets her, which is simultaneously his
most consistent failure and one of the reasons the marriage works. Paired with
the blind spot that he mistakes reticence for restraint and cannot reliably tell
which one he is doing.

**Age and change**, the section the brief flagged as unusually important, is now
the longest on the sheet and runs in six stages: the performer with a very young
child who can also go completely absent over a screen without noticing she has
stopped waiting; the opponent worth having at eight, conceding on a technicality
and enjoying the loss; the colleague at nine, who lets her break his reading of a
document and goes back to the top of the paragraph and acts on it without praising
her; the builder in her early teens who shows a tool once and then stops checking
her work, and who accepts a riser slightly proud and screws it down, teaching
fitness for purpose rather than perfection; the driving instructor who does not
raise his voice at a stall; and then the late-teen father who has run out of
subjects he can ask a real question about and switches to asking about her friends
instead, properly, and remembering the answers. The last stage is the adult
daughter, where he is an audience with a camera, and the note to a writer is that
the loss of the eight-year-old's opponent should be felt and never mentioned.

**Signature preserved without the word.** The low-commitment closing token is
described as a property, a full stop that acknowledges, declines to elaborate and
undersells whatever he is feeling, and the sheet says it is his and nobody
else's, without printing it.

## MEG

Nine of twelve stale, carrying `Voice`, `When they talk at length`, `Signature`,
`Subjects`, `Home`, `Facts` and `Known problems`.

**Removed:** all twelve. The measured words-per-line, the 54% terseness, the 4%
questions with a 20% target, the 0% hedging. The turns-per-chapter tally in Known
problems, which is a rule 2 and rule 3 hybrid.

**One target deliberately not carried over.** The old sheet set her a question
rate target of 20% and instructed a revision pass to default to a question before
a statement more often. That is a lint instruction, not a character, and under
rule 3 the tendency it was measuring is now written in words instead: her
defining construction is an escalating question with the follow-up already loaded
inside it before the first part has been answered, later clauses narrowing rather
than widening, closing exits.

**What survived, in the author's terms.** What Meg does with a decision she has
already made has its own section. She keeps asking questions at full intensity,
and the questions are not how she decides, they are how she holds the thing while
she carries it, and she is not pretending the decision is open. A writer who reads
the interrogation as an open mind gets her wrong, and so does a writer who reads
it as theatre. Paired with the thing that is easy to miss: when it goes against
her she concedes cleanly and immediately and with no residue, and goes and gets
the folder and makes the losing version workable.

Above that, `The thing to understand about her`: she will not accept anything that
costs her the ability to walk away. The corollary is fully portable and worth more
than the scene it came from, so it is stated separately: her generosity is
enormous and her acceptance is almost nil, she will give anybody anything, and she
can see the hook in a gift faster than anyone else in the room.

**What she does when Dave is wrong** is now stated: she does not tell him, she
asks him a question whose only honest answer concedes the point, and waits. When
she has been told something late she hands the minimising phrase back flat and
then asks the question that will not let it pass. The old sheet had this right and
had to quote a stale exchange to say it; it also correctly warned that the bare
repeat does not scale into a habit, and that warning is kept, unquoted.

**Age and change** is her arc and it is the loss of the room, in five stages. She
is the interface for a small child, and her competence and her role are the same
shape. Then the year her whole correct procedure produces nothing but an
instruction to wait, and she escalates the volume rather than doubt the method.
Then the separation, where everything arrives secondhand and late and her entire
instrument requires being in the room; nothing about her changes here, the world
stops giving her purchase, and watching her apply the method anyway is the point.
Then the teenage switch to reading her daughter's register instead of the content,
a real skill learned late and much less reliable than the one it replaced. Then
the adult daughter, where her care has compressed back into her hands, and the
woman who once ran a twenty-five minute interrogation of a stranger's institution
straightens a gown at a graduation and nobody remarks on it.

## Flagged separately: things believed simply wrong, not merely hard-linked

1. **PRUITT is not a silent man.** The sheet built his entire character on a total
   spoken output of two sentences in four years. Chapter 17 has him keeping up a
   conversation with two other adults at the far bench while a student ten feet
   away gets nothing. The silence is aimed at learners, and it is temporary. This
   is a better character than the one the sheet had and it is now written in.

2. **PRUITT was described as working during the scrap-bin scene.** He is talking.
   Small, but it was the load-bearing detail in a failure-mode note.

3. **WHITAKER's investigation ran ten weeks, not seven months**, and he never
   explains what necessary would mean. Corrected, and the explanation dropped in
   favour of the manuscript's version, which is better.

4. **SANDOVAL's pronoun conflict never existed.** Obsolete note removed; a single
   line records that it was checked and closed.

5. **GRANDMOTHER does have dialogue after chapter 18.** The sheet's Known problems
   said her final appearance stays in summary. Her graduation receiving line is
   one of her best scenes and the sheet was hiding it.

6. **PRENTICE's uniform delivery was written as a separator from AMMONS.** Both of
   them ask every question in the same voice regardless of what it is worth. The
   `Do not confuse with` on both sheets has been rebuilt around a cue that
   actually separates them: he explains his reasoning and states the rules first,
   she explains nothing and marks what matters with the pen.

7. **AMMONS and PRENTICE were both long sheets built on one scene each.** Not
   wrong exactly, but over-fitted by construction, per rule 4. Both are now shorter
   and both say, in the navigation section, that one scene is the whole evidence
   base.

8. **Two sheets carried Known-problems bullets about repairs to earlier versions
   of themselves** (PRENTICE's corrected citation, WHITAKER's blended paraphrase).
   Those describe the sheet's editing history, not the character, and are gone.

## Left alone deliberately

MEG's thin presence in the back half of the book, and DAVE's total absence of
dialogue at the graduation, are real gaps in the manuscript rather than defects in
the sheets. Both are recorded in the navigation sections as continuity facts a
writer should know, with the suggested fix, and neither was written up as
characterisation.

The reference documents' claim that Meg says something about her daughter having
stopped talking and got polite still does not appear anywhere in the manuscript.
Recorded in MEG's navigation section as a reference-document error to fix at
source; no chapter was touched.
