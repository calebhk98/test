# SYNOPSIS.md: what was merged, what was corrected

One new file, `SYNOPSIS.md`, at the repository root. Nothing was deleted and
nothing in `passes/review/` was touched.

## 1. What came from where

### From `passes/review/Suggested_Synopsis.md`

That file holds four separate synopses. What each contributed:

**THE ROOM** is the spine of the new document. Section 2 of `SYNOPSIS.md` is
built on it: the structure (the folder in the office, the envelope, the summer,
the collapse, the eleven years, the leaving, the world not fitting, Ruth doing
the arithmetic, the money, the file, the nine minutes, Priya), the register, and
a good deal of its sentence rhythm. Specific things taken whole because nothing
else in the repository says them:

- "She spends eleven years there, and she finishes squarely in the middle of
  ninety-one students, every single year. That fact is the engine of the book."
  This is the sentence the repository never had. It is now the hinge of section
  2 and the first theme in section 3.
- "the room was built so that somebody has to be sixty-fourth", which connects
  the school's design to Priya's refusal in the final chapter. Kept.
- "Their only remaining advantage is that he is very slightly wrong about how
  good they are." Kept as the close of the benefactor theme.
- The reading of chapter 7 as the negative image of the chat. Kept and extended.
- "Each of them explains it away as a fact about the other person." Kept.

**The final prose synopsis** contributed the second half of the voice and
several formulations that survive verification:

- "It is not a gifted program. It is a forging operation." Rephrased but kept as
  the sense of the curriculum paragraph.
- The framing of the ranking as a vaccine against ego: "they lose at things
  constantly, in front of each other, for eleven years." Kept in theme 1.
- The last movement of chapter 36, the offers of help and the mare, which is the
  best-written passage in any of the reviewer documents. Kept in outline in both
  section 2 and the chapter entry.
- "The mare keeps her own hours." Kept.

**The straight plot summary** contributed almost nothing that the other three did
not do better, and most of its factual claims are wrong (see section 2). Two
things survive: the phrase "the calibration error", which became the name of the
first theme, and the observation that each graduate meets the world through a
different institution, which shapes the ordering of chapters 24 to 31.

**The chapter-by-chapter** contributed the decision to have one at all, and the
dateline-per-chapter format. Its content was not used; section 5 is written from
the chapters and from `SYNOPSIS_FROM_TEXT.md`, because the reviewer version is
missing two chapters, misplaces one, and duplicates another.

### From `passes/review/Some_Spark_notes.md`

- **The themes section.** Its four themes are the seed of section 3 of
  `SYNOPSIS.md`. "The Burden of Exceptionalism and Isolation" became "The
  calibration error"; "Measurement vs. Reality" was kept nearly intact as
  "Measurement against reality", including the observation that the bar failure
  and the 600/600 are the same joke; "Surveillance, Secrecy, and the Unknown
  Benefactor" became "Being seen and not known" plus "A benefactor who is a
  person". "The Illusion of the Real World" was dropped, because the text does
  not support it: the outside world in this book is not fragile or easily
  manipulated, it is slow, and nobody in the cast manipulates it. Two themes were
  added that no source had: "The price of the room" and "The chat as the only
  room left".
- **The motifs section.** This is the only thing in the repository that does that
  job, and section 4 keeps its shape and three of its five entries: the encrypted
  chat, the number ninety-one, and the parking lot. "The Nine Pages" was folded
  into a wider motif of nines. "The Strawberry Magnet / The Letter" was split: the
  letter is a real motif and now has its own entry; the strawberry magnet appears
  once, in chapter 1, and is not one. Three motifs were added: the ranking sheets,
  the six Watch rules, and hands.
- Nothing else from that file was used. Its plot overview, character list and part
  summaries are superseded by material already in the repository.

### From `SYNOPSIS_FROM_TEXT.md`

Everything factual. This was the arbitrating document throughout: the datelines,
the ages table, the elapsed intervals, the cast, the per-chapter detail for
chapters 1 to 23 and 26 and 28 to 35, the through-line on what the schooling
costs Chloe's parents, and the note that the book never says what the school is
for. Section 6 is a lightly reorganised version of its timeline. Section 8 is
built on its closing observation.

Its four chapter entries that predate current text (24, 25, 27, and the absence
of 36) were written fresh from the chapters themselves.

## 2. Factual errors corrected

Every item below is a claim in one of the two reviewer documents that the current
manuscript contradicts. Chapter references are to `chapters/`.

### Structure and numbering

1. **The book has thirty-six chapters and neither reviewer chapter list has all
   of them.** The chapter-by-chapter in `Suggested_Synopsis.md` omits chapter 26
   and chapter 30 entirely, jumping 25 to 27 and 29 to 31.
2. **Part divisions.** `Suggested_Synopsis.md` uses five parts (1-3, 4-8, 10-18,
   19-23, 24-36); `Some_Spark_notes.md` uses four (1-9, 10-20, 21-29, 30-36).
   Neither tracks the book. `SYNOPSIS.md` uses three: 1-9 before Halstead, 10-23
   Halstead, 24-36 after.
3. **Chapter 9, "February", is filed inside "Part 2: Halstead".** It is the last
   chapter before Halstead.
4. **Chapter 18 is given as a verbatim duplicate of chapter 17's marksmanship
   paragraph.** The turret, the five weeks of zeroes, Odile first on 11 November,
   Chloe nine days later, the plateau at sixty and Voss taking the rifle all
   belong to chapter 18 (`18_fifteen.md`). Chapter 17 is the year of dry drill,
   live fire from October, the swinging target in November and the clay trap in
   February.
5. **Chapter 29's content is placed under a heading covering chapters 30 to 36**
   in the Spark notes. Chapter 29 is dated October 2025.

### Nadia

6. **"Nadia's company is forty people and eleven months old, making a diagnostic
   reader for independent mechanics, out of a converted fastener warehouse."**
   Wrong on every element. The company is eleven months old and four people
   besides her, it is a hiring platform, and it runs out of three rooms over a
   laundromat a hundred yards up the street from her parents' shop
   (`27_nadia.md`).
7. **Her arc has a failure in it that no reviewer version has.** The order is:
   the notebook closes at two hundred and eleven applications since November
   against fourteen answers (`23_the_first_one.md`); she negotiates her father
   into the back room and Sundays at the counter with a hard end date of the last
   day of March; the first version fails, at two hundred and twelve users since
   June, six hires, nine employers and four applications between them since
   August (`24_the_chat.md`); and in September 2023 she pivots to scraping every
   board and every company careers page she can reach, which puts nineteen
   thousand openings on the site by the first week of October. One reviewer
   version has the laundromat and no failure; one has "the back room of her
   parents' hardware shop" and no laundromat; none has the pivot.
8. **"Nadia interviews forty-one people and cannot find one who can finish a
   thought"** collapses two events a year apart. The forty-one interviews are
   July 2023, for two jobs, and thirty-nine of the forty-one say the thing they
   built does not break (`24_the_chat.md`). The "i cant find anyone who can
   finish a thought" line comes after thirty-one interviews across 2024
   (`27_nadia.md`), where she meant to sit down with twelve.
9. **"eleven of them generated by software between one and four in the
   morning."** The text says at two in the morning (`23_the_first_one.md`).
10. **"One of them apologises"** in the tire-shop confrontation. Nobody
    apologises. The door is shut and latched behind her, she is photographed, her
    plate is read out loud twice, and she is told the shop below closed at five
    so the stairs were hers alone (`27_nadia.md`).
11. **"Nadia negotiates better than anyone and cannot get hired"** is right, but
    the two places she actually negotiates on the page were missing from every
    reviewer version: her father, in `23_the_first_one.md`, and the bank manager
    in `24_the_chat.md`, whom she moves from five days to two on nine named
    payers and then gets the monthly fee waived for a year. Both are now in.
12. Her age is right in one reviewer version and unstated in the others. She is
    "Eighteen since last June" in May 2024 (`27_nadia.md`).

### Sam and the Army

13. **Chapter 25 is "Forty Targets", dated August 2023 to November 2023.** One
    reviewer version has that title, another does not; `SYNOPSIS_FROM_TEXT.md`
    has "Ten Targets" and November 2023.
14. **Not ten-week basic training.** It is a twenty-two-week infantry course, and
    Sam asks four people how long it runs and gets twenty-two weeks from all four
    (`25_forty_targets.md`).
15. **Not "ten targets and he hits ten".** Qualification is the second Thursday
    of October, in week nine: forty pop-up silhouettes from fifty to three hundred
    metres, ten exposures from each of five positions, one magazine change, three
    minutes fifty-eight. He hits forty.
16. **The captain does not praise him.** He notes it is the first forty he has
    signed for in two cycles, then interrogates the answer, then puts the card
    inside his jacket instead of into the stack. That evening he files **four**
    documents: a memorandum for record whose fourth paragraph records specialised
    instruction from an unidentified source and recommends the question be
    answered before Sam is assigned anywhere at all; a report to the supporting
    counterintelligence office; a request to the battalion S-2 for the education
    block of Sam's security questionnaire; and a behavioural health referral,
    signed last. The reviewer version has "a memo goes up, a folder is pulled".
17. **The fitness test.** Six hundred in week two, and again on the record test in
    week nine. On the Sunday between, he reruns four of the six events behind the
    barracks on a stopwatch to see whether the raw numbers underneath the scoring
    will move; two of them do, by a little. No reviewer version has this, and it is
    the clearest single illustration of the calibration error in the book.
18. **"keeps asking, for three years, when the real assessment starts."** Not
    supported. He asks through the course (August to November 2023) and once more
    at the Winter Anvil debrief in February 2024, where the major refuses the
    opinion outright.
19. **"leading to counter-intelligence investigations ... suspecting he is a
    foreign operative."** The file says unexplained specialised training from an
    unidentified source. Nobody in the text says foreign operative.
20. Chapter 26 is missing from both reviewer chapter lists. It is now in, with the
    culvert written up as a safety incident, Sam's own harsher self-critique, and
    the major's line that difficulty is set above him.

### Chapter 36 and Priya

21. **"Priya disables their vehicles, breaks their perimeter, escapes into the
    wilderness, and captures two operatives as prisoners."** What happens: she
    goes through the line, and about thirty in she puts two of the men into the
    engine blocks of the only two vehicles on that side of the field, because the
    alternative was outrunning a van on a farm track. She takes a tranquilliser
    dart on the third attempt, small dose, meant to slow her rather than put her
    out. She comes out with a hand that will not close all the way, and two
    prisoners she took because they had caught their own in the scrum.
22. **"ambushed on a remote South American ranch."** The chapter does not name a
    country, and Priya's point is that it does not matter which one she is in. She
    is in a room over a feed merchant's, reached by a bus with a cracked window
    and then a second bus.
23. **The escalation.** Two men in an unmarked van at half five in March, six in
    May, about a dozen in July, seventy-five on the Tuesday, give or take, with
    more behind the line she never reached.
24. **"Ruth delivers the final, definitive truth: they are sovereign,
    uncontainable outliers standing at the absolute apex of human capability."**
    Ruth says one thing: at the things Priya was doing on Tuesday she is somewhere
    in the top ten percent of people alive. Priya says no, twice, with the evidence
    in front of her.
25. **Priya's own better question is missing from every reviewer version.** If
    those two prisoners are what they say they are, one of them alone should be
    worth about four Sams, and there were seventy-five, so either they are lying
    about who they work for or somebody has spent a lot of money on people who are
    not very good at it.
26. **"The book ends with Ruth realizing that all seven of them are targets."**
    Ruth's list is of the seven other places any of them sleeps, and the distance
    from each to a road a vehicle could wait on, and the point of the passage is
    that it takes her until the fourth entry to notice she started at the bottom,
    with her own address last.
27. **The chat is eight people, not seven.** Chapter 36 says so in as many words.
    The seven are the signatories of the nine pages in chapter 33; Priya is not
    one of them.

### Chloe

28. **"Chloe gets cleared for the Foreign Service. The background investigator
    asks her about the eleven years. She tells him everything."** She does not.
    She gives Whitaker the eleven years, eleven languages, every class, the
    school's internal law examination (which she volunteers has no standing
    anywhere), and two published papers he has already pulled, and then refuses to
    describe a third and two others like it, because the lead researcher asked and
    everyone else had signed. Asked whether she would tell him if he said it was
    necessary, she says she would want to talk to the researcher first
    (`30_cleared.md`). The reviewer sentence refers to a line no longer in the
    manuscript.
29. **Chapter 30 is missing from the reviewer chapter list entirely.**
30. The Foreign Service exam is four hours, sat three weeks after she turns
    twenty, with the result arriving by mail six weeks later.
31. The reviewer's "authoring an anonymous, highly influential geopolitical
    intelligence blog that industry experts assume is written by a massive
    think-tank staff" overstates it. The text has readers, editors and an
    inbox, and people asking who else is on staff. No think-tank.

### Halstead itself

32. **"cohort of ninety-one students are raised in total isolation"** reads as if
    ninety-one is the school. There are eight hundred other children on the grass
    at graduation (`23_the_first_one.md`), and Chloe teaches seven twelve-year-olds
    from a year below her.
33. **"a ten-hour daily battery" / "ten classes a day" / "ten subjects a day."**
    The day is seven hours at seven, eight hours at eight and nine after Christmas.
    The sheet in her box gains a line a year: five subjects at seven, eight at
    nine (`10_april.md`, `11_eight.md`, `12_nine.md`).
34. **"students write weekly ten-page essays ... from age twelve"** in one
    reviewer version. It starts at ten, in chapter 13, and the benchmark is
    Hamilton's rate on the Federalist papers, explicitly at less than half of it.
35. **"acquiring between three and eleven world languages."** No such range in the
    text. Chloe lists eleven, in order, in chapter 30.
36. **"passing a full state Bar Examination at age sixteen."** It is the school's
    own examination, written and marked by the eleven teachers who proctor it, and
    Chloe tells a federal investigator it would not hold up in an actual court.
    Fifty-two of the year pass and thirty-nine do not; Chloe fails by four points
    out of two hundred and passes the October retake by twenty-two.
37. **"a lifetime research contract with a guaranteed $135,000 salary."** It is a
    hundred and thirty-five thousand to start, more once you carry your own
    project. Ninety-one names go up over nine days of exit interviews and eleven
    turn into yeses; ten of the eleven had already been on the badged research
    floor.
38. **"Two hundred students against forty adults"** for the April 2017 Watch. The
    text gives neither number, and listed failure number nine is precisely that
    nobody ever established how many adults were in the building.
39. **The 4 a.m. Watch grades.** "They grade it: C. Traceable agent selected."
    The actual grades are Chloe C, Sam C, Ruth B minus, Kavi B, with a response
    time of 7:50 against a standard of 6 (`15_twelve.md`).
40. **"an active, black-budget federal Tier-1 tactical raid."** The file says four
    operators. No tier, no budget descriptor, no adjective of any kind.
41. **"an RF-spoofing camera jammer ... had completely blinded federal
    surveillance teams for years."** Ruth's box finds networked video recorders on
    the networks around it, logs in with the passwords they shipped with, and
    writes noise over parts of the recordings. The file records one night, not
    years, and Ruth did not know the observation unit's cameras existed.
42. **"the power to erase government surveillance footage"** attributed to the
    benefactor in the Spark notes. It was a thirteen-year-old with a shoebox, over
    a weekend, so they would not get detention.
43. **"a multi-billion-dollar shadow education system."** No figure anywhere in
    the text.
44. **"state intelligence services have monitored the school for two decades."**
    One nineteen-year file, held by one unit, whose assessment moved from state
    actor to probably corporate and which has carried the same caveat about
    untraceable funding for nineteen years.
45. **"identified in a day and a half."** Identified inside twenty-four hours and
    escorted off. The day and a half is Theo's memory of how long the man was in
    the building (`29_the_file.md`).
46. **"Kavi and Eli build a tracing tool."** Eli builds the worm; Kavi builds the
    watcher whose only job is to notice the day the worm stops behaving like a
    reconciliation job. The division matters, because it is Kavi's watcher that
    fails to see the nine minutes.
47. **"financial footprints ... dating back to 1998."** Around 1998 or 2001,
    depending which thread you follow back; the instrument preference is dated
    from 2003.
48. **"In the summer of 2026, their monitoring worm experiences a series of
    identical, clean nine-minute pauses."** The first is a Thursday in June, and it
    happens twice more in six weeks, the second on the second Tuesday of July.
49. **"The cohort realizes that their mysterious creator discovered their
    intrusion, inspected their code, and quietly put it back in place."** That is
    Chloe's reading and Eli explicitly rejects it. The conclusion the group acts on
    is that he is not signalling, he made a mistake, and he is very slightly wrong
    about how good they are.
50. **"entering a quiet game of chess with the Architect."** They decide to do
    exactly nothing: no patch, no move, no looking at him looking at them.
51. **"Chloe's roommate ... Fen"** at camp. Her camp roommate is Priya. Fen is her
    roommate from April 2013.
52. **"the class hamster Icarus."** The hamster is Peanut; Icarus is what Chloe
    compares him to, twice, which is what gets her told she talks weird.
53. **"A receptionist warns Meg that people send mail addressed to children
    because children open it."** The district office warns that people send mail to
    houses with a child's name on it to find out which houses write back.
54. **"Chloe finishes the year at $217 to his $940."** She finishes up seventeen
    dollars against Rustem's nine hundred and forty (`18_fifteen.md`).
55. **"the Pluto cabin."** Pluto is a classroom, the last door on a ground-floor
    hall of nine brass planet plates.
56. **"the girls secure the perimeter and erase their digital footprints"** in the
    parking lot. The other three go over a chain link fence and crouch on a loading
    dock. The corridor cameras were looped before they left the building and Ruth's
    box was already running; nothing is erased afterward.
57. **"Sam dismantles the attackers with lethal efficiency."** Nobody dies. They
    check every man, put a shoulder back in, and leave.
58. **"parkour"** and **"cyber-warfare"** in the Spark curriculum list. Climbing
    and cryptography.
59. **Ruth's national statistics are placed in chapter 31.** She posts them into
    the chat in chapter 28 (September 2024 to April 2025) and stops posting in
    April; chapter 31 is where she comes back in November 2025 and says what they
    add up to.
60. **Theo's disclosure to the group.** Chapter 29 is Theo reading the file alone
    and posting one hypothetical. He tells the chat what is in it in chapter 32,
    which is where Chloe learns the 4 a.m. Watch was not a drill.

## 3. One thing left alone

Chapter 30 has Chloe start the government job in January 2026. Chapter 34 (June
2026) has her five months into it, which agrees. Chapter 36 (October 2026) has
her eight months into it, which does not; January to October is nine. This is in
the manuscript, not in the reviewer material. `SYNOPSIS.md` says only that she
starts in January 2026 and does not repeat either month count. Flagging it here
rather than resolving it, since resolving it means editing a chapter.

## 5. What this supersedes

**`SYNOPSIS_CHARACTERS_TIMELINE.md`.** All three of its parts are now covered
better elsewhere: its synopsis by `SYNOPSIS.md`, its character section by
`characters/*.md`, and its timeline by section 6 of `SYNOPSIS.md`. It is left in
place as instructed and should not be cited.

**`SYNOPSIS_FROM_TEXT.md` is not superseded.** It remains the factual reference
and the place any disagreement gets settled. It is longer and more granular than
`SYNOPSIS.md` on chapters 1 to 23, it carries the working for its own claims, and
it holds material `SYNOPSIS.md` deliberately does not: the section-by-section
through-lines, the full cast with chapter ranges, and the reconciliation notes at
the end. Its four out-of-date entries are chapters 24, 25 and 27, and the absence
of chapter 36; those four are correct in `SYNOPSIS.md`, which was written from
the chapters.
