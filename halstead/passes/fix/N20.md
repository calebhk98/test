## The fight — A–G working

Seven men. Three armed (guns down at sides except the front one's, which is
up and moving). One never engages.

- **A — front one.** Up gun, does the talking. Engaged solo, before the rush: wrist turned out and down, gun stripped, slide thrown under a car. Down first.
- **B — "the first."** Thrown by Sam into C. Down.
- **C — "the second."** Takes B's impact. Down.
- **D — "the third."** Gets an arm out; Sam takes it and throws him. Down.
- **E — "the fourth."** Swings at the back of Sam's head from the left. Nadia calls "left"; Sam says "I know" and is already turning — the swing finds nothing. Still standing, off balance.
- **F — second gun.** Draws mid-rush; Sam has the wrist before the barrel clears the pocket, disarms him, and F goes down into E (who's still there from missing his swing) — this is how E actually goes down, not a second act by Sam against him. Sam throws the gun in two pieces.
- **G — third gun, never engages.** Present in the group of seven but never named in the fight paragraph at all — found afterward, "a man who never got to it." This is the one the puzzle calls for.

A+B+C+D+E+F = 6 active, G passive = 7 total. No man is given two actions;
E's only action is the missed swing, and his going down is a consequence of
F's fall, not a re-engagement. I also reordered two paragraphs (moved the
dock-roof description to before "One of the men says something...", so the
lookouts are in position before the mugger confusion and Sam's approach,
rather than after) and cut "Sam walks at the seven of them," which was
redundant once the paragraph above it already has him walking. Labels are
not in the file — I only used them here to check the count.

## The four larger items

**1. Sam's motive.** Before:

> `"There are seven." / "I know." / "Sam." Ruth doesn't move. "Three guns." / "I know." He is already unhooking his thumbs from his pockets. "I've never had one that wasn't scored."`

After:

> `"They're adults." Ruth doesn't move. "Three guns." / "There's seven of them, and I'm basically one myself. That's fair." He is already unhooking his thumbs from his pockets. "I've fought people my whole life. None of them were adults, and none of them were strangers."`

This also resolves the "who says 'There are seven'?" note — that ambiguous
line is gone. I picked two of the four offered reasons (real adults, not
kids he already knows how to read) rather than all four, since stacking all
four in one flat-clause turn would read as a speech, which isn't Sam. Left
out: "practice for boot camp" (his enlistment isn't otherwise on the page
this early) and "not a mock fight" (redundant with "none of them were
adults, none of them were strangers" once both land).

**2. Sam's confusion.** Before: `"Seven, Ruth. With guns. It should have
been —" He stops. "It was nothing."` After: `"No, Ruth, I mean that was
nothing. Like, literally — not that it took a little effort, it took
nothing. Even if it was only seven, it should have taken something. I should
have gotten some experience, gotten hit once, they could have fired once. I
mean, they're seven adults, Ruth. Why was it nothing?"` Kept the author's
content essentially verbatim, cleaning only the one false-start typo ("not
it took" → "not that it took"). This break from his usual one-flat-clause
register is licensed by his own dial sheet: the one time he goes long is
when an assessment of himself doesn't match what he knows happened, which is
exactly this scene.

**3. Chloe's conclusion.** This was the one that needed the most rebuilding.
Before, the muggers were just unskilled ("some of them are probably not
well... couldn't have held down a job at the diner") — read as an insult
about competence, not the diagnosis the author wanted. After:

> `"People who fight like that, over somebody's phone, at two in the morning — something's actually wrong with some of them. Not hurt. Wrong the way it doesn't fix. You didn't fight seven adults, Sam. You beat up seven people who couldn't tell you their own address."`

This makes explicit that Chloe's read is: these men are disabled, that's why
mugging teenagers for phones at 2am is the job available to them, and that's
why the fight cost Sam nothing — because they don't count as a test of what
an adult can do. I also fixed two adjacent notes in the same speech: cut "so
nobody's ever taught them not to" (Chloe claiming knowledge of their
upbringing she doesn't have), and rewrote the locked-elbow line from "means
he's never fired it standing" (implies he's never fired a gun at all) to
"he wasn't going to use it. Why would he? That's police in four minutes, for
a phone and some watches off a set of teenagers" (she thinks he can shoot
fine, just wasn't going to). Also folded "And they're muggers" into her own
paragraph and cut "That's not nothing," per the note that it wasn't adding
anything once separated out. And: "thirteen-year-olds" → "ten-year-olds"
in Ruth's closing line, twice, per the note that a Halstead thirteen is
better than this.

**4. The waitress exchange.** Added one line making the belief explicit
rather than leaving it as something a reader could read as banter: after
Nadia's "The waitress would take you apart," Ruth adds "Any of us. Not just
him." Also applied Nadia's own character-sheet fix (naming "the waitress"
instead of "she," matching her signature of naming rather than defaulting to
a pronoun) and the flow-fix from the note ("Wait," he says. → "What?" he
asks.).
