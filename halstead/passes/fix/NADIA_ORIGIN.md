# Nadia origin pass

Brief: replace the diagnostic-reader company with the hiring platform from
`passes/nadia/ORIGIN_BEATS.md`, and build the origin out of the baffled job
hunt already seeded in chapter 23. Files owned: chapters 23, 24, 27, 34 and
`characters/NADIA.md`. Chapters 21, 22 and 32 read for context and left alone.

Both `passes/nadia/` documents were treated as direction rather than
specification, per the mid-pass correction. Conflicts and refusals are listed
at the bottom.

---

## What the company is now

A job site with the resume taken out. A user spends one evening on short skill
tests, the profile builds out of work she can demonstrate, and after that the
site fills the forms and submits them on her behalf, to the employers on the
site and to whichever outside boards will still accept submissions from it.
Users pay nothing; employers pay on the hire. It therefore earns by getting
people off it, which is stated once as a mechanism and never as a virtue.

The three-county limit in chapter 27 is now load-bearing rather than
decorative: two of the large boards blocked her submissions in August 2023, so
she started driving to employers and signing them in person on Saturdays. Three
counties is how far one person with a car gets. That is also what catches the
sabotage in chapter 27, so the product's real-world fragility became the plot
rather than something the book has to pretend away.

## Chapter 23, `chapters/23_the_first_one.md`

**Kept.** Everything outside the Nadia paragraphs. Inside them: the opening
three sentences verbatim (she stays, two months looking at jobs, baffled, back
behind the counter checking the till first), the sleeves-up beat before she
speaks to her father, "It's somebody's business" restructured into the same
shape of exchange, her father setting the invoice down and looking at her
properly, "What would it even do?", "She says it the way she'd say a fact about
the weather," and the unanswered close: he closes the invoice and takes a long
moment before he answers. Also kept: the complaint reaching the chat as half a
joke, Eli saying he will look into it and letting it drop.

**Cut.** The woman with the kettle and the housing portal, the tally of people
asking the shop counter for a login or a form, and the four-favours-and-a-shop
-counter product that grew out of them. That whole passage belonged to the
abandoned direction. Its function (she notices a gap and treats it as a
business rather than a favour) is now carried by the job-hunt ledger.

**Added.**

- *The trigger, dramatised.* March and April kept in the ledger hand at the
  back of a notebook: 206 applications, 14 answers, 11 of those written by
  software between one and four in the morning. One reply nine minutes after a
  fifty-minute form. A March listing still up in the last week of April, same
  four paragraphs, reposted twice with a fresh date. Six entry-level postings
  wanting two years against one wanting three that offers to train the right
  candidate.
- *The A/B test.* She sends one application to one listing twice inside an
  hour, the second copy with the listing's own sentences run back through it.
  Only the second gets a phone call. She tells Ruth, and Ruth tells her to do
  the second one every time. That is the complaint-to-friends beat the notes
  ask for, and it is left as a complaint.
- *The four angles.* Delivered as three flat statements inside the same
  exchange with her father, never as a speech, and he answers none of them
  directly: the risk-management angle (a person reading applications can be
  sued over which one he picks, software cannot, and it leaves a record), the
  unpaid-labour angle (206 of those is nine weeks of unpaid work, and whoever
  cannot afford nine weeks takes whatever is left), and the incentive angle
  (the sites charge thirty a month to keep looking, so getting her hired inside
  a week is the worst week one of them could have). The surface symptoms are in
  the narration above rather than in her mouth, so nothing reads as a summary
  of the problem.
- *Block Two.* One buried sentence, mid-paragraph, before she pushes her
  sleeves up: the thing has been in her afternoon block since the second week
  of March, in the hour the school records nothing about. Planted as already
  underway, as instructed.
- *The calibration line.* "Then it comes to nothing and I go get a job like
  everybody else. There's probably a reason nobody has built it. I can't find
  the reason. That's probably me." Delivered flat, to one person, and nobody
  in the scene reacts to it.
- *Un-naivety, planted early.* Her father asks whether the big boards let her
  do that. "For now. They'll stop me once they notice, and then I go and get
  the employers myself, one at a time, in the car."
- *Launch.* Second week of June, four days after the gowns go back, nine local
  employers who have all bought something from her father.

## Chapter 27, `chapters/27_nadia.md`

**Kept, verbatim or near.** Forty people, eleven months old, three counties.
The converted fastener warehouse, the half-wall, the idle loading dock with the
roll door on a cinder block, the concrete she resurfaced herself, forty coffee
orders and the break-room machine that keeps losing to the list. The cleaning
contract, four nights a week, the dentist and the chiropractor on the same lot,
and her interviewing that crew herself the way she interviews everyone who gets
a key. Five minutes on a chair at eight each morning and then down on the floor
all day. The chat shape ("im FINE. im insulted"). The kitchen-table week before
eight in the morning with the legal pad in the ledger hand. The state business
filings, the eight-month-old paperwork, the registered agent, the county
property records, the suite above the tyre shop, the phone call to the tyre
shop counter in the voice of somebody scheduling a delivery. The list that does
not move: payroll on the fifteenth, take-home tests graded at the same table.
No police, no lawyer, out for the afternoon. The steel stairs, the four men,
the coffee going cold, the apology, and her leaving before he finishes saying
it. The entire hiring section, including the take-home she keeps making easier,
the two-question interview, the two named candidates, the glowing references,
and the vendor she declines twice.

**Changed.**

- Product description in the opening paragraph. Assembly became the people who
  work the phones; bench legs became desk legs; "what shipped yesterday" became
  "what went live yesterday."
- The cleaning contract is now texture only. It stays because the
  interviews-everyone-with-a-key detail is worth keeping, but it is no longer
  the attack vector.
- The sabotage. The new-employer queue runs long on a Tuesday in the second
  week of May: nineteen names where a good week has four, eleven of them
  companies she has never driven to. The tells are the ones the notes name: one
  job description pasted eleven times with the company name swapped at the top
  and in the third line and nowhere else, so that one posting advertises a role
  in a city the same posting puts forty miles away; all eleven accounts created
  inside a single forty-minute window; zero hires behind any of them.
- Why it dies on contact. Employer-to-user traffic stays on her servers until
  an offer is logged, a decision she made in June to hold the support calls
  down, so the scam has to be run in writing inside a log she owns and reads.
  Nine of the eleven ask for a Social Security number and a bank routing number
  in the third message, same four sentences, comma splice and all. Three people
  have already answered.
- Her response is fast and unglamorous: deletes the accounts standing up in
  about ninety seconds, then takes the three names into the windowless office
  and telephones them one at a time at nine at night. The third call runs
  twenty minutes because the woman wants to explain how it happened and Nadia
  lets her.
- The confrontation is now about laziness rather than about an attack on her,
  since the notes are right that nothing targeted makes sense pre-fame. Her
  closing beat: "You wrote the ad one time and pasted it eleven times, and one
  of them is hiring in a city the same ad puts forty miles away. An hour of
  work, all told. That's the part I mind." She declines to call anyone, which
  is what her sheet says she does, and the three users are already talking to
  their banks so the harm is closed before she goes.
- Hiring: "the two other diagnostic-tool shops fishing the same short list of
  local machinists" became "the two other outfits fishing the same short list
  of local engineers." The vendor now pitches an applicant-screening tool
  scored off uploaded resumes, which is left entirely unremarked.
- One added sentence carries the no-friends rule: every one of the forty on the
  floor came in off the site the way a user does, tests first, and she has sat
  across the same folding table from all forty.

## What chapters 21, 22 and 32 would need

Not edited. Reported only.

**Chapter 21 (`October 2022 - March 2023`).** This is the natural home for the
job hunt starting, because it is already the applications chapter and its date
range covers exactly the months the notes want. One paragraph would do it: the
ninety-one applying to colleges while Nadia, who is not applying anywhere,
starts a different set of applications on the same evenings. It would cost
almost nothing and would let chapter 23 drop its retrospective "the two months
are March and April" framing.

**Chapter 22 (`April 2023`).** The exit interviews. Amberg runs fifteen minutes
with all ninety-one, so Nadia had one, and the chapter currently tells us only
that eighty said no. Two or three lines of her slot would seed the whole thing
properly: the notes want the company already underway before graduation, and
the strongest place to show that is her turning down a hundred and thirty five
thousand dollars a year with a half-built job site in her afternoon block and
saying so flatly, or not saying so at all. Either choice is a real one and it
belongs to the author. Chapter 23 currently plants Block Two in a single buried
sentence because 21 and 22 were out of scope.

**Chapter 32 (`The Money`).** Nothing needed. Its Nadia material is her fourth
quarter, her own live books, the shop's till and ledger, and four hundred small
transactions a week. None of it depends on the product.

**Chapter 36, outside the brief, flagged for the author.** "Nadia has four more
people on the shop floor than she had in June." With a software company "shop
floor" now reads as her parents' hardware shop rather than her own building.
It is ambiguous rather than wrong, and one word would settle it.

**Nothing else in the manuscript names the product.** A grep across
`chapters/` and `characters/` for the diagnostic reader turned up only the two
sentences in chapter 27 and the one line in `characters/NADIA.md`, all now
changed. Chapter 31's "nadia cant find anyone who can finish a thought" still
lands, since the hiring section is intact.

## Conflicts with the two documents, and what was not honoured

**1. `COMPANY_AND_CEO.md` is mostly about the other book.** Cost-plus-ten-
percent pricing, bundled housing and healthcare, the Google/Amazon sequence,
the regulatory judo, the Hollywood exchange, the decades-long training model:
none of it exists at forty people in three counties, and none of it was used.
Only the "Why she's doing this" section was applied, which is the section the
brief said was load-bearing.

**2. The notes put chapter 27 at twenty-two. The manuscript puts her at
eighteen.** She is born June 2005, the company launches June 2023, chapter 27
is dated May 2024 and says eleven months old, so she is eighteen in that
chapter and turns nineteen the following month. Every dateline was left alone.

What this changed in practice: nothing was made more experienced. The chapter
27 interview method was left exactly as written, because at eighteen it reads
correctly as a person who has run four hundred interviews in a year and refined
a method by volume rather than by age. The earlier version had to sit only
eight months back rather than four years, so it is one blunt question ("tell me
where the thing you built breaks") asked of forty-one people, against the
take-home plus two questions of chapter 27. Eight months of doing all of it
herself is enough to explain that difference, and it avoids inventing a
maturity gap the timeline cannot support.

**3. The 9,000-line claim checks out, but not as the notes state it.** Chapter
17 sets a no-libraries project for the whole year, one sentence of brief,
something that does a real job, all of it written from scratch. Nine thousand
lines across fourteen files is Chloe's specific figure for her text renderer,
not a cohort-wide number. The assignment is cohort-wide, which is enough. The
pass leans on it by saying nothing at all: no line anywhere explains how Nadia
can build the thing, and none should be added.

**4. Cross-board auto-submission would get blocked, and the book now says
so.** Writing it as though it simply works would have read as naive, so the
blocking is on the page: Eli predicts it in September 2023, two boards have
already done it in August, and her answer is to get in the car. The auto-
submission survives as a partial capability, phrased in chapter 27 as
"whichever outside boards are still accepting submissions from it that month."
This is the one place the notes' differentiator list was narrowed rather than
implemented as written, and it is what makes the three-county employer roster
in chapter 27 exist at all.

**5. The optional layering was declined.** The notes float making the scammer a
rejected candidate from her own hiring process. It was left as the four men
above the tyre shop, running it as a small business, because it preserves the
existing scene and because a personal grudge would have pushed the beat toward
the "targeted at her" reading the notes explicitly rule out.

**House Rule 1.** No sentence in the added material explains what the company
represents, what the design means, or why any of it matters. The incentive
logic appears once, in her mouth, as a remark about what a bad week looks like
for a job board. The users who nearly lost bank details are handled in three
phone calls and never mentioned again. Nobody in any scene tells her she has
done a good thing, and the narrator does not either.
