## Words left alone on purpose

- **Chapter 16, the "second line" motif is not here — that's chapter 19.**
  In 19, `second` stayed at 14 because five of its eighteen original hits are
  a single deliberate refrain across the Amberg contract-law scene (lines
  53/55/57/85/115: "your second line," repeated as the essay comes back with
  the same four blank rows). Cutting that would blunt the scene's own point,
  so only the incidental "second week/second floor" markers were touched.
- **Chapter 19, `already` (19→12) and chapter 17's `every` (19→13):** both
  chapters build a scene where the repeated word is the argument. In 19,
  Amberg's whole speech to Chloe is about a reader who "already" knows what
  she knows versus a marker who does not — nine of nineteen hits are inside
  that single exchange and were left standing. In 17, most `every` hits sit
  inside the sound-timing engineering dialogue (every box, every post, every
  hand) where the word is doing real technical work, not padding.
- **Chapter 20's fight scene (roughly lines 77-163) and the diner "hither"
  argument:** left almost untouched by design. `second`, `still`, `already`,
  `comes`, `goes`, `does`, and `somebody` inside the fight are load-bearing
  (timing, physical sequence, or Chloe's post-fight analysis) and the brief
  flagged this scene as protected, so I erred toward leaving verbs as they
  were rather than hunting for marginal swaps. `whole` in the diner scene
  (17 hits, heaviest in the book) is Ruth's own verbal tic — "the whole
  school," "the whole tour," "the whole test," "the whole argument" — and
  cutting it to zero would have erased a character voice, so it came down to
  12 rather than further.
- **`gets`/`goes`/`comes`/`does` generally:** per the house caution, these
  were only swapped where a plainer active verb was clearly more exact
  (walks/reaches/arrives/clamps/wrenches/etc.), never where the generic verb
  was already the right one (dialogue idiom like "gets paid," "what a coin
  does," "goes for," scoring language like "gets a C"). Chapter 17's `goes`
  dropped the most (23→3) because most instances were narration filler
  ("goes on a table," "goes over the rise," "goes back to Japanese") with an
  obvious more-exact replacement; chapter 20's fight-scene `goes`/`comes`
  were left alone entirely.
- **Chapter 17 word count:** trimmed four minor clauses (a tangential
  "visiting inspector" aside, a "where the drivers sleep" list item, a
  redundant file-count clause, one aside in the mother's Sunday-drive
  question) on top of the vocabulary edits. It moved from 5096 to 5046 —
  still the longest of the six and still the one furthest from the 2,600-3,600
  band, but headed the right direction rather than staying level.
