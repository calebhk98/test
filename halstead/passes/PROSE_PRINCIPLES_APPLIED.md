# What was done with the two rules files

`PROSE_PRINCIPLES_STRUCTURE.md` and `PROSE_PRINCIPLES_CLARITY.md` are research,
written by two agents who went and read the literature instead of writing down
what an English teacher sounds like. Neither agent edited a chapter. This file
records what was done with their thirteen findings.

## Applied, nine sentences

| chapter | principle | what it was |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `04_pluto` | parallelism | a list running participle, participle, adjective |
| `10_april` line 121 | dangling modifier | "checking off a list" hanging off a dummy *it* |
| `10_april` line 47 | fake agency | the far end of a room coming back down the room; it is the sound that comes back |
| `13_ten_pages` | referential failure | "Somebody is off her feet" has nobody doing anything to anybody. Now "Somebody has her off her feet" |
| `18_fifteen` line 95 | ambiguous pronoun | four pronouns, two women, thirteen words, no name to anchor any of them |
| `31_ruth` | nominalization | "Her assumption is that" for "Ruth assumes" |
| `35_nine_minutes` line 105 | dangling appositive | an elevator ride described as a kind of building |
| `35_nine_minutes` line 101 | ambiguous pronoun | a "she" after a scene break, with Ruth the last woman named and Chloe the one meant |

## Not applied, four

- `13_ten_pages` line 91, end-focus. Kavi's discovery is buried in a trailing
  relative clause, which is a real end-focus fault, but the sentence sits
  inside the protected paintball passage that earlier passes built on purpose.
  The author's call, not a pass's.
- `18_fifteen` line 219, characters as subjects. The grandmother is the
  grammatical subject through the sentence where Chloe's burns are found. The
  agent called it a judgment call and it is one: the camera staying on the
  grandmother may be the point of the moment.
- `22_the_offer` line 209 and `26_the_exercise` line 131, both inside dialogue,
  reported per instruction and left alone. A character is allowed to say a
  sentence that parses badly.

## The finding that outranks all thirteen

The structure agent was asked whether our "person in the subject of a passive
is a fault" rule is defensible. The answer is no: it is a rule this project
made up, it correlates with the truth in this book because this is a book
about named people doing things to a child, and it misfires in both
directions the moment it leaves that pattern. Two correct person-subject
passives are sitting in the manuscript right now, unflagged, and would have
been broken by the next agent handed the old rule.

House rule 8 now carries the real test instead. The animacy intuition the
author had is a real effect in the psycholinguistics of speech production,
but it predicts the opposite of what he guessed, and it is not what makes
these particular sentences read well; what makes them read well is that the
missing doer is nobody in particular.
