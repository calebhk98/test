# Iyad stops having an innocent reading

A blind reader given only the chapters, no notes and no sheets, called him
"closer to intended to target" than the other character but stopped short:
"the text never gives his interior motive or shows him alone enjoying her
discomfort; it could be compulsive information-brokering with no filter
rather than malice. The text stops short of confirming which." Two changes,
each an action rather than an explanation, because house rule 1 forbids the
narration from settling this by telling the reader what he meant or felt.

Files edited: `chapters/16_thirteen.md`, `chapters/18_fifteen.md`.
`chapters/17_fourteen.md` and `chapters/24_the_chat.md` were read in full and
left untouched (reasons below). `chapters/12_nine.md` and
`chapters/13_ten_pages.md` were read for context only, per the hard
constraint; no proposal was written to `IYAD_PROPOSED_12_13.md` because both
his beats there already fail a no-filter reading (see "Chapters 12 and 13,
checked and left alone" below).

## Change 1 — chapter 16, the cipher napkin

**Old:** nothing followed the cipher scene; it ended at

> By the end of the meal the table's five opinions have settled on hers.

**Reason:** this is the scene the blind reader flagged by name. Iyad's line
a few paragraphs earlier, "Chloe's had it a while and she'll not say. She
never says until she's certain, and she's never certain," reads as tease,
insult, or fair comment with no way to choose between them, because nothing
in the scene tests the claim against what actually happens next. It happens
next in the very same scene: Chloe answers immediately and correctly, on her
first sentence, and Nadia confirms it on the spot. A no-filter record-keeper,
confronted with his own claim being disproved in front of him, would report
the correction with the same appetite he reports everything else. What
proves selection is what he does with the disproof: he keeps the label
moving to somebody who cannot check it, and when asked the one question that
would settle it, he gives an answer that is true, unhelpful, and final.

**New (added after the paragraph above, nothing else changed):**

> Iyad tells it at breakfast two days later, to a boy who was not at the
> table that night: how the table sat on that napkin for most of an hour
> while Chloe held the answer back until Nadia took the corner off her. The
> boy asks whether she was right, in the end. "She generally is," Iyad says,
> and starts on somebody else's week.

The no-filter reading fails here because a person with no filter, holding a
claim he watched get disproved an hour earlier, would say so when a listener
asks him directly; Iyad has the true, better answer in hand and gives a
non-answer to an audience with no way to check either version, then moves on
before the question can be put a second way. That is a choice about which
version of a settled fact travels, not an absence of one.

## Change 2 — chapter 18, the sign-up sheet (selection)

**Old:**

> Iyad has more than figures. In February he has Aurel's name next to hers
> on the sign-up sheet for an optional Saturday session neither of them ends
> up going to, and he turns it into an item on his own list before either of
> them has said a word to the other about anything.

**Reason:** this is the chapter's anchor scene and the author's named
example ("the crush rumor was falsely created"). The previous pass
(`BEX_AND_IYAD_INTENT.md`) had already turned the rumor from something
"misheard" into something built from a real coincidence, which stops him
being a liar, but it left him looking like a boy who cannot help noticing
things and saying them, which is the no-filter reading in different clothes.
What was missing was proof that the sheet in front of him held more than one
such coincidence and he did something to only one of them. A record-keeper
with no filter turns every equivalent item into an item; picking one out of
several identical ones and leaving the rest exactly as they were is a
decision a camera can show without naming it.

**New:**

> Iyad has more than figures. In February he has Aurel's name next to hers
> on the sign-up sheet for an optional Saturday session neither of them ends
> up going to. Six other pairs sit just as close on that page, for sessions
> just as skipped. He leaves each of those exactly where the sheet has them
> and picks up only hers, turning it into an item on his own list before
> either of them has said a word to the other about anything.

The no-filter reading fails here because the sheet hands him six equivalent
coincidences and he does nothing with six of them; a boy who repeats
whatever he happens to see would have no reason to leave five untouched
pairs on the same page he was already reading closely enough to notice hers.

## Change 3 — chapter 18, the confrontation (catchable lie)

**Old:**

> She tells Iyad to stop, at the long table, in front of the same people he
> said it in front of. He says he only repeated what he heard and cannot
> help what he hears, and then asks her, at the volume of the far end of the
> table, for the actual number of times she has spoken to Aurel. The far end
> laughs.

**Reason:** the blind reader's second named problem. The sign-up sheet
detail that exposes "I only repeated what I heard" as false sat a full
paragraph and a scene-break earlier, so the lie only registered on a second
read, if at all. The fix is not to change what Iyad says; a boy denying
wrongdoing under pressure and then pivoting to a question that puts the room
back on the person confronting him is exactly what his sheet specifies he
does ("he has a question ready that turns the room back to the thing the
confronter least wants discussed"). The fix is to put the one fact that
contradicts him into the same breath as the confrontation, in Chloe's own
mouth, so the reader is holding it at the moment he answers rather than
several hundred words before.

**New:**

> She tells Iyad to stop, at the long table, in front of the same people he
> said it in front of. He says he only repeated what he heard and cannot
> help what he hears.
>
> "Heard it from who, though? There's a sheet outside the workshop with his
> name and mine on it, for a session neither of us went to, and that's the
> only place they've ever sat next to each other. Say who told you before
> that, or say there wasn't anybody."
>
> He asks her, at the volume of the far end of the table, for the actual
> number of times she has spoken to Aurel. The far end laughs.

The no-filter reading fails here because Chloe now names the only place the
two facts he claims to have "heard" could ever have sat next to each other,
in the same beat where he claims to have merely repeated something, and his
answer to a direct request for a source is a different question aimed at
her instead. A boy who genuinely had a source would give it; what he gives
instead is the deflection his own sheet reserves for being cornered.

## Chapters 12 and 13, checked and left alone

Both beats named in the brief were read in full. Neither needed a proposal.

`chapters/12_nine.md`: "In March what he has on her is Mandarin, and he
waits until she is most of the way down the hall with her tray before he
says it, at the volume of the far end of the table." The wait for a specific
placement of her body in the room, timed to a specific piece of material he
has held rather than said as soon as he had it, is already an action with no
no-filter reading: a no-filter broadcaster says the thing when he learns it,
not when the target is back in earshot.

`chapters/13_ten_pages.md`: "Twenty minutes later Iyad has it at the far end
of the table, pitched for both benches. He gives them Chloe's line first,
nobody scores the drill, and then the one he has built on the front of it."
Building a second clause onto her own sentence that puts her name where the
laugh lands, held for twenty minutes and delivered to the fullest audience
in the room, is construction, not transmission. Neither scene reads as
compulsive; no changes proposed.

`chapters/17_fourteen.md`, "taught them backward": already carries the
deliberate structure from the previous pass, held from a Tuesday with her
three seats down to a Thursday with her absent and the table full. No

`chapters/24_the_chat.md`, the October scheduling email: already the

Every other name in the round-robin gets a logistics line only; Chloe's line
gets the logistics and then, "in the typeface used for everything else in

register as everything true and administrative around it. Chloe's
non-engagement (answers the weekend, ignores the rest) was left exactly as
written, per the instruction not to let her win, only to make plain what she
is declining to engage with.

## Where he still reads as merely compulsive

The read-out of the loan figures earlier in chapter 18 (Iyad's list of what
everyone did with their two hundred dollars, arriving at Chloe's number "in
the voice he said Rustem's in") is deliberately left as pure signature: it is
his ordinary, non-antagonistic register, the same list read the same way for
everyone on it, and turning it into a target would blur it with the Aurel
scene rather than sharpen the contrast between the two. A reader looking at
that scene alone, in isolation from the Aurel paragraph forty lines later,
could still call it flat information-brokering with no filter. That reading
is correct for that scene and is not a defect; the book needs him to be
genuinely indiscriminate somewhere so that the sign-up sheet's selectivity
has something to be selective against.
