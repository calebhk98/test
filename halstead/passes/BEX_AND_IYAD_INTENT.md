# Bex and Iyad: making the intent visible

*This pass follows the author's fresh instruction, given after `BEX.md` and
`IYAD.md` were rewritten to license deliberateness that the earlier
`BEX_AND_IYAD.md` pass had ruled out. Where that earlier pass says a line
"has broken her" or "he has become a different and much worse character,"
check the date: both sheets record the author overruling exactly that
position afterward. This pass builds on the new sheets, not the old audit.*

## The two methods, one paragraph each, written before any line was touched

**Bex.** Bex is not building toward anything and never plans across a day,
let alone a week; the sheet is explicit that any version of her who schemes
ahead has stopped being her. What she does instead is notice, in the second
it is happening, that the room's memory is up for grabs, and take it,
gladly and at volume, to whoever in the room has the standing to make the
memory stick: a teacher, a rep, a stranger with a notebook. The tell is
never a stated reason. It is a half-beat of recognition a camera can catch,
a glance found and held before she keeps talking, a choice of audience (the
row behind her, the visitors' table, the person writing it down) over the
person she took it from, and a refusal, when confronted, to treat the
transfer as anything other than settled: not sorry, not surprised, "it was
true by the time I said it." She never plans the moment. She never misses
one.

**Iyad.** Iyad's currency is being the fixed point everybody's information
passes through: the ledger, the read-out, the boy who has the room in his
head. His deliberateness is entirely in staging he never announces: he
withholds a true, embarrassing, or damaging item through an audience it
would land soft on and produces it for the audience it will land hardest
on, in the identical flat voice he uses for the weather. For items with a
real chain behind them (the teaching mistake, the ledger figures) he can
always name where they came from, even if he won't. The one item in this
pass that cannot be traced to anyone (the Aurel rumor) is the tell: he
built it himself, out of a coincidence, and gave it out in the same voice as
everything true, so its truth is borrowed rather than earned. Confronted, he
never claims credit and never apologizes to the person it cost; he deflects
onto what he "only repeated," which the reader can now check against the
sign-up sheet and see is false.

The two are not interchangeable: Bex takes what is already true and puts
her name where the room will remember it; Iyad manufactures what is not
true and puts no name on it at all, including his own. Bex's tell is a
glance and an audience choice happening once, fast, without a plan behind
it. Iyad's tell is a wait: a beat where he had the chance to say something
with her in the room and chose the room without her instead.

## Every change: old text / reason / new text

All old-text quotations below were copied from the chapter files and
checked against them a second time before being printed here.

### 1. `chapters/12_nine.md` — the bridge (age nine)

**Old:**
> The teacher asks how they held. Bex has it before Chloe has her mouth
> open, the whole fortnight and every part of it right, and she says we in
> her first sentence but I in the rest of them. The teacher writes it on the
> sheet and moves along the table.

**Reason:** The pronoun swap (we, then I) was already the mechanism, but
nothing in it was visible to a camera as a moment of noticing. BEX.md was
rewritten specifically to allow "an antagonist who is trying, in a small
way, to land one," provided nothing shows premeditation. A single glance at
the switch itself, not before it and not planned, is the smallest possible
unit of that.

**New:**
> The teacher asks how they held. Bex has it before Chloe has her mouth
> open, the whole fortnight and every part of it right, and she says we in
> her first sentence, finds Chloe over the top of the desk before she
> finishes it, and gives the rest of it in I. The teacher writes it on the
> sheet and moves along the table.

### 6. `chapters/18_fifteen.md` — the Aurel rumor (age fifteen), two edits

**Old:**
> Iyad has more than figures. In February the thing he has about Chloe is a
> boy in the language block called Aurel, and he says it at the long table
> on a Tuesday in the middle of something else, as a thing everybody has
> known for a while. By the Thursday it has been said back to her twice.
>
> She has never heard the name in her life. She has which boy it is by
> lunch, off the language-block timetable on the noticeboard, and walks
> past his door twice that afternoon to put a face to it; he is a head
> taller than her and does not look up either time. By March people who
> have never spoken to her have it.

**Reason:** This is the anchor the author names most explicitly: "the crush
rumor was falsely created," and separately, "manufactured rather than
misheard." The old framing ("a thing everybody has known for a while") put
the rumor's origin somewhere outside Iyad, which is exactly the passive,
conduit reading the author is asking to remove. Giving him a real,
trivially small fact (two names on the same sign-up sheet, for a session
neither attended) and showing him turn it into a settled item himself
keeps faith with IYAD.md's "he does not invent, he aims": the raw material
is real, nothing is invented from nothing, but the inference built on top
of it is his, built alone, before either Chloe or Aurel had said a word.
The sign-up sheet detail is planted here so Chloe can trace the rumor to a
dead end later in the same scene, the same way she traced "taught them
backward" to a live source in the previous chapter; the contrast between a
traceable true item and an untraceable one is the tell that this one was
made rather than heard.

**New:**
> Iyad has more than figures. In February he has Aurel's name next to hers
> on the sign-up sheet for an optional Saturday session neither of them
> ends up going to, and he turns it into an item on his own list before
> either of them has said a word to the other about anything. He says it at
> the long table on a Tuesday, in the middle of something else, in the same
> flat voice he reads the rest of his list in, as a thing everybody already
> knows. By the Thursday it has been said back to her twice.
>
> She has never heard the name in her life. She has which boy it is by
> lunch, off the language-block timetable on the noticeboard, and walks
> past his door twice that afternoon to put a face to it; he is a head
> taller than her and does not look up either time. The sign-up sheet is
> still pinned outside the workshop, both their names on it in different
> hands, for a session neither of them went to. By March people who have
> never spoken to her have it.

The confrontation two paragraphs later ("He says he only repeated what he
heard and cannot help what he hears") is left exactly as written. It now
reads as a lie the reader can check against the sign-up sheet rather than
as a plain statement of fact, which is consistent with the book's existing
rule that speakers, not the narrator, are the ones who lie (`DO_NOT_FLAG.md`,
"Unreliable speakers").

## What was left alone, and why

- **The Watch stairwell scene (ch13) and the cipher tell (ch16).** Both are
  Iyad operating in his purely reflexive register (the unattributed return,
  triggered by a table reaching the same subject twice), which his own
  sheet defines as the thing that must never carry awareness. Editing these
  toward deliberateness would homogenize him with the Aurel and
  "backwards" scenes rather than sharpen the distinction the brief asks
  for.
- **The self-defense retelling (ch17), the ledger read-out (ch18), the
  corridor "fiftieth" (ch19), the Caltech geometry claim (ch21), and the
  offer read-out (ch22).** All five already contain a visible, camera-level
  timing or audience choice (a wait for an asker, a flat undifferentiated
  voice, a public hallway, Bex's own dialogue stating "it was true by the
  time I said it," a full-table read-out) and are, in the Caltech case
  particularly, the best-realized instances of intent in the whole
  manuscript. Touching them further risked exactly the flattening the
  brief warns against ("not louder").
- **The essay-slip announcement (ch14) and the admissions-status mention
  (ch21).** Already immediate, public, and either neutral or already
  working per the earlier audit; no rewrite proposed then and none found
  necessary now.
- **Chapters 1 and 2.** Read in full for the Kayleigh/Bryce contrast and
  not touched, per the hard constraint. The contrast holds: at the ordinary
  school the friction is a capability gap policed by peers who cannot do
  what Chloe does; at Halstead nobody has that gap, so the currency shifts
  to whose name the room remembers, which is exactly the shift Bex's and
  Iyad's edited scenes now make visible.
- **Chapters 23, 24, and 30.** The graduation line-up, the scheduling
  email, and the reference-interview mention remain either non-antagonistic
  or, in the email case, already a controlled adult response from Chloe
  that the reading-grade band supports. No change.

## Places the text still resists a fully deliberate reading

- **The corridor "fiftieth" (ch19)** is Bex "trying to help" in the one
  place it costs the most, and her own method (say the true thing, at
  volume, to the widest audience, without checking who is standing there)
  makes it structurally identical to a moment where she is being kind. A
  reader who wants unambiguous malice from her will not find it here, and
  should not: this is not a named anchor and the sheet's whole design is
  that her damage and her generosity share one shape. It counts as
  resistance to a fully hostile reading of Bex, not as a defect.
- **Iyad's confrontation lines** ("I only repeated what I heard") still
  read, on a first pass, as plausible rather than as a caught lie, because
  the book's own rule is that speakers lie without the narration flagging
  it. A reader who has not registered the sign-up-sheet detail two
  paragraphs earlier may take him at his word. This is deliberate (it
  matches the "unreliable speakers" design already ruled in
  `DO_NOT_FLAG.md`) but it means his deliberateness in this scene depends
  on the reader carrying forward a small planted fact rather than on
  anything stated at the point of confrontation.
