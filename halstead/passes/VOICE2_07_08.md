## 1. Re-testing the erosion judgment

Five sentences in these two chapters put something in the subject slot other
than a specific present person, in a spot where the missing person would
matter if this were the ordinary case the house rule is built to catch. The
last pass left all five alone on instinct. Here each is run through the
actual test: is the missing doer a specific person already in this scene, and
does the sentence lose something the reader needs by leaving them out? All
five hold up, but not for the same reason, and one of them turns out to be
better-grounded than the last pass knew.

### chapters/08_the_asking.md, line 241, the load-bearing case

**Sentence:** "It happens without her, somewhere in the last week of
January, so that standing at the sink brushing her teeth she works out that
she hasn't asked in two weeks, and the strawberry magnet on the fridge is
holding up a grocery list now instead of the note."

**Test:** No. There is no missing person to restore, and that is the actual
finding, not a dodge of one. "She stops asking in January" (the sentence one
paragraph earlier, line 239) already gives Chloe the active verb; this
sentence is not covering for a person who could have been named, it is
saying directly that the stopping was not anyone's act, including hers. Three
separate things the rule protects all point the same way at once:

- **Function 2, in its strongest form.** The rule normally excuses an agent
  that is unknown, irrelevant, or obvious (the Army posts soldiers). Here the
  agent is not merely unnamed, it does not exist: nobody decided, including
  Chloe. Shibatani's whole point about agent defocusing (cited in
  `PROSE_PRINCIPLES_STRUCTURE.md`, section 8) is that every language needs a
  way to say "this happened" with no doer attached, because sometimes that is
  the fact. This is that case in its cleanest form.
- **Function 1, thematic progression (Daneš), not just function 2.** The
  sentence right before ends on the rheme "stops asking [in January]." This
  sentence opens with "It," picking that event up as its new theme and
  saying more about it. That is textbook linear progression, not a broken
  topic jump.
- **Function 3.** "Her" is what the whole passage is about: a child losing a
  year-long campaign without ever choosing to stop, found out about it after
  the fact, brushing her teeth. The receiver is the paragraph's subject in
  every sense of that word.

**Verdict: correct as written, on stronger grounds than the last pass gave
it.** Not changed. Not called out, because it is not wrong.

### chapters/07_the_same_room.md, line 164

**Sentence:** "The library card ends up in the drawer in the kitchen where
the batteries are, and weeks later, when her mom asks whether she wants to go
on Saturday, Chloe says no thank you but asks her to renew them please."

**Test:** Same shape as the chapter 8 case, one chapter earlier, and it is
the first appearance of this book's specific technique for the same idea
(nobody decided, it just happened): the library habit, later the reading
itself, later the asking. Nothing in the two sentences before this one
answers who put the card in the drawer, and that is deliberate rather than
an oversight; either Chloe or her mom could have done it, and the sentence
does not care which, because the point is that neither of them chose to stop
going. Function 2 (no relevant agent to restore) and function 3 (the point is
what has happened to the routine, not who moved an object) both hold.

**Verdict: correct as written.** Not changed, not called out.

### chapters/08_the_asking.md, line 203

**Sentence:** "...a boy honking with laughter about a room-shaped object,
standing there afterward with the hallway tile cold enough through her socks
to notice, and being allowed to be wrong for a whole afternoon."

**Test:** The missing agent here is not a specific person, it is Halstead in
general (whichever adult was in the room at any given moment, none of them
named or nameable, all of them behaving the same way). That is function 2's
ordinary case, not its edge case: nobody needs telling which staff member let
her be wrong, because the answer this line is giving is about the place, not
a person in it. Function 3 also holds: this whole line is the answer to "what
was actually different," which makes the environment, not an agent, the
sentence's actual subject.

**Verdict: correct as written.** Not changed, not called out.

### chapters/08_the_asking.md, line 55

**Sentence:** "The questions keep coming in that voice, working down through
who ran it, how they found her, whether Chloe came back different, in what
way, and starting when."

**Test:** Unlike the three above, an agent here is not merely unnamed, she
was named ten words earlier: "Dr. Ammons asks every question of her mother"
(line 45, already fixed by the previous pass). Restating her name a second
time, one exchange later, in the same interview, would be the redundant kind
of naming the given-new contract warns against, not an improvement. And the
theme here ("the questions") is the direct rheme of the sentence before it,
a clean case of linear progression, the same pattern that clears the
chapter 8 flagship case above.

**Verdict: correct as written.** The previous pass flagged this one as an
uncertain call-out worth reporting; on the actual rule it clears, and I am
not carrying the call-out forward.

### chapters/07_the_same_room.md, line 116

**Sentence:** "The first math sheet arrives midmorning, and she squints at
it before she can tell that it's adding with carrying..."

**Test:** This one is not the same fault as the four above, and it is
included here because the last pass grouped it with them and I want to say
plainly that the grouping was wrong. It is not an erosion sentence; nothing
in this scene turns on who handed the sheet out. "Arrives" carries no
implied will the way "refuses" or "stays stuck" (both fixed in the last
pass) do; it is the same neutral, scheduled-object sense as "the mail
arrives." Ms. Vance is on the page two lines earlier and is the obvious,
irrelevant-to-name agent (function 2, plain case, not its edge).

**Verdict: correct as written, and not actually part of the erosion pattern
at all.** Not changed, not called out.

**What this section answers:** the last pass's judgment holds for all five,
but for two different reasons that were previously run together. Three of
the five (241, 164, 203) are the book's genuine agent-defocusing technique,
where no agent exists to restore and the missing doer is the entire point.
One (55) clears on ordinary given-new grounds once Dr. Ammons's prior mention
is counted, not because it is another instance of the erosion device. One
(116) was never the same fault to begin with. None of the five should be
carried into a future pass's list of open questions; testing settled them.

## 2. New call-out: the reverse direction

The brief for this pass named a direction the last one barely looked for:
active voice where an unimportant or invented subject displaces the actual
participants. One instance turned up.

### chapters/08_the_asking.md, line 155

**Current:** "Her mom says put your jacket on and Chloe says no, and her mom
says put it on. Then the fight is happening and her throat hurts before she
has got a few words out of it."

**Why the current voice is wrong:** "The fight" is an abstraction taking an
active verb of pure occurrence, "is happening," at the exact sentence where
two specific, named, present people (her mom, Chloe, both subjects of their
own verbs one clause earlier) stop being individuals and become an event
that simply exists. This is not the erosion device from Section 1: there is
no missing agent here in the sense of "nobody decided," there are two people
who were just doing distinct things (repeating a command, refusing it) and
whom the sentence now declines to keep naming right as one of them starts
raising her voice. The two sentences after this one ("Chloe keeps going
anyway, her voice climbing over it") make clear whose voice is doing the
climbing; this sentence arrives a beat before the text is willing to say so.

**Why it was not auto changed:** Judgment call, not obvious. A fight is a
mutual thing by definition, and "a fight breaks out" (or, here, "is
happening") is ordinary, defensible English for an event with no single
starter; forcing Chloe into the subject slot risks assigning her sole blame
for an escalation that has her mother's repeated "put it on" behind it too.
Nothing in `HOUSE_RULES.md` or `DO_NOT_FLAG.md` blocks a fix here; the
hesitation is about the facts of the scene, not a protected passage.

**Active:** "Then Chloe is shouting before she has got a few words out, and
her throat already hurts."
**Active:** "Then Chloe is shouting back at her, and her throat hurts before
she has got a few words out of it."
**Passive:** "Then voices are raised, and her throat hurts before she has
got a few words out of it." This keeps the sentence's original ambiguity
about who started it (nobody is named, same as now) while trading the
abstract "fight" for a concrete, physical image (voices, throats) closer to
what a camera in the room could actually register; offered as the more
defensible passive-direction option rather than a clear improvement.

## 3. Tally

**Changed: 0.** Nothing in either chapter met the "obvious fix, doer already
on the page, meaning survives" bar this pass; every real candidate either
cleared the actual rule (Section 1) or was ambiguous enough to call out
instead of guess at (Section 2).

**Called out: 1** (Section 2, chapter 8 line 155). The five sentences in
Section 1 are re-tested judgments reported per this pass's specific brief,
not call-outs, since none of them were found to be wrong.

**Most important finding:** the re-test of chapters/08_the_asking.md line
241, "It happens without her." Not because it turned out wrong, but because
the brief asked for the judgment to be tested rather than assumed, and
testing it surfaces a real distinction the last pass did not have: this
sentence and the two others like it (chapter 7 line 164, chapter 8 line 203)
are not merely tolerated exceptions to the passive rule, they are the
positive case the rule is supposed to protect, agent defocusing where no
agent exists, one linear-progression theme chain away from being a textbook
illustration of Daneš's own pattern. The math-sheet sentence (chapter 7 line
116) had been sitting in the project's call-out list next to these for two
passes running under a mistaken family resemblance; it is not the same
fault and should stop being reported as one.
