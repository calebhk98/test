### chapters/03_the_letter.md, line 205 - "her parents" - wrong

**Current:** "On the ninth night Chloe is supposed to be asleep, her parents having sent her back once already, but she comes out a second time in her socks, skipping the fourth stair."

**Why the current voice is wrong:** All three parts of the rule point back
toward the original passive, not away from it.

1. *Topic continuity.* This sentence, and the whole scene around it, tracks
   Chloe: "is supposed to be asleep... but she comes out." The rewrite drops
   a second subject, "her parents," into the opening clause only to abandon
   it one comma later for "she." The original passive participle ("having
   been sent back") took its implied subject from the main clause, so the
   sentence never left Chloe's side. The fix reintroduces exactly the kind
   of topic jump rule 8 exists to prevent, on a sentence that had none.
2. *The agent is obvious/irrelevant.* Which parent walked her back to bed
   the first time is not established anywhere on the page and does not
   matter to the scene: nobody but her parents lives in the house, so it is
   the "Army posts soldiers" case the rule names directly. Nothing the
   reader needs is missing from "having been sent back once."
3. *The receiver is what the sentence is about.* Chloe, not whichever parent
   put her back to bed, is what this whole paragraph is following. A
   receiver-as-topic passive is Gopen and Swan's own textbook case for when
   the passive is the stronger sentence, not a weaker one.

There is a fourth problem layered on top, from `PROSE_PRINCIPLES_CLARITY.md`
section 11's referent check: "her parents" asserts, as a fact, that both of
them together sent her back the first time. Nothing on the page supports a
joint action; for all the reader can tell it was one of them, alone, at some
point off-page. The fix did not just get the voice question backward, it
invented a small fact about the previous night that the manuscript never
establishes.

**Why it was not auto changed:** This is the specific class of edit the task
for this pass asked to be surfaced for the author's judgment rather than
reverted on my own authority, since it involves the same "was naming this
actor correct" question the task named by name (this is one of the two
school/parents changes it pointed at).

**Active:** "On the ninth night Chloe is supposed to be asleep. She already tried this once and one of them walked her back to bed, but she comes out a second time in her socks, skipping the fourth stair."
**Active:** "On the ninth night Chloe is supposed to be asleep. She already came out once and got walked back, but she comes out a second time in her socks, skipping the fourth stair."
**Passive:** "On the ninth night Chloe is supposed to be asleep, having been sent back once already, but she comes out a second time in her socks, skipping the fourth stair." (the original wording, before this line was touched)
**Passive:** "On the ninth night Chloe is supposed to be asleep, sent back once already, but she comes out a second time in her socks, skipping the fourth stair."

### Write-up required: misplaced modifier inside protected dialogue

### chapters/26_the_exercise.md, line 131

**Current:** "The man who took you had been in position since twenty-one
hundred, which is seven hours without moving through the entire drop in
temperature that arrives after midnight, and it took him under two seconds
once you finally got there."

**Why the current voice is wrong:** This is not a rule 8 fault; the clause
is active and the agent (the man in the weeds) is named. It fails a
different, adjacent principle: misplaced modifier
(`PROSE_PRINCIPLES_CLARITY.md`, principle 3). "Moving through the entire
drop in temperature" reads, on a first pass, as one unit, as though a
person could physically move through a temperature drop the way he moves
through a doorway. The intended sense is two separate facts run together
with no comma between them: he did not move, and he held that position
through the whole drop in temperature. A reader hits "without moving
through" as a phrase, then has to re-read once "that arrives after
midnight" makes the literal reading collapse. It is the same shape as a
dangling modifier's cousin, a garden path built out of missing punctuation
rather than a misplaced phrase, but the cost to the reader is the same
stop-and-reparse.

**Why it was not auto changed:** This is the major's dialogue, delivered as
one continuous speech, and house rule 1's dialogue protection (never rewrite
what a character says) covers it regardless of how the sentence would score
on any other measure. It is reported per this pass's brief, which asks for
exactly this: a wrong sentence a house rule keeps me from touching.

**Active:** "which is seven hours without moving, through the entire drop in
temperature that arrives after midnight" - the minimum fix, a single comma,
every word kept.

**Active:** "which is seven hours holding still through the entire drop in
temperature that arrives after midnight" - recasts the negative ("without
moving") into a positive verb, so there is no longer a phrase that can be
misread as one unit in the first place.

**Passive:** "the position was held, without a move out of him, through the
entire drop in temperature that arrives after midnight" - technically
available, but a poor fit: it is more formal than anything else in the same
speech, and the sentence's actual problem is a missing comma, not a wrong
voice, so passive does not address the defect either way. Given because the
format asks for one, not as a recommendation.
