# Scene purpose audit

Lens: what each scene does for the book and what would break if it were cut, as the counterweight to a cut-hunting review.

## Chapter 01: Before

**1a. Mom on the phone ("hand pressed flat against her forehead").** Shows the family: mom as advocate, fighting the school over "the next step". Plants the folder Mrs. Aldana keeps and the idea that the school only documents. Sets mom's temperament (pushes institutions, wants action). Later: mom's advocacy drives the evaluation and every later fight with schools. Cut: the book loses why mom is primed to act. Verdict: useful.

**1b. Worksheet, coffee cup on the car roof, "What was the question?"** Establishes Chloe's pattern recognition, boredom, and drifting attention, and the teacher reading it as inattention. The coffee cup is a small image of her noticing everything while appearing absent. Later: the ADHD-evaluation framing, and the "I don't know" she gives. Cut: the attention misreading loses its first concrete example. Verdict: essential.

**1c. Library card argument ("Then you'd have four").** Shows her logic-lawyering, the adult who answers with a rule, and a small kindness (Thursday cart). Plants her appetite for books and the rule-fairness instinct. Later echoes: she argues rules in many chapters. Cut: loses a charming but redundant demonstration. Verdict: useful (partly duplicates 1d and the recess scene).

**1d. Dinner retelling the train-station book.** Shows her depth of reading and analysis (the locker key), the family ignoring or half-listening, and mom's laugh. Sets the norm of her talking too much and being half-heard. Later: the "talks weird" and masking arc. Verdict: essential (it is the proof of ability).

**1e. Recess tennis-ball game, Kayleigh cheats.** Shows her rule-based sense of fairness failing socially: she cannot see why nobody minds. Introduces Kayleigh Burns. Later: Kayleigh's "You talk weird" in 1f. Verdict: useful; the same job as the library argument but for peers.

**1f. Peanut the hamster / Icarus / "You talk weird."** The pivot: the moment Chloe learns that her natural speech is a social cost. Starts masking. Later: dumbing-down in school across the book. Verdict: essential.

**1g. Clouds / "Water" / Fridays best paper.** Shows the masking mechanism in operation (full answer swapped for short one), and the cost: she writes the minimum. Verdict: essential. Slight overlap with 1f, but it shows the habit forming.

**1h. Conferences and the car ride home.** Teacher's report: strongest reader but "trouble focusing". Parents' confusion; dad's "Do you like school?" and her "It's good." Shows family communication gap and starts the diagnostic thread. Verdict: essential.

**1i. Doctor visit.** Medical gatekeeper says "None of this is medical" and recommends an attention evaluation. Plants the evaluation. Verdict: essential (it is the cause of March 7th).

**1j. Christmas at grandmother's, cousin Dana.** Shows extended family, Chloe's social stumbles ("it's not really a subject"), and the dad/mom friction in the car. Plants dad's distance. Later: dad's role. Cut: loses grandmother and Dana (check later for payoff). Verdict: useful.

**1k. "Chapter four" in reading, book taken.** Teacher punishes advance reading; Chloe concludes the fix is to sit better, so she performs boredom. Mom's joke then her mouth drops: mom sees it. Verdict: essential (completes the masking decision and the breaking point).

**1l. The sticky note, MARCH 7.** Time-marker and setup for Ch. 2. Verdict: essential, very short.

Open setups: Mrs. Aldana's folder; Kayleigh Burns; the "short answer" habit; library/books as refuge; dad quiet; mom's stare of worry.

## Chapter 02: March 7th

**2a. Waiting room (empty fish tank, Spanish poster, NO CELL PHONES sign).** Shows her observational humor and literal reading; dad absent because of work ("saying sorry"). Mom's "I only get to see the outside of you" plants mom's limit as an interpreter of Chloe. Later: dad's absence pattern; mom as the one who shows up. Cut: loses the relief and a family beat, nothing structural. Verdict: useful.

**2b. The testing session with Ben Prentice.** Establishes the full profile: block design fast, verbal reasoning very high, working memory ordinary, processing speed (the marked-shapes sheet) weak. Shows the limit (neat marks, speed) as well as the gift. Introduces the idea of a composite hiding a split. Later: the numbers and the "one big number" logic recurs; Ben's characterization of the speed gap. The coffee-cup story reappears here, told aloud. Cut: the whole diagnostic basis is gone. Verdict: essential. Honest note: it is long, and several subtests are rendered at similar length (blocks, digits, matrices, symbols); this is the cuttable-by-trimming part, but the speed sheet and the "why do people..." questions are load-bearing.

**2c. Overheard conversation, "put it in the folder."** The adult verdict: she is fine, but a good number will not move a school. Plants "gifted program starts in third grade" (two-year gap), the central institutional problem. Later: every school-fight scene. Cut: the book loses its first statement of the problem. Verdict: essential.

**2d. Car, "What's a percentile?" and ice cream.** Shows mom withholding, Chloe choosing not to know, the family hiding information from her kindly. Plants Chloe's wondering about her number. Later: when numbers are finally revealed (check). Verdict: essential.

**2e. "After the appointment, nothing changes."** Shows that the diagnosis changed nothing, and Chloe's rule: finish first, sit still, keep answers short. Sets the normal. Verdict: essential.

**2f. Last day of school, report card on the refrigerator.** Time passing and her isolation ("not one of them has needed to learn hers"). Strawberry magnet replaces the sticky note. Verdict: useful, nearly duplicate of 2e but gives the end-of-year state.

Open setups: Ben Prentice and the written report; gifted program in third grade; what Chloe's number was; Dad's absences; Mrs. Aldana.

## Chapter 03: The Letter

**3a. The envelope and the dinner reaction.** Plants the central mystery: a typed letter addressed to "Miss Chloe Kessler", free month, no one applied. Establishes the parents' differing instincts (mom suspicious, dad open). Chloe wants it because of "Miss", being treated as a person. Later: the letter in her bag (end of ch.), the unexplained list, who pays. Cut: no book. Verdict: essential.

**3b. Calling the district, postmark, neighbors, calling the number.** The investigation: no such school, postmark four states away, Ellie Peterson did not get one, and the rep answers every question instantly ("Nobody has that ready unless people ask it a lot"). Plants "networks", "their list is usually pretty good", over a hundred children. Later: the scouting network and the question of who sources the list (check ch. on the money/file). Establishes mom's investigative skill used again in the endgame. Verdict: essential.

**3c. Website, teachers, mom's search finding nothing.** Dad and the voice jokes (family warmth and relief); the teacher bios (Olympic swimmer, SEAL) establish the school's quality; dad's silence on the bios and "open four years" plants his private worry; mom finds no outside trace of the school. Later: dad's suspicion and the school's lack of any outside footprint. Verdict: essential. The "train station voice" is a family motif (relief and tie to ch.1 dinner).

**3d. Nine days of argument, "It says Miss", overheard stairs talk.** Shows the parents wrestle and decide because Chloe has been "flat since Christmas". Gives the emotional reason for saying yes: they are worried about her. Later: the guilt/justification they carry about sending her. Verdict: essential.

**3e. Library hold, packing, the drive, mom at hotel, dad's last Friday.** Library hold plants her book habit and her bending of rules. Packing and the shoes joke are family warmth. Mom checking the emergency number plants her vigilance. Shows time passing and the departure. Cut: library scene is a third rules-argument with the librarian (duplicate of 1c by now) but ends with a hold, a pattern the book can use. Verdict: useful; the library bit could go without harm, the packing joke and emergency-number check should stay.

Open setups: who wrote the letter and who made the list; "networks"; how the school pays; postmark; school open four years; Ellie Peterson not invited; the envelope in her bag.

## Chapter 04: Pluto

**4a. Arrival, registration, orientation (the "same day" promise).** Establishes the school's competence and its apparent safety rules: any adult will take you home, no arguing. Mom underlines "same day" twice. Plants the policy that is later tested (check Ch. 20-ish onward for whether the exit promise holds). Shows how the outside (parents) sees it. Verdict: essential, the policy is a setup.

**4b. Priya and the horse monologue.** Introduces Priya, her talk-too-much-and-corrects-people trait, and shows kids who are more like Chloe. Chloe lies down with her shoes on: homesick. Later: Priya as roommate and friend (check). Verdict: useful; also gives humor.

**4c. Phone call and hotel nights.** Shows mom's vigilance and promise ("stay right where you are. I'm coming"), and Chloe's reliance on home. Plants the rule that parents live half a mile away, and that Chloe can leave. Later: weaning from the phone (4e), the family-visit rule paying off in later parent scenes. Verdict: essential for mother-daughter bond and for showing the school really lets her go.

**4d. Pluto room name and the "room-shaped object" joke.** The panic response (her body goes to the October memory) and Sam's joke ending it: first belonging. Introduces Sam and Ruth and the Pluto motif: a thing not a planet but still real, i.e. these kids. The Pluto argument ("they just changed the rule for what counts") is a theme statement about categories and labels, which fits the book's concern with gifted labels. Verdict: essential.

**4e. Dining hall, Sam, the pool with a lid, the fourth-night phone not dialed.** Chloe tells Sam "She says I talk weird. So mostly I just keep quiet" (reveals the wound); weaning off the phone; mom leaves on Sunday. Verdict: essential (the emotional turn from homesick to staying).

**4f. Kavi and the volcano.** Introduces Kavi, the silent boy who corrects Sam. Sets the pattern: people here correct each other and survive it. Later: Kavi-Sam friction. Cut: loses Kavi's intro and the humor. Verdict: useful.

**4g. Ruth corrects Mr. Doyle; "Are you in trouble?"** Chloe watches for the punishment that does not come: tests the new normal. Teacher thanks Ruth for the correction. Establishes the school's culture of being right being fine. Later: Chloe slowly unlearning masking. Verdict: essential.

Open setups: same-day-home promise; Chloe's masking; Pluto group (Sam, Ruth, Kavi, Priya, Owen); Owen's worksheet under the table (copying, worry); mom at the hotel; teacher Mr. Doyle.

## Chapter 05: Behind

**5a. Speed of the room; the timed writing prompt.** Shows what the setting feels like: no empty stretches, Chloe finally has material to work at pace; shows her slow-writing limit (timer callback to Ben's speed test) and a real limit: Owen produces two sentences. Marisol introduced. Sets the sense that being not-fastest is new. Later: Owen leaves; her writing as limit. Verdict: essential.

**5b. The bridge build (Sam's flat-and-wide vs Ruth's triangles).** Establishes the group's dynamic: Ruth is right and unheard, Sam is confident and wrong, Chloe sees Ruth might be right and defers ("Okay"). Shows a failure caused by Chloe's deference, and a prediction habit (writes a low number; "nobody let me"). Failure analysis ("every corner of a square carries its angle alone") shows learning from failure. Later: Chloe's habit of silently knowing and not speaking, and mistakes explained after the fact; the triangle idea likely recurs (bridge/structure motif). Verdict: essential; also first demonstration of group-intelligence failing for social reasons.

**5c. Mr. Baptiste and dividing fractions; Chloe's night derivation.** Her first real "behind": she can do the procedure but not the why; the conversation moves past her question; she works it out alone at night with a drawn rectangle ("Six."). Shows the book's definition of intelligence as understanding not speed. Chloe shared it with Sam in the hall. Later: callbacks to the "why does it work" instinct. Verdict: essential.

**5d. Swimming with Deb.** Shows she is behind physically (can't swim) plus Sam's competitiveness; Ruth's face when she swims the length. Plants pool (website, dad's "look at the pool") payoff. Later: swimming may recur. Cut: loses a small payoff of the website and Ruth's vulnerability. Verdict: useful.

**5e. Owen goes home; "wasn't having fun."** First departure; Chloe cannot fit "it's fun" with "he left"; Ruth blunt about the crying. Introduces the pattern of kids leaving (doors open, mattresses stripped) and the school honoring its exit promise. Plants questions about who leaves and why. Later: departures; the offer/pressure; Chloe's perspective on weak and strong. Verdict: essential.

**5f. Moon is a liar / tidally locked at dinner.** The group's banter, now Chloe wins speech; she checks for damage and the sentence "comes back clean". Marks the end of her self-censoring in this setting. Verdict: essential for tone and her arc; partly duplicates the Pluto debate (4d) in form but shows progress.

Open setups: Ruth's triangle idea; Owen and later departures; Chloe's hot ears when conversation passes her over; Marisol; Mr. Baptiste; the "packets belong to whoever works in them" culture.

## Chapter 06: The List

**6a. Parent hotel breakfast.** The outside view: parents who also do not know where the list came from ("a list is a good list ... at what"), bus is a rumor, phishing fears. Shows that the mystery is shared, and that adults go quiet and drift away from the hard question. Plants the unanswered "who made the list" and dad's silence. Later: the list question in the money/file chapters. Verdict: essential, only scene where parents compare notes (though check for a duplicate in later parent-gathering scenes).

**6b. Last-night dinner; Chloe tells dad everything.** Recap of the month (Gary the CPR dummy, bridge, fractions on a napkin, Spanish, signing, language puzzle, wood shop, computers/server room, chess, karate). Plants a pile of skills for later: CPR, pressure on a bleed, glue for cuts, cooking, signing, Spanish, the cold server room, the robot arm. Honest note: she repeats the bridge and fractions (already shown in ch.5) and the wood shop is repeated on purpose as a tic. That recap duplication is the one stretch that could be shortened, but the skills list is a setup bank and the napkin explanation is where dad sees what Chloe actually is ("the rightness belongs to him and not to you"). Dad pockets the napkin. Verdict: essential, but trimmable.

**6c. Hall outside Pluto: the fall "day where they look at you."** Plants the in-fall evaluation, the group's plan to all get in, Kavi's parents' interest, Ruth's weekly letters, and Chloe's calculation of the four-hour drive. Chloe's "I just have to work it out... how hard can that be" (parents will say yes) is irony for what follows. Later: the fall test day; Ruth's letters; the group staying together. Verdict: essential.

**6d. Departure morning (hair tie, handshake with Kavi).** Relationship beats: Priya hair tie, Kavi's palm. Verdict: useful.

**6e. Car home; dad calls mom, "what's the name for that."** Dad reports what the month produced; mom: "Four hours away, Dave, and free". Parents share the unease and amazement: "all three" (four hours, free, they wrote to her). Chloe fakes sleep: she listens in. Plants parents' concern and that the question of what this place is persists. Verdict: essential.

Open setups: fall evaluation day ("a whole different day, with different people doing the looking"); Ruth's weekly letters; the list and its source; the CPR skill (Gary), bleed pressure, glue; the server room; the napkin in dad's pocket; Ruth/Sam/Kavi/Chloe pact.

## Chapter 07: The Same Room

**7a. Goodbyes at the loop; Ruth at the flagpole ("promise me you'll make it in").** Ruth extracts a promise; Chloe promises to ask every day. Sets up the group pact and gives Chloe a binding obligation. Later: Chloe's daily asking; Ruth as the one who needs her. Verdict: essential.

**7b. No last names; mom calls the school to pass the number along.** Plants a major fact: the kids know only first names, and the school will not give contact info, only relay. Establishes the school's control of contact between families (a design, and a worry). Chloe cries about it. Later: waiting for a call that may not come; later chapters on contact, letters from Ruth (check whether the letters ever arrive). Verdict: essential. Also tender family beat (mom's hand on her head).

**7c. Waiting for the phone.** Time passing and Chloe's longing; the school says it sent the message to "all three numbers". Plants the unreturned call, a thread to track. Verdict: useful (conveys duration of silence); also sets up how isolated she is.

**7d. "Can I go there for the year?" in the car.** Moves plot: the question is asked. Short. Verdict: essential.

**7e. Library return, retelling to the librarian.** Third retelling of the month (after dad and the hotel). The librarian is the one adult who responds with rule plus warmth ("That's a real month, that is"); contrasts with the later book-abandoning scene. Honest note: the retelling duplicates 6b nearly word for word (Gary, bridge corner, fractions); that duplication is on purpose to show how she keeps retelling, but it is dead weight in volume. Verdict: useful, trimmable. The ending ("Chloe takes her four off the cart") sets up the reading drop in 7h.

**7f. Second grade, Ms. Vance, the group.** Establishes the return to the old normal: same kids (Bryce, Kayleigh, Aiden R.), repeated first-grade math. Sets the contrast with camp. Verdict: essential.

**7g. The time capsule.** Chloe says something thoughtful ("mailing something to a stranger"), is mocked by Kayleigh and Bryce, hides her page, and rewrites to "I hope you like fifth grade." Plants the time capsule: a box opened in fifth grade (check payoff). Reinforces the peer-wound. Verdict: essential (setup plus emotional beat).

**7h. Library books unfinished; card in battery drawer.** Shows decline: she stops reading, the central loss of the pre-Halstead year. Callback to the battery drawer in ch.1 (locker key at aunt's house with batteries). Verdict: essential (visible symptom of the cost).

**7i. Asking every day; tally on the back of a worksheet.** Time passing, pressure on parents, Chloe's tally (column of marks). Plants the tally; later payoff? (check). Verdict: useful; could be trimmed but sets the tally motif.

**7j. Mom calls Chloe's school about acceleration.** Shows the institutional wall in detail: third grade is the floor, testing window is spring only, program starts the fall after. Mom's two pleases. Pays off ch.2's "two years until the lever". Verdict: essential: it is what makes Halstead the only option.

**7k. Chloe cries at her desk in October.** The breakdown: unexplained crying. Ms. Vance is kind. Chapter ends on it (the cry that becomes the pivot for the parents). Verdict: essential.

Open setups: pact with Ruth; the families' callback; tally marks; time capsule; Ms. Vance; the kitchen battery drawer; the fall test day at Halstead; Chloe's reading collapse.

## Chapter 08: The Asking

**8a. Dr. Ammons: depression, "Did anything happen to her there?"** Adult institutional response reads Chloe's collapse as depression and probes the camp as a possible cause (same adults the whole time, overnight, far away). Chloe answers "It was hard." Introduces a clinical outsider's mild suspicion of Halstead (adult safety concerns). Plants weekly therapy and mom's "I said I'd ask you first." Also mom's anger at the implication. Later: Ammons may recur (check). Verdict: essential: outside view of the school plus the depression thread.

**8b. Mrs. Prahl's Thursday program (knights and knaves).** Shows the school's tool: an hour a week of enrichment; the puzzle is too easy; "When does it get harder?" Chloe is waiting for the turn that camp provided. Plants Mrs. Prahl (who also kept the bridge on the windowsill by Chloe's retelling in ch.7: possible continuity glitch, the name Prahl appears in 7e as camp teacher; flag). Introduces Dev and Hana. Shows the fast group is still too slow; she keeps the packet to herself. Verdict: essential: it shows what the ordinary school's best offer is.

**8c. The jacket fight ("I hate you so much").** The kitchen blowup; mom reveals she lies awake "every night since the letter came". Most honest mother-daughter fight. Later: reconciliation silently (the bread and salt). Verdict: essential.

**8d. "Nobody was mean."** The definition of what Halstead was to Chloe; Bryce thinks she talks weird; "That isn't bullying. It's only true." Mom notices and misreads ("you'll make friends"). Verdict: essential: the book's emotional thesis for Halstead's pull.

**8e. The bike on Christmas; Grandma and Dana again.** The parents try to buy relief; Chloe hid knowing about the bike. Dana scene is a repeat of the Christmas scene in ch.1, deliberately: same conversation, now she gives shorter answers. Verdict: bike useful, Dana repeat is duplicate-by-design (could go if trimming; but works as a "year later nothing changed" mirror).

**8f. She stops asking; the envelope under socks.** Time passing; the parents see Thursdays "helping"; dad says "yeah, all right" (change of heart begins). Envelope kept: motif. Verdict: essential (sets up the parents' turn).

Open setups: Ammons therapy; Mrs. Prahl, Dev, Hana; the envelope; dad's "yeah, all right"; strawberry magnet motif; bike; mom's "Every night since the letter came."
Continuity flag: Mrs. Prahl is named in ch.7 as holding the bridge on the windowsill (camp) and here as the gifted-pull-out teacher at Chloe's school. Possibly a deliberate echo or an error; the Chloe retelling in 7e sounds like she is telling the librarian about camp, not Mrs. Prahl. Check.

## Chapter 09: February

**9a. February: the sheet, the back seat at lunch, plant diagram.** Shows the flat depressive routine and her coloring slowly inside the lines. Time passing. Verdict: useful; the plant-diagram coloring is a small image of slowing herself down on purpose.

**9b. The floor between bed and wall: "Take my birthday."** The emotional low: she bargains everything (dishes, bike, Christmas, birthday) to be let go to Halstead. Dad stands in the kitchen with the dishcloth. Later: she returns the bike (9d), the "deal" logic. Cut: the book loses its single most direct statement of her need and the justification for the parents' yes. Verdict: essential.

**9c. Parents' kitchen-table argument.** "What kind of family sends a seven-year-old away to live in a building four hours from her mother?" Mom needs dad to say it aloud; dad has always thought yes. Reveals the marriage's pattern (mom speaks, dad stands in doorways). Plants mom's guilt for the whole rest of the book. Verdict: essential.

**9d. March 14: "We're saying yes." The bike returned; "it was a deal."** Payoff of the asking arc and the 8f hint. Chloe's attempt to honor the deal; parents refuse the deal frame ("You cannot buy this off us"). Plants Chloe's transactional thinking (what do I owe) that probably recurs. Also dad had already sent the form in secret. Verdict: essential.

**9e. The house offer.** Halstead offers a free furnished house minutes from campus, contingent on enrollment. Mom refuses: "they are not getting the roof over her head as well"; dad wants it ("every day of the week instead of ten weeks a year"). Major plant: the company-house lever (leaving costs shelter), parents' split, and mom's instinct about control. Later: chapters 22 (the offer) and 31/32 (the money) must pay this. Cut: breaks the marriage subplot and the later money/offer logic. Verdict: essential.

**9f. April visit: Mrs. Okonkwo, the gray-bearded man, no test.** Introduces Mrs. Okonkwo (admissions-like guide) who will not say who is enrolled, who denies Ruth's "fall evaluation" claim ("Somebody said something to her in a corridor"), and says rooms are named after planets since the building went up. The bearded man asking "what would you do with an entire day" is the actual evaluation: a test that is not a test. Later: Chloe's belief about how admission works; school information control; Ruth's misinformation. Verdict: essential.

**9g. The drive home; mom still in the car.** Closes on mom unable to leave the car: her fear. Verdict: essential: ends the chapter on the cost.

Open setups: house offer; mom's refusal; dad's yes; the evaluation (the day and what it measured); Mrs. Okonkwo; "Halstead will take a student in any month"; Ruth's misinformation; Ammons therapy winding down; dad reads contracts for a living (skill for later contract scene).

**10i. End of term: rocks into shoebox, summer goodbye, notebook.** Closes with Ruth's summer, Wednesday call plan. Plants the notebook of three names and numbers. Verdict: useful.

## Chapter 11: Eight

**11a. Summer at home: bike, talking, hot dog debate, Spanish with mom, curfew fight, grandmother's "she's put on weight."** Shows Chloe healed and loud at home; parents' relief; mom's laughing; the curfew fight where Chloe pre-loads an argument and "always stops the moment she knows" (mom's belief that Chloe never fights her: fits to later). The grandmother's remark confirms she had been thin at Christmas. Plants the family's costs (counting the days). Honest note: this is a long, warm section with three or four similar exchanges (hot dog, Spanish, curfew, grandma). Relief and family: the book needs a "before-it-goes-wrong" family warmth sequence at least once; the hot dog and curfew are the strongest. Verdict: useful, trimmable. Hint of later: Mom saying Chloe has "never fought me, not once".

**11b. September: Margie; Spanish becomes self-directed; logic; politics (leaders' families and who they owe).** Plants the school's pedagogy of ownership ("it carries on being yours") and the politics class: learning networks of who owes whom. This is an in-world skill with thematic payoff: tracing relationships and money behind institutions (check: Ch.29 file/32 money). Verdict: essential (politics class), useful (Margie, Spanish shelf).

**11c. Nerf (Coach Bell, "watch the entire flight").** Introduces the school's Nerf game: both sides get good at the same rate; "the easiest thing in this building to hit". Skill and a recurring structure that later chapters probably use (the Nerf finals, Chloe's skills). Verdict: essential if Nerf recurs; check.

**11d. Afternoon block: wood shop mallet (oval), team sports (no passing), drawing hands, ninetieth of ninety.** Shows limits and failures: she ranks last of ninety in dance and drawing at end of term, reads it, keeps going. Plants block sheets and Mrs. Okonkwo as the registrar. Verdict: essential (first time Chloe is bad at things and lives; also the posting of the sheet).

**11e. Mrs. Okonkwo: "No." Cannot drop dance or change team.** Shows the school's rule inflexibility: some things the school will not bend on. Plants Mrs. Okonkwo as the one who says no (later, the exit or offer?). Verdict: essential, because it shows the school's control and sets a baseline for when it does bend.

**11f. Electives list; Sam climbing; choir, flat; apple-throwing at the drainpipe (bracket rule).** Friend group time; the bracket rule is a late-added rule joked and carried into the summer (echoes the Pluto "they just changed the rule" theme and the one-bounce rule in ch.1, rule applied selectively). Sam's overhang plant. Verdict: useful; the apples are relief, and the bracket rule is a thematic echo; the choir bit is the most cuttable.

**11g. June notebook: ahead in three subjects.** Pays off the April list (10d, behind in four). Verdict: essential, one paragraph.

Open setups: Sam's overhang goal; Coach Bell and Nerf; the ranked block sheet (ninety names); Mrs. Okonkwo saying no; mom's belief about Chloe not fighting; politics wall; Margie.

## Chapter 12: Nine

**12a. The sheet gets one more line; Kavi's three denied electives.** The school's schedule is age-gated and imposed: "Programming is on every ten-year-old's sheet", cryptography at thirteen.
