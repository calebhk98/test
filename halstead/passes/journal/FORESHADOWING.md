# Foreshadowing and prediction read

Lens: a first-time reader predicting what happens next, scoring each prediction as the book answers it.

## Chapter 01: Before

No earlier predictions to score.

Clues I noticed:
- Chloe's "full answer" gets swapped for "the smaller one" to fit in. Sets up masking, and probably a place where she is finally allowed the full answer.
- "Clouds are the ocean going somewhere else": her own idea of what she is, a thing that goes elsewhere.
- Mom says "all you are giving me is something that already exists." School has nothing to offer her. Sets up a program that does.
- The train-station book: a ticket, a locker key nobody put in the bowl. Likely a mirror for the siblings or for a later "who keeps the key" motif.
- Dad "keeps his eyes on his plate" and makes "his usual noise." Something is off in the parents' marriage or in Dad's view of her.
- The sticky note: "MARCH 7, a time, and a name."

Predictions:
- Next chapter is the March 7 evaluation. Specific: she will be tested by a psychologist and will either hide or show everything. 70% she performs well, and the result is "not ADHD, something else" (giftedness). 
- Someone will notice she is far above grade level and a recruiter or program will follow. Halstead is probably a school for gifted kids. Confidence 60%.
- Her parents will split on what to do. Dad skeptical, Mom pushing. 55%.

Theory of what Halstead is: a selective school or program for very bright kids, probably with a darker side. Confidence low, 30%. At this point it is only a name I have not seen.

## Chapter 02: March 7th

Scoring:
- Evaluation happens: right. Result "not ADHD, extremely bright, undersold by the number": right, and nearly spoon-fed, since Ben says the ceiling was hit outright.
- Parents split: partly. Dad is absent from the appointment ("tried to move two things and managed neither"), which is a small version of it.
- A program recruiting her: not yet. But the gifted program "starts in third grade" is "the only lever the school has for her." That is a plain setup.

Clues:
- "A bad number is the kind that gets a school to do something, and hers is going to read as good." The system fails kids who are too far off the curve. Thematic engine for the book.
- "there is nothing above that on the page for me to give her." Tests have a ceiling. Someone will eventually build a test without one.
- The lesson she learns: "finish first, sit still, keep your answers short." Masking becomes a rule.
- The empty fish tank, and the Spanish poster she read by accident. Probably texture, maybe a hint that she notices what others miss.
- "They're the covers for the ends of you." Odd image, maybe just voice.

Predictions:
- Next chapter is a letter (title 03). I predict it comes from a program, addressed to her parents, after Ben's report or a test score is passed along. 65%.
- Her parents will be torn by the prestige and the cost, and Dad will be the wary one. 55%.
- The report will go to the school and be filed. 80% it is mentioned again.

Theory: Halstead is a program for extreme outliers, a boarding school or residential thing. Someone is collecting kids at the ceiling of the tests. 40%.

## Chapter 03: The Letter

Scoring:
- Letter from a program: right. Halstead is a free summer session for gifted kids: right, and I guessed the shape. The "who pays" question is raised openly, so the book is asking for the puzzle.
- Dad wary: wrong, mostly. Mom is the suspicious one, Dad is the one who quietly says go. Dad studied the teacher photos obsessively, so he is hiding some worry or interest.
- Parents argue: right.

Clues (heavy):
- "there is no school by that name working with the district in any capacity." Official invisibility.
- Nobody else got a letter; "the one child on the street anybody would put on a list" did not. The list is not teacher-based. Someone sees the kids another way.
- "their list is usually pretty good" and "networks." Scouts. Someone is testing kids from somewhere.
- Every question "answered straight away, as though the questions had all been expected." Scripted, rehearsed. The call centre has done this many times.
- "no news coverage... not one person anywhere who has been there to say a word about it afterward." No alumni. Strong: kids who go do not talk, or do not come back to the normal world.
- Open four years, a "website that hasn't been touched." "very old money or very careless."
- Teachers: Olympian, SEAL, two master's degrees. Overqualified. A SEAL teaching nine-year-olds suggests security or something else.
- The postmark: a city four states off, dated eleven days before. Planted. Probably pays off.
- "Miss Chloe Kessler": it treats her as a person. That is the hook.
- The different man answering the emergency number each time, and the front desk at midnight.
- Her mom: "a building I have yet to see." "They have never once not filled it."
- Dad: "has been open four years" but the letter says it runs "every year." Minor inconsistency in my head: maybe it is more than a summer program.

Predictions:
- The summer is wonderful for Chloe, the first place she is not alone. 80%.
- There will be something slightly off that only a reader would catch, such as a rule about not telling, no phones, or kids who are "regulars." 55%.
- She will make her first real friend there, and that friend will matter late in the book. 60%.
- Her parents will eventually learn something that makes the "free" meaning clear. 70%.

Theory: Halstead is a funded program that recruits and tracks outliers, perhaps for a government or a private interest, using the summer session as a long audition. Payer unknown, maybe a foundation or the government. Confidence 40% on the shape, 10% on the payer.
