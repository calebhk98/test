## Instance 1

**13_ten_pages.md:132, Chloe is ten.**

> Twenty minutes later Iyad has it at the far end of the table, pitched for both
> benches, with a phrase of his own on the front of it: the bit about the year
> still in it, the north stairwell off the bottom of the sheet, and Chloe's name
> on the end of it, standing in it while it went uncovered. The far end laughs
> and takes up both lines at once, and is still on them when the plates come
> back. Chloe finishes what is in front of her without looking up, and carries
> her tray down the far side of the hall.

**Shape 2, receiving a scene**, with a touch of shape 1. Iyad turns a drill
write-up into a joke with her name on it and nothing changes in her. She finishes
eating, she leaves by a different route. No decision gets made, no one hears
about it.

**What she does now:** eats without looking up, walks out the other way.
**What a ten-year-old would do:** this is the youngest chapter in the span, and
the flattest reaction in it, which is backward - a ten-year-old mocked in front
of both benches goes red and stops eating, and either says something or tells
somebody the second she's clear of the hall. The fifteen-year-old two chapters
away manages a direct confrontation; the ten-year-old here manages less than the
paintball scene forty pages earlier in the same chapter, where she already tells
Ruth.

**Proposed:**

> Her fork stops over the plate. She carries the tray out the far door and finds
> Ruth on the step, and tells her the whole of what Iyad said, both lines,
> before the door has finished swinging.

## Instance 2

**14_sixty_degrees.md:81-84, Chloe is eleven.**

> The hour after that opens with it. The teacher has the numbers disagreeing on
> the board before anybody has sat down, and says that the disagreement is where
> the interest is, but the arithmetic is not. Bex brought it to him after dinner
> on the Thursday, and he goes at it for the rest of the hour. Bex turns round
> in her seat to say to the row behind her how good it is, at the volume she
> says everything at, and twists further round to find Chloe's row before she
> gets to the end of the sentence.
>
> Chloe has her own pages in the bag under the desk and leaves them in it, and
> does not turn round to see whether Bex found her. After that she does her
> turning over on the walk down to the field.

**Shape 3, declining**, once but flatly: Bex is looking for her, mid-sentence,
and Chloe chooses not to be found. There is no physical beat attached to the
choice and nothing comes of it; the thinking she does afterward is about the
astronomy question, not about Bex.

Chapter 14 is the only chapter of the four with rebuilt Bex/Iyad material that
has no beat matching the standard anywhere in it - this is its only one, and it
misses.

**What she does now:** keeps her bag shut, doesn't turn round, thinks about
something else on the walk to the field.
**What an eleven-year-old would do:** feel it in her ears and do something
petty and immediate about not being scooped a second time.

**Proposed:**

> Chloe keeps her bag zipped under the desk. She does not turn round to see
> whether Bex found her, and her ears go warm for the rest of the hour anyway.
> On the way out she catches Kavi in the corridor and gives him both numbers
> herself, out loud, before Bex is through the door.

## Instance 3

**14_sixty_degrees.md:221, Chloe is eleven.**

> Number seven says that at no point in the two hours did anybody establish how
> many adults were in the building, and she stands in front of that one with
> her arms crossed until somebody behind her needs the space, because she spent
> those hours on a landing while the number of teachers upstairs went

**Shape 2, receiving a scene.** She reads the failure, recognizes it names her
own two hours, and the beat ends with someone needing to get past her. It is
still on her mind at dinner three lines later, but nothing gets done with it -
the topic changes to Sam's bannister instead.

**What she does now:** stands and reads it, moves aside.
**What an eleven-year-old would do:** take the unfairness of it to somebody
specific rather than sit with it through a whole dinner.

**Proposed:**

> She stands in front of it with her arms crossed until somebody behind her
> needs the space, then finds Ruth on the stairs and makes her read it again,
> slower, and asks her whose job the counting was ever supposed to be.

## Instance 4

**17_fourteen.md:55-56, Chloe is fourteen.**

> By the week after, the round the year has is a round Bex called from the edge
> of the mat. Chloe stands at the sink with her hands under the water and lets
> it run, and says nothing to Ruth about it either, that night or the nights
> after.

**Shape 3, declining, repeatedly.** This is the clearest instance in the span,
because the same chapter shows her doing the opposite thing eleven lines later:
"Who told you?" to a stranger at the sinks, out loud, about a rumour that isn't
even about a fight she was in. Here, about her own fifty seconds, with Ruth
right there, she says nothing "that night or the nights after" - the text
states the non-attempt twice, across more than one night, which is exactly the
habit the brief says a book stops noticing.

**What she does now:** lets the tap run, says nothing to Ruth, then or later.
**What a fourteen-year-old would do:** she's already shown, in this chapter, that
she can walk up to someone and ask a direct question. The gap is the withheld
version of the same thing, aimed at a friend rather than a stranger.

**Proposed:**

> Chloe stands at the sink with her hands under the water and lets it run. She
> finds Ruth at breakfast and tells her the round again herself, the fifty
> seconds and the knee, in her own order this time, before anybody else gets to
> it first.

## Instance 5

> Iyad reads the page out at the long table in the week Hark has the ledger up

**Shape 2, receiving a scene.** Iyad reads her number aloud in a mocking voice

**What a fifteen-year-old would do:** given who she is by now (she out-argues
Hark about the loan two paragraphs later), turn the joke into the numbers

**Proposed:**

> Iyad reads the page out at the long table in the week Hark has the ledger up

> pudding and says it in the voice he said Rustem's in. Chloe finishes the

> it in, what Rustem's number was going to look like against what he'd actually
> risked to get it. Sam asks him what his own came to, and he tells him that as
> well.

## Instance 6 (lower priority)

**18_fifteen.md:105-107, Chloe is fifteen.**

> On each of the next Thursdays she sits on that mat after the room empties,
> running the last twenty seconds of Odile's round again, and every time she
> gets to the end of it with the identical twenty seconds and no new reason for
> them.

**Shape 1, auditing instead of reacting**, and repeated across weeks with an
admitted null result each time. This one isn't a humiliation beat - it's
watching a friend struggle - so it's a smaller fix, but the shape is the
textbook one: she goes back over something the same way, over and over, and the
book states outright that nothing comes of it.

**Proposed:**

> On each of the next Thursdays she sits on that mat after the room empties,
> running the last twenty seconds of Odile's round again. By the fourth
> Thursday she stops running it in her head and asks Odile straight what the
> room sounded like from the floor, since that's the one part of it she
> doesn't have.

## Totals and answers

**Instances found: 6** (5 full, 1 lower-priority) across the six chapters.

**Worst chapter: 14 (Sixty Degrees), age eleven.** It is the only one of the
four chapters with rebuilt Bex/Iyad material that has no beat anywhere in it
matching the fixed standard - its one Bex scene (Instance 2) and its one
drill-failure scene (Instance 3) both go unanswered, and it is also the chapter
where the flattest reaction sits at the youngest end of the span next to the
oldest, most composed one (ch13's Iyad scene, one chapter earlier, at least gets
a change of route).

**Initiates-a-scene ratio:** counting bounded interpersonal scenes across the
six chapters (roughly eighty-five of them, using the section breaks and the
distinct incidents inside each section), Chloe sets a scene in motion in about
fifteen of them - close to **one in six**. The clearest cases where she does
start it: the paintball insight in 13, volunteering as the decoy in 15, catching
her own teaching error and correcting the class in 17, the sink confrontation in
17, going to the range office for the ninety cards and then to Voss with them in
18, and stopping Iyad at the table in 18. Everywhere else - drills, essays,
Watches, dinners, her parents' questions - the scene is set up by a teacher, a
sibling-aged peer, a scheduled event, or a parent, and she answers it. The ratio
holds fairly flat across the span; it does not visibly improve by fifteen, it is
only that the things she does within someone else's scene get more direct.

**Three to fix first:**

1. **Instance 4 (17:55-56).** The clearest failure in the span, because the
   same chapter proves she can do the fix eleven lines later and simply doesn't
   do it here, with a friend, about her own fifty seconds, across more than one
   night.
2. **Instance 2 (14:81-84).** The one chapter of the four rebuilt this week
   that still has nothing landing the standard; fixing this closes that gap.
3. **Instance 5 (18:121).** The only instance in the span with no reaction
   recorded at all, at the oldest and most capable end of the age range, right
   next to a scene that shows exactly what she's capable of doing instead.
