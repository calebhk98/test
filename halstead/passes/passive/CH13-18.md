# Passivity pass, chapters 13-18

Span: 13_ten_pages.md (age 10), 14_sixty_degrees.md (age 11), 15_twelve.md (age 12),
16_thirteen.md (age 13), 17_fourteen.md (age 14), 18_fifteen.md (age 15).

**Revision note.** The brief changed after the first delivery: the stillness exemption
is gone, and no scene in the book gets held up as a standard, including the two named
ones and the two I had personally cleared. This file now carries three tiers:

1. The original six, unchanged.
2. Instances found by going back over the span for exactly the material the old
   exemption told me to skip: watching, waiting, declining, holding back, choosing
   silence, or registering something accurately and moving on.
3. The three scenes I previously left unflagged as meeting a standard, re-judged from
   first principles (want visible before the scene resolves; she does something to get
   it; the attempt costs, fails, or creates the next problem; the scene ends somewhere
   different because of what she did). All three fail at least one leg of that test and
   are now instances rather than models.

Still out of scope, per the corrected brief: a character genuinely not in the scene
(Sam's dating gossip in 17, Ruth's synth debugging in 17, the tail of the parents'
argument in 17 once Chloe stops being spoken to), and reading a group chat without
replying. Ch15 line 197 (Ruth reading the vial) and ch16 lines 15-51 (the Marek grading
scene) stay out of this file on the original task's own instruction, which is separate
from the brief and wasn't touched by the correction. The over-answer beats in 14 and 18
stay out for the same reason - they are long-account-for-long-account exchanges, not
stillness.

**Total instances: 21.** Six original, twelve found under the restored stillness
standard, three re-judged out of "standard" and into "instance."

## Tier 3: re-judged, no longer a standard

Both scenes the brief named, and both scenes I personally cleared in the first
pass, fail the same test once it's applied instead of assumed: a visible want,
an attempt that costs or fails or creates the next problem, and a scene that
ends somewhere different because of what she did.
