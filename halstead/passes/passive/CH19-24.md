## Instance 1

**File:line:** chapters/21_the_applications.md:143, 151, 153, 155-163
**Chloe's age:** sixteen, into seventeen (October 2022-March 2023)

**Passage (exact):**

> Chloe's fork stops on the way up. The representative writes it into the notebook he has had out since Monday, then asks Bex what she would do differently, and Bex tells him, watching him write it down, and what she tells him is what is written on the back of the April drawing. She stays at the table after he has moved down it to Sam, telling the two who arrived late enough to have missed the first telling.

> The drawing lives in a folder Nadia keeps. Chloe gets it out that evening and reads her own working off it, looking for the Thursday she carried it in to breakfast, and whether Bex was at that table, and Bex was at that table, and at that point Chloe was still saying the thing out loud.

> She does not go and find the representative to say so. She gets as far as the corridor outside the reading room, once, and turns round.

> She calls home that Sunday and gets most of the way through the week before she gets to it, near the end and folded in with everything else: that a rep asked about the long field and Bex answered for all of it, the geometry included, and got every part of it right.
>
> "Well, did she get it right?" her mother says.
>
> "She got every part of it right. That's not the part I called about."
>
> "Then what's the part?"
>
> Chloe doesn't have a short answer for that one, and the line stays quiet long enough that her mother asks if she's still there.

**Which shape, and why:** Stacks three of the five. Shape 1 (auditing instead
of reacting) in the evening with the drawing; shape 3 (declining, and the
text names the decline: "does not go") at the corridor; shape 2 (receiving a
scene) on the phone, where the scene's whole content is that she cannot say
what is wrong and the call just sits there until it ends. The credit taken
is for the one thing in the chapter that is unambiguously hers, the geometry
on the drawing, and across four separate opportunities to do something
about it, spread over most of a week, she does nothing that costs her
anything or costs Bex anything. The chapter even tells us she got close
once, in the corridor, and had it undone by nothing external, only by
turning around.

**What she does now:** Confirms the injustice privately against a folder
only she looks in, walks to where she could raise it and walks back, then
on the one call where an adult asks her directly what is bothering her, she
cannot produce the sentence.

**What a child that age would do:** A sixteen-year-old who just watched
somebody else get credited, in front of a college representative, for the
one piece of work she can prove is hers, does not sit on it for a week. She
says something wrong to Bex at the next meal and has to live with having
said it. She corners her once, gets a bad answer back, and has nothing to
do with the bad answer except carry it. She does not need to win the
exchange. She needs to have had it.

**Proposed:**

> The drawing lives in a folder Nadia keeps. Chloe gets it out that evening,
> finds the Thursday she carried it in to breakfast, and finds Bex at that
> table too, saying nothing, while Chloe was the one talking. She closes the
> folder and sets it back on the shelf, corner squared to the edge, the way
> she'd leave a tool she meant to pick up again.
>
> At breakfast the next morning she sits across from Bex instead of down the
> table, with Sam still getting his tray settled beside her.
>
> "You told him you did the geometry."
>
> Bex doesn't stop with her fork. "I did do the geometry. Eventually."
>
> "I drew it on the back of an exam script in April. You were at the table
> when I did it."
>
> "And I'm the one who said it out loud on Tuesday, in a room with somebody
> writing it down. That's the version he's got now."
>
> Chloe doesn't have anything to put next to that. She eats the rest of
> breakfast standing at the counter, tray in one hand, and doesn't go
> looking for the representative afterward either.

## Instance 2

**File:line:** chapters/24_the_chat.md:489, 491-501, 503
**Chloe's age:** eighteen (September 2023)

**Passage (exact):**

> Days pass before Kavi comes back with anything. When he does, he says he asked and was told it's internal. Internal how, Chloe asks. That's the word they used, is all Kavi has for her. He asked about the formulation too, and got that word for it as well.
>

>
> kavi: i know
>
> chloe: im not asking for the process im asking whats in it. thats on the side of every box in every pharmacy in the country
>
> kavi: i know that too. i asked twice
>
> chloe: fine
>
> Chloe leaves it. There is a version of the afternoon where she keeps pulling at it, and she can feel the shape of that version while she types the last message, and she goes and does her reading. The offer sits in the log between the question about the hard step and Kavi asking twice, and the next thing anybody types is about something else.
>
> The chat moves on within minutes. Chloe leaves the tab open on her desk for the rest of the evening, the cursor sitting in the empty box where a reply would go.

**Which shape, and why:** Shape 5, almost to the letter of the brief's own
description: "there is a version of this where she does Y" is the exact
construction on the page. The narration states the road not taken and gets
the credit of having named it without spending an action on it. This sits
in the framing narration around the chat, not in the chat itself, so it is
in scope even under the instruction to judge chapter 24 mostly on framing.

**What she does now:** Types "fine," closes nothing, opens no other avenue,
and the chapter spends its last image on an empty reply box.

**What a child that age would do:** She offered, five lines earlier in the
same conversation, to build the thing herself if someone got her the
ingredient list. Being told "internal" a second time by an institution is
exactly the kind of wall an eighteen-year-old with her specific habits goes
around rather than accepts. She has a bathroom cabinet, a search engine, and
a friend running a company built on getting answers out of guarded systems.

**Proposed:**

> Chloe doesn't answer the thread again. She finds the empty box from the
> winter before at the back of the bathroom cabinet and reads what's printed
> on the side of it under the lamp: a name, a dosage, nothing else, the
> insert long gone with the rest of the box. She photographs the label and
> sends it to Nadia alone, not the group.
>
> chloe: can you find what this actually is without going through the internal thing
>
> nadia: give me the batch number too
>
> chloe: dont have it. thats gone with the box
>
> Nadia comes back an hour later with three ingredient names off a supplier's
> safety sheet, the kind meant for a pharmacist, not a customer, and Chloe
> writes all three down on the back of her hand before she loses the message
> in the scroll.

## Instance 3

**File:line:** chapters/20_the_parking_lot.md:45-55
**Chloe's age:** sixteen (June 2022)

**Passage (exact):**

> "I'm doing the forge both sessions again, same as last year," Chloe says.
>
> "You said that like it was going to be different this year." Something in Ruth's face concedes the point before Ruth herself has agreed to concede it.
>
> "It's not not going to be different. It's just also, on top of whatever else it is, going to be the forge, as it always is, same anvil, same sessions, same walk down there every morning before anyone else is up."
>
> Chloe has the next part of that ready, but gets as far as the first word of it.
>
> "You take the thing you're worst at and you stay in it," Nadia says, to the table rather than to Chloe. "Every year. It's the only pattern you've got."
>
> Chloe takes her hand off the table and puts it in her lap; by the time anybody answers, Nadia is back to dividing the bill.

**Which shape, and why:** Shape 2 (receiving a scene) laid directly on top
of shape 4 (somebody else drives). The text tells us she has a response
ready ("the next part of that ready") and then withholds it from her rather
than having anything stop her; Nadia's line is allowed to be the last word
at the table, and the physical beat that follows is retreat, not response.

**What she does now:** Withdraws her hand into her lap. Nobody, including
the narration, comes back to what she was about to say.

**What a child that age would do:** Sixteen, told in front of two friends
that staying in her weakest thing is "the only pattern" she has, does not
let a half-formed sentence die in her mouth. She either forces the first
word back out even if it comes out clumsy, or she picks a fight with the
premise rather than the phrasing.

**Proposed:**

> "You take the thing you're worst at and you stay in it," Nadia says, to the table rather than to Chloe. "Every year. It's the only pattern you've got."
>
> "I'm not worst at it anymore. I haven't been for two years." Chloe's fork is still in her hand, and she sets it down harder than the plate needs. "Pick something you've actually watched me do before you hand me a pattern."
>
> "You're not worst at it," Nadia says. "You're just still the only one who's there every morning nobody's making you be." She goes back to dividing the bill.
>
> Chloe's hand goes back to the fork, and she doesn't eat with it for a while.

## Summary

**Instances found:** 5 (3 substantial, 1 lower priority, 1 minor/optional).

**Worst chapter:** 21 (The Applications). It carries the most sustained
instance in the span, the Bex-credit sequence, which runs across three
separate scenes and the better part of a week without Chloe doing anything
that costs her or anyone else something. This is also the chapter where the
brief's complaint lands hardest: sixteen turning seventeen is old enough
that watching credit for her own work get taken, three times over, without
so much as a bad confrontation, reads as absence rather than restraint.

**Initiates-a-scene ratio:** Counting the scenes across chapters 19-24 that
center on Chloe (roughly 27 by my count), she opens about 9 of them: taking
her own paper to Amberg (19), writing practice papers unprompted and giving
them to Kavi (19), going to find Kavi to ask him why (22), calling home to
report the decision (22), asking Priya what's in the bag (23), researching
the Foreign Service eligibility page and bringing it to her father (23),
posting the laptop and the medication questions to the chat (24), and the
"store window" argument she starts in the letters scene (21). The other
roughly 18 arrive already in motion: Sam, Bex, the corkboard, Amberg's
roster, driving instructors, Marek, Pruitt, her mother and father's
questions, the heist's planning split three other ways, the fight (Sam's
call), the diner arguments, Bex speaking for her at the visitors' table,
Nadia's job search, her grandmother, and the chat threads other people
start. That's roughly one scene in three that she starts herself; the
Amberg exit interview (22) is deliberately not counted either way, since
the interview itself is mandatory and structural, but her answer inside it
is the most decisive thing she does anywhere in the span.

**Three to fix first:** Instance 1 (chapter 21, the Bex arc), Instance 2
(chapter 24, the "version of the afternoon" line), Instance 3 (chapter 20,
the forge callout).
