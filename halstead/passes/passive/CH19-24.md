# Passivity pass, chapters 19-24

Span: 19_sixteen.md, 20_the_parking_lot.md, 21_the_applications.md,
22_the_offer.md, 23_the_first_one.md, 24_the_chat.md. No chapter edits made.

**Revision note.** The brief changed after the first delivery: the
stillness exemption is gone, and neither the chapter 19 corridor scene nor
the chapter 21 Caltech scene is held up as a standard any longer. This
revision re-reads the whole span under the new terms, adds every stillness
instance that was previously passed over, and re-judges both named scenes
from first principles rather than treating either as settled. The original
five instances all held on re-check and are kept below, unchanged, marked
CONFIRMED.

Chapter 20 lines 71-131 (the fight) and chapter 22's Amberg speech remain
out of scope on the coordinator's separate, standing instruction for this
span; that instruction was not part of the brief and was not touched by the
brief's revision. Everything else in the span, including both previously-named
scenes, was re-checked.

Chapter 22 was checked specifically for whether the rewritten line ("it's
school, you do it and then you leave and do the next thing") arrives as a
decision or as a decision already made. It arrives as a decision. Amberg
asks "Reason?" and the narration says "She does not have to find it," then
gives the line, and the scene that follows (four days later, the phone call
home) has her hold the line against both parents pushing back on it line by
line. Nothing in chapter 22's decision scene is flagged; two other chapter 22
scenes are, below.

## The two scenes the brief used to name

**Chapter 19's corridor scene (lines 7-23).** Re-read against the four-part
test: she wants nothing stated in the scene, she is relaying and defending an
opinion rather than pursuing anything, and the scene's one real turn (Sam
deciding to withdraw ten pages) happens to him, not to her. Nothing in it
matches shapes 1-5, so it produces no instance of the kind this brief tracks.
But it also does not clear the four-part bar itself: nobody could point to
what Chloe wanted here or what it cost her to get it. It reads as voice and
information delivered at the reader, in-character, rather than a scene she is
in for herself. That is a real defect and worth the author's attention, but
it is a different defect than passivity, so it is named here rather than
logged as an instance with a proposed fix.

**Chapter 21's Caltech scene (lines 101-115).** Chloe is not a character in
this scene. It is the representative and the escort, in a room Chloe never
enters; she is mentioned once, by title, as someone unavailable until four.
The brief's own carve-out for a character genuinely not in the scene applies
directly, so this cannot generate a Chloe-passivity instance. Its unfitness
as a standard is a different problem than the one this pass measures: the
scene that proves her work is rigorous happens entirely without her in the
room, which is on-theme with several of the instances below (credit for her
work being handled, argued over, and settled by other people) but is a
structural fact about the chapter, not a thing she failed to push against.

**Which shape, and why:** Stacks three of the five. Shape 1 (auditing instead
of reacting) in the evening with the drawing; shape 3 (declining, and the
text names the decline: "does not go") at the corridor; shape 2 (receiving a
scene) on the phone, where the scene's whole content is that she cannot say
what is wrong and the call just sits there until it ends. The credit taken
is for the one thing in the chapter that is unambiguously hers, the geometry
on the drawing, and across four separate opportunities to do something
about it, spread over most of a week, she does nothing that costs her
anything or costs Bex anything. The chapter even tells us she got close
once, in the corridor, and had it undone by nothing external, only by
turning around.

**What she does now:** Confirms the injustice privately against a folder
only she looks in, walks to where she could raise it and walks back, then
on the one call where an adult asks her directly what is bothering her, she
cannot produce the sentence.

**What a child that age would do:** A sixteen-year-old who just watched
somebody else get credited, in front of a college representative, for the
one piece of work she can prove is hers, does not sit on it for a week. She
says something wrong to Bex at the next meal and has to live with having
said it. She corners her once, gets a bad answer back, and has nothing to
do with the bad answer except carry it. She does not need to win the
exchange. She needs to have had it.

**Proposed:**

> The drawing lives in a folder Nadia keeps. Chloe gets it out that evening,
> finds the Thursday she carried it in to breakfast, and finds Bex at that
> table too, saying nothing, while Chloe was the one talking. She closes the
> folder and sets it back on the shelf, corner squared to the edge, the way
> she'd leave a tool she meant to pick up again.
>

> table, with Sam still getting his tray settled beside her.
>

>

>

>

> writing it down. That's the version he's got now."
>
> Chloe doesn't have anything to put next to that. She eats the rest of
> breakfast standing at the counter, tray in one hand, and doesn't go
> looking for the representative afterward either.
