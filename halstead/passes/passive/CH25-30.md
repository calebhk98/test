# Passivity pass, chapters 25-30

Span covers 25 (Sam, POV), 26 (Sam, POV), 27 (Nadia, POV), 28 (Chloe, 19), 29
(Theo, POV), 30 (Chloe, 20).

**Revision note.** This replaces the first pass, which cleared 25, 26, 27 and
29 outright and held two scenes up as the standard for a fix. Both calls were
wrong per the corrected brief: there is no stillness exemption, and no scene
in this book is a model to match. This version re-reads all six chapters
against that: every watch, wait, decline, held-back word, or accurately-
registered-and-dropped beat is logged, and every judgment is made from first
principles (visible want, an attempt, a cost or failure or new problem, an
ending attributable to the attempt) rather than by comparison to any existing
scene. **None of the four chapters come back clean.** Chapter 29's "Bloods
drawn at the debrief" paragraph and chapter 30's "Hebrew is the second year's"
paragraph are untouched, per the protection notice.

Two verdicts appear below. **FAIL** means the passage does not clear the
first-principles bar and a fix is proposed. **PASS (logged)** means the
stillness is real and is logged as instructed, but tracing the attempt
through to where it lands still shows a visible want, an act, a cost, and a
different ending attributable to it — so no fix is proposed, and the working
is shown so the call can be checked or overruled.

A handful of stillness beats are procedural rather than a live choice against
which the character could plausibly push (a soldier freezing at an unlabeled
sound in the dark, standing in a bus line): these are logged in a short list
at the end of each chapter's section rather than written up in full, since
there was no alternative on offer for the character to decline.

## Chapter 25 (Sam, POV)

### Instance 1 — FAIL

**chapters/25_forty_targets.md:105-115**

> This is approximately where he starts to have a problem: he is doing well.
> [...] But none of it is reconcilable with a single thing he knows about
> himself. [...]
>
> So he goes looking for the fault in the measurement.
>
> His first thought is that everybody here is tired in a way school could
> only approximate, since school let you sleep through the night and this
> place treats the night as available.
>
> His second is arithmetic, and it is better. [...]
>
> His third accounts for everything the first two leave lying around, and it
> is the one he keeps: this is a screen. [...]
>
> The explanation survives a week of examination, after which he stops
> examining it.

**Shape:** 1, auditing instead of reacting, textbook. Something genuinely
strange happens to him (he is unaccountably the best in his platoon at
things he has always been ordinary at) and his entire response, across three
paragraphs, is to reason about it. First principles: does he want something
visible here? Yes, to understand what he is. Does he do anything to find
out? No — every step of this passage is internal. Does the attempt cost him
anything, fail at anything, or create a new problem? No; it simply stops.
Does the scene end anywhere different because of something he did? No — he
holds a private theory and the theory changes nothing about how he trains,
what he says, or what happens next. This is exactly the shape the corrected
brief describes: registers something accurately, and moves on.

**What he does now / what an eighteen-year-old would do:** Reasons his way
to a comfortable theory and quietly retires the question. An eighteen-year-
old who has just discovered he might be being sorted for something tests it,
even a little, even badly, rather than sitting with a theory he never puts
under any weight.

**Proposed:**

> The explanation survives a week of examination. To find out whether it
> holds, he starts holding some of himself back on the land-navigation
> course, taking it half a step slower than he can manage and watching for
> anybody who marks him down for the difference. Nobody does. Three weeks of
> a slower time on his own sheet is what the experiment costs him and
> nothing else, so he stops running it and goes back to doing the course at
> the only speed he actually has.

### Instance 2 — FAIL

**chapters/25_forty_targets.md:129**

> Sam writes to his grandmother on Sunday nights with the pad braced on his
> knee, in block capitals, because he gave up on cursive somewhere around the
> age of ten. What she gets is the cold, a drill sergeant who yells at
> everybody and means well by it, the length of a meal. The six hundred stays
> out of the letter, on the grounds that it would require a paragraph of
> explanation, then still amount to a number when he got there.

**Shape:** 3, declining, dressed as a piece of reasoning rather than shown
as a choice. Under the old brief a single decline was "character." Under the
corrected one it is still logged, and on inspection it fails anyway: the
decision happens entirely off the page as a justification ("on the grounds
that"), not as anything he visibly does.

**What he does now / what an eighteen-year-old would do:** Talks himself out
of it in a sentence of narrated reasoning. An eighteen-year-old with a
number like that in his pocket starts to tell her and stops, on paper, where
it can be seen costing him something.

**Proposed:**

> Sam writes to his grandmother on Sunday nights with the pad braced on his
> knee, in block capitals, because he gave up on cursive somewhere around the
> age of ten. He gets as far as the six hundred once, the number sitting
> alone in the middle of a fresh sheet, before he tears the page free and
> starts again from the top. What she gets is the cold, a drill sergeant who
> yells at everybody and means well by it, the length of a meal, and no
> number anywhere on the page by the time he seals it.

**Logged, minor, no fix:** *25:221*, "Okoro takes the whole enquiry for a bit
and laughs; Sam permits him this, on the grounds that it is a perfectly
reasonable thing to laugh at." A held-back reaction to being laughed at, but
there is no live alternative on offer inside a two-line exchange at the

## Summary

**Instances found:** 22 logged across the span — 18 written up in full (2 in
ch25, 3 in ch26, 2 in ch27, 4 in ch28, 5 in ch29, 2 in ch30) plus 4 minor
structural ones noted briefly (1 in ch25, 1 in ch26, 2 in ch27) where no live
alternative was on offer. Of the 18 full instances, 10 fail first principles
outright and carry a proposed fix (1, 2, 5, 7, 8, 9, 10/17 as one continuing
instance, 12, 16, 18); 1 fails on its exact wording while the arc around it
passes (16); the remaining 7 are logged per the no-exemption rule and, worked
through to where the attempt lands, clear the bar (3, 4, 6, 11, 13, 14, 15).

**Do chapters 25-27 and 29 still come back clean?** No. Every one of them now
carries at least one FAIL: 25 has two (the screening-theory audit, the
grandmother letter), 26 has one (letting the "how long were you in there"
question go), 27 has one (the hiring-pool unease dropped in chat), and 29 has
one (the self-contradicting "nothing" line, though the arc it sits inside
still passes). What holds up from the first pass is narrower than "no
instances": in each of these four chapters the character's larger arcs, once
followed all the way to where they land, do generally clear the bar — Sam's
culvert rumination becomes a public confession, Nadia's dark-room silence
becomes a chosen deflection, Theo's two quiet days become a message that
reopens Ruth's silence. What does not hold up is the claim that nothing in
