# Passivity pass, chapters 31-36 — surgery record

Second pass on `passes/passive/CH31-36.md`. That file logged 23 full instances
(plus two noted-only and a cross-cutting tic pattern). This pass triages to
13 kept, 1 defended-and-left-alone, and 9 dropped, then edits the 13 directly
in the chapter files. All quotations below are the exact old and new text as
they now stand (or stood) in the files; every "new text" block was checked
against the file after editing.

## Worst instance

**35-4, the group's "we do exactly nothing" exchange.** A named policy of
collective inaction, agreed to unanimously by a group that litigates every
clause of a legal document elsewhere in the same story, with no alternative
raised and rejected on the page. This is where the passivity costs the book
most: it is not one viewpoint character declining to act, it is the entire
cast doing it together, at the point in the manuscript where an outside
threat is most concrete. Fixed below.

## Best instance (defended, not edited)

**31-9, the drafts that die and the stopped posting.** Kept exactly as
written. This is the instance that makes the chapter's real break work: Ruth
drafts, deletes, and finally goes quiet through most of a school year, and
then the very next scene in the chapter is her breaking that silence
unprompted ("ruth: ok / im going to say a lot of things..."), disclosing
everything to the group in one unbroken push and immediately demanding
answers ("so who did this... i want to know why it was me"). The decline in
31-9 is what makes the eventual disclosure cost something — it is earned
silence right before real, first-person action, not a substitute for it.
Editing 31-9 to give her a smaller, private outlet (as the first-pass report
proposed: sending a draft to one person before deleting it) would soften the
one moment in the whole span where a character actually breaks pattern on
her own initiative, for no gain. Left alone.
