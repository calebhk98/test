# Passivity pass, chapters 25-30: triage and surgery

Second pass on `passes/passive/CH25-30.md` (22 instances logged, 18 written up
in full). This document is the triage and the edits made to the chapters
themselves. All quotations of "old" text below are copied from the chapters
as they stood before this pass; all "new" text is copied from the chapters as
they now stand.

## The kept eleven

Ranked roughly worst-first. Two of the eighteen logged instances (10 and 17)
are one continuing failure across two chapters and are surgery'd as a pair,
which is why eleven edits cover ten of the report's numbered instances.

### 1. Chapter 25:107-115 — the screening-theory audit (worst in the span)

**Old:** "So he goes looking for the fault in the measurement. [...] The
explanation survives a week of examination, after which he stops examining
it."

**Reason kept:** Largest block of pure audit-with-no-attempt in the span,
inside the chapter that most looks, on a fast read, like it has no passivity
problem at all. This is the one I'd fix first if only one fix were on offer:
a genuinely strange fact about himself gets three paragraphs of reasoning and
zero test.

**Action:** Left the three paragraphs of reasoning (they're real
characterization and set up the theory a reader needs), replaced only the
closing sentence that let him stop without ever spending the theory.

**New:** "The explanation survives a week of examination. To find out
whether it holds, he starts holding some of himself back on the
land-navigation course, taking it half a step slower than he can manage and
watching for anybody who marks him down for the difference. Nobody does.
Three weeks of a slower time on his own sheet is what the experiment costs
him and nothing else, so he stops running it and goes back to doing the
course at the only speed he actually has."

Passes the four tests: he wants to know if the theory is true; he acts (runs
a deliberate underperformance, on the record, on a sheet the cadre actually
read); it costs him three weeks of a worse recorded time; the scene ends with
the theory disproven by evidence rather than merely retired by boredom.

### 2. Chapter 25:129 — the grandmother letter

**Old:** "Sam writes to his grandmother on Sunday nights [...] The six
hundred stays out of the letter, on the grounds that it would require a
paragraph of explanation, then still amount to a number when he got there."

**Reason kept:** The decline happened entirely off the page as justification
("on the grounds that"). Companion instance to 29's grandmother call; a good
place to show the two chapters treating the same beat differently.

**New:** "He gets as far as the six hundred once, the number sitting alone
in the middle of a fresh sheet, before he tears the page free, balls it, and
starts again from the top. Okoro, writing his own letter on the rack across
the aisle, watches the paper go into the bin and says nothing about it until
the second sheet is half full. 'Number too good to send her?' 'Something
like that.' Sam keeps writing."

Now dramatized rather than narrated: Okoro sees the torn page and asks about
it; Sam gives a peer a partial admission he withholds entirely from his
grandmother, which is a different disclosure than either "tell her" or "tell
nobody." Checked against later chapters: Okoro never appears outside 25-26,
so nothing downstream depends on what he does or doesn't know.

### 3. Chapter 26:85-93 — "how long were you in there"

**Old:** "Sam looks back down the last thirty feet of the cut [...] and lets

**New:** Added a real question and a real answer: "'What gave me away,' he

He asks; the answer is worse than the headline number he already had

sharper, more specific version of the same humiliation, filed alongside the
culvert lesson that pays off later in the AAR. Ends differently: he now
knows the actual margin rather than just the number of hours.

**New (28):** "She reads the message a second time, then does the

## The best, defended

**Chapter 26:26-31 — the fence-line contact.** "Then he withdraws back into
the pines, gets down behind a root ball with the rifle laid across it, and
waits twenty minutes for something to happen. [...] Sam settles the front
sight on the shape and stays with it through the third. / Two rounds."

This is stillness, logged per the no-exemption rule, and it should be kept
exactly as written. Run through the four tests: he wants to win the contact
from a position of advantage rather than blunder into two men in the open
(visible in the choice to withdraw rather than push forward); he does
something to get it, reading a rhythm in the nearer man's weight shifts and
firing on it; the attempt resolves the contact in his favor, immediately and
concretely, both harnesses going to tone; the scene ends somewhere it would
not have ended had he stayed lying in the open or crossed the fence blind.
Nothing here needs "fixing" because the waiting is the mechanism of the
action, not a substitute for it, which is the entire distinction the brief
is drawing. A pass that "fixed" this by giving him something to think about
during the twenty minutes would be adding interiority to a beat that is
already doing the job.
