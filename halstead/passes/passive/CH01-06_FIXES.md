# Passivity pass, chapters 1-6: triage and surgery

Span: chapters 3-6 only. Chapters 1 and 2 are locked by the author; every
instance the first-pass report logged in `01_before.md` or `02_march_4th.md`
is dropped below with reason "locked," unedited, exactly as instructed.

Of the nineteen instances in `passes/passive/CH01-06.md`, eight fall in the
locked chapters. Eleven fall in chapters 3-6, which is fewer than the ceiling
of fifteen, so all eleven are triaged rather than cut for space. Ten are
edited. One is kept and defended without a change.

## Worst in the editable span

**Instance 19 (06:217-255), the closing beat of the whole six-chapter unit.**
It repeats, word for word in method, the fake-sleep-and-say-nothing technique
that opens chapter 1 (Instance 5, also locked): parents talk about her in the
front seat, she holds still and gives them nothing. Chapters 3 through 6 show
her getting louder, more argumentative, and more willing to interrupt adults
(the letter, the stairs, the fractions packet, the moon argument); ending the
span on the exact silence it started with throws that development away at
the one moment readers are positioned to notice it. Fixed below.

**Instance 16 (05:173-175), the runner-up.** The purest and longest audit
passage in the span, at the highest stakes in its chapter (a friend gone
because he was unhappy), sitting at a table next to the one person who
already has the answer. Fixed below.

## Best in the editable span, defended

**Instance 17 (05:221-223).** Kept as written; not edited.

> Partway into the walk back it catches up with her: Kavi, the moon, her own
> mouth already moving ahead of the check she has run on every sentence since
> November.
>
> She goes looking for the damage anyway, out of habit, and the sentence
> comes back clean, as do the ones after it.

This one is doing real work. It sits at the tail of a dinner-table argument
that runs the length of the scene before it, where Chloe holds her ground
against Ruth for a dozen exchanges running, out loud, in front of the whole
table. By the time this paragraph starts, she has already wanted something
(to be right about the moon), done something about it (argued it, repeatedly,
badly, in public), and paid for it (Sam's "stuck" jab, Ruth's flat
correction, the argument going nowhere she can declare a win). The paragraph
itself is not a scene; it is the two lines of connective tissue between one
scene and the next, and it exists to callback the trauma-driven verbal
self-check the book planted in chapter 1 (locked Instance 2) and is due to
pay off later. The first-pass report's own proposed fix for this instance was
to have her "kick a clump of grass loose just to have done something with the
extra minute" - which is exactly the trap this brief's second half warns
against: dressing up the same non-event in a physical gesture changes nothing
that happens. Nothing here needs to happen differently, because the event
that mattered already happened, on the page, one paragraph up.

## Kept and edited

### Instance 9 - `03_the_letter.md`

**Old:**
> A minute goes by before her mom says something too quiet to make out from
> the top of the stairs, her dad says he knows, twice, and somebody starts
> the dishwasher. At that, Chloe goes back up the rest of the stairs on her
> hands and knees, to keep the boards quiet, and gets into bed with her socks
> still on.

**Reason:** She gets confirmation, from her father's own mouth, that she has
gone quiet and unhappy, and does nothing with it - not even come down and say
so. Shape 2.

**Action:** She comes down instead of retreating, and says the one thing she
came down to hear confirmed.

**New:**
> A minute goes by before her mom says something too quiet to make out from
> the top of the stairs, her dad says he knows, twice, and somebody starts
> the dishwasher. Chloe comes down the rest of the way, in her socks, and
> stands in the kitchen doorway until her dad sees her.
>
> "I heard that part," she says. "The quiet part."
>
> Neither of them answers her right away, and it's her mom who crosses the
> kitchen first.

Test check: she wants to be seen and heard (visible in the doorway); she says
so, out loud, where her father can hear it; it costs her the silence she'd
built the whole chapter around, and gets no immediate answer back; the scene
ends in a kitchen confrontation instead of a bed she climbed back into
unnoticed.

that goes quiet because one of them fell asleep.

### Instance 14 - `05_behind.md`

**Old:**

> next table, still working out at her own speed whether a diagonal is the

**Reason:** She works out the correct engineering answer entirely in her own
head, a beat too late to use it, and the scene moves on without her ever
spending it. Shape 1 into shape 2.

**Action:** She proposes the fix out loud and Sam turns her down, for a
specific, checkable reason.

**New:**
> Ruth goes and does her own, and Chloe watches her the whole way back to the
> next table, working out at her own speed whether a diagonal is the entire
> argument Ruth thinks it is. By the time she has the answer, Sam has already
> laid a bead of glue along the first joint.

> span.
>
> "It's already glued, Chloe. It's not coming apart now."
>
> "Just the middle one."
>
> "There's no dowels left. Ruth took the last of them for hers." Sam goes
> back to the joint with his thumb, checking it's set.

**Continuity note:** the report's own proposed fix (having Sam pick up
another dowel and act on her idea) was checked against the rest of the
chapter and rejected. Later in the same chapter, the bridge "folds sideways,
all at once, exactly the way Ruth's square went over," and chapters 7 and 8

added would make the bridge behave like Ruth's triangle, not her square, and
contradict all three passages. The fix keeps the bridge a square - Chloe
speaks up and is turned down for a concrete reason - so the later failure
and both downstream callbacks still hold.

Test check: she wants to strengthen the bridge and says so, visibly, to Sam;
it fails, for a stated reason; the scene ends with a real proposal and a real
no, not a correct idea that stayed in her head.
