# Passivity pass, chapters 7-12: triage and surgery

Span: chapters 7-12, all editable (chapters 1-2 are locked and outside this
span). `passes/passive/CH07-12.md` logged 29 instances (28 written up, plus
one, the chapter 12 Mandarin call-out, counted there but filed under
`passes/BEX_AND_IYAD.md`). Fifteen are kept and edited. One is defended and
left unedited. Thirteen are dropped for weakness. One is dropped because a
separate pass already owns the lines.

## Worst in the span

**Instance 28 (`chapters/12_nine.md:113-119`), the bridge.** The report's own
top pick and the clearest failure in six chapters: Chloe audits the fortnight,
rehearses the correction into a pillow, and nothing she does leaves the room.
The scene the plot reaches is identical whether or not she rehearses at all.
Fixed below, and the fix is deliberately small: one real, spoken attempt, one
real and unhelpful answer, one lasting behavioural change, with the private
loss (Bex and the teacher never get corrected) left standing.

**Runner-up: Instance 8 (`chapters/08_the_asking.md:45,49,81`), the Dr. Ammons
appointment.** A seven-year-old's own psychiatric intake conducted almost
entirely through her mother, including one question that is actually about
her safety, answered only by a hand going still. Fixed below.

## Best in the span, defended

**Instance 6 (`chapters/07_the_same_room.md:209-217`), the crying scene.**
Kept exactly as written; not edited.

> "Did somebody say something to you? Because you can tell me if they did,
> and you are safe either way."
>
> Chloe moves her head from side to side with it still down on her arms.

This reads as an instance under the letter of the brief (no words, no
decision, the scene ends the moment Ms. Vance moves on), but it is doing real
work and cutting it would cost the book more than keeping it. The whole point
of the scene is that Chloe does not yet have the word for what is wrong with
her, and the book says so explicitly two chapters later: "The word for the
whole thing is nowhere in her" (`09_february.md`). A seven-year-old who
cannot name a diffuse, monthslong depression is not withholding an answer she
has; she does not have one to give. The arc pays this off on its own schedule
across the span: wordless headshake here, a flood of bargaining that still
can't produce the actual word in the "please" scene early in chapter 9, and
finally one plain sentence her mother can act on ("This, this, all of this").
Giving Chloe a gesture or a line here, the way the first-pass report proposed
(pushing the math sheet toward Ms. Vance), would not change what happens next
in the scene or the chapter; it would just be a more active-looking version of
the same non-answer, which is the exact trap the second half of this brief
warns against. The silence is the fact the chapter is built to establish, not
a place the character is ducking a choice.
