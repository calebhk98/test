## Scene 1 — The letter and the dinner table (lines 5-65)

**Content:** Chloe finds an envelope addressed to her personally at the mailbox,
opens it badly, and reads a one-page letter about a month-long free summer program
called Halstead. She brings it to dinner. Her parents read it in turn, argue briefly
about whether anything is really free, and give her a non-answer. She asks again
once more on Sunday and then stops asking.

**Possible purpose:** looks like it exists to establish the letter as an object,
put a name and a first taste of "free" in front of the reader, and show the
parents' baseline dynamic (mom suspicious, dad more willing to take things at face
value) before either of them has any information.

**What I think it tells the reader:** may be doing the work of making the letter
feel ordinary-strange rather than sinister yet — the family's response is domestic
bickering, not alarm, which could be there so the alarm in scenes 2-3 has somewhere
to climb from.

## Scene 2 — The phone calls (lines 69-139)

**Content:** Over several days, her mom calls the district (no such school on record, and
a warning about the postmark), calls the neighbors (nobody else got a letter), and
finally calls the number on the letter itself, where an unnamed person answers every
question about safety and logistics but not the question of how they got Chloe's
name, beyond the phrase "their list is usually pretty good." Chloe overhears in
pieces from the stairs.

**Possible purpose:** looks like the investigative turn of the chapter — each of the
three calls closes off one ordinary explanation (a school program, a neighborhood
mailing, a scam that can't answer basic questions) and leaves the family with
something that behaves exactly like a legitimate, well-prepared organization while
answering nothing about its own origin.

**What I think it tells the reader:** may be building the specific unease of the
book — not "this is obviously fake" but "this is suspiciously competent" — through
the parents' own vocabulary (network, usually pretty good) rather than through
narrator comment on it.

## Scene 3 — The website and the nine days (lines 143-211)

**Content:** Friday night, her dad reads the Halstead website aloud to Chloe until he
gets to the staff bios, where his manner changes and he re-reads the page twice.
Sunday, her mom searches independently and finds nothing outside the school's own
site. Monday morning, Chloe reads the staff bios herself and asks her dad about them;
he confirms it's the bios he kept returning to and adds that the site itself looks
old and untouched. Over the following nine days her parents argue on and off about
whether to send her, her mom asks Chloe why she wants to go, and on the ninth night
Chloe overhears her parents' clearest statement yet: that she has gone quiet since
Christmas.

**Possible purpose:** looks like this scene is doing double duty — it deepens the
mystery (the credentials read as too polished, the site as too old for a
four-year-old school) while also surfacing the actual emotional argument for going,
which is not about the free month but about Chloe herself having become quieter.

**What I think it tells the reader:** may be setting up that the parents' real
disagreement was never fully about safety, since the scene that finally moves them
is about Chloe going quiet rather than about the network or the postmark.

## Scene 4 — The library and the drive (lines 215-249)

**Content:** On the Thursday before she leaves, Chloe negotiates with the librarian
over her checkout limit, loses, then gets a book put on hold instead. The family
packs the night before with a running joke about the packing list. They drive her
themselves, her mom checks the room and staff once there, and her dad arranges to
pick her up on the way out the door.

**Possible purpose:** looks like a closing beat that shifts the register from dread
to ordinary family texture — a kid's negotiating tactics, a joke about shoes,
parents doing normal check-in behaviors — right before she is actually sent off.

**What I think it tells the reader:** may be there to let the reader feel the
family's normal love and competence directly before the send-off, rather than only
through the investigation, so the ending of the chapter is warmer than its middle.
