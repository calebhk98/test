## 1. Scenes

The chapter breaks into eleven sections at the file's own `___` marks.

### Scene 1 — Camp pickup and Ruth's promise (lines 5-13)

**What appears to happen:** Pickup day at camp. Sam has already gone and Kavi
disappears into the crowd of parents without a goodbye on the page. Ruth finds
Chloe at the flagpole, hugs her, and extracts a promise that everyone gets into
Halstead next year. Ruth's mother calls her and she goes.

**Content, in order:** the loop full of cars; Sam's early exit reported
secondhand; Kavi absorbed by adults and gone before a goodbye happens on the
page; Ruth's hug and the promise, dialogue both ways; Ruth's mother calls her
name; Ruth leaves.

**Possible purpose:** looks like it plants the goal the rest of the chapter
runs on. The promise ("we're all getting in") appears to be the engine for
Chloe's later campaign to get into Halstead full time.

**What I think it tells the reader:** hedged, but it may be establishing that
Sam and Kavi leave without ceremony while Ruth's exit is given a full scene,
which could be setting up why Chloe later can't get Sam's or Kavi's last name
but the chapter still centers Ruth in the "getting in" thread.

### Scene 2 — Names nobody has (lines 17-57)

**What appears to happen:** Chloe wants to call Sam. Her mom tries to look up
Sam, then Ruth, then Kavi, then Priya, by last name, and Chloe has none of
them. Her mom calls the camp's school directly instead and leaves a message.

**Content, in order:** Chloe asks to call Sam; her mom asks who Sam is; Chloe
gives facts instead of a surname; this repeats for Ruth, Kavi, and Priya, each
time with more specific facts and no name; her mom gets the camp folder and
calls the school, asks for the message to be passed along, and hangs up.

**Possible purpose:** may be setting up the specific shape of what Chloe knows
about people, facts and habits rather than the administrative facts (surname,
address, school) that would make contact possible for an adult.

**What I think it tells the reader:** hedged. It might be doing double duty:
showing Chloe's mother's patience under four rounds of the identical problem,
and showing what "knowing someone" means to Chloe.

### Scene 3 — Waiting for the phone (lines 61-69)

**What appears to happen:** Days pass. Chloe stations herself near the phone,
takes wrong calls, and eventually stops sitting in the kitchen but keeps
checking the phone at night.

**Content, in order:** she reads in the phone's sightline; near-miss calls
(grandmother, wrong numbers) each handed off; on the ninth day her mom warns it
could take weeks; Chloe asks for a number; her mom won't give one; she stops
sitting in the kitchen but keeps checking the hook and still runs for it.

**Possible purpose:** looks like a compression scene, turning "days pass" into
something a reader can watch rather than being told about.

**What I think it tells the reader:** hedged, possibly showing an early,
milder version of the waiting behavior that recurs and escalates later in the
chapter (scene 9's tally marks).

### Scene 4 — The car, the big question (lines 73-85)

**What appears to happen:** In the car, Chloe asks to attend Halstead for the
full year, not just the summer program. Her parents defer the conversation to
"tonight," at a table.

**Content, in order:** Chloe asks; her mom asks her to clarify; Chloe clarifies
"the whole thing"; a beat of parental hesitation (mom looks out her window,
dad's hands come up the wheel); her mom defers to a sit-down conversation.

**Possible purpose:** may be the hinge scene: the actual ask that the rest of
the chapter's waiting is about. Notably, we are never shown the "tonight"
conversation onscreen; its non-answer seems to be what scene 9 is reacting to.

**What I think it tells the reader:** hedged. Possibly demonstrates evasive
adult behavior on a big topic (change the venue, not the answer) without the
narrator naming it as evasive.

### Scene 5 — The library, twice over (lines 89-101)

**What appears to happen:** Chloe returns her overdue library book and tells
the librarian, at length and with visible pride, everything she did at camp.
Takes four new books.

**Content, in order:** the book is overdue by a week; the librarian needles her
about it; Chloe answers with a run of specific camp facts (CPR dummy, the
bridge, fractions); the librarian is satisfied; Chloe takes four books and
finishes two by Friday.

**Possible purpose:** looks like a baseline. Chloe here is fluent, proud, and
comfortable holding a stranger's attention, which the later library scene
(scene 8) seems to undercut.

**What I think it tells the reader:** hedged, may function mainly as contrast
material for scene 8 rather than carrying weight of its own.

### Scene 6 — Second grade, the math sheet (lines 105-113)

**What appears to happen:** Second grade starts. A first math sheet turns out
to be material she already knows from last year. She checks her answers twice
and they hold.

**Content, in order:** Ms. Vance, seating groups, a rule about swapping groups;
the first math sheet is carrying, which she recognizes from Mrs. Aldana's
class; she finishes, checks it all again, and it is unchanged.

**Possible purpose:** may be establishing an academic mismatch (the work is a
year behind what she can do), which could be one half of a two-part contrast
with scene 7's social mismatch.

**What I think it tells the reader:** hedged; see the note on the "relieved"
line below, which I am not proposing a fix for.

### Scene 7 — The time capsule (lines 117-141)

**What appears to happen:** A class time-capsule exercise. Chloe writes an
elaborate answer, is singled out for how she talks, is mocked by two
classmates, and destroys and replaces what she wrote.

**Content, in order:** Ms. Vance introduces the exercise and its framing (you
are writing to yourself, three years older); Chloe starts early and thinks out
loud; Ms. Vance calls on her; Chloe explains the metaphor further, at length
and fast; Kayleigh and Bryce both comment on how she talks; Chloe covers her
paper with her hand; she claims she "just meant" something smaller; she
scratches out what she wrote and substitutes a short, safe line.

**Possible purpose:** looks like the social-mismatch half of the contrast
started in scene 6, and possibly the chapter's clearest instance of masking
costing her something visible and then working (she does successfully replace
the answer before anyone sees it), which the calibration note in the brief
asks me to watch for.

**What I think it tells the reader:** hedged, but this may be showing masking
in the middle of failing and then partly succeeding within the same scene,
rather than as an established, cost-free skill.

### Scene 8 — The books stop moving (lines 145-159)

**What appears to happen:** Chloe can no longer get through a library book. She
returns what's finished and skips getting new ones for a week; her mom
notices.

**Content, in order:** Monday through Wednesday, she rereads the same lines and
loses her place; Thursday, she turns in the finished two and keeps the
unfinished ones in her bag; her mom asks why; Chloe deflects twice ("a lot
going on," "a lot of school stuff"); the library card goes in a drawer.

**Possible purpose:** looks like the direct inversion of scene 5, same
activity (library, reading), opposite result, and might be marking a decline
rather than repeating a beat already made.

**What I think it tells the reader:** hedged.

### Scene 9 — Asking, all day, every day (lines 163-181)

**What appears to happen:** Chloe asks about Halstead constantly across a
compressed span of days, is put off, argues back, and starts a private tally
of days without an answer.

**Content, in order:** a list of settings where she asks (breakfast, car,
over her mom's phone call, a chore list under her mom's pillow, past bedtime);
a bedtime exchange where she pushes for a date and is told to go to bed; she
starts a mark for every day without a yes.

**Possible purpose:** may be the chapter's clearest escalation beat, moving
from scene 3's quiet phone-watching to open, repeated confrontation.

**What I think it tells the reader:** hedged, matches `DO_NOT_FLAG`'s ruling
that the Halstead-asking repetition in chapters 3, 7, and 8 is deliberate and
that in this chapter "it is the only thing in her head." I am not treating the
frequency itself as a defect.

### Scene 10 — The school on the phone (lines 185-197)

**What appears to happen:** Her mom starts calling the school directly, asking
about testing for third grade. Chloe overhears the tail end of it.

**Content, in order:** Chloe comes down for water and catches the end of a
call; her mom presses on whether the "third grade floor" policy has ever had
an exception; learns the testing window is only in spring, for a program a
year off; hangs up; sends Chloe to bed.

**Possible purpose:** looks like the parents' side finally starting to move,
in contrast to scene 9's stonewalling, and may be delivering the actual
logistics (the year-long wait) that explains why nobody can give Chloe a date.

**What I think it tells the reader:** hedged. Note: the second paragraph of
this scene (line 187) is quoted in `HOUSE_RULES.md` itself as the "after"
version of the worked tone-construction example. I have treated it as already
settled and have not proposed touching its content; see the paragraph note
below for the one caveat.

### Scene 11 — The desk (lines 201-219)

**What appears to happen:** Chloe cries at her desk in class for no reason she
can name. Ms. Vance comes to her, asks a series of ruling-out questions, and
the chapter ends before Chloe can finish an answer.

**Content, in order:** she cries, unprompted, mid-morning; the class reacts;
Ms. Vance crouches beside her and asks if she's hurt, if someone said
something; Chloe answers no each time, once aloud and once by shaking her
head; asked what it is, she says "I don't know" and the chapter cuts her off
mid-sentence.

**Possible purpose:** may be the chapter's payoff scene, landing an
unexplained, uncaused break after a chapter of things not being said.

**What I think it tells the reader:** hedged; see the paragraph note on line
201, which looks like it pre-states the very thing the ending proves.

## Chapter-wide note: the "because" reassurance construction

Thirteen lines in this chapter use the shape "[statement or question],
because [reason]," all inside dialogue, all adults reassuring or explaining to
Chloe: the mother eight times (21, 29, 45, 49, 67, 75, 151, 155), the
librarian once (95), and Ms. Vance four times (123, 205, 209, 213). Checked by
grep against the file; count confirmed.

`DO_NOT_FLAG` rules that Meg and Dave sounding alike is deliberate because
they are both her parents, but that ruling does not cover the librarian or Ms.
Vance, who share the identical construction here. This looks like it could be
a single adult-explaining-to-a-child register applied uniformly across three
otherwise distinct characters, which is the kind of overused-technique case
`HOUSE_RULES.md` says should come down in rate rather than to zero. I am not
proposing to remove the construction, only to thin it in a few of its weaker
spots; see Feedback.

## 4. The chapter whole

Eleven scenes. I did not find one I think should be cut outright; each seems
to carry a distinct job (the promise; the search for contacts; the wait; the
ask; a fluent baseline; an academic mismatch; a social mismatch; a reading
decline; an escalation; the parents' side finally moving; the unexplained
break). Two mergers look concrete enough to name.

**Scenes 2 and 3 could combine.** Scene 2 ends on the mother's line, "Okay,
honey, and all we can do now is wait for those families to call us back."
Scene 3 opens, "Chloe waits for the phone, day after day." The word "wait" is
already doing the bridging work in both directions; removing the break and
running scene 3 as the direct continuation of scene 2's last line, rather than
as a new section, would use a connective the prose already has instead of
adding one.

**Scenes 6 and 7 could combine.** Scene 7's own opening line, "The shoebox has
foil taped all over it, and in the second week Ms. Vance stands it up on the
front desk," already carries its own time-cue forward from scene 6 (which
does not specify a week, but reads as early in the term). The same room, same
teacher, and an existing "in the second week" marker would let the break come
out entirely, with scene 6's closing beat (checking the math sheet, hands in
lap) running straight into scene 7's opening image (the shoebox already on
the desk) as one continuous school-day.

I looked for a third candidate in scenes 9 and 10, which sit close together in
time (both "the last week of September" territory) and could plausibly run
together, but scene 9 is entirely Chloe's own escalating campaign and scene 10
shifts to the mother acting on the phone without Chloe driving the beat. I
think keeping the break marks that shift in whose action the reader is
watching, so I am not recommending this one, only noting it was considered.

I also looked at scenes 5 and 8 (both library scenes) as a possible duplicate
pair, since the brief calls out merging "two scenes doing the same job with
the same character," but these two seem to be doing opposite jobs on purpose,
a working, fluent Chloe and then a struggling one, rather than the same job
told twice. I would not merge or cut either.
