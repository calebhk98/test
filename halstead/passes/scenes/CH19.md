## 1. Scenes

The chapter has three `________________` breaks, making four sections, and
covers April 2022 through June 2023. Like other survey chapters in this book,
it moves across several mostly-independent threads (the bar exam and its
retake, a drama exercise, the practice-paper regime, driving instruction, the
forge, and a closing summer conversation at home) rather than a single
throughline. Scene numbering runs straight through the chapter, not restarting
at each break.

### Scene 1 — The exam room (April, the long examination room) — 1 paragraph

What appears to happen: the bar is sat over two days in a supervised room.

**Content:** Individual desks a meter apart, windows open against the heat,
the sound of the room pens and an occasional chair leg, the teachers who wrote
the paper proctoring and marking it themselves.

**Possible purpose:** may exist to establish the room and its stakes (the
people grading it are the people who wrote it) before the corridor scene gets
into what was actually written.

**What I think it tells the reader:** may be setting a controlled, adult-scale
setting against which the more personal beats that follow (Sam's page count,
the corkboard) can be read.

### Scene 2 — Sam in the corridor (that same day, the stairwell and corridor) — 9 paragraphs

What appears to happen: Sam catches Chloe right after the exam and they
compare how they each answered the same question.

**Content:** Sam asks how long her answer to question three ran; he gave
fourteen pages and still had more; she gave four, on the reasoning that past
the answer and the reasoning, extra pages are padding a marker gets nothing
extra for reading. He goes quiet, then says he'd like to withdraw ten of his
pages.

**Possible purpose:** may exist to put a theory in Chloe's own words — that
economy beats completeness — ahead of the next two scenes, which appear to
test that theory against an actual mark scheme.

**What I think it tells the reader:** may be setting her up to be
confidently, specifically wrong, in a way the Amberg scene later corrects
rather than simply contradicts.

### Scene 3 — Results day (days later, the corkboard hall) — 11 paragraphs

What appears to happen: the pass list goes up; Chloe fails by four points;
Kavi passed and immediately regrets saying so out loud; Bex works out and
announces the exact margin.

**Content:** Fifty-two pass, thirty-nine don't. Kavi passed by six and covers
his mouth a half-second after saying so. Chloe tells him it's fine, the
numbers are just numbers. Bex arrives at volume with the fraction already
worked out — a fiftieth, the closest miss on the board. Alone afterward, Chloe
goes back over whether she'd actually finished the fraction herself or was
still working on it.

**Possible purpose:** may exist to show three different responses to one
public, numeric verdict — Kavi's guilt, Bex's reassurance delivered as
precision, and Chloe's own quieter doubt about whether she was actually as
close to the answer as Bex.

**What I think it tells the reader:** may be a smaller, private version of the
chapter's larger pattern — an answer that looks complete until measured
against someone faster or fuller. Not confident this reading is intended
rather than my own pattern-matching across the chapter.

### Scene 4 — Amberg's office (later that week) — 9 paragraphs

What appears to happen: Amberg walks Chloe through why a correct answer still
failed.

**Content:** Her second line has the right answer and a single tick; the four
marks under it (the rule, its source, why a shed is covered, what happens if
it burns) are all unclaimed. She argues the rest follows automatically for
anyone who reads the answer. He tells her a marker has about ten seconds a
page, can't rebuild her reasoning for her, and can only mark what's actually
written down — that she wrote the answer for a reader who already had her
head, and the man marking it wasn't that reader.

**Possible purpose:** may exist to supply, at length, the explanation the
corridor and corkboard scenes withheld.

**What I think it tells the reader:** may be establishing the rule the rest of
the chapter's practice-paper material (Kavi's marking, the May–September
stack) goes on to apply.

### Scene 5 — The drama exercise (unspecified day, the drama room) — 6 paragraphs

What appears to happen: an improvisation exercise; Sam performs at once,
Chloe hesitates.

**Content:** "Two of you, brothers, one of you has sold something belonging to
the other." Sam is up immediately; Chloe, the following week, stops twice in
the first thirty seconds. The teacher calls this editing; she calls it
choosing, on the grounds that an ordinary line has to earn its place. She sits
back down.

**Possible purpose:** may exist to run the "economy vs. completeness" tension
from Scenes 2–4 through a second, unrelated arena — here the criticism runs
the opposite direction, that she's cutting too much rather than padding.

**What I think it tells the reader:** may be underlining that her instinct to
cut toward what matters shows up outside the exam room too, and that it draws
pushback here as readily as it earned her a fail there.

*Note on this scene's opening:* the first line of the section is spoken
dialogue with no location given before it; the room is only identifiable from
"the drama teacher" a clause later. See Feedback (low confidence — some
scenes in this chapter open on a location sentence, others don't, and I'm not
sure this is inconsistent rather than deliberate).

*Note on this scene's internal shape:* the single paragraph compresses two
separate class days — Sam's turn and, "the following week," Chloe's — without
a break between them. See Feedback.

### Scene 6 — The first practice paper (second week of May, the library) — 5 paragraphs

What appears to happen: Chloe writes her first practice paper and Kavi marks
it two out of four.

**Content:** Her answer repeats its own conclusion in different words instead
of reasoning it out; Kavi says a repeated sentence is worse than a gap, since
it tells the marker she didn't know where to stop. She argues it's the same
information; he says the marker doesn't have her head. He tells her to write
the actual event — the fire, not the rule about it — and she does.

**Possible purpose:** may exist to dramatize, at practice-paper scale, exactly
the rule Amberg stated in Scene 4.

**What I think it tells the reader:** may be showing her apply that rule for
the first time under someone else's judgment rather than her own.

### Scene 7 — The May–September stack (that period, presumably the library) — 2 paragraphs

What appears to happen: a summer's worth of practice papers, marked three
different ways.

**Content:** Kavi marks some, Nadia some, Chloe marks the rest herself; the
rate at which she stops early falls through May and settles by August. Kavi's
margin fills with notes; Nadia writes two words, *said nothing*. Chloe
rewrites the paragraph.

**Possible purpose:** may exist to show the Scene 6 lesson repeated at volume,
with two markers' very different styles standing in for two kinds of
feedback.

**What I think it tells the reader:** may be that repetition under feedback,
rather than one insight, is what closes this particular gap — using plain
numbers (a rate that falls, then settles) rather than a stated feeling to
carry it.

### Scene 8 — Driving instruction (September–March, unspecified grounds outside the school) — 1 paragraph

What appears to happen: a year of staged driving emergencies, compressed.

**Content:** An ignition killed at speed in October (she overshoots her mark
by thirty meters); a sabotaged brake pedal in November; a wet skid pan in
January; by March she's reversing faster than she and her father managed the
previous July.

**Possible purpose:** may exist mainly to establish the scale and escalation
of the training before Scene 9 isolates one instructor's specific coaching.

**What I think it tells the reader:** may be less about any single lesson here
than about pace — four distinct staged failures in one paragraph, a much
denser compression than this chapter uses for its other threads (compare
Scene 6's single practice paper getting five paragraphs). See Feedback.

### Scene 9 — Delacroix and the pole (within the same period, a car on a course) — 5 paragraphs

What appears to happen: Delacroix corrects where Chloe is looking, not what
she's doing with the wheel.

**Content:** He tells her to look at the gap past the pole, not the pole
itself; she says she already is; he says she isn't, that her hands only do
what her eyes hand them. She looks at the gap. The car goes through it.

**Possible purpose:** may exist to isolate one piece of driving instruction as
its own scene, distinct from the montage that precedes it.

**What I think it tells the reader:** may be a physical version of a pattern
that recurs elsewhere in the chapter — what you give your attention to is
what you get back — though the scene itself doesn't state that comparison.

### Scene 10 — The October retake (October, the same exam room) — 3 paragraphs

What appears to happen: Chloe sits the retake and passes clearly.

**Content:** A forklift question gets the same structure Amberg described —
answer, rule, source, scope, consequence — finished in nineteen minutes. She
passes by twenty-two. Kavi and Sam both text.

**Possible purpose:** may exist as the payoff for Scenes 4, 6, and 7 — the
same four-mark structure, executed cleanly under exam conditions.

**What I think it tells the reader:** may be showing the lesson landed, using
a numeric contrast (four points under in April, twenty-two over in October)
rather than a stated feeling about the improvement.

### Scene 11 — Aftermath (November, December, February, the library) — 1 paragraph

What appears to happen: Chloe keeps returning, involuntarily, to thinking
about April.

**Content:** Three separate occasions, months apart, sitting with something
else open in front of her; she still shuts books harder than she means to and
can't say why.

**Possible purpose:** may exist to mark that passing the retake didn't fully
close the question for her, in contrast to how cleanly the numbers resolved in
Scene 10.

**What I think it tells the reader:** may be leaving this genuinely
unexplained, which reads as consistent with the brief's note that a cost can
show without the narration naming it.

### Scene 12 — Marek and the practice papers (December, a corridor, then two evenings a fortnight apart) — 4 paragraphs

What appears to happen: Chloe lends her whole stack of practice papers to a
younger student, who returns it with one new page of his own.

**Content:** Marek, sitting the bar himself in April, asks for the stack; she
gives it to him, marked and unmarked, schemes clipped behind. A fortnight
later it comes back unread except for one new page: he's written the shed
question again, with the fire moved to Sunday. "That's better than theirs,"
he says, "delighted with it," already leaving. That evening she works his
version herself, and it takes her the whole evening.

**Possible purpose:** may exist to hand the Scene 6 lesson ("write the actual
event") forward to someone else, with Marek now setting Chloe the exercise
rather than the reverse.

**What I think it tells the reader:** this is the scene with the flagged named
state — "delighted with it" — the one place in this exchange where the
narration names Marek's reaction rather than showing it. Flagged as an
instance per instructions; no fix proposed.

### Scene 13 — The forge (a Thursday, presumably within the same period) — 5 paragraphs

What appears to happen: Pruitt notices Chloe has been leaving the forge early
for weeks and sends her home with the tongs she made.

**Content:** "You're off early." "Paper on Thursday. Same as the last few."
Working with his back to her, he says she's had a paper on Thursday for
weeks, and tells her to take the tongs on the rack — the ones she made — home
with her.

**Possible purpose:** may exist to close the practice-paper thread on a
wordless, domestic note, after an adult (Pruitt) who has evidently tracked her
absences without commenting on them until now.

**What I think it tells the reader:** may be offering a quieter model of
notice-and-respond than Amberg's or Kavi's much more verbal versions earlier
in the chapter, though I'm not confident that contrast is intended rather than
coincidental.

### Scene 14 — Summer, the general shape of it (June–July, home and the reservoir) — 2 paragraphs

What appears to happen: a montage of the first weeks home.

**Content:** She sleeps through most of the first week, then sleeps long and
rides to the reservoir most days; in July she finds a longer road and a hill;
she reads novels she can't later summarize. The rest of the time is long
afternoons on the back steps she and her father built, with a wasp working one
board every day.

**Possible purpose:** may exist to mark a period of recovery after the
exam-and-practice-paper year, using sleep and unremembered novels rather than
any stated feeling.

**What I think it tells the reader:** may be showing a cost of the year just
finished without naming it as one.

### Scene 15 — The back steps: mother, then father (the same summer, one afternoon into evening) — 23 paragraphs

What appears to happen: Chloe's mother asks, once, whether she's resting or
avoiding the house; her father then asks about nearly everyone else in the
chapter's cast.

**Content:** Her mother offers the unfinished garage shelf as something to do,
says she isn't really asking about the shelf, and lets it go. Her father asks
about Sam (stalled at forty percent, apparently unbothered), then Ruth
(disciplined about something that visibly bores her), then Kavi, then Priya —
landing on whether Priya deliberately let Nadia win a negotiation months
earlier. Chloe is still answering that last question when the sun goes down
and her mother turns the porch light on.

**Possible purpose:** may exist as the chapter's closing scene, gathering
nearly every character introduced earlier (Sam, Ruth, Kavi, Priya, Nadia) into
one conversation, in contrast to the chapter's earlier one-on-one structure.

**What I think it tells the reader:** may be putting two parental approaches
to the same withdrawn-looking child side by side in one continuous scene — the
mother naming the pattern directly and dropping it, the father asking
outward, indirect questions about other people instead. I'm hedging on
whether that contrast is intended versus simply being two people with two
different questions, and on whether the mother segment and father segment are
better read as one scene (my count) or two — nothing in the text marks a clean
break between them beyond the change of speaker, but the sun's position moving
continuously from "coming round the corner of the garage" (Scene 14) to "goes
off the garage roof" (end of this scene) reads to me as one unbroken run of
time, which is why I've kept it as a single scene.
