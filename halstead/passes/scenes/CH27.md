## Scene 1 — The sign (lines 13-17)

**Location:** the street window of the building, from outside, on a ladder.
**Time:** a specific Sunday morning in April.

**Content:** Nadia hangs the company's name in vinyl on the window herself; the second word
goes on crooked and stays that way because fixing it would tear it. Bev, watching from the
window, asks who will ever read it. Nadia says she reads it every morning and goes back down
for the squeegee.

**Possible purpose:** may exist to establish, in one small dated moment, the gap between how
Bev sees the business (invisible, unread) and how Nadia sees it (hers, checked daily) — a
gap the confrontation scene later tests at much higher stakes.

**What I think it tells the reader:** possibly that Nadia's persistence here is private and
unwitnessed on purpose — nobody has to see the sign for it to matter to her, which may be
doing some work ahead of a chapter that's largely about her operating without an audience.

   confidence it's the latter.
2. "'Who is ever going to read that?...'" — No issue.
3. "'I read it every morning,' Nadia said, and went back down the ladder for the squeegee."

## Scene 2 — The discovery (lines 21-37)

**Location:** the front room, then the back room, of the office over the laundromat.
**Time:** continuous, the Tuesday evening of the second week of May, into the night.

**Content:** Nadia reads a long employer-signup queue and finds thirteen fraudulent
accounts posting the same job ad, all created inside one half-hour window and asking new
users for bank details. She deletes the batch and telephones the people who answered,
including a woman who apologises repeatedly on a twenty-minute call. Afterward she sits with

**Possible purpose:** may be doing the work of establishing Nadia's method before showing
her apply it against people three times her age two scenes later — noticing a discrepancy
nobody asked her to notice, reading a log she built specifically so she could.

**What I think it tells the reader:** possibly that her response to being targeted is
procedural before it's anything else — delete, then call, in that order — and that the only
visible sign of what it costs her is the several minutes she spends afterward not doing
anything at all.

## Scene 4 — The research week (lines 73-85)

**Location:** the kitchen table at home; the county's filing and property records; the
tire shop's phone line; the car, parked out front, on the seventh day.
**Time:** the week following the discovery — several distinct mornings, one throughline.

**Why I'm reading this as one scene rather than several:** the brief's own worked example
(a letter found at a mailbox, brought to dinner, asked about again the following Sunday)
treats several distinct moments spread across days as one scene when a single throughline
runs under all of them. Here the throughline is the same investigation from first morning to
last, closing on the decision to drive out — I think that clears the same bar.

**Content:** Nadia traces the fraudulent accounts' state filings to a single registered
agent, Hanley, whose address is a suite above a tire shop. She calls the tire shop's front
counter under a pretext to learn the office's hours, keeps running the rest of the business
in parallel, and on the seventh day drives out alone.

**Possible purpose:** may exist to show the investigation as something she does around the
edges of an unchanged schedule (payroll still clears, she still signs employers) rather than
something that displaces it.

**What I think it tells the reader:** possibly that nothing about this week reads as
building toward violence or danger from her side — she tells no one what she's doing, calls
no lawyer, and the most aggressive thing she does is ask a tire-shop clerk a mild question
about hours.

## Scene 5 — The confrontation (lines 89-161)

**Location:** the office above the tire shop.

**Content:** Nadia climbs the outside stairs and walks into a room of four men running the

## Scene 8 — The year of hiring (lines 195-253)

**Location:** mostly the kitchen table and the back room where she interviews people; no
single fixed place.
**Time:** roughly a year, last July through the following April, several distinct dated
moments under one throughline.

**A judgment call, stated plainly:** this section could also be read as six or seven separate
micro-scenes (two candidate interviews, Tomas's fix, Bev's trip, Manny's fix, the vendor
pitch) stitched together with general essay-style narration about her hiring philosophy. I've
chosen to treat it as one scene, on the same basis as the brief's own worked example — one
throughline (testing and refining what she looks for in a hire) runs under every dated moment
in it, the same way one throughline held the letter-mailbox-dinner-Sunday example together.
If a second reader would rather see this as several short vignettes, I think that's a
defensible alternative reading and I'd rather flag the choice than hide it.

**Content:** Nadia posts a fifth open role. She describes the test generator she built and
