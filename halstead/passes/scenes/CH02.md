## Scene 1 — The waiting room (lines 5-29)

**Content:** Chloe and her mom wait for the appointment. Chloe checks the fish tank for
fish that aren't there, asks about the intake form her mom is filling out, gets a partial
answer, and reads the waiting-room posters and signage while thinking about her dad, who
isn't there.

**Possible purpose:** looks like it exists to set the terms of the whole chapter before
anyone measures anything — an adult giving a child an honest but incomplete answer, on
purpose, out of care rather than evasion, which is then the shape of everything Ben and her
mom do to her for the rest of the chapter.

**What I think it tells the reader:** may be doing the work of establishing Chloe as
someone who notices when an answer doesn't cover the question, without yet having the
standing to demand a better one — a disposition the room, and later the report, will act
on without asking her.

## Scene 2 — The testing session (lines 33-225)

**Content:** Ben (Dr. Prentice) introduces himself and the rules, then runs Chloe through a
full battery: block design, similarities, digit span, picture concepts, coding, a juice
break with an off-protocol conversation about finishing early, vocabulary, letter-number
sequencing, matrix reasoning, comprehension, symbol search, and cancellation. He ends the
session, declines to rate her, and takes her materials away.

**Possible purpose:** looks like the evidentiary core of the chapter — building, subtest by
subtest, the case that her nonverbal reasoning and verbal reasoning are both exceptional
while her hands and her strategy of giving Ben the shortest answer he'll accept both drag
the visible number down. `CH2_TESTING.md` names this mechanism directly; the scene as
written appears to already be built to it (fresh at Coding rather than tired, Cancellation
visibly faster than Coding, the digit span at eight-forward-six-back left alone).

**What I think it tells the reader:** may be showing masking as a technique in the middle
of being learned rather than one she already has — she guesses at what answer Ben wants,
checks with him mid-task whether she's allowed to slow down for neatness, and the two
places the scene lets her go all the way (the hour-and-year answer, the extra Similarities
booklet) are exactly the two untimed, unscored moments where guessing what the room wants
doesn't cost her anything.

## The chapter whole

**Chapter totals.** Paragraphs before suggested merges: 13 + 86 + 23 + 15 + 7 = **144**.
Paragraphs after: 11 + 84 + 23 + 13 + 7 = **138** (137 if Scene 5's optional cut is taken).

**Does each scene need to exist?** All five look like they're doing different jobs: the
waiting room sets up the pattern of partial-but-honest answers; the testing session is the
evidence; the corridor is where the finding meets its own limits, out of Chloe's reach; the
car is where that withholding reaches her directly; the last section converts one morning
into a school year. None look cuttable, and I would not merge any of the five into another
— the waiting room and the car both involve her mom giving her an incomplete answer, but
they're doing it at opposite ends of the appointment and the parallel seems to be the point
rather than a redundancy.

**Where a merge looks possible.** Not between these five scenes, but inside the testing
session. It's written as one continuous real-time scene — the same two people, the same
room, one uninterrupted morning — but it's divided by eleven `________________` breaks
between subtests (after paragraphs 7, 20, 30, 35, 41, 54 [going into the juice interlude],
63, 66, 76, and 86 in my numbering above, corresponding to the section breaks at lines 47,
75, 93, 99, 111, 125, 153, 167, 175, 183, and 205 in the file). Nearly every paragraph on
the far side of one of these breaks already opens with "Then" — "Then the cards want nine
blocks," "Then a booklet stands up," "Then he says numbers," "Then the numbers come back
with letters," "Then the standing booklet comes back," "Then it's the kind of question." The
break and the connective word are doing the same job twice. What already carries the reader
across each seam, without adding anything: the "Then," the same room, the same watch, and
Ben's stack of sheets, all already established and already recurring.

The one break in this run that marks a genuine change rather than a new subtest is the one
before the juice interlude (line 125) — Chloe actually leaves the table, washes her hands,
and gets a paper cup of juice from a refrigerator, which is a real change of activity and
location inside the room. If only one break in this stretch survives, that's the one I'd
keep; the other ten could become plain paragraph breaks, with the "Then" already doing the
bridging work the hard break is repeating.

I would not extend this to the break before the corridor (line 227) or before the car (line
275) — those mark real scene changes (a different room, then a different location entirely)
and should stay.

## Feedback

**"Introducing himself as Ben, he asks what he should call her, then sits down on a chair no bigger than hers."**
Narrated speech that should be spoken — a self-introduction and a direct question to a child.
Action: convert to dialogue.
Replace with two options: (1) "'I'm Ben,' he says. 'What do you want me to call you?' He sits down on a chair no bigger than hers." (2) "He introduces himself as Ben and asks, 'What should I call you?' Then he sits down on a chair no bigger than hers."

**"Chloe looks at him hard and files the last part with everything else adults say when they want you to sit still."**
"Files" gives Chloe an adult, administrative verb the brief names as cut from chapter 1 for the same reason ("Chloe files that"). "Everything else adults say when they want you to sit still" states a general rule about adult speech as settled fact rather than her own specific reaction.
Action: cut the generalization, keep her in a concrete, unnarrated moment.
Replace with two options: (1) "Chloe looks at him hard and doesn't say anything." (2) "Chloe looks at him hard. She's heard grown-ups say that kind of thing before."

States the tactic and its effect rather than showing the silence and letting the reader watch it work. Part of a three-instance pattern with the two entries below.
