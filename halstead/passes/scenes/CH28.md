# Scene audit: Chapter 28, "Nineteen"

Read before writing: `CLAUDE.md`, `passes/HOUSE_RULES.md` (rule 1 in particular), and
`passes/DO_NOT_FLAG.md`. Nothing in this chapter looks to me like it touches a `DO_NOT_FLAG.md`
ruling directly, though the three near-identical trivia exchanges near the end made me check
the "lists" and repetition rulings twice before deciding how to treat them (see Scene 12-14
below and "The chapter whole").

Settled going in, per instruction, not raised below: the dialogue-only ", because"
construction (this chapter has at least two, both inside Chloe's explanations to Deb, both
left alone); questions punctuated as periods, already fixed book-wide; and the six retired
trailing-clause shapes in `HOUSE_RULES.md`, which I haven't gone looking for.

Two chapter-specific notes I was given going in, both followed: the paragraph beginning
"Polish goes on the autumn schedule" (line 21) is newly added and protected, so I've walked it
below only to place it in the chapter's structure, not to propose anything against it. And
line 9's "gets furious" is flagged below as a named-state instance with no fix proposed, per
instruction.

Same method note as prior audits: I did not take the six `________________` marks as scene
boundaries. A scene is one time, one place, one action, whichever way the break marks fall.

## Scene count, up front

**14 scenes**, plus **12 paragraphs of connective/background material** that I don't think are
scenes (three paragraphs of habitual office description up front, one paragraph introducing
college before the registrar scene, the protected language paragraph, two thin one-sentence
paragraphs that set up a pattern rather than dramatizing anything themselves, two paragraphs
of pure blog-growth summary, and three paragraphs closing the chapter on Ruth's silence).
Those are walked below but excluded from the scene average.

**84 total paragraphs in the chapter.** 72 of them sit inside the 14 scenes; the other 12 are
connective. Average scene length: 72 / 14 = **5.1 paragraphs**. That average is almost
entirely carried by the four group-chat scenes (18, 14, 10, and 10 paragraphs). Take those
out and the other ten scenes run 72 − 52 = 20 paragraphs over 10 scenes, or **2.0 paragraphs
each**.

I don't think this chapter has the vignette problem in the form the brief describes it,
because most of the short units genuinely change place or person (loading dock, then a phone
call with the registrar, then Deb's desk, then Deb's desk again on a different day) rather
than being the same scene cut into pieces. Where I do think the chapter repeats itself is in
content rather than structure: three separate scenes in a row make the same point about
Chloe learning to translate jargon into something relatable (certification, discount, her
mother's refund), and three chat scenes in a row run the identical shape (Ruth asks a
surprising statistic, the chat over- or under-guesses, someone gets corrected, Sam takes the
joke). Both are addressed in "The chapter whole" below.

The chapter's own dateline ("*September 2024 – April 2025*") announces up front that it's
covering eight months, and I've read the short-scene structure as a deliberate consequence of
that choice rather than an accident.

since it isn't mine to add a cost to a beat that may be doing something else on purpose.

## The chapter whole

**Scenes found: 14** (the weekend question; the registrar; the filing; the decimal; Deb and
Tyler; the certification page; the discount; the call with her mother; writing the first
piece; the man who emails; four chat scenes), plus 12 paragraphs of connective/background
material across seven separate spots.

I don't think this chapter has the vignette problem as the brief defines it — nearly every
short "scene" here changes place, person, or both, which is what the brief says makes a
boundary real rather than accidental. What I do think is worth tightening is repetition of
content rather than fragmentation of scenes:

**One merge I'd make with real confidence:** fold Scenes 6, 7, and 8 (the certification page,
the discount, and the call with her mother) into one scene, along with the thin lead-in at
line 37. All three are the same demonstration — Chloe finding the plain-English version of a
technical fact — told three times in a row with escalating stakes (a coworker, then a
coworker who starts using the result, then family). What would carry across each seam: Deb's
own line, "Okay, but what does that actually mean," recurs almost verbatim between the first
two beats already, and the mother beat could open on the same phrase Chloe uses on herself
("Chloe hears herself do it again") rather than starting fresh. Concretely: drop the
sentence-paragraph break at line 37, let it open the certification paragraph directly, and
remove the paragraph break inside line 49 only far enough to keep the office-generalization
sentences as the closing beat of the discount scene rather than the opening of a new one, with
the mother's phone call as this larger scene's final movement. Resulting size: about 6
paragraphs, one scene, in place of 7 paragraphs across three units.

**A second, lower-confidence observation, not a merge I'm proposing:** the three trivia chats
(Scenes 12, 13, 14) run an identical structure back to back. I considered recommending
dropping one, but `DO_NOT_FLAG.md`'s ruling that accumulating repetition is "a device aimed at
the reader on purpose" and should "never" be flagged makes me want to raise this as a question
for whoever holds the book's calibration on that rule, rather than assert it as a finding.
