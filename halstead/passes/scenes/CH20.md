## Not a scene — pre-departure summary (lines 5-7)

**Location:** the school generally (dining hall, corridors, the patrol's route).
**Time:** "the fortnight before" — not a single moment, a compressed span.

**Content:** a flat claim that this is the first time anyone has snuck out since the school
opened, then a list of what each of the four did to prepare: Nadia socially engineered a
badge, Chloe looped the corridor cameras, Ruth readied her jamming box, Sam clocked the
patrol's timing.

**Possible purpose:** looks like it may exist mainly to get four separate skills on the page
quickly, without spending a scene on each.

**What I think it tells the reader:** may be setting up that the escape itself will read as
effortless, so that whatever goes wrong later reads as a surprise rather than a plan
failing.

### Notes

1. "They are the first to sneak out since the school opened." — No issue. Reads as a plain
   claim a camera (or the school's own record) could support; it doesn't tell the reader
   what to feel about it.
2. "In the fortnight before, the preparation divides itself between them: ... Ruth brings her
   box, which throws all its ugly things simultaneously at any recorder in range and writes
   noise over whatever it reaches; ..." — "ugly things" is vague where the sentence otherwise
   works hard to be specific (a badge duplicated, a hallway looping, a patrol interval
   timed). Possibly worth a concrete noun in its place — static, garbage frames, false
   signal, whatever the box is meant to be putting out.

## Scene 1 — The departure (line 9)

**Location:** outside the dorm building, at the fence.
**Time:** roughly 11:40pm, that same night.

**Content:** Kavi backs out at the last second to finish something of his own; the other
three go over the fence, the building dark except for one lit window on the third floor.

**Possible purpose:** may be setting Kavi's absence up as its own small character beat
rather than only a headcount detail — the lit window in the last clause could be his.

**What I think it tells the reader:** hedged, but it may be doing some work toward making
the group's competence feel casual (nobody hesitates at the fence) right before a night
that is going to test that competence for real.

### Notes

1. "Kavi turns it down at the last second, saying he wants to finish something, and from
   Kavi this always means he has already started it and would rather see the end of it than
   the far side of a fence, so the rest of them go over the fence twenty minutes short of

   be a case of house rule 1's "explaining a character to the reader." Possible fix below.

## The chapter whole

Eight real scenes (plus the two-paragraph summary before scene 1) against four section
breaks. Three of the five marked sections already hold more than one scene, which is the
brief's point demonstrated inside a single chapter rather than argued for in the abstract.
This doesn't read to me as the vignette failure mode — the diner and the fight-plus-argument
carry 72 of the chapter's 79 scene paragraphs between them — but the five short scenes
around those two set pieces (the summary, the departure, the walk, the dorm return, the
chat) could plausibly come down to fewer, larger units.

**A possible merge: the departure and the walk to town (scene 1 into scene 2).** Both are
one paragraph; both are transit, at night, with no dialogue. What would carry across the
seam: the fence-climb ends on "twenty minutes short of midnight," a time cue already in the
prose, so removing the break at line 11 and opening the walk paragraph with something that
carries the same clock forward (rather than a fresh scene-establishing sentence) would knit
them into one "leaving" scene rather than two beats separated by white space. I'm not
confident this is worth doing — the two paragraphs are already adjacent and read cleanly in
sequence — but it's the kind of merge the brief asks after.

**A possible merge: the walk-home argument and the dorm return (scene 6 into scene 7).**
The break at line 163 separates two paragraphs that are otherwise continuous: the group is
still arguing about the waitress in the last line before the break, and "They're back over
the fence a few minutes later" picks up immediately after. What would carry across: the
argument itself could run one clause further, into the climb back over the fence, rather
than stopping cold at a break mark — for instance, ending the walk-home paragraph mid-motion
rather than mid-sentence, so the chat-and-breakfast scene (which does have its own real time
jump, night to morning) is the first place a hard break is earned rather than the second.

**Not proposing:** any change to the shopping cart or the fight, per the protected span; and
no merge of the chat/breakfast scene backward, since it's separated from the dorm return by
an actual jump in time (that night to the next morning), which is a real scene boundary
rather than a section mark doing the dividing.
