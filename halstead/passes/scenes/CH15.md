## Scene 1 — The forge, September to early October

**Location:** the forge, a low building past the kitchens.
**Time:** the first week of September through the second week of October.

**Content:** Chloe starts visiting the forge in September and is increasingly embedded
there by October (her own bench, her own tongs, missing dinner). In the second week of
October, Pruitt tells her without stopping that her heat control has been wrong all
afternoon; she works out what he meant over a few more tries and starts getting it right
more often than not.

**Possible purpose:** may be introducing both a new skill-thread for the fall and a
teaching style — correction without explanation, an expectation that she diagnose her own
mistake — that appears to recur with Pruitt through the rest of the chapter.

**What I think it tells the reader:** may be an early instance, in a new setting, of a
pattern the chapter keeps returning to: nobody explains the fix to her, she has to arrive
at it herself.

### Paragraph walk

1. "The forge is a low building past the kitchens, open Thursdays and most of Sunday, with the doors open in all weather, so the heat reaches her a good way out along with the smell of coke and scale. In the first week of September she walks down for a chisel and stays the whole afternoon watching a man called Pruitt make it. By October she has a bench in the corner nearest the door, her own tongs on the rack above it, and a habit of missing dinner, so on the fourth time that month Sam waits in the doorway in his coat, and she talks at him the whole way back across the grass with a burn on her wrist she keeps to herself." — No issue. Both "so" clauses report a physical or plot chain (open doors carrying heat outward; a habit producing a specific, dated response from Sam) rather than a meaning for the reader, which I think keeps this on the camera side of the line rather than the explaining side.
2. "Pruitt runs the place off a whiteboard carrying the Thursday names and a list of low stock. For the first month the men work around her and leave her alone, so she has no way of telling whether any of it is right until the second week of October, when he passes behind her with an armful of bar." — No issue, on reflection. The "so" here sets up plot-necessary information (why the coming line lands as news to her) rather than naming what a surface causes, means, or costs the way the ruled trailing-explanatory examples do; it resembles the banned shape without doing the banned work, which `HOUSE_RULES.md`'s 2026-08-29 update specifically warns against treating as broken.
3. "\"Your heat's wrong and it has been all afternoon.\"" — No issue.
4. "Without stopping, he leaves her to work out what he meant, which takes a few minutes and another attempt, after which she is right more often than she is wrong." — Possible issue: this reports an outcome ("she is right more often than she is wrong") rather than showing the attempt that gets her there. May be deliberate compression given how much time this chapter covers; flagging it as summary standing in for a demonstrated beat rather than as a rule violation.

4 paragraphs, none thin. No merge needed within the scene.
