## The rule I used for chat-transcript scenes

This chapter is mostly group chat, and a chat transcript is not automatically one scene
or automatically many. The test I used: **a chat exchange is one scene when it is a
single live, real-time back-and-forth** — people answering each other inside the same
sitting, even across a pause the text itself marks as short ("It's almost a full minute
before anyone answers"). The narration's habit of cutting to where each person physically
is when they read or type (kitchen table, shop counter, lab floor) is camera detail
seating the reader in the scene the house rules require ("missing staging"), not a
location change that starts a new scene — a phone call is one scene even though the two
callers are in two different rooms, and I treated a live group chat the same way.

Where the text instead marks real elapsed time between messages — a different day, "the
following week," people replying whenever their own day gets to it rather than answering
each other in the moment — I did not treat that as one continuous scene. This chapter
doesn't have that problem as often as chapter 33 seems to (from what I can see of it in
passing); the long chat runs here read as single sittings. I've flagged the one place a
break sits inside a live chat that keeps running across it, because that's the brief's
point in reverse: the break isn't a scene boundary there either.
