### 1. Arrival and registration (lines 5-21)

**What appears to happen.** Chloe and her parents arrive on the first day and
get through the registration table.

**Content.** The hallway is loud with children and parents (7-11). The parking
lot, on the way in (13). The registration table; the folder, lanyard, and
dining card handed over; a short exchange with the woman running it (15-21).

**Possible purpose.** This may be establishing the scale of the place before
Chloe or the reader has met anyone in it by name — a hundred-odd children, most
with a parent still attached, in a hallway loud enough to be the "first thing."

**What I think it tells the reader.** Possibly that Chloe is one of a great
many in this scene rather than singled out yet, which the following scenes
(the room, the classroom) start to undo one relationship at a time.

### 2. Orientation (23-45)

**What appears to happen.** A woman with a clipboard runs through the
building's rules for a room of new arrivals and their parents.

**Content.** Chloe kicking a chair leg, then stopping (25). Rules given in
summary: meals, laundry days, the nurse's office, lights-out (27-33). A quoted
line about being taken home the same day if she wants (35). A crying boy
nearby (37). A second quoted line, on being sick in the night (39). A man's
question about the laundry bags, answered again (41). The mother writing on
the map, and what she wrote (43-45).

**Possible purpose.** This may be doing double duty: giving the reader the
building's practical shape, and giving the mother a specific thing to fix on
and underline twice, which the scene lets the reader discover rather than
stating outright.

**What I think it tells the reader.** Possibly that the "same day, no
argument" line is aimed less at the children in the room than at the parents,
and that Chloe's mother hears it that way.

### 3. The room, and Priya (47-73)

**What appears to happen.** Chloe is shown to her room, where a girl named
Priya, already unpacked, talks at her about horses until she falls asleep.

**Content.** The room, the window, Priya already settled in (47). Chloe's own
bag left zipped (49). Priya asks whether Chloe rides; Chloe doesn't know what
that means (51). The pony/mini/draft-horse explanation, running long (53-61).
A dispute over whether a specific horse counts as a horse (61-65). Priya's
whole account of the horse, the rival, and the show in April, uninterrupted
(67). Chloe sitting through it (69). Priya asleep within a minute (71). Chloe,
still awake, lying on top of the covers with her shoes on (73).

**Possible purpose.** This may be introducing Priya through the specific shape
of how she talks (at length, unprompted, indifferent to whether the listener
wants it) rather than through a description of her, and using Chloe's
non-reaction (hands still, letting it run) to say something about Chloe
instead.

**What I think it tells the reader.** Possibly that Chloe's silence here is
politeness rather than disinterest, and that her own unopened bag and shoes
kept on are the more important information in the scene, sitting quietly
underneath Priya's monologue.

### 4. The call at the front desk (75-83)

**What appears to happen.** Unable to settle, Chloe goes down to the lobby
phone and asks her mother to come.

**Content.** Chloe waiting for a crying boy ahead of her to finish (75). Her
mother picking up immediately (77). "I want to come there" (79). Her mother's

**Possible purpose.** This may exist mainly to get the mother into the
building without spending time on the mechanics of it, and to let "I want to
come there" stand alone as the whole of what Chloe says.

**What I think it tells the reader.** Possibly that the request costs her
something to make, given how long she waits and reads a noticeboard first
rather than going straight to the phone.

### 5. The hotel room (85-103)

**Content.** The sleeping arrangement, door open, bathroom light on (85). "Do

**Possible purpose.** This may be building a specific offer (any night, if she

saying so.

**What I think it tells the reader.** Possibly that "yes, anything" is the
most exposed thing Chloe says in the chapter, and that her mother's willingness
to talk about nothing in particular, rather than about what's wrong, is itself
the comfort on offer.

### 6. Outside the Pluto door (107-133)

**What appears to happen.** Waiting in line outside the room she'll be

**Content.** The planet names on the doors down the hall, ending at PLUTO

**What I think it tells the reader.** Possibly that the argument is really

for everyone else in the line.

## The chapter whole

**Vignette pattern, and which scenes belong together.** With fourteen scenes
across roughly 3,000 words, half of them under seven paragraphs, I think this
chapter reads as a string of short, distinct pieces rather than as four or
five sustained scenes. Some of that looks structural to what the chapter is
doing — it covers two and a half weeks — but I think at least three of these
fourteen are really one continuous thread each, told across a location or time
cut the way the author's own letter example does, and should be reported (and
possibly written) as one unit rather than several:

**Scenes 3+4+5 — "the first night."** The room and Priya (3), the call at the
front desk (4), and the hotel room (5) are one continuous action — Chloe
unable to settle, going to find her mother, and being brought back — cut
across three locations with almost no dramatized travel between them ("The
hotel is half a mile away, but by the time..." elides the walk entirely). I
think what carries across the seams is Chloe's physical state: still dressed
in scene 3 ("with her shoes on"), and her mother still in her shoes at the
start of scene 5 — the same image, on two people, holding the thread together
across the cut.

**Scenes 6+7+8 — "Pluto, day two."** The door argument (6), meeting Sam inside
(7), and that night's recap call (8) are one thread about the same piece of
information — Pluto's reclassification — moving from an argument among
strangers, to a joke with a new roommate, to Chloe's own retelling to her
mother. What carries across is the material itself (the same fact, restated
three times in three registers) rather than an object, which I think is the
more interesting seam of the three groupings here, since it's the same content
changing shape rather than a physical thing moving forward.

**Scenes 9+10+11 — "the mother's last days."** The dinner confession (9), the
failed fourth-night call (10), and the Sunday departure (11) read to me as one
run building toward the same event — the mother leaving — with the failed
phone call as the quiet middle beat between an admission at dinner and a
goodbye that overexplains itself (the number taped in twice, the instructions
given twice). What carries across is the phone itself: reached for on the
fourth night and not used, then not mentioned again until the mother is
already in the doorway.

**Scenes 12 and 13, and 14: a pattern rather than a merge.** Scene 12 (the
volcano) and scene 13 (mass and weight) put the same shape — a precise,
factual correction — next to two different children and let the reader watch
what each one does with being right. I don't think these should be merged;
the difference in what happens afterward (Kavi shrugs it off and returns to
his drawing, Ruth needs it acknowledged and doesn't get that, and Chloe
watches all day for a consequence that never comes) is what a merge would
erase. Scene 14 is the delayed punchline to scene 12's setup, arriving after
scene 13 has come between them — a callback across a gap rather than an
adjacent pair, so I'm not proposing to move it, only naming that it is doing
that job.

**Scenes 1 and 2** (arrival and orientation) sit next to each other on the
same morning and could be read as one long "drop-off day" scene, but I think
