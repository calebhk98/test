# Scene audit — Chapter 13: Ten Pages

Paragraphs are numbered 1-91 in reading order, skipping the title, the date
line, and the three `________________` marks. The chapter has three section
breaks (four typographic sections) but, per the brief, those are not scene
boundaries and are not used below.

## 1. Scenes

The chapter braids four separate threads across a school year (September to
April): the essay assignment with Hearn, self-defense/paintball/Watch, the
Thanksgiving dinner, and the essay thread's later stretch through Christmas
into spring. Within each thread, time keeps jumping in single sentences
("That night," "On the Tuesday," "In November," "The following week"), so a
scene here is usually short by construction. I count what looks like 20 of
them. That is a high count for a chapter this length, and I say more about
why in section 4.

**Scene 1 — Classroom, September, one class period.** (¶1-4)
Content: Hearn writes Hamilton's name and output rate on the board, then
assigns one ten-page essay a week.
Possible purpose: looks like it is establishing the terms of the essay
thread that runs the rest of the chapter — the standard she is being held to.
What I think it tells the reader: may be setting up Hearn's flat, unadorned
teaching voice, which the rest of his appearances rely on.

**Scene 2 — Chloe's room / school, September.** (¶5-7)
Content: she has written long essays before; that night she reads Federalist
10, writes over the weekend, and the essay comes back with critical feedback
about only presenting one side.
Possible purpose: appears to be the first data point in a "she gets marked
down for the same thing" pattern that recurs later (the third-page note, the
blank mark).
What it tells the reader: hedged — maybe that her early attempts are
confident but one-sided.

**Scene 3 — Classroom, Tuesday.** (¶8-9)
Content: she tells Hearn she read Federalist 10; he says it's Madison's and
moves on.
Possible purpose: looks like a small, deliberately flat correction — no
follow-up from him.
What it tells the reader: maybe nothing more than that he doesn't soften it.

**Scene 4 — Montage, September-November.** (¶10-13)
Content: she works out Hamilton's numbers and starts reading them in
authorship order; the second essay's drafting process (outline, redrafts,
one draft written against her own view); Hearn's November note about the
third page.
Possible purpose: seems to be tracking her process maturing without her
grades necessarily improving — one fix (the third page) doesn't fix the rest
of the essay.
What it tells the reader: hedged, maybe that competence arrives unevenly,
page by page rather than all at once.

**Scene 5 — Mat room, September-November.** (¶14-15)
Content: self-defense starts with falling and slow single-attacker drills
("ones"), moves to "twos" in November.
Possible purpose: looks like groundwork for the January 10v1, so that scene
doesn't start from nothing.
What it tells the reader: hedged.

**Scene 6 — Mat room, January (one afternoon).** (¶16-27)
Content: Kowalczyk introduces the 10v1 drill and explains the math behind
ten attackers; Chloe manages four then six seconds; she, Sam, and Ruth sit
in the corridor after, comparing numbers.
Possible purpose: appears to be the drill's real introduction, with the
corridor scene showing how the three of them process it differently
(Chloe wants the full minute, Ruth and Sam are content with four).
What it tells the reader: maybe that Chloe measures herself against the
stated standard even when nobody else in the room does.

**Scene 7 — Montage, March-June.** (¶28)
Content: single paragraph tracking the class's 10v1 numbers into spring —
Sam's fourteen seconds, Chloe's eleven by June.
Possible purpose: closes out the drill's arc for the year.
What it tells the reader: see Feedback — "but two of them she wasted" may be
doing more self-audit than a child would.

**Scene 8 — Pool, unspecified time.** (¶29)
Content: one paragraph on swimming getting harder over the year.
Possible purpose: unclear. It sits between the 10v1 close-out and the start
of paintball with no connection to either.
What it tells the reader: this one reads as an orphan — see section 4.

**Scene 9 — Mat/paintball room, a Thursday in October.** (¶30-38)
Content: everybody is bad at paintball for weeks; Ruth vents at the wall
bars about always being a beat behind; Chloe works out the "watch the
barrel, not the ball" idea out loud, to Ruth.
Possible purpose: looks like the chapter's centerpiece insight-scene — the
one idea in the chapter that is dramatized rather than reported.
What it tells the reader: maybe that she reasons out loud, at someone,
rather than in silence, and that the insight arrives mid-sentence rather
than as a decision.

**Scene 10 — Same room, the following Monday.** (¶39-40)
Content: Chloe and then Ruth land their first hits.
Possible purpose: the payoff to scene 9.
What it tells the reader: hedged.

**Scene 11 — Same room, the following days and weeks.** (¶41-45)
Content: the technique spreads — Bex picks it up from Chloe's shrug, tells
Bell, it goes up on the board and becomes how the year is taught; Chloe
replays Thursday afterward; Kavi, Nadia, and Priya get it in turn over
November.
Possible purpose: seems to be showing the idea leaving her hands once it's
out loud — she doesn't get named credit, Bex does.
What it tells the reader: maybe something about what she does and doesn't
mind having taken from her, though the scene doesn't say so directly.

**Scene 12 — Hall, second week of September.** (¶46-51)
Content: a briefing on Watch — the scoring rules read out, the range of
drill types described.
Possible purpose: setup for her own first drill later in the chapter.
What it tells the reader: hedged.

**Scene 13 — Building Two / corridor, a Thursday.** (¶52-59)
Content: her first drill happens; the sheet goes up Friday with an F next to
every name; she reads it twice, in the corridor, and Ruth joins her the
second time; they argue about whether item six (the uncovered stairwell) is
fairly aimed at her.
Possible purpose: looks like the fairness-confusion pattern from chapter 1 —
she isn't confused about the rule, she's confused about why the standard
doesn't account for what the drill made possible.
What it tells the reader: maybe that she reads the sheet as a personal
accusation before Ruth talks her down to a shared one.

**Scene 14 — Dining hall, a later dinner.** (¶60-61)
Content: she makes the same argument about the scoring at dinner; Iyad picks

Possible purpose: shows the idea traveling a second time, this time without

What it tells the reader: hedged — see Feedback on the time gap between this
and scene 13.

**Scene 15 — Montage, September onward.** (¶62-63)
Content: Russian, music, and algebra summarized in three short beats.
Possible purpose: filler texture for the school year — breadth without
depth.
What it tells the reader: hedged.

**Scene 16 — Home dining room, Thanksgiving.** (¶64-76)
Content: her father asks about her tariffs essay; a back-and-forth argument
about how to tell a real strategic-industry case from a rhetorical one;
interrupted by the pie.
Possible purpose: the one scene in the chapter that is a single unbroken
conversation, no time-jumps inside it.
What it tells the reader: maybe that this is the only place in the chapter
she gets pushed on an idea by someone on her side, rather than graded or
drilled.

**Scene 17 — Corridor / school, December, into the Christmas break.** (¶77-80)
Content: an essay comes back with no note at all, just a mark; Theo gets the
same and doesn't care; more essays go in before break; over Christmas she
rereads two more Federalist papers at her grandmother's.
Possible purpose: the blank mark as a harder thing to argue with than a
critical one.
What it tells the reader: hedged — see Feedback on ¶78's last sentence.

**Scene 18 — Grandmother's house, Christmas.** (¶81-83)
Content: her grandmother offers her a chair, she says she's been looking at
it, her grandmother mentions Chloe's mother did the same thing on this same
floor.
Possible purpose: a small three-generation echo.
What it tells the reader: hedged.

**Scene 19 — Her room / school, February.** (¶84-88)
Content: a harder essay in the evening, handed in the next morning; it comes
back with only a small note; the essay after that takes a full stuck night,
but this time every page works the way only the third page used to; she
moves on to Burke.
Possible purpose: looks like the chapter's actual turning point — the thing
set up by the November third-page note pays off here.
What it tells the reader: maybe that the fix generalizes only once, quietly,
without anyone remarking on it.

**Scene 20 — Dining hall, April.** (¶89-91)
Content: Sam asks if Hearn has gotten easier; she says no, he hasn't changed
anything; Sam asks why she isn't miserable about it anymore; she says she
doesn't know.
Possible purpose: closing beat for the essay thread — no answer given for
what changed.
What it tells the reader: hedged, and see Feedback on ¶89's sequencing.

## 4. The chapter whole

Twenty scenes in a roughly 3,900-word chapter is high — the brief's rule of
thumb is four to six for a chapter this size, and chapter 1 came down from
thirteen sections to five. This chapter isn't the same shape as chapter 1's
problem, though, and I think that matters for what the fix should be.
Chapter 1 was one throughline (the letter) cut into artificial pieces by
section breaks. This chapter is four genuinely separate threads — the essay
assignment, self-defense/paintball/Watch, the Thanksgiving dinner, and the
essay thread's second half — running in parallel across a school year, each
one legitimately needing more than one scene to cover September to April.
That's a different problem from vignettes, but it produces a similar
symptom: a lot of short units.

Concrete merges, by thread:

**Essay thread, scenes 2-3-4 (¶5-13).** These are already one continuous
piece of prose — first essay, "That's Madison," the Hamilton reading
project, the second essay, the November note — connected by the school year's
own clock ("That night," "On the Tuesday," "That evening," "In November").
I don't think any sentences need to move; I think this should be read and
counted as one scene rather than three, since nothing changes location and
the throughline (her learning what the assignment actually wants) doesn't
break at any of the points I initially treated as boundaries.

**Self-defense thread, scenes 6-7 (¶16-28).** The March-June progress
paragraph (¶28) is a direct continuation of the January drill scene, tracking
the same numbers (seconds held) the corridor conversation was already
arguing about. What carries across: the seconds count itself — four, six,
nine, then Sam's fourteen and Chloe's eleven. Treat as one scene closing on
the June figure rather than two.

**Paintball thread, scenes 9-10-11 (¶30-45).** Already effectively one
scene — frustration, insight, first hits, spread. What carries across: the
"hose" language itself, which travels intact from Chloe's line to Ruth
(¶36) to Bex's report to Bell (¶41), which is the object doing the bridging
work the brief asks for. The only paragraph that doesn't belong is ¶44 (the
marker-weight theory), which doesn't connect backward or forward to
anything and should go.

**Scene 8, swimming (¶29), does not need to exist where it is.** It has no
connection to the 10v1 close-out before it or the paintball opening after
it — no character, no callback, no bridge. If the author wants a beat about
swimming in this chapter, it would need a link to the "watch what's coming,
not what already happened" idea the paintball scene works out — resistance,
reading someone's intent, is arguably close kin to the reactive-vs-anticipatory
lesson that follows it. As written, I'd cut it rather than force a bridge that
isn't in the text now.

**Watch thread, scenes 13-14 (¶52-61).** I'd leave these separate — the drill
day and the later dinner conversation are genuinely different days, and
merging them would erase that Ruth argues her down first and Chloe still
makes the same case to a wider table later. If anything, this pair
needs a small addition (a "the next evening" or similar) rather than a
merge, since the current text lets a reader assume it's the same dinner
already covered in ¶57.

After these three scene-level merges and the one cut, the count comes down
from 20 to roughly **14** (two three-scene groups reduced to one each, minus
two more; the numbers: 20 − 2 (scenes 2-4) − 1 (scenes 6-7) − 2 (scenes 9-11)
− 1 (scene 8 cut) = 14). Fourteen is still above the four-to-six range the
brief gives for a chapter this length, but I think the remaining count is a
property of the four-thread structure rather than the vignette problem
chapter 1 had — further reduction would mean cutting an entire thread (for
example, deciding the chapter doesn't need both the Watch drill and the
Thanksgiving dinner) rather than combining paragraphs, and that's a call
about content, not prose, so I'm flagging it rather than recommending it.
