## Stage three: clear each paragraph against the list

Findings are numbered for reference. Each is tagged with the checklist category it falls under and, where warranted, marked *unsure*.

**1. [Voice / Reaction] L8 — "None of them have that kind of patience. The program does."**
This is the narrator stating a flat fact about all seven of them and drawing the reader's conclusion rather than showing it. Taken as a claim about the cast, it doesn't hold up against the rest of the material: Ruth is established as someone who sits on an unsolved problem for months without going public (RUTH.md: "Works at a stuck problem alone for months before mentioning to anyone that she's stuck," citing her ten-month MIT silence) and Kavi keeps a literal years-long list of unsolved questions he works privately (KAVI.md: "keeps a list of every question he's tried to answer and failed... the ones that have stayed open for years"). Two of the seven people the sentence is dismissing are specifically defined elsewhere by exactly the patience it says none of them have.

**2. [Order / Objects] L5, L11, L31 — "The financial one takes eleven weeks" vs. a month of testing plus two more months of results.**
Line 5 states the whole financial investigation takes eleven weeks. Line 11 gives a month of testing against Nadia's systems "before it goes anywhere." Line 31 then gives "the following two months" for what it finds once deployed. A month plus two months is about thirteen weeks before Ruth has even started building the proof described afterward — already past the eleven-week total the chapter opens on. The chapter's own dateline (L3, "November 2025 – February 2026") spans roughly sixteen weeks, which doesn't resolve the mismatch either.

**3. [Order / Reaction] L11 → L31 — the deployment itself is never shown.**
The tool is tested for a month "against Nadia's own systems before it goes anywhere" (L11). The very next paragraph is the result of running it against the actual target for two months (L31). There's no paragraph for the handoff — nothing confirming the test passed, nothing placing the tool where it's actually supposed to be watching. The chapter skips directly from private test to live result.

**4. [Reaction / Position — highest confidence] L135 — "chloe: theo that was a drill."**
Per SYNOPSIS_CHARACTERS_TIMELINE.md's own account of the ch15 "Twelve" intrusion — the same night Theo is describing at L126 (independently confirmed as the same event by chronology/BOOK.md, which cites ch27 dating "the ch15 intruder drill" to "six years ago," the identical phrase used here) — Chloe was one of the four students who physically fought, tied up, and monitored the vital signs of the real armed men. She would know with certainty that it was not a drill; she wrote the incident report from inside it. Having her assert "that was a drill," requiring Theo to correct her with "it wasnt a drill. i read the file" (L138), gives her the wrong side of a fact she has first-hand, physical knowledge of.

**5. [Objects / Reaction, cross-referenced against character sheets] L126, L129 — "four of us put them on the floor."**
Theo states he personally was one of the four who subdued the intruders. But SAM.md, describing the identical scene, places the four as Chloe, Kavi, Ruth, and Sam — "Sam gets there last... while Kavi and Ruth are already working the problem," implying a fixed four that does not include Theo. THEO.md itself only says he "was present the night of an armed intrusion... which he experienced then as an unannounced overnight drill" — present at the school, not necessarily one of the four combatants. If Theo wasn't one of the four, his "four of us" misattributes his own role in the group's history, and it goes unchallenged by Sam, who (per his own sheet) was actually there. *Unsure* only insofar as the reference material's own account of who exactly made up "the four" isn't perfectly unambiguous, but the weight of it points away from Theo.

**6. [Then-the-context: how fast they work] L123 — "we spotted him in a day."**
The infiltrator's identification is dated elsewhere as "a day and a half" in two places — SYNOPSIS_CHARACTERS_TIMELINE.md's timeline table ("The infiltrator: one day and a half, then gone") and THEO.md's own facts sheet ("identified within a day and a half"). A separate line inside SYNOPSIS's own character section says "twenty-four hours," so the reference material isn't fully self-consistent either — flagging this *unsure*, but the majority reading is "a day and a half," not "a day."

**7. [Then-the-context: how fast they work, unsure] L126 — "six years ago."**
Chronology/BOOK.md verifies "six years ago" as an accurate figure specifically as stated in Chapter 27, where Theo is "in his eighth month" at his job (roughly age 18–19). Restating the same "six years ago" here, without the interval since Chapter 27 accounted for, likely undercounts the true gap by at least a year or two. Flagged *unsure* since chronology/BOOK.md itself lists Chapter 32's exact date as underdetermined.

Aside from Chloe's phone on a desk at L141, no paragraph in the chapter places anybody anywhere. Nadia's threat, Ruth's three-part proof, Theo's disclosure of an armed government raid on the school he grew up in — all of it happens with zero physical staging. This may be the established format for this book's chat chapters, but per the brief's own instruction ("a conversation happening nowhere is a finding"), it's worth logging: the single most consequential chapter in this stretch of the book (identifying "him," learning the government has a file, learning the school was really attacked) gives the reader nowhere to picture any of it happening.

Never specified further — accounting software, the diagnostic-hardware company's production systems, something else. A category word standing in for a particular one.

**14. [Redundancy, minor] L120 and L152 — Theo restating his own point.**
L120: "theyve been wrong twice and theyre still wrong." L152: "they have nineteen years of being wrong." The second doesn't add information the first didn't already establish; it's the same claim in slightly different words, from the same speaker, a few lines apart in the same conversation.

**15. [Objects, unsure] L34 — "somewhere around 1998 or 2001."**
Two candidate start years are given for the financial trail, seven or eight years before the school itself is placed as having been founded elsewhere in the reference material (chronology/BOOK.md and SYNOPSIS both put Halstead's founding at "roughly nineteen years before the end of the book"). It's plausible the founder was building capital before founding the school, so this isn't flagged as a contradiction — only noted, *unsure*, as a gap the chapter doesn't address: what was he doing with a hidden financial operation for the better part of a decade before there was a school to fund.

**16. [Objects, minor] L114 — "somebody handed the new guy a retirement box."**
Whose retirement, and how a departing federal employee's personal effects came to contain material "so far above" a brand-new analyst that he shouldn't have seen it, is never addressed. Left as an unexplained object with no follow-up anywhere in the chapter.

**17. [Order, unsure] L108 — "Eleven minutes pass before Theo posts again."**
Eleven minutes is offered as the gap between Nadia's guess that the government might have a file (L105) and Theo confirming it. What Theo actually discloses in that gap goes far beyond confirming a guess — full operational history, including an incident he was told for years was a drill. Eleven minutes reads as a short deliberation window before deciding to break with a stated instruction ("nobody repeats any of this," L114) about material this sensitive, though this is consistent with Theo's established pattern of running the whole disclosure out once he starts (TA.md, "When they talk at length"), so it's logged *unsure* rather than as a firm contradiction.
