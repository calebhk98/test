## Stage two: sit in every chair

**Aymar (background student, stopwatch).** *I am* a classmate timing the clay machine on my own
initiative. *What I've been given* is a printed card that contradicts forty timed throws I've
personally logged. *What I'd actually do* is exactly what's shown — hand the number to the
instructor and let the safety-relevant fact stand, once, without pushing further once he's moved
the line on. The text's Voss does not contradict him or concede on the page; two days later a new
card just appears. That gap (finding #12 below) is real: the text shows Aymar being right and Voss
never says so.

**Voss (marksmanship instructor).** *I am* the instructor for live weapons, responsible for both
safety and pace. *What I've been handed* is a subordinate correcting my printed material to my
face, in front of the whole line, with data. *What I'd actually do* is either defend the card, ask
a clarifying question, or concede on the spot — Voss does none of these; he says "the card says
nine" and, three lines later, changes the subject to "Firing point four, you're up." The chapter
never closes the loop on whether he was persuaded, embarrassed, or simply didn't care; the new
card two days later is the only evidence, and it's narrated, not attributed to him.

**Kowalczyk (self-defense instructor).** *I am* running a headcount-based drill with a hard rule

and this checks out cleanly against her established voice (eyes on the board, no exception-making).
No finding here; flagged only because the exercise should be run on staff too, and this one holds up.

**The engineering teacher (unnamed, one line).** *I am* the instructor who received both the
