## Stage one flags (required by the brief)

- **Resisted restatement:** (L29, L32) "Then the last paragraph." / "And a line under that." Both
  name the physical/structural position of text *within the document itself* (a paragraph, a line
  underneath another line) rather than describing an event in the world. Restating them as "next,
  the file said..." loses the odd meta-register; restating them literally ("then the last
  paragraph [of the document happened]") doesn't parse as an event at all.
- **Said something that could not happen:** (L8) Theo's real-time physical reaction to reading his
  own file (closing the laptop, leaving for twenty minutes) cannot be content of the file being
  surveyed, and cannot be something the viewpoint character witnessed without being physically
  present — see Stage three #1.
- **Already said, in different words:** (L72) "ruth: it says here it doesnt match any known
  method" restates L29's "No known method" almost verbatim — see Stage three #5.

## Stage two: sit in every chair

**Theo (L8), given the note in his own file.** I am a federal analyst who has spent his career
reading documents for a living. What I've just been shown is something in my own dossier severe
enough to make me shut a laptop and leave the room. What I would actually do: per Theo's own sheet
this is exactly his established pattern — process privately, say nothing yet, come back later with
it worked out. Consistent. But note for Stage three below: nobody in the chat that follows
acknowledges his absence, asks where he is, or references the note that supposedly rattled him —
six people are shown reading the same trove of files and only Theo's reaction gets reported, then
dropped.

**Ruth (L69–L146), being told her own competence back to her by a federal document.** I am the
person who built the tool. What I've just read is a government assessment calling my weekend
project unexplainable six years on. What I would do: by her sheet, exactly what she does here —
go quiet, then finally admit the size of it under pressure from someone she trusts (Kavi), and end
up asking the harder question ("how good are we"). This tracks her established arc.

**Kavi (L83–92), reading the same file next to her.** I am the one person whose correction of her
she accepts. What I've just seen is Ruth minimizing something enormous in real time. What I would
do: push her to actually read her own words back rather than let her flatten it — which is what
happens. Consistent with his sheet ("the one person whose correction of her she accepts").

**Nadia (L104, L110), reading in from outside the specific memory.** I am the one who wasn't
necessarily there for the Waffle House night (unstated whether she was one of the four). What I've
been shown is a stranger's-eye statistic: six years, still unsolved. What I would do: state it
flat, without editorializing, which is exactly her register. Consistent, but see Stage three below
— nothing establishes whether Nadia was one of the four or is speaking as an outsider, and the
text doesn't say.

**Eli (L137–146), asked twice by Ruth how good they are.** I am someone who has only ever been
ranked inside Halstead. What I've just been asked is a question I structurally cannot answer, per
his own sheet's Known Problems #1: he has never had an outside comparison class. What I would do:
say "I don't know" honestly, twice, then land on the one true data point he has. Consistent —
though the sheet itself flags that the chapter never shows the reader *why* his ignorance is
structural rather than falsely modest; a reader meeting this line cold has no way to know that.

**Sam (L52–58, L128), present for an incident where — per the file — he did all the fighting.** I
am the one who (per L23, "one of the four did all of it") single-handedly took seven armed men.
What I've just read is a federal case file about my own fight. What I would actually do: Sam's
established pattern (per his own sheet) is to run a long, point-by-point correction any time an
official record of him doesn't match what he knows happened. Here the record seems to match what
happened (fast, one of them did it, three watched) — so his silence on the substance and his
focus on the shoe/trolley trivia is plausible. Marked *unsure* rather than a clean finding, since
nothing in the file actually misstates his role for him to correct.

**Chloe (throughout), whoever is doing the reading that produces paragraphs 1–10.** I am the
viewpoint character. What I've apparently been given is not just my own dossier but insight into
Sam's file being "thickest," and into Theo's private, physical reaction to a note in *his* file,
in real time. What I would actually know, tight-third: my own file's contents, plausibly a
comparison across files if I'm physically paging through the trove — but not a solitary,
simultaneous, offsite reaction from Theo unless I witnessed it or was told about it. This is the
single largest crack in the chapter; see Stage three.

## Stage three: clear each paragraph against the list

1. **Voice/Position — the strongest single finding.** (L8) "Theo's has a note on it that makes
   him close his laptop and go outside for twenty minutes." This is a live, present-tense account
   of Theo's private physical reaction happening as he reads his own copy of the file — not
   something a dossier could contain about itself, and not something the viewpoint character
   (confirmed as Chloe by L116, "Chloe reads yes twice...") could perceive unless she is either in
   the room with him or told about it afterward by someone else. Nothing on the page supplies
   either. Compare L2's "Sam's is the thickest and the newest," which at least could be Chloe's
   own comparison while physically paging through the trove — L8 cannot be that. *unsure whether
   this rises to a plot error or is meant as omniscient-narrator license, but it is a clean break
   in an otherwise tight-third book.*

2. **Contradiction — "one line" vs. three lines.** (L66) "Ruth goes quiet, then posts one line."
   is followed immediately by three separate `ruth:` messages (L69, L72, L75) with no reaction
   from anyone in between. The narration promises one line and the text delivers three. This isn't
   a stylistic quibble — it's a countable claim the dialogue directly contradicts.

3. **Objects — unattributed "sloppy" line.** (L23) "Two of them independently report being told,
   during or just after, that this had been sloppy and needed more practice." No indication of
   which of the four students said this, or any reaction to the fact that a kid critiqued armed
   robbers' technique mid-robbery. The detail just sits there. *unsure — could be intentionally
   left oblique as part of the documentary voice.*

4. **Specificity — "the two units either side."** (L29) Corroborating cameras are "the Waffle
   House, the two units either side, a traffic camera, and a municipal camera up the road" — "the
   two units either side" never says what kind of building. Given the whole passage reads as a
   summarized government document, bureaucratic vagueness may be in-voice rather than a flaw;
   flagged *unsure*.

5. **Redundancy.** (L72) "ruth: it says here it doesnt match any known method" restates fact
   already given directly to the reader at L29 ("No known method"). Arguably justified as
   character beat (Ruth reacting to reading it herself) rather than filler — *unsure*.

6. **Chronology — "six years back" contradicts the chapter's own place in the timeline.**
   (L14–15) "an incident report from six years back" describing the Waffle House/shopping-trolley
   night. `chronology/BOOK.md` (sections 2.1 and 3.4) independently derives, from this exact line
   plus ch34's position directly after ch33 ("four months into" the job Chloe starts at 20), that
   Chloe is roughly 20–22 here — putting the diner night at roughly age 14–16. That does not
   comfortably match the diner night's current placement in the manuscript (immediately after
   "Sixteen," implying 16–17), and BOOK.md flags this exact arithmetic as broken, in contrast to
   a parallel "six years ago" reference in ch27 that checks out cleanly. This is a corroborated,
   pre-existing dating problem and this chapter's own line (L14) is the evidence that exposes it.

7. **Ruth's age at time of the tool vs. her own account of why she built it.** (L69, L95, L98,
   L131) Ruth says four separate times she "was thirteen" when she built the box "so we wouldnt
   get detention" — i.e., she frames the build as tied to needing to avoid detention, which reads
   as tied to the sneak-out itself. If the incident is genuinely "six years back" from a
   ~20–22-year-old present (L14), that puts the incident at ~14–16, a year or two after she says
   she built the tool at thirteen. The chapter doesn't show her reconciling this — it's left to
   the reader to assume the box predates the incident and was simply reused. *unsure, tied
   directly to finding 6 above.*

8. **Reaction — Theo is set up and then dropped.** (L8) establishes Theo's private reaction to his
   own file, but he never appears in the group chat that follows (L40–146), and nobody in the chat
   references him, his file, or his reaction at any point across 36 more chat lines covering the
   same trove of files. Given the character sheet confirms he's normally part of this same core
   group chat (cited directly against chapters/32 and 33), his total absence — unremarked — from a
   conversation about the very files that just upset him is a gap. *unsure — consistent with his
   sheet's "wants it to be somebody else's problem," but the chat's total silence about him is
   still conspicuous.*

9. **Objects — the "channel" vs. the camera-box are two different unknowns, never reconciled.**
   (L11) "collection against the channel comes back empty" refers to the graduates' communication
   method. (L21) Ruth's box is about camera corruption, a different capability entirely. The
   chapter never states whether these are meant to be the same unsolved mystery or two separate
   ones the file-writers haven't connected — it reads as if the reader is expected to already
   know, which (per the brief's caution on institutional vocabulary) may be fair for a book this
   far in, but is worth flagging as a possible unclear join. *unsure.*

10. **Position — where is anyone, physically, during the chat?** L8's Theo is "outside." Nobody
    else is placed anywhere — not the room, not the country, not even "at home" or "at Halstead."
    A conversation this emotionally loaded (Ruth's confession, Kavi's confrontation) happens with
    zero physical staging outside the "goes quiet" beat at L66, which is itself about
    conversational silence, not body or location.

11. **Order — Kavi's "read that back" (L86, L92) responds to text posted before a scene-break
    divider (L78) separates it from his reply.** If the divider marks a time-gap, "read that
    back," referring precisely to what Ruth just typed, implies no gap; if it marks no time-gap,
    the divider's presence is doing nothing. Not clearly a factual error, closer to a formatting
    ambiguity, but it sits right at the joint the brief asks to check ("does this depend on
    something that has not happened yet"). *unsure, low confidence.*

12. **Voice — Nadia's standing relative to the incident is never established.** (L104, L110)
    Nadia comments on the Waffle House file with outsider-flavored language ("and six years later
    the federal government wrote...") but nothing in the chapter (or her own character sheet,
    which lists her as appearing from ch16 on but never places her at the diner night) confirms
    whether she is one of the four who were there or a bystander in the group commenting from
    outside. The chapter reads ambiguously on a fact that actually matters (was she one of the
    four, or wasn't she).

13. **Reaction sizing — "All seven come back with a blank on what the four looked like" (L26)
    gets no reaction paragraph before the chapter moves straight to camera footage (L29).** Seven
    armed adults being unable to describe four teenagers who calmly disarmed and treated them is
    exactly the kind of remarkable-thing-passing-without-reaction the brief flags — though per the
    brief's own "competence is not an omission" caveat, this may simply be the documentary voice
    declining to editorialize, consistent with the register of the whole opening section.
    *unsure, low confidence, likely a non-finding given the caveat.*

14. **Voice — narrator authority over "the thickest and the newest" (L8) is unsupported.** No
    reason is given for why Sam's file would be thickest (more incidents on record?) or newest
    (most recently updated?) — a comparison stated as fact with nothing behind it. Weak on its
    own; paired with finding 1, it's part of the same passage's odd vantage point over material
    the viewpoint character shouldn't have full access to.

15. **Redundancy — Ruth's "i was thirteen and it was a weekend and i wasnt trying" is stated in
    close variants four separate times (L95, L98, L101, L131) within sixty lines**, without any of
    the retellings adding new information the others didn't already carry. This is flagged even
    though it reads as a plausible emotional-loop beat (per her sheet, she "re-derives... from the
    start" under Kavi's pressure) — the repetition itself is the point of that scene per the style
    sheet, so this is marked *unsure*, closer to a style note than a hard finding, and included
    only because the brief asks for redundancy to be checked explicitly.

16. **Voice — the document-structure asides sit oddly next to the live-reaction content in the
    same passage.** (L29) "Then the last paragraph" and (L32) "And a line under that" both
    describe the physical layout of the file being read (this is its last paragraph; here's a
    line underneath a line) — pure document description. That register is incompatible with L8's
    same-passage claim to know Theo's real-time bodily reaction to reading his copy. The section
    can't consistently be "these are the file's own words" and "here is what happened as someone
    read them" without saying whose experience is being reported.

17. **Order — Ruth's "so we wouldnt get detention" implies a standing, repeated need the synopsis
    doesn't support.** (L69) The phrasing is general/habitual ("so we wouldnt get detention," not
    "so we wouldnt get caught that night"), suggesting the box was built for ongoing use against a
    recurring problem. `SYNOPSIS_CHARACTERS_TIMELINE.md` describes the sneak-out this incident
    report covers as "the first students ever to do it." If it really was their first unauthorized
    trip off campus, a bespoke anti-detection tool built specifically "so we wouldnt get detention"
    sits oddly with there being no prior detention risk to build it against. *unsure — could simply
    mean the box was built for ordinary daytime mischief and only later repurposed for this one
    outing, which the text doesn't rule out either way.*

18. **Reaction — no shown trigger for Ruth going quiet.** (L61–66) Immediately after Sam's "we were
    so bad that night" (L58), a scene break, then "Ruth goes quiet, then posts one line." Nothing
    on the page shows her re-reading the "No known method" line or otherwise marks what tips her
    from banter into the confession that follows — the pivot happens entirely off-page, between
    two section dividers, without so much as a beat establishing what she's looking at. *unsure —
    plausible that she's simply been sitting with L29–32 since the file survey and this is a
    delayed reaction, but the text doesn't say so.*

19. **Order/specificity — "read it back" is answered with something else.** (L92–95) Kavi's literal
    demand is that Ruth reread aloud what she wrote ("no. read it back"), referring back to her own
    line at L72 ("it doesnt match any known method"). Her actual answer — "i was thirteen" — isn't
    that line read back; it's a different fact entirely. The scene plays as Ruth complying in
    spirit (finally engaging honestly) rather than literally doing the thing Kavi asked for, which
    the dialogue never marks or lampshades. *unsure — likely intentional shorthand, since Ruth's own
    character sheet treats this exchange as her "giving in," but the literal mismatch between
    instruction and response is there on the page.*

    only ever had two ranked rooms in his life (an unmeasured home, and Halstead). This is a

21. **Reaction — Chloe is silent throughout Ruth's confession.** (L69–146) Aside from the passive

    Kavi/Ruth exchange or the "how good are we" question that follows, despite her own character

22. **Objects — "the channel" (L11) and Ruth's camera-box (L21, L75) are presented as separate
    unsolved mysteries in the file and never explicitly reconciled.** The standing surveillance

23. **Specificity — "a traffic camera, and a municipal camera up the road" (L29).** "Up the road"

24. **Reaction — seven armed adults unable to describe any of four teenagers (L26) gets no

25. **Voice — unexplained comparative claim.** (L8) "Sam's is the thickest and the newest" is
