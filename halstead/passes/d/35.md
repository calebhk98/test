Three things noticed while restating:
- L90 resists clean restatement — "we keep not noticing" reads as a literal claim of ongoing ignorance, but the chapter's entire premise is that they did notice. It only works as "we keep *acting like* we haven't noticed," which isn't what it says.
- L46 and L49 together can't be restated without contradiction: Eli names "follow it" as one of only three legitimate responses, then treats "put it back running" as proof the actor *isn't* doing one of the three things — but leaving a tool live to observe it is what "following" looks like.
- L107 said something I'd already logged: the "wrong ... three times" figure. Ch32 (already read for context) gives this figure as two, not three — flagged below.

## Stage 2: sitting in the chairs

**Ruth**, handed the group's "the worm went quiet and came back clean" report (L17): her established move under uncertainty is a flat declarative, never an open question — she "collapses an open question into two concrete options" or states a correction outright (characters/RUTH.md). What she does on the page (L20) is ask a bare, ungrounded question with no options attached. That's a real gap between what the sheet says she'd do and what she does.

**Eli**, told by Chloe that the man is signaling (L34-37): his own rule is never to soften a correct call to spare feelings (characters/ELI.md, "Would never" #2). What he does (L43) is open the correction with "im sorry." Small, but it's the one thing the sheet says he doesn't do.

**Kavi**, in the position of explaining why he alone caught the gap (L55): his established habit under anything that could read as impressive is to name the actual mechanism or cost, never to assert personal exceptionalism (characters/KAVI.md, "Signature," "Would they say this?"). What he says is "the only reason i built that is because im me" — no mechanism named, just the claim of being himself. That's the one move the sheet says isn't his.

**Sam**, reading the same chat as a currently-serving soldier (L84): what he'd actually want, per his sheet, is a fact, a duration, or a rule ("his questions request a fact, a duration, a rule, a number"). "so we do nothing" is a flat declarative summary rather than a request for either, but it's short and closed the way his lines are — this one holds up.

**Theo**, reading that the man made one mistake (L93-96): per his own sheet this ends-on-an-open-question habit is documented as intentional (characters/THEO.md:170, citing this exact line). Checked against the sheet and cleared — not a finding, noted only so it isn't mistakenly flagged.

**Chloe** (viewpoint throughout): what she knows going in is that the group has a 99.99%-certainty identification of the man (ch32) and a live tool watching him (ch33); what she's handed here is evidence he found that tool. Her established reaction to hard information on the page elsewhere in the book is physical and specific — "reads that one twice and sets the phone face down on the desk before she picks it back up" (chapters/32_the_money.md:141). What ch35 gives her at the equivalent moment (L104) is only a time and an hour, no physical tell. Minor, flagged below under Position.

## Stage 3: findings

Checked against the brief's two non-findings before writing each of these down: none of these turn on a character not having been told something on the page, and none turn on flat competence/calm being read as a missing beat.

1. L11, "He pulls the logs." — "He" has no antecedent anywhere earlier in the chapter; the first name given is "eli" at L17, six paragraphs later, in dialogue, which only makes "he" = Eli an inference, never a stated fact. **Objects.**
2. L20, "ruth: could it be the government" — Ruth's sheet: "questions | 0% measured; she asks by flat declarative, never a question mark" and "NO if the line ends in a question mark rather than a flat declarative demand" (characters/RUTH.md). This line is an open question with no options collapsed into it, which isn't a form the sheet allows her. **Voice / context.**
3. L23, "eli: if it was the government we would be in custody." — stated as settled fact with no supporting reasoning, and in tension with ch32's own portrait of a government that has watched the school for nineteen years without ever moving to custody (chapters/32_the_money.md:117,120). *Unsure.*
4. L46, "you kill it, you follow it, or you feed it garbage. you dont put it back" — "follow it" ordinarily means leaving the tool live to watch it, which is functionally "putting it back." The line treats these as mutually exclusive without drawing the distinction (covert restore vs. overt follow) that would make them not the same thing.
5. L49, "eli: unless you think nobody will notice it went" — immediately adds a fourth case ("someone confident enough to restore it") to the "one of three things" just laid down at L46, which the three-item list doesn't have room for as stated.
6. L55, "kavi: ...the only reason i built that is because im me" — see Stage 2. Names no mechanism, which is the one move his sheet says isn't his; also fails **Specificity** (a person built to reach for the particular mechanism reaches instead for a claim about himself).
7. L43, "eli: chloe im sorry but no" — softens a correction with an apology; characters/ELI.md, "Would never" #2: "Would never soften a correct technical call to spare someone's feelings." *Unsure — could be read as chat-burst texture rather than the "would never" rule engaging.*
8. L61, "eli: hes very slightly wrong about how good we are" — no concrete measure given, in a book (and a chapter) otherwise built on exact figures: nine minutes, six weeks, "99.99" in ch32. **Specificity.** *Unsure.*
9. L90, "eli: it keeps running and it keeps dying and we keep not noticing" — see restatement note; resists being written back without contradicting the chapter's own premise that they did notice. Only coheres as "we keep acting as if we haven't," which the line doesn't say.
10. L58, "hes not signalling. he made a mistake" — this conclusion follows immediately after Kavi's explanation of why only their tooling would catch the gap (L52-55), but that explanation shows only that they alone could detect it — it doesn't bear on whether the man's action was deliberate or accidental. The two lines are placed as if one supports the other. *Unsure.*
11. L107, "and which has three wrong answers on file" — contradicts chapters/32_the_money.md:120: "theo: they think its probably a state or a company. theyve been wrong twice and theyre still wrong." The manuscript's only stated count anywhere is two. **Context/continuity.**
12. L107, "seven people in their twenties settled it in eleven weeks" — chapters/32_the_money.md gives eleven weeks for the build-and-test phase alone (32:5, 32:11: "The financial one takes eleven weeks" / "They test it for a month"), then states the identification itself — the shape, the proof, the "its him" — took "the following two months" on top of that (32:31). By the chapter's own math the man wasn't actually identified in eleven weeks; that number covers only the tool-building. **Context/continuity.**
13. L107, closing sentence as a whole — the narrator states the dramatic irony directly (a government hunting a man that seven kids already caught) rather than dramatizing it, in the story's own closing beat. **Voice.** *Unsure — a chapter-ending summary line may be a deliberate exception to the general no-editorializing rule.*
14. L107, "does her job, which is the job of a government that has been trying to find this man" — Chloe's actual job, per chapters/30_cleared.md and characters/CHLOE.md, is the Foreign Service (a diplomatic posting), not intelligence or law enforcement; the sentence equates her specific job with the government's broader manhunt as if they're the same pursuit. *Unsure — likely intentional irony, flagged in case not.*
15. L8, "A check-in doesn't arrive." — "check-in" as a mechanism belonging to the worm/watcher doesn't appear anywhere else in the manuscript (checked with grep across all 35 chapter files); it's introduced fully-formed with no setup. **Objects.**
16. **Position** — across the bulk of the chapter's dialogue (L17-23, L34-61, L72-96), there is almost no physical grounding for any speaker. Contrast chapters/24_the_chat.md, which gives every speaker in the same chat an elaborate physical situation for each message (phone against a monitor, texting one-handed while counting a till, etc.). The only embodied beats in ch35 are Eli pulling logs (L11), Ruth's hesitation before sending (L69), and Chloe reading at 1am (L104). *Unsure — chat compression may be a deliberate pacing choice for a higher-stakes scene.*
17. **Position**, viewpoint asymmetry — Chloe is the book's viewpoint character throughout, but she isn't grounded anywhere in the first two of the chapter's three chat exchanges (L17-61); she only appears, reading, at the third (L104). No signal marks why the first two get no reaction beat from her at all.
18. Between L64 and L69, no elapsed time is given, unlike the chapter's other two section breaks, which both state one explicitly: L31 "It happens twice more in six weeks," and the L99→L104 jump to "one in the morning." The middle break is the odd one out inside its own chapter. **Order.**
19. L5, "The worm goes quiet on a Thursday in June." — no specific date, unusually vague next to the book's habit of marking days precisely elsewhere (e.g. chapters/06_the_list.md:5, "the last Wednesday," "twenty-seven days in"). **Specificity.** *Unsure/minor.*
20. L31, "It happens twice more in six weeks." — the two further incidents are never individually dated, only totaled, unlike the specific "Thursday in June" given for the first. *Unsure/minor.*
21. L8, "Same state, same position, exactly as it was." — "position" has no clear referent for a software process. *Unsure/minor.* **Specificity.**
22. L14, "reports a healthy process across the whole window" — "the whole window" is never given a duration or boundary. *Unsure/minor.* **Objects.**
23. L34-37 (Chloe's "message" theory) — draws a reply only from Eli; nobody else, notably not Ruth (established as unable to leave an unjustified claim standing "in anyone's mouth including her own," characters/RUTH.md), responds to it at all. **Reaction.** *Unsure.*
24. L78, "ruth: thats the only thing we have" — treats the information-asymmetry (he doesn't know they know) as the *only* asset they hold, discounting the 99.99%-certainty identification and financial history built across chapters 32-33. *Unsure — may be deliberate emphasis rather than a literal claim.* **Specificity/logic.**
25. L17, "somebody stopped it and started it and cleaned up after themselves" — gender-neutral ("somebody... themselves") for the intruder, while every reference to him from L34 onward in the same chapter switches to "he/him" with no stated moment inside ch35 where the group settles on that. (Gender was fixed in ch32 — "its him. one man," chapters/32_the_money.md:73 — so the shift is defensible, but nothing in ch35 itself marks it.) *Unsure/minor.*
26. L37, "thats a message" — Chloe's read of intent, but no motive is offered anywhere for why the man would want to send one rather than simply staying hidden, which Eli's own framework at L46 treats as the default safer move. *Unsure/minor.* **Specificity.**
27. L87, "we dont look at him looking at us" — in tension with the fact that they keep Kavi's watcher running continuously (L14, and implicitly ongoing) — the plan explicitly includes automated monitoring, i.e., "looking," even while the line says they won't look. Readable as "we won't tip our hand," but on its face it contradicts the surveillance apparatus already established as staying on. *Unsure/minor.* **Order/logic.**
28. Chloe's reaction at L104 lacks the physical tell the book elsewhere establishes for her processing hard news — compare "Chloe reads that one twice and sets the phone face down on the desk before she picks it back up" (chapters/32_the_money.md:141). Ch35 gives only a time and an hour of sleep lost. *Unsure/minor.* **Reaction/Position.**
29. L84-90 (the decision to do nothing) — ch33 built an explicit stop-mechanism for exactly this class of contingency: "what happens if any one of the seven says stop, which is that all of it ends inside the hour with no argument" (chapters/33_the_other_one.md:43). Ch35 presents the clearest evidence yet that the operation has been detected by its target, and nobody invokes or even mentions that mechanism — the decision to keep running is reached (L84-90) as if the option to stop inside the hour didn't already exist on the page. *Unsure.* **Order/Redundancy** — a safety valve introduced two chapters earlier goes unmentioned at the one moment it was built for.
