## Stage three: findings

Both non-finding checks were run against every candidate below before it was kept: (1) is this a character not knowing something, not the text being wrong, and (2) is a flat/absent reaction just this cast's trained competence. Neither carve-out applies to the items kept.

**Arithmetic / factual consistency**

1. Contact counts are 2, 4, and 1 ("a two-man contact," line 14; "four of them," line 17; "by a man," singular throughout line 20), but Sam's own recap calls them "three separate two-and-fours" (line 43) — the third contact was a lone attacker, not a two-to-four-person team. This isn't a knowledge gap: Sam lived contact three and felt it was one man ("he's got the man's weight going where he wants it," line 20), so he isn't misremembering a fact he never had.
2. The same line is a voice violation on top of the arithmetic: SAM.md's dial table gives him "0% [hedging] on anything factual [or] tactical... that stays absolute," yet "two-and-fours" is loosely wrong about a tactical fact he was present for — out of character for the one person in the cast who is never sloppy with a number.

**Reaction**

3. The major gets one silent beat ("The major's eyebrows go up," line 40) in response to Sam's mild question (line 37), then no reaction of any kind — no line, no gesture, nothing — to the much bigger provocation at line 43 (an unsolicited critique of the exercise's design plus a request to make it harder). The text jumps straight to "It goes upward anyway" (line 46) without showing what the major actually said or did.
4. The third-contact attacker (line 20) spent six hours in position, "does everything right" per the report, and loses in under two seconds — and then never appears again in any capacity. No reaction, no consequence, no aftermath shown for the person the paragraph is grammatically about ("The man does everything right... He has the position, the timing, the surprise, and the ground").
5. *Unsure.* The final text, "sam: i genuinely thought there was a real one" (line 61), gets no reply from Ruth or Nadia in the chapter — the exchange just stops. Group-chat scenes elsewhere in the book do cut off this way, so this is weak.

**Position**

6. The entire debrief (lines 28–46) has no staging at all: no room, no other people mentioned, nothing beyond "Sam sits forward" (line 37) and "The major's eyebrows go up" (line 40). A multi-line conversation with real stakes happens nowhere.
7. *Unsure.* Nothing in the debrief indicates whether this is a one-on-one session or a group debrief with Sam's squad present — it reads as one-on-one by default, but that's inferred, not stated.
8. *Unsure.* The four texts (lines 49–61) carry no time or place marker — unclear if this is the same evening as the debrief or some later point.

**Objects**

9. "He gets a start point and a compass" (line 8) — neither is mentioned again; there's no beat confirming he reached the start point or used the compass at all.
10. "costs him a thumb that swells up for a week" (line 17) — introduced as a cost of the culvert contact and never referenced again, including at the debrief where that exact contact is the subject of extended dialogue (line 43).

**Joining words**

12. "anyway" in the same line implies a contrast ("despite X") that the page never supplies — despite the major's silence, despite it being an offhand remark rather than a formal complaint, despite something else entirely; the missing side is never named.

**Voice**
