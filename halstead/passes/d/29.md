## Stage two: sit in every chair

**The major (line 34, 40, 43–46).**
- *I am ...* an officer running a four-day debrief for a junior trainee, having just watched him take a well-set ambush apart in under two seconds.
- *What I've been given ...* a blunt question ("was that the assessment, or still setup?"), and, after I answer with nothing but a raised eyebrow, an unsolicited critique of my exercise's design plus a request to make it harder.
- *What I'd actually do ...* say something. The text gives me one silent reaction beat (line 40) to the smaller question and nothing at all — no line, no gesture, no reaction — to the bigger provocation at line 43, then cuts straight to "it goes upward anyway" (line 46). We never see what I said, only that a report of it travelled.

**The third-contact attacker (line 20).**
- *I am ...* opposing-element soldier who has held one position for six hours waiting for a single trainee to pass at night.
- *What I've been given ...* the payoff: perfect position, timing, surprise, and ground — the report's own words.
- *What I'd actually do ...* the takedown I set up for six hours to execute. The text confirms it happens, but the whole thing — contact, reversal, resolution — is under two seconds and entirely narrated from Sam's side; the man who is the grammatical subject of "the man does everything right" has no beat once contact starts.

**Sam (line 34, 37, 43).**
- *What I know* — four days just got reviewed and the major paused on contact three, then asked for questions.
- *What I don't know* — whether the exercise is over or this was only a lead-in to something bigger.
- *What I'd do* — per SAM.md, correct a mismatch between the record and what he knows happened, point by point, rather than let it stand. Line 43 matches this. Consistent characterization, not a finding.

**Ruth, texting (line 52).** Per RUTH.md she states rather than asks. Sent "how" — a bare one-word question, no assertion. Flagged for Stage 3 / context check below.

**Nadia, texting (line 58).** Signature move per NADIA.md is naming the addressee rather than using a pronoun, and a single flat statement with no elaboration. "nadia: sam" fits exactly. Not a finding.
