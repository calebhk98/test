## Restatement

1. (line 5) Statement of premise: the exercise lasts four days and Sam is the one mostly being acted upon during it.
2. (line 8) Sam is briefed beforehand: he'll walk between two points at night, an opposing force is in the area and has been briefed to try to take him, they choose when to hit him, and he's given a start point and a compass.
3. (line 11) They attack him three times total.
4. (line 14) Attack one: two men hit him at a fence line; he deals with it and privately calls it fine afterward.
5. (line 17) Attack two: four men hit him in a culvert with his retreat blocked; this is the one that gets his full attention, takes about eleven seconds, and costs him a swollen thumb for a week.
6. (line 20) Attack three: at 3:40am on night four, one man who waited six hours in position hits Sam from behind on bad footing, does everything right (position, timing, surprise, ground), and the whole thing takes under two seconds — Sam has already turned the man's weight against him before he's consciously registered a person is there.
7. (line 28) At the debrief, a major reviews the four days and pauses on the third contact.
8. (line 31) Sam waits for a critique, assuming one is coming, and privately has his own criticism ready about the culvert incident.
9. (line 34) The major asks if there are questions, at the end.
10. (line 37) Sam sits forward and asks whether that (the third contact) was the actual assessment or still setup for something further.
11. (line 40) The major's eyebrows go up (a reaction beat, no dialogue).
12. (line 43) Sam, assuming that was the assessment, gives his own critique: the culvert was bad, he blames himself for leaving the back door open and being lazy about timing, and says if they run it again he'd want it harder or with more attackers, because three two-on-Sam/four-on-Sam contacts over four days is a lot of walking for little payoff.
