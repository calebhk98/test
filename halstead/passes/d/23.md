## Stage two: sit in every chair

**Ruth's mother**, asked "Is he in yet?" about the brother's Halstead status — being the mother of the boy in question, she would know his status better than anyone; having Meg supply the answer ("Any day now... He's already looking at dorms") instead of Ruth's own mother answering her own question is backwards. What Ruth's mother has actually been told/knows on the page isn't shown — a candidate finding, see Stage 3.

**The recruiter** (104), handed a signature before he's finished the sentence on form one — a professional processing enlistment paperwork daily would expect exactly this from a confident recruit; his flat, habit-driven question ("Anybody try to talk you out of this") fits a man asking it for the hundredth time. No mismatch.

**The staffer in the school polo** (41), handed her own institution's failure by a stranger ("First time we've actually run one of these") — what she'd actually do is apologize and keep working, which is exactly what's shown. No mismatch.

**Priya's parents** (26), given a program that runs out and a crowd of 91 names — clapping for every single name is a big, specific reaction to being handed nothing but a list; it fits people who don't distinguish "their child's moment" from "every child's moment." Matches their sheet exactly (father electrician, mother urgent-care front desk) — confirmed, not a finding.

**Kavi's father** (41), handed a stranger's structural complaint about the stage — agreeing "in real detail" fits a physics professor's disposition toward a load-bearing question. No mismatch.

**The grandmother**, handed three strangers' children in a row (Sam, Kavi, the unnamed girl) — what she'd actually do, per her established pattern of reading everything at the pace of an ordinary childhood, is exactly what's shown: pats, soft tags, a verdict on people she's met for ninety seconds. Matches her sheet closely; the one place it strains is Kavi (below, and Stage 3).

**Nadia's father** (119–134), handed a specific, checkable business fact by his daughter — his first move, "It is a cost," and his long silent pause before an answer we never see, are consistent with a man being talked into something rather than instantly agreeing. Not a mismatch by itself, but the withheld answer is a candidate under Stage 3 (Reaction).

**Chloe**, told the exam math doesn't work for her (179–182) — what she knows at that moment is only what the eligibility page says; she has not yet been told anything by an outside authority. Her reaction — close the laptop, go work at the forge — is consistent with her established coping pattern elsewhere in the book (working with her hands when something doesn't have an answer yet). No mismatch. Told by her father to "find something to do" with the two years (200), she eats ham instead of answering — a real dodge, but not established elsewhere as inconsistent with her; flagged only as a possible **Reaction** gap under Stage 3, unsure.

## Stage three: checklist findings

Findings are numbered. Confidence noted; unsure ones are marked as such and still included per the brief.

0. **Stage-one flag carried forward — a restatement that says something that could not happen.** Line 26: "Eleven years of pickups and drop-offs means she has watched this particular boy grow **six inches in a single summer**." Six inches (15 cm) of height in a single summer is roughly triple even a fast adolescent growth-spurt rate; restating this plainly is what surfaced it. It's compounded by the "pickups and drop-offs" itself: Sam boards, and his family is four hours away in Pittsburgh (`characters/SAM.md:87,194`) — routine local pickups and drop-offs, sustained for eleven years, don't fit a boarding student from that distance unless the phrase means only start/end-of-term transport, which the text doesn't specify. Medium-high confidence on the growth-rate claim; unsure on the commuting reading.

1. **Objects — a fourth form that was never introduced.** Line 104: "The recruiter slides **three** separate forms across the desk and starts explaining what each one means... but he reads **the fourth form** properly, twice, because it's the one about pay grade." Only three forms are ever placed on the desk; a fourth is read from nowhere. High confidence.

2. **Order/self-contradiction — packing has already started, in the same evening the text says it hasn't.** Line 101: "That evening, **before anyone's started packing anything**, or booking anything, or signing anything, they are all still on the same patch of grass... **Ruth is already on her third phone call about a dorm assignment.**" A phone call about a dorm assignment is exactly "booking," in the same sentence that denies anyone has started booking. High confidence — the contradiction is inside one paragraph.

3. **Order — packing had already started before that same paragraph's claim, twice over.** Line 95: "**Chloe carries her own box** down the ground-floor hallway, a forge mallet... wedged in next to a stack of notebooks" — packing, earlier the same day. Line 107: "Ruth goes to MIT, with a box of her own things **labeled and half packed before the ceremony even starts**" — packing, before the day even began. Both directly contradict line 101's "before anyone's started packing anything." High confidence.

4. **Joining word / logic — "before he tells his mother" implies a telling that never happens.** Line 140: "Eli takes a job on a security team three states from where he grew up, and tells the group chat about it **before he tells his mother**. **She finds out from a photograph he posts a month later**..." "Before he tells his mother" presupposes he does tell her, later; the next clause instead shows she never hears it from him at all — she finds out independently, from a photo. The two clauses can't both be true. Medium-high confidence.

5. **Reaction — a mark left with nobody, including the narration, following up on it.** Line 8: a student "grabbing hard enough at whoever's handing out the certificates **to leave a mark**." Whose mark, where, and to what consequence is never touched again — the object introduced (a mark on a person, mid-ceremony) does nothing afterward. Unsure — this may simply be scene texture, but per the brief's own aim ("A single word with nothing to attach to counts"), flagging it.

6. **A name with nothing to attach to.** Line 17: the sound tech is caught "telling somebody named **Carla**" — a name introduced and never used again, anywhere in the chapter. Low-confidence/unsure; likely intentional background texture, included per the brief's instruction that a bare unattached word counts.

7. **Chronology cross-check — "Is he in yet?" treats an already-resolved event as still pending.** Line 38: Ruth's mother asks "Is he in yet?" (meaning Ruth's younger brother's Halstead admission), and Meg answers "Any day now... He's already looking at dorms" — i.e., as of graduation, not yet admitted. But chapter 20 already has Ruth say "My brother's in. Unsure — it intersects with chapter 20's own unresolved dating problem (already flagged in BOOK.md), so this may be inherited rather than a fresh error, but it is live in this chapter's text as written.
