What I've been handed to reassign is a nineteen-year, twice-revised, still-open file that documents an unauthorized use of force resulting in four federal operators tied up and released with a verbal warning — a serious, still-live matter. What I would actually do: flag its sensitivity, brief the new hire on handling, or route it to someone senior instead of a first-year analyst. The text shows none of that — no briefing, no warning, nothing beyond "the kind of job that goes to whoever is newest." *(unsure — bureaucratic backlog-dumping is plausible on its own; it's the total absence of any handoff context for a file this hot that reads as a gap.)*

**The infiltrator, placed on staff (l.28–37).** I am a professional plant, trained to hold a cover identity. What I've been given is a classroom of eleven-year-olds. What I would actually do: not radiate "wrongness" detectable by children within a day, and not still be unmade and gone by Thursday. This one isn't new to this pass — it's the premise of the whole file entry, not a slip — but it belongs on record because the "day and a half" burn time given here is the standard the rest of the chapter measures competence against.
