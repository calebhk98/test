## Stage two: sit in every chair

**The unnamed "somebody" who hands Theo the file (l.8).** I am whoever manages case backlogs when an analyst retires. What I've been handed to reassign is a nineteen-year, twice-revised, still-open file that documents an unauthorized use of force resulting in four federal operators tied up and released with a verbal warning — a serious, still-live matter. What I would actually do: flag its sensitivity, brief the new hire on handling, or route it to someone senior instead of a first-year analyst. The text shows none of that — no briefing, no warning, nothing beyond "the kind of job that goes to whoever is newest." *(unsure — bureaucratic backlog-dumping is plausible on its own; it's the total absence of any handoff context for a file this hot that reads as a gap.)*

**The infiltrator, placed on staff (l.28–37).** I am a professional plant, trained to hold a cover identity. What I've been given is a classroom of eleven-year-olds. What I would actually do: not radiate "wrongness" detectable by children within a day, and not still be unmade and gone by Thursday. This one isn't new to this pass — it's the premise of the whole file entry, not a slip — but it belongs on record because the "day and a half" burn time given here is the standard the rest of the chapter measures competence against.

**The report's author, one of the four operators (l.54–72).** I am a captured, tied-up operative writing an after-action account. What I've just been asked, by the children who beat me, is what grade I'd have given them. What I would actually do: the report answers this as a straight factual note ("The author records that he was asked, directly, what he would have given them," l.66) with no recorded answer and no visible reaction to being asked at all — not amusement, not offense, nothing. Given the "Watch" scoring culture established elsewhere, an operator playing along or refusing to play along would both be informative; the file gives neither. *(unsure — could be the report's own flat register doing this on purpose.)*

**Sinclair, "an adult" (l.69–72).** I arrive to find four tied federal operators and four children on the floor. What I would actually do, per how this character is built elsewhere: check on the children first, ask who's monitoring, ask for numbers. The account here skips straight to sending the kids to bed and threatening the operators — plausible if the report-writer only recorded what applied to him, but it means the chapter's version of this scene, read on its own, shows an authority figure who addresses the intruders before or without visibly addressing the children's welfare.

**Theo, at the infiltrator revelation (l.40).** What I know at this moment: a man I once thought was pretending to be somebody was actually a state or corporate plant, caught by my own eleven-year-old class. What I would do: react, even privately. The paragraph ends flat, straight into a section break, with no physical or emotional beat attached — contrast with the hand-over-the-mouth freeze given to the *next* revelation (l.89). *(unsure — see Stage three, Reaction.)*

**Ruth, receiving the hypothetical (l.109–112).** What she's been asked, in the chat's own words, is how badly she'd want to know a secret Theo can't actually tell her. What she does is answer in one word, breaking six months of silence to do it. Measured against her own established register this checks out — no finding here on its own — but see Stage three for the load that one word is being asked to carry against what happens later in her own arc.

## Stage three: clear each paragraph against the list

### The headline finding — the chapter's own dateline

Every chapter from 20 onward carries an explicit real-world dateline on line 3. Reading them in file order:

    26_ten_targets.md   : November 2023
    27_the_file.md       : October 2025
    28_nadia.md          : May 2024
    29_the_exercise.md   : February 2024
    30_cleared.md        : September 2025 – January 2026

**1. [Order]** Chapter 27 is dated **"October 2025"** (l.3). Read in book order, that is a jump of nearly two full years past its predecessor (Nov 2023), and the two chapters that follow it, "Nadia" and "The Exercise," are dated May 2024 and February 2024 — both more than a year *before* October 2025. A reader moving straight through the book hits October 2025, then falls back to February 2024, then has to wait until chapter 30 (Sept 2025) to catch back up to where chapter 27 already put them. This is a hard, quotable date conflict, not an inference — `chronology/BOOK.md`'s own chapter-by-chapter table checked this exact cluster of chapters (rows 27–29) and lists no date for any of them beyond in-chapter job-tenure references, meaning its "Underdetermined" verdict on this cluster was reached without ever citing the datelines actually printed on the page.

**2. [Order]** Chapter 27's internal math for Theo's age doesn't reconcile with the same dateline against the manuscript's other fixed point. Chloe (Theo's exact age-cohort per `characters/THEO.md`) is explicitly six years old in July 2012 (`chapters/04_pluto.md:57`, dateline `chapters/04_pluto.md:3`). Aged forward from that anchor, she is twelve around 2018, and October 2025 is roughly thirteen years after July 2012 — putting her (and Theo) at about 19 that October, not 18. But l.51 ("Six years ago") and l.83 ("He was twelve that year") only work out to eighteen if October 2025 is the year they turn eighteen (12 + 6 = 18). Run either direction — forward from the 2012 anchor, or backward six years from the 2025 dateline — and the two methods land about a year apart. *(Not unsure: both halves of this are quoted, dated text, not inference about tone or intent. The exact birth month is unknown, but every birth month in the already-established Aug–Feb window produces the same roughly one-year gap.)*

### Paragraph-level findings

**3. [Voice]** l.31: *"Theo reads it three times, because he remembers it."* The narrator states his internal cause directly. `characters/THEO.md`'s own known-problems list flags this exact line as the one confirmed violation of a rule the rest of the manuscript holds to — that only the book's single point-of-view figure gets a stated reason from the narrator, and everyone else's interior has to surface as action or speech.

**4. [Voice]** l.63: *"The report notes, in the flat voice these things are always written in..."* — the narrator generalizes about how "these things are always written," telling the reader how to read the register rather than letting the flatness demonstrate itself.

**5. [Joining words]** l.97–100: *"...does the only thing available to him, which is nothing, because every page of it is stamped classified... So he posts in the chat."* What's on the left of "So" is that nothing is available to him; what's on the right is an action — soliciting his friends' reaction to classified material he's just said isn't his to disclose. The two sides don't sit together as cause and effect without a step the text skips.

**6. [Redundancy]** l.48: *"Then there's the entry."* This sits immediately after the horizontal-rule section break (l.43) that already signals a new document section. The sentence does the same job the formatting device just did.

**7. [Objects]** l.37: *"All of them clocked it, four or five started asking questions..."* — "them" has no explicit antecedent in this paragraph or the one before it. Only Theo has been named; "his classmates" is never stated, only inferable.

**8. [Position]** l.5–80 (the whole file-reading sequence) carries no physical staging at all — no room, no desk, no time of day beyond "eighth month." The only two positional details in the entire chapter are "Theo sits back" (l.80) and "puts his hand over his mouth" (l.89), both in the final third.

[Reaction — unsure]** l.40: *"Theo had assumed, for seven years, that the guy just quit."* This is where the infiltrator revelation ends — realizing a childhood teacher was a state or corporate plant, caught by his own eleven-year-old class — and it runs straight into a section break with no physical or emotional beat attached, unlike the hand-over-the-mouth freeze given to the very next revelation (l.89).

**10. [Reaction — unsure]** l.66: *"The author records that he was asked, directly, what he would have given them."* No reaction from the operator to being asked this — by the children who'd just tied him up — is recorded in either direction.

**11. [Position — unsure]** l.69–72: the arriving adult (Sinclair, unnamed here) is shown addressing the four operators; nothing on the page shows him attending to the four children first, though that's established as his character elsewhere (`characters/CHLOE.md`'s Halstead-staff notes).

[Specificity/Voice — unsure]** l.20: *"...the same caveat at the bottom of the page that has apparently been at the bottom of the page for nineteen years..."* — "apparently" hedges a claim about the file's own contents; unclear whether this is Theo inferring continuity or a fact the file states outright.

**13. [Specificity — unsure]** l.57 gives "under two minutes" (time inside the first building) and l.63 gives "a response time of nearly eight minutes" (the figure the children argue about). These are two different clocks measuring different things, sitting six lines apart with nothing marking that they aren't the same measurement.

**14. [Objects — unsure]** l.28: *"The school's stated reason is recorded word for word..."* claims verbatim quotation, but no quotation marks follow — inconsistent with the chapter's own convention of marking exact wording (the "theo:"/"ruth:" chat lines, l.103–112).

[Objects]** l.54 establishes a single four-page report "written by one of them." l.60 then introduces "the next entry," "written from memory, after the fact" — without saying whether this is a continuation of the same document (perhaps its page two, per l.54's "what matters starts on page two") or a separate entry by a different hand. **19. [Specificity — unsure, small]** l.8: *"...the kind of job that goes to whoever is newest at the desk."* A category description with no particular attached — plausibly intentional shorthand, but reaches for the general case rather than a specific one.

**20. [Objects — unsure, small]** l.11: *"It takes him a day and a half to work out what he's holding."* What specifically tips him off that this is his own school is never stated.

**21. [Objects/plausibility — unsure]** l.8: a nameless "somebody" hands a brand-new analyst a still-open, nineteen-year, twice-revised file that documents an unauthorized firefight with federal operators, with no briefing or flagged sensitivity shown on the page.

**22. [Position/plausibility — unsure]** l.83: *"...everyone was annoyed about it because it was a school night and the alarm had caught them all flat-footed..."* "them all" reads as the whole student body, not just the four involved — in tension with an incident that, per the rest of the file, needed to stay contained and secret rather than wake the school.
