## Stage two: sit in every chair

**I am Theo.** A federal analyst who has just spent years learning exactly what these systems catch and what a violation costs. What I've been handed is a proposal to commit the same kind of intrusion, against a much better-resourced target, while several of us — me included — hold active clearances. What I would actually do: refuse outright, not object-and-comply after nine days with no shown deliberation. The text gives me his signature move (on-record objection) correctly, but the *nine days* of resistance never surface as anything but a jump-cut between line 11 and line 46 — no second exchange, no visible reasoning that moves him from "stupidest thing" to signing.

**I am Ruth.** What I've just been given is a bare assurance with no mechanism attached ("I go away for fifteen years, so I'm not going to be wrong," line 35) — precisely the kind of outcome-only, unreasoned claim my own sheet says I never accept ("Accept 'it worked' as a reason not to fix a plan that got there by luck" is on my Would Never list). What I would actually do: press him for the actual mechanism — which cameras, which gaps, how he verified it — the way I do everywhere else in the book (chapters/32_the_money.md, chapters/34_the_files.md). What the text has me do: nothing. The exchange ends on Eli's assertion with no reply from me at all before the section break.

**I am Eli.** What I've just been challenged on is my own risk math, by someone whose corrections I want pointed at me (per my own sheet). What I would do, and what the text has me do, match: restate confidence rather than retreat (line 35). No finding here — this is the character working as documented.

## Stage three: findings

Findings are numbered; *unsure* is marked where marked.

1. Line 8, "Every one of them knows the law cold," is directly contradicted by chapters/19_sixteen.md:25, where Chloe herself fails the bar exam: `"Fail," she says. "By four points."` *unsure* — chapters/19_sixteen.md:53–55 gives a possible out (the four points were for "showing your work," not for wrong substance), but the chapter-33 line makes no such distinction and reads as flat, universal mastery.

2. Line 8's claim is narrator assertion, not demonstration — a straight "Voice" violation on its own terms (telling the reader what to think about the group's knowledge) rather than showing it, and it is the one line in the chapter making a factual claim about competence rather than describing an event.

3. Line 8 says Ruth "quotes the sentencing ranges from memory, twice, at Eli," but the chat that immediately follows (lines 11–35) shows Ruth speaking to Eli exactly twice ("thats not the same," "and if youre wrong about the cameras") and neither line contains a number, a statute, or anything resembling a quoted range. *unsure* — the two "twice" instances could be meant as off-page, with this chat merely illustrative, but as written the payoff for the claim is on the page and isn't there.

4. Related to #3: "the sentencing ranges" (plural) never receives a single concrete figure anywhere in the chapter attributed to Ruth. The only number given for a penalty ("fifteen years," line 35) comes from Eli, about himself, not from Ruth's citation.

5. Line 26 ("thats not the same") and line 32 ("and if youre wrong about the cameras") are both corrections/challenges from Ruth to Eli, and neither names him. Her own sheet states this naming is her exclusive, unbroken signature: "Says the other person's name inside the sentence that is correcting them. No one else in the cast owns this move," with both cited real examples ("It is not better, Sam." / "That is not what any of the maths says, Kavi.") including the name. Two corrections in one chapter break that pattern.

6. Line 35's assurance goes unanswered — no reply from Ruth, no visible acceptance, before the section break and the jump to "Everyone signs off" (line 46). Given Ruth's sheet ("Would never: Accept 'it worked' as a reason not to fix a plan that got there by luck") the size of the gap between her live doubt (line 32) and the group's unanimous sign-off is a reaction that's owed and not shown. *unsure* — the compressed chat style elsewhere in the book (ch32) sometimes elides responses too.

7. Line 43: "Chloe writes the rules because she's the one who writes." *unsure* — Theo's sheet establishes his literal profession and best subject as producing written analysis ("reading things and saying what they mean," his job description), which sits in more direct tension with "the one who writes" than anything established for Chloe, whose own sheet never names composition/rule-drafting as her particular lane. The line asserts a role without addressing the more obviously-qualified candidate in the room.

8. Line 49, "her hand steady through all nine of them, and she does not stop," resists restatement: it is not clear whether "she does not stop" means her hand doesn't stop being steady, her writing doesn't pause, or she doesn't stop (abandon/reconsider) the larger undertaking. The sentence supports all three readings simultaneously.

9. Line 43 sets up an explicit protocol — "the response if it's noticed," and a unanimous-stop clause that ends everything "inside the hour with no argument" — that is never invoked or referenced again. Chapter 35 is the one moment that protocol exists for (the worm behaves anomalously and secrecy is directly at risk: "somebody stopped it and started it and cleaned up after themselves," chapters/35_nine_minutes.md:17), and no character mentions the nine pages, the stop clause, or "the response if it's noticed" at that moment or afterward.

10. Line 43, "the limits it keeps to," is never given a single concrete example anywhere in the chapter. *unsure* — could be deliberate compression (we don't need to see the rules, only that they exist), but the book elsewhere favors exact specifics (three-to-five-day gaps, forty minutes, 2003) over category labels, so the total absence of even one example here is a departure from the book's own habit of specificity.

11. Chapter dating: chapters/32_the_money.md ends on Eli's "so lets go and read it," and per chronology/chapter_dates.json ch32 spans "November 2025 – February 2026" while ch33 spans "May 2026 – June 2026." *unsure* — there's an unexplained roughly three-month gap between the decision voiced at the end of ch32 and the proposal chapter-33 opens with; nothing on the page accounts for what happens between February and May, though this may simply be intended as unremarkable elapsed time before Eli formally proposes it.

12. Line 32, "and if youre wrong about the cameras," is worth flagging against Ruth's own established expertise: her sheet documents her building "a general-purpose camera-defeating tool at thirteen" and being the group's networks/cameras specialist. *unsure* — it's Eli here who claims mechanical mastery over camera locations (line 29) while Ruth, the character with the actual camera-defeating track record, is the one voicing doubt about them; this may be justified by the shift from ordinary local cameras (her box) to whatever the second worm's target uses, but the text doesn't draw that distinction on the page.

13. Line 46, "Theo signs off last and says again that this is insane," depends on an off-page persuasion the chapter never shows — nine days of holding at "no" (line 5) collapse into agreement with no intervening scene, argument, or stated reason. This is consistent with Theo's established pattern of objecting then complying, but the *mechanism* of the nine days (what changed, who talked to him, what he read) is absent, leaving "and then he signs off" doing all the narrative work alone.

14. Line 8's "the same examination" leans on the reader already knowing this is the bar exam from chapters/19_sixteen.md with no gloss in-chapter — acceptable per the brief's own carve-out for unglossed institutional vocabulary in a linearly-read manuscript, so **not** counted as a finding; noted only so a later pass doesn't re-flag it.

15. Line 11's "the stupidest thing any of us has ever done" is a superlative claim made in dialogue (Theo's opinion, not the narrator's), so it is not held to the same fact-check standard as line 8; given the group's history (ch32's felony-level financial intrusion, ch20's armed confrontation), it's plausible on its face that a second intrusion against the government's own surveillance of them is comparatively the riskiest yet. Not counted as a finding — noted to show it was checked and cleared.
