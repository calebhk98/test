## Stage two: sit in every chair

**I am Theo.** A federal analyst who has just spent a career learning exactly
what these systems catch and what a violation costs. What I've been handed
is a proposal to commit the same kind of intrusion, against a much
better-resourced target, while several of us — me included — hold active
clearances. What I would actually do: refuse outright, not object-and-comply
after nine days with no shown deliberation. The text gives me my signature
move correctly (on-record objection), but the nine days themselves are a
jump-cut between line 11 and line 46 — no second exchange, no visible
reasoning that moves me from "stupidest thing" to signing.

**I am Ruth.** What I've just been given is a bare assurance with no
mechanism attached — "I go away for fifteen years, so I'm not going to be
wrong" (line 35) — exactly the kind of outcome-only, unreasoned claim my own
sheet says I never accept ("Accept 'it worked' as a reason not to fix a plan
that got there by luck" is on my Would Never list). What I would actually
do: press for the mechanism — which cameras, which gaps, how he verified it
— the way I do everywhere else in the book. What the text has me do:
nothing. The exchange ends on his assertion with no reply from me before the
section break.

**I am Eli.** What I've just been challenged on is my own risk math, by
someone whose corrections I want pointed at me. What I would do, and what
the text has me do, match: restate confidence rather than retreat (line 35).
No finding here — the character works as documented.

**I am Chloe**, viewpoint character, one of the seven, and the one with the
most to lose personally (an active federal clearance). What I'm being
"handed" is the actual work of the plan — writing the operating rules. The
text never lets me be told, asked, or shown anything on the page in this
chapter; I appear only in narrated summary, never in my own head or my own
voice, so there is nothing to check "what would I do" against. That absence
is itself the finding (#16 below).

## Stage three: findings

Numbered; *unsure* marked where warranted per the brief.

1. Line 8, "Every one of them knows the law cold," is contradicted by chapters/19_sixteen.md:25, where Chloe herself fails the bar exam: `"Fail," she says. "By four points."` *unsure* — chapters/19_sixteen.md:53–55 offers a possible out (the four points were for "showing your work," not wrong substance), but line 8 makes no such distinction and reads as flat, universal mastery.

2. Line 8's claim is pure narrator assertion, not demonstration — the narrator telling the reader what to think about the group's competence rather than showing it — and it is the only line in the chapter making a factual claim about ability rather than describing an event.

3. Line 8 says Ruth "quotes the sentencing ranges from memory, twice, at Eli," but the chat that immediately follows (lines 11–35) shows Ruth speaking to Eli exactly twice ("thats not the same," "and if youre wrong about the cameras"), and neither line contains a number, a statute, or anything resembling a quoted range. *unsure* — the "twice" could be meant as off-page, with this chat merely illustrative, but the payoff for the claim as printed isn't there.

4. Related to #3: "the sentencing ranges" (plural) never receives a single concrete figure attributed to Ruth anywhere in the chapter. The only number given for a penalty ("fifteen years," line 35) comes from Eli, about himself — the person paragraph 8 says is being lectured, not the one doing the citing.

5. Lines 26 and 32 are both corrections/challenges from Ruth to Eli, and neither names him. Her own sheet states the naming is her exclusive, unbroken signature — "Says the other person's name inside the sentence that is correcting them. No one else in the cast owns this move" — with both cited real examples ("It is not better, Sam." / "That is not what any of the maths says, Kavi.") including the name. Two corrections in one chapter break that pattern.

6. Theo's two lines in this chapter (11, 17) never use his documented signature tic — one word capitalized mid-sentence for stress inside an otherwise lowercase line — described in his sheet as "theirs alone." *unsure* — his sheet already flags this signature as thin generally (only two confirmed instances elsewhere), so this may be a continuation of a known gap rather than a new one.

7. Line 35's assurance goes unanswered — no reply from Ruth, no visible acceptance, before the section break and the jump to "Everyone signs off" (line 46). Given her sheet's "Would never: Accept 'it worked' as a reason not to fix a plan that got there by luck," the gap between her live doubt (line 32) and the group's unanimous sign-off is a reaction that's owed and not shown. *unsure* — the compressed chat style elsewhere (ch32) elides some responses too.

8. Line 43: "Chloe writes the rules because she's the one who writes." *unsure* — Theo's sheet establishes his literal profession and best subject as producing written analysis ("reading things and saying what they mean," his job description), a more directly-established writing credential than anything on Chloe's own sheet, which never names composition or rule-drafting as her particular lane. The line asserts a role without addressing the more obviously-qualified candidate in the room.

9. Line 49, "her hand steady through all nine of them, and she does not stop," resists restatement: unclear whether "she does not stop" means her hand doesn't stop being steady, her writing doesn't pause, or she doesn't stop (reconsider/abandon) the larger undertaking. The sentence supports all three readings.

10. Line 43's "it" ("They test it for five weeks... the limits it keeps to...") refers all the way back to "the second worm" in line 5, and the entire intervening 30-line block (lines 8–35) never mentions the worm again — restating paragraph 43 on its own requires reaching back over a full scene to recover what "it" is.

11. Line 43's "the limits it keeps to" is never given a single concrete example anywhere in the chapter. *unsure* — may be deliberate compression, but the book elsewhere favors exact specifics (three-to-five-day gaps, forty minutes, 2003) over bare category labels, so the total absence of even one example here departs from that habit.

12. The protocol set up in line 43 — "the response if it's noticed," and a unanimous-stop clause that ends everything "inside the hour with no argument" — is never invoked or referenced again. Chapter 35 is the one moment it exists for (the worm behaves anomalously and secrecy is directly at risk, chapters/35_nine_minutes.md:17), and no character mentions the nine pages, the stop clause, or "the response if it's noticed" at that moment or afterward.

13. Line 32, "and if youre wrong about the cameras," sits oddly against Ruth's own established expertise — her sheet documents her building "a general-purpose camera-defeating tool at thirteen" and being the group's camera/networks specialist. *unsure* — it's Eli who claims mechanical mastery over camera locations (line 29) while Ruth, the character with the actual track record, voices the doubt; this may be justified by a shift from ordinary local cameras (her box) to whatever the second target uses, but the text doesn't draw that distinction on the page.

14. Line 46, "Theo signs off last and says again that this is insane," depends on an off-page persuasion the chapter never shows — nine days of holding at "no" (line 5) collapse into agreement with no intervening scene, argument, or stated reason.

15. Line 5's own claim — "Eli proposes the second worm" — is never dramatized: no line of dialogue anywhere shows him actually making the pitch. The shown chat opens already mid-argument about risk and legality, after the proposal has apparently already landed off the page.

Line 32's conditional ("and if youre wrong about the cameras") has no "then" clause within its own paragraph — read in isolation, per the stage-three rule on joining words, the consequence is supplied only by the next speaker's paragraph (line 35), not by Ruth's own line. *unsure* — almost certainly intentional (Eli completes her sentence in the next turn), but the rule calls for flagging a missing side.
