## Stage two: sitting in the chairs

**The two adults with Kavi (5).** *I am* — unidentified; the text never says. *What I've just done* — apparently helped a small boy up from something. *What I'd actually do* — the scene never lets me be anyone; I'm never named as staff, parent, or counselor, so there's no way to check whether my action makes sense for who I am.

**Ms. Vance, asked "What was that, Chloe?" (123).** *I am* the teacher who just heard a seven-year-old mutter something about mailing a package to a stranger, mid-assignment. *What I've been shown* — an unusually adult, faintly bleak simile from a kid staring at her own paper. *What I'd actually do* — a teacher this attentive (she crouches to the floor for a crying kid three paragraphs later) would likely follow up on that line, not let two classmates absorb and answer it for her while she goes silent until "calls time" eight lines later. The text has her ask once and then vanish from the scene until the tape-and-cabinet beat.

**Kayleigh Burns, hearing "mailing something to a stranger" (125–127).** *I am* a first/second-grade classmate who runs recess and states one flat observation, never explains it, never seeks Chloe out. *What I've just heard* — Chloe explaining her own writing at length, unprompted, to the teacher. *What I'd actually do* — per her own sheet, register the mismatch once, flatly, and go back to what I was doing. The text has her do exactly this ("Why do you talk like that?" and no follow-up) — consistent.

**The librarian (85–93).** *I am* the branch librarian enforcing a four-book limit she's held to before. *What I've just been handed* — an overdue book and then nine unbroken minutes of unsolicited detail about a mannequin, a bridge, and fraction division. *What I'd actually do* — this fits: she waits Chloe out, stamps the card, and holds the rule regardless. Consistent with her one scene.

**Chloe herself, with the librarian (87, 91).** *What I know* — per her own established pattern (CHARACTER SHEET, CHLOE.md), she gives "the shortest, safest, most compliant answer available" to institutional adults, and the one place she goes long, unprompted, is with her father — "that holds even for an adult she likes." *What she does here* — delivers two long, breathless, unprompted monologues to a librarian she's spoken to only in the context of book returns. This is the clearest character-consistency gap in the chapter (see Finding 5 below).

## Stage three: findings

Line numbers verified against the file with `grep -n` immediately before writing.

1. **(5)** "Kavi is over by the double doors with two adults, from whom **she** gets a hand up, before a family walks between them, and by the time they pass, **he's** gone." Kavi is male (KAVI.md, used throughout as "he/his"), and the same sentence correctly uses "he" nine words later. The pronoun "she" has no antecedent — there is no other person in the sentence for it to refer to. Genuine finding, not unsure.
2. **(5)** "from whom she gets a hand up" — a hand up from *what*? Sitting, kneeling, a step, a curb — never specified. Vague where a physical, particular detail is expected (Specificity).
3. **(5)** "with two adults" — never identified as parents, camp staff, or anyone else, and Kavi's own sheet gives named, specific parents (a pharmacist mother, a physicist father) who could have been used here. Position/Specificity.
4. **Order, cross-chapter.** Chapter 6 ends with the car already an hour into the drive home from camp ("An hour into the drive she's got her head against the cold part of the window," 06_the_list.md:80). Chapter 7 opens with the flagpole goodbye (7, "Ruth finds her at the flagpole and hugs her hard enough to hurt"), an event that has to precede getting in the car. The goodbye is narrated after the departure it precedes, across the chapter boundary, with no signal of a rewind. This is independently flagged in `chronology/BOOK.md` §2.2.
5. **Voice/character consistency (87, 91).** Chloe delivers two long, unprompted, enthusiastic monologues to the librarian about camp. Per CHLOE.md's "Voice in one paragraph" and "When they talk at length," she gives institutional adults "the shortest, safest, most compliant answer available" and holds the long, true version back — "that holds even for an adult she likes" — with the one exception being her father. A librarian she has no established closeness with getting the full unhedged monologue twice in one scene runs against that rule as stated.
6. **(45)** "her mouth goes tight at the corners, the way it does right before." Right before *what*? The comparison is left uncompleted — no noun phrase follows "before." Phrase resists restatement.
7. **Context / date conflict.** SYNOPSIS_CHARACTERS_TIMELINE.md line 23: "**In September** she cries at her desk and 'I don't know' is the completely true answer." Chapter text, line 197: "**In the first week of October**, Chloe puts her head down on her desk... and... cry[s] with her face in her arms." September and October are stated flatly on each side; one of the two documents has the wrong month for this scene.
8. **Context / staging conflict.** SYNOPSIS_CHARACTERS_TIMELINE.md line 265 (on Ms. Vance): "Also never looks up during the four seconds in which Chloe is told she's got weirder, which is a more damning portrait of ordinary schooling than anything Aldana does" — i.e., the intended beat is that Vance is inattentive *during* the Kayleigh/Bryce exchange. But the chapter has Vance actively engage right beforehand ("What was that, Chloe?", 123), and the only "not looking up" line in the text (137, "while Ms. Vance is over at the cabinet with the tape, not looking up for any of it") is temporally displaced to the later scratch-out beat, after the "weirder" exchange has already closed (131–135). The specific damning image the synopsis describes isn't the one on the page at the moment it's meant to land. Unsure whether this is the synopsis describing an intent not fully executed, or a genuine mismatch — flagged either way.
9. **Reaction (41–43).** Chloe's chin tucks down and she wipes her eye — a clear, described distress cue — and the very next line has Mom immediately press on ("Priya, then. Chloe, you slept in a room with Priya for twenty-eight days.") with no beat of acknowledgment before continuing the interrogation. Quote on either side: "her chin tucks down and she scrubs the back of her wrist across one eye." (41) / "'Priya, then. Chloe, you slept in a room with Priya for twenty-eight days.'" (43).
10. **Reaction, unsure (199–213).** Chloe cries loudly "in front of everybody" (199) at her desk, and no classmate reaction is shown anywhere in the scene, though the chapter has already established this same class reacting vocally to a smaller oddity (127–129, Kayleigh and Bryce both comment on the time-capsule line). Twenty-six other seven-year-olds registering nothing at all to loud crying is a gap, though it may be a deliberate narrowing of focus onto Vance and Chloe.
11. **Objects (161).** "a chore list she has made herself, folded small and left on the pillow" — introduced and never resolved; no reaction from a parent finding it, no mention again in the chapter.
12. **Redundancy (161, 163–177).** The summary line "She asks one more time coming back downstairs in her pajamas, twenty minutes after bedtime" (161) is immediately followed by the same beat dramatized in full, restating the same setting details ("Chloe is standing in the doorway in her pajamas, one foot on top of the other," 173) — the summary and the scene do the same descriptive work back to back.
13. **Redundancy, unsure (49, 183).** "settles into a long stretch of listening with her hand flat on the counter" (49) and "her mom is at the counter with her hand flat on it" (183) — the identical physical beat (hand flat on the counter, mid-phone-call) recurs verbatim in two different scenes. May be intentional motif; flagged as possible redundancy regardless.
14. **Voice (183).** "Her voice has gone careful, extra polite, the tone that she uses when she already knows the answer and has to hear it again anyway." The narrator states what the tone *means* rather than dramatizing it and letting the reader infer — telling rather than showing what's supposed to be inferred from a vocal register.
15. **Voice, unsure (25).** "She looks pleased about it in a way that puzzles Chloe." Narrator asserts a specific, unexplained emotional subtext (Mom's pleasure at the request to call Sam) that is never resolved anywhere in the chapter. May be deliberate held-back information for a later chapter; flagged regardless since nothing in this chapter cashes it in.
16. **Joining word (7), unsure.** Ruth's very first line at the flagpole opens "So we're all getting in." No prior sentence in the text establishes what "so" follows from — the left side of the connector isn't on the page (it may rest on an earlier, off-chapter camp conversation).

Possibly reconcilable (different axes of "better"), but neither text explains the discrepancy.
19. **Specificity, unsure (93).** "same as it was in June" — asserts an established library visit/rule from June that this chapter doesn't otherwise reference or dramatize; taken on faith from outside the chapter.
20. **Position (5).** Chloe's own location at the start of the opening scene is never established before Ruth "finds her at the flagpole" (7) — the reader doesn't know where Chloe is standing during the loop/pickup description that opens the chapter until Ruth arrives there.
21. **Reaction (123–137).** Once Kayleigh and Bryce comment (127, 129) and Chloe visibly shuts down (covers the page, 131; talks to the desk, 133), there is no further adult intervention or acknowledgment in the scene of a child having just been told twice, by name, that she's "weird" — Vance's only subsequent appearance in the scene is procedural (calling time, taping the box), not addressed to what just happened.
22. **Order/plausibility, unsure (99–109).** "It works on the first day of second grade exactly the way that it worked in April" (109) depends on the reader recalling a specific April scene from chapter 2 (Mrs. Aldana asking, in April, whether things are okay at home, and the "finish first, sit still" pattern working "every day until the end of the year," 02_march_4th.md:119) — coherent once traced back, but the callback is not self-contained within this chapter and could easily read as an unearned forward reference on a first pass.
23. **Specificity (61).** "a woman who wants to know whether her dad is the registered owner of the vehicle" — a plausible period wrong-number call, but oddly specific and unexplained; it does nothing else in the paragraph or chapter (minor, Objects-adjacent).
24. **Reaction, unsure (17–29).** Mom's request for Sam's number is met first with visible pleasure ("she looks pleased about it in a way that puzzles Chloe," 25) and then, three lines later, faint irritation implied by the phone-book-and-pointed-question routine — the shift from pleased to procedurally impatient across just a few lines isn't given a beat to turn on.
25. **Specificity (5).** "somebody's little brother, who is screaming about a shoe" — deliberately anonymous background noise; fine as scene-setting, but paired with finding 3 (the unnamed "two adults"), the opening paragraph leaves three separate people in the crowd unidentified in ways that matter later (Kavi's escorts) and ways that don't (the little brother) — the two registers sit oddly close together.
26. **Voice (137).** "while Ms. Vance is over at the cabinet with the tape, not looking up for any of it" — narrator supplies the interpretive frame (this is *why* Chloe can safely rewrite the page) rather than simply showing Vance's position and letting the rewrite imply the reasoning.
27. **Redundancy, unsure (27, 33, 37, 41, 45).** Five consecutive paragraphs each restate the same fact in slightly different words — Chloe doesn't know a friend's surname — for Sam, then again structurally identical for Ruth, Kavi, and Priya. Likely intentional (an escalating rhythm), but flagged per the checklist's instruction that repeated information doing one job counts.
28. **Specificity, unsure (91).** "on the floor of my room, at night" — ambiguous whether "my room" is the camp dorm room (where the canonical fraction-derivation scene is set, per CHLOE.md, chapters/05_behind.md) or her bedroom at home; the surrounding sentences are all about camp, so context favors camp, but the phrase alone doesn't specify which room, and this is a scene recounted well after leaving camp.
29. **Position (183–193).** The call in this scene is about school admission policy ("Third grade... nothing before third grade at all," 185), but the exchange never states which "school" is being called — Chloe's local district, a magnet/gifted program, or Halstead itself. The prior scene (159–179) is entirely about Halstead specifically, so the referent silently shifts.
30. **Objects (177).** The tally of marks "on the back of a spelling worksheet under her pillow" is introduced at the very end of a section and never revisited again within this chapter — consistent with it being a hook for a later chapter, but per the checklist it still qualifies as an object whose follow-through isn't shown here.

33. **Voice, unsure (93).** "That's a real month, that is" — the librarian's judgment of Chloe's camp achievements is delivered as settled narrative fact via her dialogue with no independent verification; minor, since it's in-character dialogue rather than narration, but borders on the text validating Chloe's account through an adult mouthpiece rather than showing it stand on its own.
