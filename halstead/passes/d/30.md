## Stage two: sit in every chair

**Whitaker, at L54 ("There's a third one...").** I am a federal background investigator, twenty-one years in, specializing in unconventional-background candidates. What I would actually do: note it, ask who asked her to hold it, and decide later whether it blocks the file — not push a compliant, forthcoming subject on a matter of someone else's confidentiality, since pressuring her would teach her to manage the story instead of just telling it. The text has him do exactly this (L57, L75, doesn't push past "I'd want to talk to her first"). Matches his method as established (WHITAKER.md L29-31, L103). No gap.

Worth flagging, at low confidence since it partly rests on sheet material rather than the chapter itself.

## Stage three: clear each paragraph against the list

Findings below are numbered continuously. "Unsure" markers are honest, not hedging filler.

1. **[L48 vs L54-72] Contradiction — "She tells him everything."** The sentence is immediately followed by three separate, explicit refusals to describe content: the third project (L54, "I've been asked not to say what it's about, and I said I wouldn't") and two more of the same kind (L72, "Two others... Same thing. Same reason."). "Everything" cannot be squared with three named exceptions two lines later. Even read charitably as "everything she's willing to say, fully honestly, including the fact and reasons for what she withholds," the word choice is exact enough elsewhere in this scene (Whitaker's whole method is precision) that "everything" reads as a claim, not a hedge — and the claim is false on its face. This is the single strongest finding in the chapter.

2. **[L42, L28, L95, L98, header L3] Arithmetic — the seven-month investigation cannot reach "the autumn."** The chapter's own dateline is "September 2025 – January 2026" (L3). The exam is "three weeks after she turns twenty" (L28) — mid-to-late September, given her August birthday (CHLOE.md L6). The investigation "takes seven months" (L42) and is introduced immediately after the exam with no stated gap. Seven months from mid-September lands in mid-April — not "the autumn" (L95), and not compatible with a January start (L98) either. This is not a new observation: chronology/BOOK.md §3.3 already flags the identical arithmetic problem at the project level ("Seven months from July lands in February, not autumn" — that version assumes a June birthday the current file no longer uses, since L98 already reads "August," not "June" as BOOK.md's quote records; the birthday fix has evidently been applied, but the seven-months/autumn mismatch it also flagged has not). As written, the chapter cannot place exam, seven-month investigation, autumn clearance, and January start on one consistent calendar.

3. **[L60 vs CHLOE.md L184] Unsure — one compartmentalized project or three?** The chapter has Chloe withholding three separate items (the "third one" at L54 plus "two others" at L72), each apparently a distinct research project under a distinct promise. CHLOE.md's own gloss of this scene calls it "the one compartmentalized project" (singular). Might just be loose sheet language rather than a chapter error, but the chapter's plural is unambiguous and worth a flag given how precisely Whitaker counts ("Anything else in that category?").

4. **[L8 vs L14] Unsure — the "well under half" comparison doesn't hold on its own numbers.** The blog runs "six thousand words" two or three times a week (up to ~18,000 words/week). The school benchmark she offers is "ten pages a week" (~2,500-3,000 words). Read as a word-count comparison, the blog is several times heavier than school, not "well under half" of it. The line likely means something looser — total days/effort committed, not output volume — but the chapter never disambiguates which measure "well under half" is measuring, and the one number it does give (six thousand words) points the other way.

5. **[L51] Unsure — unclear speaker for "Yes, boarding, from seven. Yes, that's young."** This montage paragraph compresses a Q&A into indirect summary with no dialogue tags. It's not clear whether "Yes, that's young" is Whitaker's interjection, folded into the summary, or Chloe pre-empting his reaction. Minor, but it's the one place in the paragraph where attribution isn't recoverable from the text.

6. **[L75, referring back to L54-72] Unsure — antecedent of "it" in "Would you tell me if I said it was necessary?"** Three separate withheld items have just been named (L54, L72). The question's "it" could mean the specific project just being discussed, or the whole withheld category. Chloe's answer at L78 ("hold the line... for the other two") treats it as being about one item with two others left standing, which resolves the ambiguity for the reader after the fact, but the question itself is not clearly scoped when asked.

7. **[L84-98, entire closing section] Position — the highest-stakes exchange in the chapter has no physical setting at all.** From "There's a third one" (L54) through "He writes that down too" (L81), two people conduct a multi-turn exchange about a federal security clearance with zero grounding: no room, no desk, no notebook described directly in the chapter (the half-page/page-turn beats gesture at a notebook but never name one), no indication of where this is happening on either of the two visits (L42). A conversation this consequential happening nowhere is exactly the kind of gap the brief's Position check is aimed at.

8. **[L5] Specificity — "gets big" is never given a measure.** Readership, inbox volume, subscriber count: none of it is quantified anywhere in the chapter, unlike the concrete numbers the rest of the paragraph does supply (six thousand words, five hundred, two or three times a week). "Big" is the one load-bearing claim left vague.

9. **[L20 vs chapters/25_nineteen.md:17] Unsure — "twenty-one credits" reused verbatim from a different term.** Chapter 25 has her carrying twenty-one credits "in the spring" of her first college year (spring 2025 on the working calendar). Chapter 30's L20 gives her the identical number, "twenty-one credits," apparently for a later term (around the Sept 2025 exam). Could easily be coincidence — some students hold a flat course load across terms — but it's the same specific figure recurring for what should be a different semester, with nothing marking it as a repeat rather than a re-use.

10. **[L89] Reaction — the funding question is the one disclosure in the scene with no visible reaction beat.** Every other item in the interview gets a described response from Whitaker: doesn't look up (L57), fills half a page (L69), turns back a page to cross-check (L75), writes it down (L81). The funding/principal-donor question — the one item WHITAKER.md's own interior-life material treats as the case's genuine unresolved unease — gets none of that; L89-92 moves straight from question to "She says it anyway. It's true." with no described beat from him at all. Flagged at lower confidence since the "he should react more" expectation leans on sheet material describing feelings that aren't shown on the page in this chapter.

11. **[L51] Redundancy, unsure — "two published papers, both verifiable" are never identified.** Two internal research-adjacent papers appear earlier in the book (chapters/17_fourteen.md:111, acknowledgments only, stays inside the department; chapters/18_fifteen.md:131, sixth-of-nine author, under NDA). Neither is described elsewhere as externally "published" and "verifiable" the way L51 implies. Possibly two entirely new, off-page papers from the unshown college years — plausible for a summary chapter — but worth flagging since it sits one sentence away from the withheld project that clearly *is* the chapters/18 paper, inviting the reader to conflate the two.

12. **[L8] Unsure — object with no agent: "The summaries start getting picked up on their own."** Picked up by whom, and to where? No aggregator, platform, or sharer named or implied anywhere in the chapter; "on their own" doubles down on the vagueness rather than resolving it. The single concrete detail this paragraph otherwise leans on (word counts, cadence) makes the missing agent more noticeable, not less.

13. **[L8 vs L11-17] Unsure — "an inbox" (written) drifts into "conversations" (spoken) with no marked shift.** Para 2 establishes contact via "an inbox." Paras 3-5 then describe an exchange in terms that default to spoken interaction — "People keep asking," "she keeps telling them," "conversations end there" — without ever re-anchoring back to the inbox. Minor, and "conversation" is common informally for an email exchange, but the chapter doesn't say these are the same channel.

14. **[L98 vs chapters/23_the_first_one.md:98] The chapter's own math contradicts an explicit rule stated earlier in the book.** Chapter 23 states the rule directly: "You sit the exam at twenty and you're appointed at twenty-one." Chapter 30 has her starting the job "in January" (L98) while she "turns twenty-one in August" (same line) — meaning, by the chapter's own arithmetic, she starts the job while still twenty, not twenty-one. Already identified independently in chronology/BOOK.md's fix list (item 3) as a contradiction that survives either birthday reading; it's included here because it's chapter 30's own numbers that create it.

## Two candidate findings cleared and dropped

- The "fourteen months to get big" (L5) vs. "readership... by the following spring" (L8) initially looked like two conflicting timelines for the same milestone. Checked against chapters/25_nineteen.md:83 (blog starts "in September" of her nineteenth year): fourteen months from that start lands within chapter 30's own dateline, and "readership by the following spring" reads as an earlier, partial milestone on the way there, not a competing final date. Not a finding.
- The funding/donor answer (L89) initially looked like an undersized non-reaction from Chloe given her established curiosity about institutional rules. Cleared against the brief's own trap: the justification ("she was seven when she arrived, about as likely to wonder... as a child is to wonder who pays for a park") sits in the same sentence as the flagged line, which is exactly the pattern the brief warns is not a finding. Not a finding.
- The second-person "the way you enjoy a crossword" (L31) initially looked like a Voice slip — narrator addressing the reader directly rather than staying inside Chloe's POV. Checked against the rest of the manuscript (chapters/02, 05, 07, 21, 25 all use "the way you'd..." constructions in narration) and it's house style throughout the book, not a chapter-30-specific problem. Not a finding.
