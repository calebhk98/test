## Stage two: sit in every chair

**Sam, on "somebody tried industrial espionage on me" (L11).** I am Sam: best fighter in my year,
the one who takes point before anyone assigns it, checks on people I've hit before I do anything
else, walks into danger first. What I've just been told is a friend was physically infiltrated by
a paid operative. What I'd actually do: ask if the guy is still out there, ask if she needs
anything physical done about it — not just "are you ok" (L14) and then nothing until "what did
they say" (L53) six exchanges later, after she's already confronted the funders alone. The text's
established Sam is protective to the point of reflex; here he asks once and goes quiet through the
riskiest part (her solo visit to "four guys" backing the operation). *Unsure* — his character sheet
also permits treating her as capable and backing off, so this may be proportion rather than a gap.

**Eli, on the same reveal.** I am Eli: the group's actual security professional, the one whose
whole worldview is "a rule with a known enforcement mechanism," who finds vulnerabilities for a
living. What I've just been handed is a live physical-intrusion vector against a friend's company —
exactly my subject. What I'd actually do: ask how the stick was supposed to trigger, what the
delivery mechanism was, whether her air-gapping holds elsewhere. What the text has me do instead
is infer the personnel outcome a half-beat early — "so hes fired" (L32) — the one line in the
exchange that requires no technical knowledge at all. The character built to have the sharpest
technical reaction contributes the least technical one.

**Ruth, on "is anyone elses hiring weird" (L79).** I am Ruth: I correct claims for a living, and I
do not offer an explanation until I've built the case for it. What I've just been asked is whether
a friend's hiring pipeline is broken. What I'd actually do, on my own dial, is ask what the
take-home looks like, what the interview structure is, before diagnosing it — not skip straight to
a confident verdict. What the text has me do is deliver "thats a hiring pool problem. youre a
startup, the good ones are elsewhere" (L94) as a flat, uncaveated assertion, on a subject (startup
hiring economics) nothing on her sheet establishes her as informed about. *Unsure*, but worth
flagging because her diagnosis is also — per Nadia's own character sheet (see Stage Three) —
probably the wrong one, and nobody in the scene tests it the way Ruth tests everything else.

**Kavi, on "i also went and found who paid for it" (L38).** I am Kavi: I attack the mechanism, not
the person, and I register surprise almost never — my one documented register for a startling fact
is to ask about it, and only about the unresolved part of *how* it works. What I've just been told
is that a friend personally traced a funding chain in a week. What I'd actually ask is how — what
she used, what the trail looked like. What the text gives me instead is "you did what" (L41), a
flat register of alarm at *that* she did it, not curiosity about *how*. This is the one line in the
scene most out of register for the character delivering it.

**The four men above the tyre place, on being confronted (L47, L56).** I am one of four people who
funded and ran a repeated, multi-attempt infiltration against a functioning company — not a
one-off dare. I have just been told, in person, by the founder herself, that she traced me in a
week and isn't calling the police. What I'd actually do is some mix of bluff, minimize, or
threaten back — the operation was expensive and deliberate enough that simply folding costs
nothing. What the text gives the group is a single unexplained beat: "one of them apologised"
(L56). No name, no reason given for the operation in the first place, no reaction from the other
three. The one-line people the brief specifically flags — the ones a writer stops thinking about —
are exactly these four, and the text stops thinking about them the moment they've served the plot.

## Stage three: clear each paragraph against the list

Findings below are numbered continuously. Each has been checked against the brief's "Two things
that are not findings" before being written down — where competence or partial knowledge was the
live question, that reasoning is noted explicitly.

1. **Object never established.** (whole chapter, esp. L8, L20-23) Nadia's company is described by
   size, age, and radius of local fame, but the chapter never once states what it makes or sells.
   Given the chapter is titled after her and centers entirely on the company, this is a real gap -
   not a style complaint, an absent load-bearing fact. (NADIA.md's own sheet supplies "small
   diagnostic hardware for independent repair shops," but that never appears on this page.)

2. **Ambiguous pronoun referent.** (L8) "She's forty people and eleven months old" - the closest
   antecedent noun is "company" (L5), but "Nadia" is also a "she" one clause earlier, and the
   personification switch (a company being "forty people... years old") briefly reads as being
   about Nadia herself before the numbers resolve it. *Unsure* - resolves fine on a second pass.

3. **Dangling connective, no payoff.** (L64) "The hiring changes Nadia's mind" resists restatement:
   changes it about what? Nothing before this line is a mind Nadia holds that gets revised by
   anything after it. The chapter ends at L100 with the problem stated as unresolved unease, not
   with any visible change of mind. This is a joining-word failure at the paragraph level - the
   whole paragraph is the "joining word," and it has nothing on its right-hand side.

4. **Established character trait absent where the text sets it up.** (L70-91) Nadia's own character
   sheet names this exact interview scene as the place she "notices her own bar has drifted... she
   doesn't defend the drift, she names it flatly and starts fixing it in the same breath" (NADIA.md,
   "Personality off the page" / "Humiliated"), and separately that "she keeps a fast read on people
   who needed a slow one... and doesn't notice the bar moved until the pass rate is under a third"
   (NADIA.md, "Interior life"). The pass rate is stated as under a third at L70. But nowhere in the
   chapter does Nadia connect her own repeated easing of the take-home to the outcome - she never
   names the drift, only reports unfocused unease ("doesnt feel right though," L100). Her
   documented signature move (immediate, flat self-correction) is the one thing missing from the
   one scene built to trigger it. Checked against "competence is not an omission": this is not
   about a missing reaction to a threat, it's a documented, specific behavioral signature that the
   text sets up and then withholds.

5. **A wrong diagnosis is left standing as the last word.** (L94-100) Ruth's "thats a hiring pool
   problem. youre a startup, the good ones are elsewhere" is external-cause reasoning. Per finding
   4, the sheets establish the actual cause as Nadia's own drifting standard - not the applicant
   pool. Nadia's own last line ("doesnt feel right though," L100) gestures at doubting Ruth's
   answer but never tests or corrects it. The chapter closes on the wrong explanation, unchallenged,
   from the one character in the cast whose entire defining trait is not letting an unjustified
   claim stand (RUTH.md: "cannot leave an unjustified claim standing, in anyone's mouth including
   her own"). *This is the same finding as the Stage One friction noted at the top of this report,
   now confirmed against the character sheets rather than just the internal restatement.*

6. **Missing connective between two flatly opposed claims.** (L76 vs. L91) "She hires excellent
   people... by every measure available to her, very good" (L76) sits directly across from "i cant
   find anyone who can finish a thought" (L91) with no *but*, *yet*, or *even so* anywhere between
   them. Read plainly, one paragraph says the hires are excellent and the other says she can't find
   anyone competent. The irony is presumably intentional (glowing paper vs. thin interview
   performance) but the text supplies no joining word to signal that a turn is happening rather
   than a contradiction.

7. **Causal mismatch.** (L67) "She interviews four hundred people that year, because she's growing
   and because she does all of it herself." Doing all of it herself is a bottleneck, not a driver
   of volume - it explains why the process is exhausting for her, not why there were four hundred
   candidates. The two clauses are joined by "and" as if both cause the same effect; only the first
   actually does.

8. **Scale tension, unresolved.** (L8 vs. L67) "known only within three counties" sits against
   interviewing four hundred people "that year." A company with purely local, three-county name
   recognition drawing four hundred applicants is a large number for that footprint, and nothing in
   the chapter accounts for where the volume comes from (regional trade schools, remote postings,
   word of mouth). *Unsure* - plausible, but the text doesn't do the work to make it so.

9. **Signature violation, already flagged in the sheet.** (L35) "obviously i fired him, he came
   here to steal from me" uses the pronoun rather than naming the cleaner, contradicting Nadia's
   established signature (naming the person under discussion instead of defaulting to a pronoun,
   NADIA.md "Signature: theirs alone"). The sheet itself proposes the fix ("the cleaner... the
   cleaner") and cites this exact chapter as the weak instance. Confirmed against the actual text.

10. **Ruth asks a genuine question.** (L26) "what did he do when it didnt work" - a real, open
    question, against Ruth's own dial ("questions | 0% measured... never a question mark," RUTH.md).
    *Unsure* - the same pattern (Ruth asking plain, unhedged questions in chat) recurs elsewhere in
    the manuscript (e.g. chapters/24_the_chat.md:260, :292), so this may be a standing gap between
    the sheet and the book rather than a chapter-28-specific error.

11. **Kavi reacts with alarm rather than mechanism.** (L41) "you did what" registers surprise at
    *that* Nadia acted, not curiosity about *how* - against Kavi's dial ("On the rare occasion he
    asks anything, it targets one specific unresolved mechanism," KAVI.md). See Stage Two above.
    *Unsure* for the same reason as #10 - Kavi asks non-mechanism questions elsewhere too (e.g.
    chapters/24_the_chat.md:269, "whats the horse called").

12. **Eli asks an open question.** (L82) "whats weird" is a genuine unresolved question, against

13. **Sam's question doesn't match his established type.** (L53) "what did they say" is
    conversational curiosity, not a question about duration, a rule, or a number - the three

14. **Chronology tension between chapters.** (L3, L8) The chapter states "May 2024" and "eleven
    months old," which requires the company to have been founded around June 2023. But
    chapters/23_the_first_one.md:110-137 has Nadia spend "two months looking at jobs" after
    graduation, then return to the family shop, and only "by June she's started something of her
    own, and by September she's hiring" - and chapters/24_the_chat.md, explicitly dated to "the
    first September that finds every one of them somewhere else" after graduation (chapter_dates.json:
    "24": "September 2023"), shows Nadia still behind the counter with no company at all, having
    just failed two months of her *own* job interviews ("since none of the two months of interviews

    the June/September immediately after that (i.e. June/September 2024), the company would not
    exist yet in May 2024, one month before its own founding, contradicting "eleven months old."
    If it refers to June/September 2023 instead, it contradicts ch24's September-2023 snapshot of
    Nadia still jobless at the shop. Either reading breaks against one of the other two chapters.
    *Unsure* in the sense that the manuscript's own dating is ambiguous here (chronology/BOOK.md
    documents comparable ordering problems elsewhere, e.g. chapter 20), but the contradiction
    itself is real and checkable from the primary sources.

15. **Ruth's chat presence may not fit her documented state.** chronology/BOOK.md's timeline places

16. **Ruth opines outside established expertise.** (L94) "youre a startup, the good ones are

18. **Group drops off after the topic shifts.** (L79-100) Sam, Eli, and Kavi are all active
