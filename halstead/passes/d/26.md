1. **(L5)** Sam likes the Army right away.
2. **(L8)** He likes the food, the people, and the schedule (which he's had daily since seven, minus a brief gap over the summer); he's polite to everyone, especially to a Sergeant Ruiz who yells at him for four weeks and whom he likes a lot.

14. **(L49)** Sam explains real "accuracy," as he defines it, as two soldiers firing live rounds to intercept each other's rounds in flight on a shared count; he's at about 40%, people from his school hit 90%, and he calls himself "forty percent of a rifle."

18. **(L61)** The captain asks where that is.

21. **(L74)** The report is four paragraphs: three describing the range, a fourth asking where a 19-year-old private learned this, using the phrase "appears to have received specialised instruction from an unidentified source," and recommending the question be answered before he's assigned anywhere sensitive; by the following Thursday a woman three levels above the captain, in an office built for exactly this question, is reading it.

## Stage 2 — Sit in every chair

**The captain (unnamed), first exchange (L28–L43).**
*I am* a captain observing or reviewing a range qualification.
*What I've just been given* is a soldier's perfect qualification score, and then,
when I ask about it, a claim from that soldier that the score is meaningless and
that his real number is 40%, contradicted by the sheet in my hand.
*What I would actually do* is ask what he means before restating the sheet at him
a second time, or ask what "accuracy" means to him — press on the actual claim.
What the text has him do instead: recheck the sheet silently (L31), then simply
repeat "you hit ten out of ten" (L37) without asking Sam to explain the "forty"
he'd just offered. **Finding: the captain's second line (L37) is not a response to
anything Sam said between L31 and L37 — Sam hasn't yet explained what "forty" is a
percentage of, so the captain restating the ten-of-ten score reads as talking past
him rather than answering him.**

**The captain, second exchange (L49–L55).**
*I am* still that captain.
*What I've just been given* is a description of live rounds being fired at each
other and intercepted in flight, on a shared count, by teenagers — an
extraordinary, safety-relevant claim if true, and a nonsensical one if false.
*What I would actually do* is challenge the claim itself: ask how that's possible,
ask if he's serious, at minimum register surprise on the page.
What the text has him do: "There's a silence" (L52), then pivot straight to
"Where did you go to school" (L55). **Finding: the single most extraordinary
sentence in the chapter — a description of live-fire round interception — gets a
bare, undescribed silence as its only on-page reaction, and the follow-up question
is about Sam's school, not about the claim itself. Nothing shows the captain's
attention snagging on "school" over the far stranger material in the same speech.**

**The captain, writing the report (L72–L74, off-page).**
*I am* that captain again, now filing paperwork.
*What I've just done* is turn a private's unprompted, oddly humble self-correction
into a four-paragraph flagged concern that reaches someone three levels up within
the week.
*What I would actually do*, on the page, is show some decision point — a beat of
belief, doubt, or escalation — between "the captain doesn't say anything" (L43)
and a report using the phrase "specialised instruction from an unidentified
source" (L74). **Finding: the entire escalation from confused silence to formal
security-flagged report happens off-page with no shown moment where the captain
decides to take the claim literally and act on it.**

**Sergeant Ruiz (L8), mentioned, never present.**
*I am* the sergeant who yells at Sam weekly and whom Sam likes; established as
Sam's main point of contact in training. *What I've been given*: nothing — I don't
appear in the range scene. **Finding (unsure): if Ruiz is established in the same
paragraph block as Sam's primary training relationship, his total absence from the
scene that produces a security file on Sam is never addressed one way or the
other — no line says Ruiz wasn't there, or was there and said nothing, or heard
about it after.**

**Sam (viewpoint-adjacent), at each beat he's given something.**
*What I know*: I hit every target; the score is being called the best of the
cycle. *What I don't know*: what "accuracy" means to the Army versus what it means
at Halstead, and that offering the distinction unprompted will read as a threat
assessment rather than a correction. *What I would do*: say the true thing,
assuming (per his sheet — see Context, below) that an honest answer is what's
being asked for. This matches the text exactly (L40, L49) and matches his
character sheet's stated blind spot, so it is not a new finding — noted here only
to confirm the chair-sit was done for the viewpoint-adjacent figure too, per the
brief's instruction.
