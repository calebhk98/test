## Stage two — sitting in the chairs (representative pass)

- **The district-office staffer (lines 41, 44–46).** *I am* a phone-desk employee at a school district office. *What I've been asked* is to check whether an unfamiliar residential school has any relationship with the district. *What I'd actually do* is check a vendor/partner list and call back — which is what happens. But the text also has her volunteer, unprompted, a specific and confident theory about a child-targeting scam pattern ("people who send things to houses with a kid's name on the outside... that's how you find out which houses write back," line 46) and a caution to "be careful." That's a specific, pre-formed theory about a targeting scheme, not the kind of thing a district clerical worker would have ready-made for a call like this unless she'd been briefed on it before — it reads as author-knowledge placed in a one-line character's mouth. Finding.
- **The school's phone representative (lines 50–74).** *I am* whoever answers a school's general line and fields a call from a suspicious parent. *What I've been asked* is where a specific child's name came from. *What I'd actually do*, if I ran a legitimate program that gets this exact question from every family, is have a ready answer — not force a parent through three separate long silences (53, 55, 58) before finally giving a vague non-answer ("networks... usually pretty good"). The withholding reads as intentional plot mystery rather than an error, so not logged as a finding on its own, but it sets up finding on 63 below.
- **The librarian (lines 121–126).** *I am* a children's librarian being asked to bend the four-book limit. *What I'd do* is exactly what's shown — decline flatly, then find a workable middle ground (holding one book). Matches; no finding.
- **Chloe, viewpoint character, told/given/asked things throughout.** At the dinner table she is told nothing and simply watches (line 33) — consistent with her established compliance-trained economy. In the library scene she reasons like a lawyer for a six-year-old (line 120) — see the Stage 3 finding on that line; it's a real tension with her age even if the character sheet already flags similar moments elsewhere in the book as an open authorial question.
