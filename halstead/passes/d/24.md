## Stage Two: Sit in Every Chair

**Chloe's doctor** (L529). I am a physician being asked by a patient about a medication she's taken
before but can no longer identify. What I've just been asked is to identify a pill from a
description and a spelling. What I would actually do: ask what it was prescribed for, check
whether it might be a foreign or discontinued brand name, check the generic/pharmacological class
against symptoms, possibly ask the patient to bring in the packaging or check a pharmacy record —
not stop at one failed search. The text gives just "typed it in and got nothing," which reads as a
dead end manufactured for the plot rather than what an actual doctor's next several moves would
look like. *(unsure — this may be intentionally compressed for pacing.)*

**The retail clerk** (L394). I am selling computers off a shelf. A customer comes back asking if
we have "the proper ones... out the back," implying specs far beyond anything a consumer store
would stock (1TB of memory). What I'd actually think: she's joking, since no retail laptop remotely
approaches that spec. This holds up — not a finding.

**Ruth's roommate** (L118–127). I am a college student meeting my assigned roommate for the first
time this term, already particular about my things. What I've just noticed is two inches of
displacement in a spice rack. What I would do: notice fast, say something, if I'm as particular as
described. Holds up — not a finding.

**Eli's boss** (referenced throughout, never appears). I am a manager who made an offhand joke
about a corporate bug bounty to a junior employee. What actually happens next, off the page, is
that employee spends an afternoon probing the company's live systems, finds five real
vulnerabilities including one thought impossible, and gets his employee "a meeting with a
lawyer" (L101). Nothing in the text shows the boss reacting to any of this — not disciplining Eli,
not marveling at him, not explaining why he made the joke in the first place. Given how much
narrative weight this episode carries (it recurs across three other chapters per Eli's character
sheet), the total absence of the boss's own reaction, even secondhand, is worth flagging under
Reaction below.

**Eli himself, on the underlying decision** (L31–107). I am a security-minded new hire. What I've
been told, verbally and offhandedly, is that my boss says there's a bounty, with "no rules anywhere
on the site" (L34) to confirm it. What would I actually do: before probing a live employer system
without written authorization — which is unauthorized access regardless of what a boss joked about
— a security-minded person would want that in writing first, precisely because (per his own later
established character) Eli treats risk as "a rule with a known enforcement mechanism" to be
calculated, not waved through on a rumor. Acting on an unverified verbal claim to test production
systems reads as a gap between his later-established caution and his behavior here. *(unsure — could
be read as youthful overconfidence rather than an error, and the chapter's own outcome, "a meeting
with a lawyer," partly punishes the choice.)*

**Ruth, on Eli's escapade** (L69–104). I am the group's most reliable corrector of unjustified
claims, someone who treats a rule broken on a hunch as something to flag. What I've just watched a
friend do is probe his own employer's live systems on the strength of a joke, find five holes,
turn them in, and end up facing a lawyer. What I would actually do, given her established character
(RUTH.md: "does not let an old wrong statement go unaddressed," "rejects being asked to accept a
claim on outcome alone"), is ask exactly how much legal exposure that meeting represents, not just
"which one was the cheat" (L75). The text has her focus on the technical trivia of which bug was
gamed, not on whether her friend is in real trouble. See Reaction, below.

**Kavi, teaching the twelves and eating with them** (L473–509). I am a researcher who chose to stay
at the school. What I've just been asked is whether I'm "allowed" at the adult table or paid a real
wage. What I'd actually say is exactly what's on the page — flat, minimal, "enough" — which matches
his profile precisely. Holds up — not a finding.

**The doctor / Nadia / Kavi, on "internal"** (L517–552). Three separate people (a doctor, Nadia
checking online, Kavi checking with contacts) all independently hit the same wall around one
unlisted pill. Sitting in the chat's collective chair: what I've just learned is that my old school

them do. See Reaction, below.

## Stage Three: Checklist

### Joining words

- **L204, "so":** "eli: so thats nobody" concludes "nobody" from responses that included Sam's
  "maybe" (L198) and Priya's non-answer (L201), neither of which is a firm no. The "so" claims a
  clean count the preceding lines don't actually support. Ruth calls the tally out immediately
  ("you said it like a scoreboard," L219), which suggests this may be deliberate characterization
  of Eli's bluntness rather than an authorial slip. *(unsure.)*
- **L95, "anyway":** "eli: anyway. no speedrun" — closes the bounty thread cleanly; the "anyway" has
  a clear left side (the joke wasn't real) and right side (so, no payout). Holds up.
- **L107, "same... running":** ties the encryption's untested status to "the same nine years
  running," which is the arithmetic problem detailed under Order below — the joining phrase asserts
  a duration the rest of the chapter can't support.

### Objects

- **L350, "Somebody posts a screenshot":** the poster is never named, which breaks the chapter's
  otherwise consistent practice of attributing every chat contribution to a specific person. Ruth's
  very next line, "mine says something almost identical" (L353), reads as if she might be the
  poster owning up to it, or might be a second, unrelated example — the text doesn't say which, and
  the screenshot itself (who it belonged to, whose name was blacked out) is never resolved.
- **L541–549, the pill:** never named on the page even provisionally (no brand, no description
  beyond "small," "white," "twice a day"). Given three separate characters independently try and
  fail to identify it, the total absence of any identifying detail beyond color and dosage frequency
  is a notably thin object for a plot beat this load-bearing. *(unsure — likely deliberate, since
  the mystery is the point.)*
- **L60, "the internal network map... the ticket he's supposed to be closing":** both introduced and
  then dropped; the ticket in particular never resurfaces to show whether Eli's actual job suffered
  from the afternoon spent hunting bugs. Minor.

### Reaction

- **L101–107:** Eli reports "a meeting with a lawyer" as the outcome of unauthorized probing of his
  employer's systems. Nobody in the chat asks whether he's about to be fired, sued, or otherwise in
  real trouble. Given the cast includes a federal analyst (Theo) and the group's most rigorous
  risk-corrector (Ruth), the absence of any follow-up concern about the actual legal/employment
  stakes is a sizeable reaction gap. Quote before: "eli: i got a meeting with a lawyer" (L101).
  Quote after: "eli: which honestly has been the most interesting thing thats happened to me since i
  started" (L104) — the chat simply accepts this framing and moves on.
- **L549–555:** the group learns their old school issued them an unlisted, untraceable medication
  that a doctor has never heard of and that doesn't exist in any searchable database. The only
  visible reaction is Chloe's — she "leaves the tab open... the cursor sitting in the empty box
  where a reply would go" (L555) — which is understated to the point of ambiguity for a discovery
  this alarming, especially set against a cast that includes someone whose literal job is chasing
  exactly this kind of institutional gap (Theo) and someone constitutionally unable to let an
  unjustified claim stand (Ruth). Neither says anything on the page. *(flagged with lower confidence
  — the muted ending may be intentional dread rather than an oversight, and this scene doubles as
  connective tissue to the "internal" thread paid off in ch34.)*
- **L204–219:** Eli's "so thats nobody" gets one pushback (Ruth) but no reaction at all from Priya,
  whose own non-answer ("what timezone are we even doing this in," L201) is arguably the most
  evasive reply in the thread and goes completely unremarked by anyone.

### Position

- The chat-line sections are plain text exchanges with no staging, which is appropriate to the
  medium and not a finding. The narrative interludes (L11, L60, L115, L280, L315, L432, L473) all
  give concrete physical staging and hold up under this check.

### Redundancy

- **L14 and L17** both restate the cast's response-time patterns for overlapping people (Theo,
  Nadia, Sam are covered in both) within three paragraphs of each other, before any chat content has
  even started. L14: "Some of them answer inside a minute no matter the hour... Others answer in
  bursts." L17: "A message can sit read for hours... Theo reads everything within minutes... Nadia
  reads fast and answers fast, except... Sam goes dark for stretches." The second paragraph mostly
  re-specifies what the first already established in more general terms, for the same three named
  people, before the chapter's chat scenes give any of it dramatic weight.

### Order

- **L5 vs. L107 and L552 — the headline finding.** L5 states: "The chat is nine years old. They
  wrote the encryption themselves at thirteen." L107 states the encryption itself "has gone nine
  years without anyone who had real reason to try it... the same nine years running." L552 repeats
  this: "The lock they built at thirteen... the same lock nine years running." Both L107 and L552
  assert the encryption/lock, built at age thirteen, has *itself* been running for nine years — which
  would put the cast at roughly 22. But every other time marker in the chapter (Ruth three weeks
  into her first year at MIT, Theo freshly started at his first job "in July," Chloe's laptop dying
  "the week before her first classes start," Sam in the early stretch of basic training) places this
  chapter right after graduation, at roughly eighteen to nineteen — which is also the only reading
  consistent with L5's own claim that "the chat is nine years old" (started around age nine, per the
  chapter's own arithmetic). A lock added partway through a nine-year-old chat's life cannot itself
  have been running for the chat's entire nine years; L107 and L552 both say it has. The chapter
  contradicts its own arithmetic twice, in two different places, about the same object.
- **L347:** "In the space of one evening the same names move from a federal supervisor's notes to a
  fall-and-catch drill run by a sergeant to the exact gigabyte count on a store laptop." This
  sentence sits between the trust-falls scene and the performance-review scene, and lists the
  gigabyte/laptop beat as though it has already happened — but the laptop scene doesn't actually
  appear until L373–427, after the performance-review scene that immediately follows this line, and
  it's introduced there with its own separate time marker ("the week before her first classes
  start," L373) rather than being tied to "that evening." Read charitably this is a preview
  sentence; read literally it cites an event out of the order the text actually delivers it in.
  *(unsure.)*

### Voice

- **L552, "Whatever internal means, it's a wall each of them keeps running into."** This sentence
  steps outside the scene to tell the reader what the preceding exchange means, rather than letting
  the exchange's flatness (Kavi's one-word answer, Chloe's unanswered question) carry it. A mild
  instance of the narrator glossing the scene's significance instead of just showing it.
- **L107, the build-pipeline paragraph.** Similarly steps back from the chat to draw an explicit
  thematic parallel ("Their own encryption has gone nine years...") between Eli's employer and the
  group's own chat — narrator-supplied interpretation rather than a moment any character voices.

### Specificity

- **L107, "somebody like Eli looking at it sideways."** After a chapter otherwise full of exact
  technical specifics (a build pipeline exploited "sideways," a 16GB/1TB laptop, "nine other
  guys... at the same folding tables"), this phrase reaches for a category ("somebody like Eli")
  rather than naming the particular thing he actually did to find the flaw. Minor, and possibly
  intentional understatement given the technical mechanism was never going to be spelled out.
