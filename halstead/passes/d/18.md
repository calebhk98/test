## Restatement

1. Chapter heading: "Chapter Eighteen: Fifteen."
3. Dateline: September 2020 – July 2021.
5. All year they've been hitting clays 90–100%, steady through end of September; in October Voss shows them a new lane and a "turret."
7. The turret fires a live round across a 60m lane on a fixed cycle; students stand behind glass and shoot to intercept it on a shared count.
9. Narrator states flatly: they are shooting bullets out of the air.
11. Five weeks of zero hits for everyone; Kavi calculates someone will finally hit something around March; Priya tells him to eat.
13. Odile gets the first hit on 11 November; the noise draws people in from the far end of the range.
15. Chloe gets hers nine days later; a flat crack, no new mark on the far paper, and she's still aiming when told to clear.
17. Her hit count then climbs fast: 20 by end of November, 41 by mid-December, 60 by the last week of January.
19. Then it stops.
21. Mid-February Wednesday: she scores 61, then 59, adjusts her count timing, then scores 60 again, and reloads before the card's even marked.
23. Voss lets her shoot five more series; on the sixth he takes the rifle from her.
25. Voss: "Off the point."
27. Chloe: "One more."
29. Voss repeats it, names her, notes she's tried the same number four different ways in ninety minutes, sends her to dinner.
31. She eats without registering what's on the plate.
33. She tries her old tricks — counting on her leg "the way it did when she was nine," varying her breath-hold point, collecting six weeks of cards on her floor, chasing a pattern that turns out to be wind through the lane, until the card frays apart in her hands.
35. Later that month she asks the office for every card in the year; a woman hands her all ninety, unsecured.
37. Four evenings' work: eleven people have plateaued — five at 60, three at 58, two at 55, one at 63 since November — and the text says the curve "flattens in the same place" for all eleven regardless of where they started.
39. Voss reads the four evenings' work in the doorway of the range office.
41. Chloe: "That's every card in the year... Eleven of us stopped in the same place and it doesn't matter where we started."
43. Voss: "It's a real thing."
45. Chloe: "So what is it?"
47. Voss squares the pages, hands them back, and sends her to point six because her block already started.
49. Ruth comes down on a Saturday in April to time her session for two hours, because Chloe asked.
51. Ruth: "Your count's clean."
53. Chloe: "I know my count's clean."
55. Ruth clarifies she timed the whole session — Chloe's release is tighter than her own by a hundredth, and Ruth is at 91% — so the problem's elsewhere.
57. Chloe: "Where?"
59. Ruth: doesn't know, offers a toffee instead.
61. At dinner that week, Sam, not meeting her eyes, tells Chloe she's at 60.
63. Chloe: "I know I'm on sixty. And?" — her own plate sits untouched.
65. Sam: he's at 40.
67. Chloe asks how that doesn't bother him.
69. Sam: he's at 40 and does other things; tells her to go be miserable at Ruth instead, who's at "ninety-something."
71. Section break.
73. Self-defence: still ten-on-one, a minute on the clock, three rounds an hour; her yearly average is 41 seconds, down from 45 at fourteen.
75. The bad round: a Tuesday in November, first round, she comes out at 38 seconds, on her feet before it's called, already knowing the number.
77. Kavi, from the line: "First one's always short... It recovers by the third."
79. She gets 40 on the second, 39 on the third; afterward she reworks it at the mat edge; Odile asks her something on the way out that she answers badly, so she resolves to answer properly at dinner.
81. On the walk back she tallies that Odile is up seven seconds and Sam up four on last year, and two boys she used to beat are now ahead of her by nine and eleven seconds.
83. She corners Kowalczyk on a Friday at the mat-room door as the next group files past.
85. Kowalczyk: "Yeah."
87. Chloe: "That's it? That's all you've got for me?"
89. Kowalczyk explains at length (while shelving wraps) that the ten-year-olds she used to beat had no experience, that these fifteen-year-olds have five years of training now, and predicts it'll happen again at eighteen when the boys finish growing.
91. Chloe: "That's still not an answer. What am I supposed to do?"
93. Kowalczyk: "It's the only one there is... You just have to learn faster," and sends her back in.
95. Second Thursday in March: Odile goes into the middle; by 40 seconds thirty people are watching through a propped door; Chloe calls count with eleven others; at a minute the room reacts audibly; Odile holds nine more seconds, then goes down on hands and knees.
97. Chloe is second to reach her; Odile's breathing is ragged, arms shaking; Chloe stays down with her the rest of the hour.
99. On each of the next two Thursdays, Chloe replays the last twenty seconds of Odile's round after the room empties, finding nothing new.
101. Section break.
103. Hark hands out $200 envelopes in October for a year-long spending exercise; leftover money in June is theirs to keep.
105. Hark: everyone loses money in life; better to lose $200 now under supervision than more later alone.
107. Rustem buys nine dead motors at auction for $11 each, four running by Christmas; in January he asks Chloe for $200.
109. She checks his numbers, offers $120 at 8% secured by the motors; he argues the rate for eleven minutes and loses; insists she see the barn first; she goes Saturday, finds nearly sixty motors there, a third beyond saving; leaves her offer unchanged.
111. She lends $40 to Fen at 9% against a table saw, and $30 to Priya at 12%, unsecured, "because it is Priya."
113. All three repay her; she ends the year at $217 versus Rustem's $940.
115. Hark puts the ledger up in April; Chloe (or Hark — see findings) stands at the back, arms folded, reading all three columns before speaking.
117. Hark: "You bought a piece of somebody else's ceiling."
119. Chloe defends the Rustem call: he was capped at $200 and she wasn't; she didn't need to know bearings, just whether he did, and he'd "already sold four."
121. Hark: "You had the right numbers and the wrong answer. How much of it could you have lost?"
123. Chloe: "Thirty."
125. Hark: contrasts Rustem's five burned motors and Priya's $80 loss against Chloe's cautious $17 profit — "barely beating inflation."
127. Chloe wipes the board and sits down.
129. March: she ties her first suture, slower than the boy next to her, marked "steady" plus a time; also doing accounting, tax, and Hindi; by June the topology book's been stuck on the same proof since Easter.
131. Her name lands sixth-of-nine on an internal paper's author list in March (previously only acknowledgments); she rereads it four times and looks up the other eight authors.
133. Dr. Sandoval catches her in the corridor outside the lab in April, after a session.
135. Sandoval: frames what follows explicitly as a request, not an instruction.
137. Chloe sets her bag down: "All right, shoot."
139. Sandoval explains the NDA, that she won't ask a fifteen-year-old to sign one, that talking would land the consequences on the four signatories, not on Chloe.
141. Chloe: "So you're asking me not to talk about it."
143. Sandoval: "You can't be held to anything. I can. So asking is what I have."
145. Chloe loses sleep, snaps at Sam and apologises, spends a week building the case against complying and then the case for it (from Sandoval's side, her own, and the four names on the form); she's quiet at dinner Wednesday and Sam checks on her twice.
147. She simply stops talking about her afternoons; within about four days it's no longer a conscious decision.
149. Section break.
151. Her father comes out with the car keys on the second Sunday of summer, walks past her to the passenger door, says "Get in."
153. Chloe: points out school will teach driving next year, he doesn't have to.
155. Father: doesn't care, opens his own door, claims that door as his, tells her "Other side."
157. He takes her to an empty church lot; she stalls eleven times in the first half hour, four in the second; by the third Sunday she's driving past the reservoir with his coffee still full at the eleven-mile mark.
159. Father: "You're looking too far ahead."
161. Chloe: "You said look ahead."
163. Father: clarifies, points with the cup at a truck: "What's he doing?"
165. Chloe reads the truck's likely move in detail — hands still at ten-and-two, white-knuckled.
167. Her father drinks some coffee and tells her to carry on.
169. By end of July she can drive; the clutch is still hard; at dinner her father tells her mother, laughing, that she reads a junction like a fifteen-year veteran but pulls from a stop sign like a child.
171. A week later her grandmother takes her hand at the table to look at a ring, and keeps holding it after the ring's gone round.
173. Grandmother examines the hand: a March burn, a smaller burn, and a knuckle set crooked from a self-treated injury at eleven at night.
175. Grandmother runs a thumb over it all while the ring conversation continues above them; Chloe lets her.
177. Grandmother: "And what did this one to you, then?"
179. Chloe: "It's nothing, it's from school. I made a mistake in class, but it wasn't that bad."
181. Grandmother turns the hand over once more, sets it down, pats it twice, and asks the aunt where she got the ring.

## Findings

1. Line 33: "The count goes on her leg walking down to the range, the way it did when she was nine." The leg-counting habit is established in chapters/14_sixty_degrees.md ("she starts practising it without a bow at all, walking to dinner in the dark with four fingers going against the side of her leg"), a chapter dated September 2016 – April 2017 per chronology/chapter_dates.json. Chloe (born August 2005, per characters/CHLOE.md) is eleven throughout that span, not nine. Chapter 12 ("Nine," dated September 2014 – July 2015) contains no counting-on-the-leg reference at all. The callback age is wrong.
2. Lines 37 and 41: "The curve flattens in the same place for all eleven, whether they started at forty or at twelve" and "Eleven of us stopped in the same place and it doesn't matter where we started" — directly contradicted by the data given one sentence earlier in line 37 itself: "five of them at sixty, three at fifty-eight, two at fifty-five, and one... at sixty-three." Four different plateau values are asserted to be "the same place" twice, once by the narrator and once by Chloe.
3. Line 37: "whether they started at forty or at twelve" contradicts line 11: "For five weeks every card in the year reads zero," which establishes that everyone, without exception, began this specific drill at 0%, not at 40 or 12.
4. Lines 7–15 (position): the round is fired "down the lane" (i.e., along its length) but is then said to cross the shooters "left to right," and a "paper down at the far end" registers misses — an object never mentioned in the setup at lines 5–9. Unsure whether this is a real geometry problem or just underdescribed blocking.
5. Line 23: "Voss gives her five more series. On the sixth he is at her shoulder with his hand out for the rifle." It's unclear whether "the sixth" counts from the start of the session (i.e., only three of the five permitted series are used) or is a sixth series she takes without having been given it. Unsure — possibly just loosely worded.
