20. The pattern of double-explaining spreads to other coworkers and her mother; Chloe gets fast at producing the second version and sees it as ordinary courtesy.

21. Chloe starts a blog in September because her evenings are hers and she needs to fill them.

22. Her first blog piece, done in one Saturday, is about a fishing dispute where two countries describe the same event differently; she reads both countries' newspapers, checks the treaty against both summaries, finds a sentence neither quotes anymore, sits in the dark until midnight producing 6000 words plus a 500-word summary, and posts it in four places.

24. Her blog topics keep shifting through various international disputes; by November she posts 2-3 pieces weekly on top of her job and 21 credits, mostly written 11pm-2am on a laptop propped against work manuals, and by January commenters are reading whole pieces and arguing about treaty text.

25. A man emails in February asking whether she has a research team, citing two of her pieces and asking how many people it takes to produce that much alongside a full-time job.

27. He doesn't reply.

28. She rereads his message once, closes the tab, and leaves it there, having told him the truth and recognizing it as just arithmetic.

29. In a chat group, ruth asks what percentage of Americans speak a second language conversationally; ruth says 25% (mostly from immigration, not school); the others guessed 85-90 and are far off.

30. After nine minutes of silence, nadia teases theo about parking; the conversation moves on naturally.
