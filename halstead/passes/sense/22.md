# Pass One: Chapter Twenty-Two

## Restatement

1. Amberg personally conducts fifteen-minute exit interviews with ten students daily for nine days starting the first Monday of April, using a daily posted list and a runner's call system.

2. Chloe's interview slot is Wednesday of the second week at three o'clock, in a small office with just a desk, two chairs, and a one-way clock, with her folder already open.

3. Amberg offers her four things: full-time research work, three times standard entry researcher pay (minimum $135k), full housing/food/car/medical coverage, and a degree funded on her schedule with the position held indefinitely.

4. Chloe mentally calculates the offer at roughly $155k annual total and keeps her hands flat on her knees while thinking through it.

5. Chloe is weighing something deeper—her history teaching here, her work in the building, her four-year routine, and the fact that this place sent her a meaningful letter when she was seven.

6. Chloe declines.

7. Amberg asks why.

8. Chloe explains that it's her school, and the principle is that nobody stays at their school—you move on to the next thing.

9. Amberg writes it down without arguing, closes the folder neatly, and spends the remaining four minutes silently reading through the rest of the file like a teacher reviewing a mark scheme.

10. At exactly fifteen minutes, both stand, and the next student is already waiting in the doorway.

11. Over nine days ninety-one names are posted, and by day three the word spreads through the building; by day six a second sheet must be taped below the first.

12. April proceeds normally with the twelves' History class, open forge hours, and standard lunch times.

13. Six chairs line the wall outside Amberg's office, always at least two occupied, with the coffee cart smell and repeated runner footsteps creating a familiar rhythm.

14. While waiting eleven minutes, Chloe observes a boy mechanically re-reading one page, a girl staring silently at the door, and another boy with a spreadsheet showing three different arithmetic versions.

15. A girl from materials emerges before Chloe's turn, sits hard in the chair by the window with her bag still on, and says half to herself that she'll think about it.

16. A boy waiting for the four o'clock slot stands ready with a laptop bag already on, as if heading to a business meeting.

17. Chloe asks "Six?" referring to how quickly he went in and came out.

18. He explains he's had a desk here since he was fifteen, so the offer just made it official, then walks past her with a half-wave.

19. By the second week, the salary numbers stop being novel—everyone calculates versions aloud reaching over $100k, and eventually someone writes the sum on the whiteboard with another adding a question mark.

20. Two boys from the year below occupy a chair together briefly between interviews.

21. One asks if everyone gets the car included; the other confirms it.

22. The first checks his phone before asking which specific car.

23. The second boy says Amberg didn't specify which car.

24. A boy exits an hour before Chloe's turn and announces loudly that he refused the offer because his mother would disown him if he skipped college for money; three people laugh while he keeps reading his phone flatly.

25. A grinning girl two years younger than Chloe sits down and repeats the offer details twice excitedly while two boys waiting behind her let her have the space.

26. A boy from Chloe's year emerges with visibly shaking hands, stands at the window gripping the sill until he steadies, leaves without answering if he's okay, and the runner marks his name more carefully than the others.

27. The interesting fact afterward is that exactly eleven students said yes, all of whom had previous access to work on the upper floors; the eighty who declined return to finishing their school year.

28. Kavi is one of the eleven, and word spreads naturally before any official announcement; Chloe hears it in the common room before dinner and seeks him out.

29. Chloe finds Kavi in the network room with his back to the radiator, coiling stripped cable and turning the copper end between his fingers repeatedly while his eyes are elsewhere, as the overhead light buzzes unfixed.

30. Chloe asks him why.

31. Kavi answers that he's saying yes because the work he wants to do is happening in this building and he's here, still turning the cable.

32. Chloe objects that it's school.

33. Kavi responds that it stopped being school for him, explains that school teaches on others' schedules toward written answers, but here there are no written answers yet—he brought them a false positive rate they couldn't verify because nobody had checked it, which can't happen in a classroom where the job is already knowing, and he calls it "a lab that also has children in it" flatly before moving on.

34. Chloe watches the cable, recognizes this echoes what the laptop-bag boy said in six words, and leaves Kavi to strip the next foot of cable with the same methodical wrist turn.

35. Chloe calls home that night from her floor with her back against her bed, the phone already warm against her ear.

36. Her mother answers on the second ring already pacing her kitchen and asks how the interview went.

37. Chloe says she declined.

38. Her mother asks no to what, then realizes she doesn't know what was being offered.

39. Chloe lists the four components the way Amberg stated them: research, triple the salary, benefits, and the funded degree option.

40. The pacing stops.

41. After seconds, it resumes with her mother asking "Three times what."

42. Chloe explains forty-five is the entry rate elsewhere, so she'd get a hundred and thirty-five plus the rest.

43. Her mother repeats back "A hundred and thirty five" and mentions the car.

44. Chloe adds medical and the degree.

45. Her mother asks about the contract length.

46. Chloe says she doesn't know and didn't ask.

47. Her mother asks whether Chloe would have had to leave the dorms.

48. Chloe admits she didn't ask that either.

49. Her mother goes quiet with the specific pause she uses over mortgage statements, and the phone line stays silent through one full length of her kitchen-pacing loop.

50. Her mother calls to her father and he comes to the phone, both audibly breathing.

51. Her father states she said no.

52. Chloe picks at the carpet edge waiting for the challenge she already anticipates.

53. Her father asks what the catch is.

54. Chloe says there is no catch and repeats her reason: it's her school, nobody stays, you move to the next thing.

55. Both parents accept it; her mother asks three times in different phrasings whether Chloe is sure and gets the same answer each time before stopping; her father waits through it before asking if anyone else said yes.

56. Chloe answers "Kavi."

57. Her father gives a short cut-off laugh and says "Good for him."

58. Her father asks about Ruth and Sam, the two whose names he already knows.

59. Chloe confirms both made the same choice: Ruth to MIT, Sam to the Army.

60. Her father says "Good," with full feeling behind it.

61. Before ending the call, her mother states the thing she will repeat every summer for two years: about the nearby state school, its real cost like they've always said, and that they'll handle it the way they manage everything else, all said in the flat voice of reading a mortgage clause.

62. Chloe says okay.

63. Her mother says okay back and clicks off first.

64. Chloe sits with the dark phone in her lap for a while before getting up to find food.
