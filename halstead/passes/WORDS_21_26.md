## A note on the coordinator's mid-task correction

Partway through, I was told another agent had been converting active
`gets`/`goes`/`comes` into passive constructions (`is sent`, `is added`,
`is spent`) and that the whole span had been thrown out for it, since the
book runs at 4.81% passive sentences against a 7.88% corpus median — a
deliberate strength. I checked my own edits against this and found four
places where I had swapped one passive for another without noticing: `gets
called`→`is called` and `gets caught`→`is caught` in chapter 23, `gets
reviewed`→`is reviewed` in chapter 24, and `gets handed`→`is handed` and
`gets read`→`is read` in chapter 26. All five are reverted to the original
`gets` wording. The one genuine passive-to-active fix I made — "he gets
walked through it" → "she walks him through it" in chapter 21, which gives
the sentence an explicit agent rather than just changing which auxiliary
sits in front of the participle — is kept, since that is a real reduction in
passive voice, not a relabeling of it.

## A note on quotation length

Two edits, caught only after the fact, shortened a direct quotation: "Down
to the limit, in the end. I cut it." in chapter 21 (I had cut "in the end")
and Chloe's "Ruth's still going to MIT..." line in chapter 22 (I had cut the
first "still"). The brief said explicitly not to shorten existing speeches,
since the book's mean words per quotation sits at 18.10 against a floor of
18.0. Both are reverted to their original wording; the `end` and `still`
counts above reflect those quotes intact.

## Words I decided not to cut, and why

- **twice**, chapter 21 (11, unchanged): every single instance is doing
  specific work — the mother calling twice and getting "fine" both times,
  the officer rereading the accreditation number, the girl correcting a
  cipher schedule twice from memory, Chloe taking the letters out twice in
  the week to recount them. This chapter uses repeated checking as a
  running characterization device; cutting any of them would have been
  cutting the point, not the padding.

- **gets**, chapter 21, lines describing the March decisions ("Ruth gets
  MIT... Kavi gets MIT, Caltech and Chicago... Sam gets all but one... Odile
  gets in everywhere... Chloe gets all but two... the middle of a year gets
  the state school"): this is a deliberate parallel-structure list, and the
  last one is a direct callback to the chapter's opening line ("The middle
  of a year always ends up at the state school"). Left the whole cluster
  alone.

- **every**, chapter 22, the three appearances of "The review happens every
  March": Amberg says it once in the interview, and Chloe repeats it
  word-for-word to her mother and then to her father over the phone — the
  narration says outright that she "keeps the parts in Amberg's order."
  This is a verbatim repetition device, not overuse; touching any of the
  three would have broken the echo.

- **still**, chapter 22, most of the phone-call scene (lines ~195-235):
  this is the chapter's climax and almost entirely dialogue between Chloe
  and her parents. "Still" is carrying real freight there — "the door still
  open in a year," "I still think," "it's still the same name now" — and
  cutting it for a rate would have meant flattening the one scene the
  chapter exists to deliver.

- **already**, chapter 23: this chapter's throughline (most explicit in
  Priya's speech — "Every single year of my life has had something already
  decided for me before I got there") is about things being decided in
  advance. Most of the 24 instances of "already" are that same idea showing
  up in different characters (Ruth's box packed before the ceremony starts,
  Sam already signing before the recruiter finishes, Chloe already knowing
  the next two years). Cut what was safely redundant (4 instances) and left
  the rest.

- **goes**, chapter 23, the results list ("Ruth goes to MIT... Theo goes
  into the federal government... Priya... goes to South America"): same
  parallel-structure device as the `gets` cluster in chapter 21. Left
  intact.

- **before**, chapters 23, 25, and 26 (18, 10, and 23 respectively, almost
  unchanged): each of these chapters uses "before" to carry a genuine
  procedural or competence motif — people in chapter 23 doing things before
  they're asked (Sam signing before the recruiter finishes his sentence,
  Ruth packing before the ceremony starts), and chapters 25-26 are a tightly
  sequenced training/exercise narrative where "before" is load-bearing
  temporal logic, not scaffolding. I found almost nothing safe to cut
  without either breaking a causal chain or landing inside the protected
  officer speeches.

- **end**, chapters 22, 23, 25, 26 (largely unchanged): nearly every
  instance is either idiomatic ("the other end of the line," "on my end"),
  a literal physical detail (the far end of a rifle, the end of a bed,
  "kitchen-end stairs"), or inside the protected "everybody leaves school"
  reasoning in chapter 22, which the brief named directly as untouchable.

- **second**, chapters 22, 23, 25 (largely literal): most of the book's
  heaviest concentrations of "second" are literal ordinals in a chapter
  built around sequence (second week, second platoon, second contact,
  second paragraph) rather than filler "for a second" constructions. Cut
  the handful that were genuinely the filler kind and left the rest.
