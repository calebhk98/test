# Voice pass 2, chapters 17 and 18

Scope: `chapters/17_fourteen.md`, `chapters/18_fifteen.md`. Worked from
`halstead/`, read one chapter at a time, checked every quoted sentence
against the file with grep before writing it down. Dialogue not touched
anywhere. Protected and left alone, unread for defects beyond what was
already on record: Iyad's manufactured rumour and the Chloe/Iyad
confrontation in 18 (lines 125, 143-155), and Bex's account of the fight at
the sinks in 17 (line 55).

This pass runs on the real rule (`HOUSE_RULES.md` rule 8): a passive is
correct when topic continuity holds, when the missing agent is unknown,
irrelevant, or obvious, or when the receiver of the action is what the
sentence is about. The test on any suspect sentence is whether the missing
doer is a specific person already in the scene whose absence costs the
reader something. Model used throughout: sonnet, per rule 7.

## Changed, 2

### 1. chapters/17_fourteen.md, line 67

**Before:** "One of the twelves tells it at their own table, where it is
funny and nothing else, and it takes two days to get from there to Iyad."

**After:** "One of the twelves tells it at their own table, where it is
funny and nothing else, and it is carried from there to Iyad within two
days."

**The judgment, corrected.** The last pass (`VOICE_17_18.md`) called this
out and left it, on the reasoning that the doer is genuinely diffuse: this
is the rumour mill, deliberately never attributed to one carrier
(`DO_NOT_FLAG.md`), and naming a student would invent a fact. That reasoning
about the agent is right. But it answers the wrong question. Whether the
diffuse-agent defense holds is the test for whether a *passive* is licensed
here (it is, under rule 2: the agent is genuinely unknown and irrelevant,
ninety students passing something hand to hand with no single carrier to
name). It says nothing about whether the sentence as written is a passive at
all. It is not. "It takes two days to get from there to Iyad" gives the
rumour an active verb of motion ("get") with the rumour itself as the
grammatical subject doing the getting. That is the third thing the brief
warned about: not a person deleted from an active sentence, and not a
correct passive, but an inanimate subject taking an active verb of intent,
which is the book's own separate recurring fault and has nothing to do with
whether a person was dropped.

Once the two questions are separated, the fix is low-risk rather than a
judgment call: swap the verb for a genuine passive, which is exactly the
construction rule 2 licenses for an unknown, diffuse agent, and which
requires inventing nothing. The last pass's own **Replacement 1** ("the year
below carries it to Iyad within two days") does not fix this either. It
solves the wrong problem by inventing a different fake agent, a collective
noun cast as a single active doer, which is no more supported by the text
than a named student would be. Its **Replacement 2** ("within two days it
has been carried from there to Iyad") is the right shape; this edit keeps
that shape but preserves the original word order and count, which matters
because chapter 17 is over its word ceiling.

**Referent check:** "it" refers to the story about the backward teaching,
established two sentences earlier and carried across every sentence in the
paragraph; the rewrite does not touch that chain.

**Word count:** the clause is eleven words before and after. Chapter total
unchanged at 5,036.

### 2. chapters/18_fifteen.md, line 17

**Before:** "Voss delivers it like a thing he has said a thousand times to a
thousand students, and it is round the year by Thursday and back at Chloe's
own table before the end of the month, quoted with the settled confidence of
something printed in a manual."

**After:** "Voss delivers it like a thing he has said a thousand times to a
thousand students, and it has been carried round the year by Thursday and
back to Chloe's own table before the end of the month, quoted with the
settled confidence of something printed in a manual."

**The judgment.** Same case as line 67, and the last pass treated them as a
pair on the same (correct, as far as it went) diffuse-agent reasoning, and
left both. "It is round the year by Thursday" is not a passive either; "is
round" is a copula plus an adverbial standing in for "has gone round," which
still gives the line itself the active work of circulating. Ninety students
independently repeating something Voss said to all of them at once is
exactly the unknown/irrelevant/obvious agent case, rule 2, so the passive is
the right voice; the sentence just was not written as one. This edit makes
it one, names no carrier, and switches "back at" to "back to" because
"carried" wants a destination, not a location, which the original copula
did not need. No fact is added: the agent stays exactly as absent as it was.

**Referent check:** "it" is the line Voss says, named in the same sentence's
opening clause; unchanged by the edit.

**Word count:** chapter 18 has no ceiling problem (4,689 of a 2,000-5,000
band); this went from 4,689 to 4,691 words, two words for the whole chapter,
comfortably inside the band and inside the book's own 2,600-3,600 average
target for a single instance.

## Called out, not changed, 1

### chapters/18_fifteen.md, line 219

**Current:** "In both her hands she spreads it open, where there is a burn
across the base of the thumb from March and a smaller burn in the web
between her fingers."

**Why the current voice is wrong:** By Gopen and Swan's topic-position test
(`PROSE_PRINCIPLES_STRUCTURE.md`, section 5) and by rule 3 of house rule 8
(the receiver of the action is what the sentence is about), this sentence
should probably run passive. Chloe's hand, and what it shows, is what the
paragraph is actually tracking: the sentence before it and the sentence
after it are both about the hand's condition (the burns, the crooked
knuckle), not about the grandmother's actions as such. But the grammatical
subject of this sentence is "she," the grandmother, an active examiner; the
person whose story this actually is appears only as "her hands" and "it," an
object being looked at, at exactly the sentence carrying the chapter's most
private information. That is Williams's version of the same failure: the
character the sentence is about is not in the subject slot.

**Why it was not auto changed:** This is a known, standing judgment call,
not a fresh find. `PROSE_PRINCIPLES_STRUCTURE.md` (line 623) and
`PROSE_PRINCIPLES_APPLIED.md` (line 27) both raised it and both left it,
on the reasoning that the distancing may be the point: the whole scene
holds Chloe at a controlled remove on purpose, all the way to her own
deflecting line a few sentences later ("I'm not telling you which
corridor"), and a version that puts her more squarely in the subject slot
risks tipping into naming what she feels about the scars, which would spend
one of the five uses rule 1 allows a declared state and has not yet spent
here. Nothing in `HOUSE_RULES.md` blocks a fix outright; this is a judgment
call about whose story a specific sentence is telling inside a scene built
to underplay exactly that story, and I am not confident enough either way
to override two earlier passes that reached the same "leave it" conclusion
on the same reasoning. Reporting it again anyway per the brief: the author
asked to see the sentences the rules are protecting, not to have this pass
quietly agree and go silent.

**Active:** "She spreads Chloe's hand open in both of hers: a burn across
the base of the thumb from March, a smaller one in the web between her
fingers."

**Passive:** "In her grandmother's hands it is spread open, a burn across
the base of the thumb from March and a smaller one in the web between her
fingers." (This puts "it," Chloe's hand, in the subject slot as the
receiver, per rule 3, while keeping the grandmother on the page as the named
circumstance rather than dropping her; the earlier structure-file pass
judged no workable passive existed here, but I think this is one, since it
does not invent an agent, it just relocates the one already present from
subject to a "her grandmother's hands" phrase.)

**Third option, closest to distance-preserving:** "Her hand goes still while
her grandmother spreads it open: a burn across the base of the thumb from
March, a smaller one in the web between her fingers." (Carried over from
`PROSE_PRINCIPLES_STRUCTURE.md`; puts Chloe's hand first without fully
losing the grandmother's action, and is the version I would pick if the
author decides the current line loses too much.)
