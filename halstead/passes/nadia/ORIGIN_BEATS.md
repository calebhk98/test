# Nadia Origin Beats (18-22) — Loose Notes, Not Locked

Hedged on purpose. These are directions, not commitments.

---

## The Trigger

Still in school, not college-bound, already planning the company before graduation. Starts as an ordinary 18-year-old thing: applying for jobs. Hits the standard wall, ghost listings, keyword-filtered auto-rejection, the experience-for-experience loop. Complains to friends the way anyone would.

The difference is what she does with the complaint. She may run it the way she runs an open math problem, lay the whole shape out at once rather than fixing one visible symptom. Possible angles she sees together, not necessarily in this order or all of them:

- The surface problem: bad matching, ghost postings, applicant-tracking software rejecting people before a human looks.
- Why it's like this now and wasn't always: mass software-mediated hiring may be a risk-management choice (a documented process is harder to sue over than a human judgment call) more than an actual attempt at good matching.
- The downstream cost: job-hunting itself as unpaid, unbounded labor, hitting hardest whoever can least afford months of it.
- Why nobody's fixed it: platforms that profit from engagement (more searching, more premium subscriptions) may be structurally uninterested in getting you hired fast. Possible line, something like "why would a company that makes money off you being unemployed want to get you a job quickly."

Don't need all four hit hard. Even two or three in the same breath should read as "sees it all at once" rather than a trail she follows one clue at a time.

## What Makes It Not Just Another LinkedIn

A few candidate differentiators, pick what fits:

- No resume. Profile built from demonstrated work and short skill tests instead.
- Applies for the user automatically, across boards, instead of making them retype their life into forty portals.
- Possibly built to get users off the platform fast, the opposite incentive of a normal job board. This one's a nice quiet echo of the anti-engagement dating app she builds later, worth keeping if you want that thread seeded early.

## Where It Lives Pre-Graduation

Probably her Block Two elective, the unrecorded, self-chosen slot (same slot that gives other students things like the forge). Nobody tracks it, nobody would've noticed if she'd dropped it. Programming/networks as her standing pick fits without needing new invented structure.

No need to explain why she can build it well; everyone at that school can code at that level (9k-line no-library assignments already established). Not a plot point, just a given.

## Hiring, First Employees

Confirmed direction: no classmates, no friends, no shortcut through her existing circle. She interviews strangers herself, running her own test. Worth keeping this test loosely consistent with the two-question, edge-of-competence interview style she's already running at 22 (per the Nadia chapter), maybe an earlier, rougher draft of the same instinct rather than an unrelated method.

Open question, still yours: what specifically fails a candidate on her test. Possibly the same "stops at the edge of tested competence" tell as her adult version, possibly something rougher/less refined since this is her first attempt at designing it.

## The Sabotage Beat

Landed on: fake employer accounts. Someone posts convincing-looking listings through her own platform, then asks matched users for SSN/bank info before any real offer, standard advance-fee/identity-theft pattern, aimed at the platform category generally, not at her specifically (she isn't known yet, so nothing targeted makes sense pre-fame).

The sloppiness that annoys her, possible tells:
- Boilerplate job descriptions copy-pasted across multiple fake postings with only the company name swapped.
- No real hiring history behind the "employer" accounts.
- Account creation timing/posting cadence that doesn't resemble real employer behavior at all.

Tone target: not scary, not a real threat to her, more insulting. A functional, real-world-effective scam method (this one does work on most platforms) aimed with zero effort at a target where it was never going to survive first contact. She catches it fast and is more annoyed at the laziness than alarmed at the attempt.

Optional layering, not required: the person behind it could be a rejected candidate from her own hiring process rather than a purely external actor, gives it a personal edge without needing corporate-scale espionage. Can also just be anonymous/automated-ish if you'd rather keep it impersonal.

## Loose Shape, 18 to 22

- 17-18: builds it quietly as her own elective project, nobody watching.
- 18, around graduation: launches small, real but modest user base.
- Sabotage beat happens somewhere once there's enough real activity to be worth targeting as a category, but well before anyone big has noticed her specifically.
- Early 20s: real, growing operation. First hires trickle in through her blind process.
- By 22: lands at (or just before) the Chapter 27 material you've already written, small team, still personally on the floor, still doing everything herself.

Wider ecosystem (housing, food, medicine, etc.) stays offscreen or barely hinted at in this book. That build-out belongs to the other book; this one only needs the seed to check out in hindsight.
