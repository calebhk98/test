# Nadia, second pass: the author's notes on the first attempt

The first pass replaced the diagnostic reader with a hiring platform. The
author and several outside reviewers then read it. Everything below is his,
either in his words or directly from a reviewer he has endorsed.

`ORIGIN_BEATS.md` and `COMPANY_AND_CEO.md` are still directions, not canon.
Where they conflict with the manuscript or with this file, this file wins.

---

## 1. The business does not scale, and she has to find that out the hard way

> *"If she only places people with employers on her own site, she's a boutique
> technical staffing agency, not a scalable platform."*

The real product has to be a **network of networks**: it scrapes other job
boards *and* individual company career pages, and it matches from both sides,
because neither side will join a platform the other side is absent from. She
solves the chicken-and-egg by taking both.

**But she does not start there.** The author is explicit:

> *"She can try it your way 1st, but 10% of software is writing it 90% is
> getting users. Let her fail. Let her change to something else."*

So the first version is the one she has now, it does not work, and the reason
it does not work is that nobody comes. The pivot to scraping is what she does
about that.

## 2. Test generation is automated, and that is the point

She would never hand-write a test for every skill. She builds something that
generates them, so that she can cover every skill without doing any of it
manually.

Narratively this is the device that explains a reviewer's objection:

> *"Nadia complains in Chapter 27 that almost everyone fails her tests, so she
> dumbs them down to Halstead 12-year-old level, and the pass rate is still
> under 33%."*

If she is not writing the tests herself, she has no direct sense of how hard
they are, which is why she can be this wrong about it for this long.

## 3. Scale it down hard

> *"Don't give her 40 full-time employees, a warehouse, and a dedicated
> cleaning contract within 11 months on bootstrapped revenue. Make it 4-6
> people, high-margin, hyper-local, and messy. That makes her look more
> competent, not less."*

Four to six people. No warehouse, no cleaning contract, no forty coffee orders.

## 4. There is no friction anywhere, and she never fails

> *"She complains about the market, and then poof she has a site with 900
> users... She needs to fail at this first. She needs to spend six months
> coding an ugly beta that nobody uses. She needs to get rejected by 50
> employers before she gets her first sign-up."*

Give her the ugly beta. Give her the months of nobody. Give her the employers
who say no.

## 5. Nobody in this book is mean, and that is not realistic

> *"Let's have conflict, not everyone is nice. The book as is, is way too nice.
> No one is mean. That is unrealistic."*

The worked example is the scam confrontation, where the scammer currently
concedes gracefully and apologises:

> *"Scammers above a tire shop do not politely concede moral and technical
> superiority to an 18-year-old girl walking into their room unannounced. Give
> them confusion, hostility, or dismissiveness, and let Nadia use her
> Halstead-level leverage/threats to shut them down."*

## 6. She should not think she can take four men

She can. She must not think so.

> *"She should think she is an 18 yo female, against 4 people like Sam. She can
> easily fight them, and win. We can solve that by thinking that it's a legal
> issue, no one will really try anything against her. She is naive, she is not
> wise."*

So what carries her into that room is a belief that nobody would actually do
anything, not a belief that she could handle it if they did.

## 7. She has no emotions, and she is supposed to be a teenage girl

This is the largest note and it comes from four reviewers independently.

> *"She never once feels bad. Two hundred and six applications and fourteen
> answers is an experience that produces shame in a real person, and Nadia's
> only registered emotion across the whole sequence is offense at
> inefficiency."*

> *"We do not get her fear. We do not get a night where she's lying awake
> wondering if she's made a mistake... A normal teenager starting a company
> would be scared."*

> *"She doesn't seem to have emotion. She appears to be a robot... She never
> raises her voice, never loses her composure, never has a moment of genuine,
> unguarded laughter or tears in the text. Where is the moment she is
> irrationally angry or sad?"*

> *"Count her lines and an overwhelming share are refusals or category
> corrections... She almost never expresses appetite, enthusiasm, or
> preference."*

The book is third limited and mostly not from her point of view, so interiority
is largely unavailable. The author's answer is that it therefore has to arrive
in **what she says and does**: a line to her father while she is starting it, a
line in the chat about the income being a knife edge on a tightrope, somewhere
she laughs at something stupid, somewhere she wants something out loud.

She needs at least one of each of these across her material: a joke, an
admission of confusion, asking for help, wanting something for no strategic
reason, getting something socially wrong and having to recover, and being
plainly upset.

## 8. She never negotiates, in a book that says she is good at it

> *"her only named number in the entire book is 81% on the negotiation board,
> and she never negotiates on the page. Not once."*

Two places to put it: **the bank**, and **an argument with her father about
being allowed to do this at all**.

## 9. Her hires are too weak for the company to function

> *"who is doing the work that requires going past the edge? Because a startup
> requires everyone to go past the edge constantly. If her forty people are all
> waiting for instructions at the boundary, the company should be stagnant."*

Make the people she hires genuinely good. **She still reads them as
disappointing**, because her comparison class did ninety thousand lines with no
libraries in a month and the whole school did it. Both things are true at once
and neither is commented on.

## 10. She interviews too many people herself

Downgrade the count. Show her **trying to rely on her own software** and being
the bottleneck anyway. Keep some hand interviews, because other scenes use them
to show what she is like.

## 11. The psychological jump is missing

> *"She goes from frustration to construction with no hesitation, no doubt, no
> moment of standing in her parents' kitchen thinking 'wait, is this a thing a
> person just does?'"*

The author wants pre-graduation scenes: her applying for jobs, complaining with
friends, being a normal teenager who is not going to bother with college.

> *"Why do normal teens not go to college? Why would a normal teen act like
> her? That is why she tries to get a job. It's only after failing, the system
> is broken, we need a scene where she decides if she can't get a job, she'll
> make her own job getting other people jobs."*

**And: what jobs was she applying for? What were her goals?** The manuscript
never says.

## 12. She should not be working for her parents while doing all this

She was job hunting and running a company and behind her parents' counter. Pick
one; the counter is the one to drop, or to reduce to something occasional.

## 13. Chapter 16 contradicts everything else about her

Chapter 16 has her say things flatly and land as an accusation. The rest of the
book has her as the best reader of people in the cast:

> *"She calls Chloe's tell at the napkin, twice. She talks about the weather
> for forty seconds and walks off with a badge. She lets the scam victim talk
> for twenty minutes. She gives the customer his change instead of the answer.
> She asks Priya privately rather than in the group. She reads Theo's
> supervisor generously when nobody else does."*

Change the flatness line to something else.

## 14. Chloe and Nadia are supposed to have friction, and the book never pays it

The relationship is described as difficult and then written as frictionless.
The cost is never shown. Scenes are needed where they are not easy with each
other.

## 15. Her relationships are one-dimensional

> *"We never see her get genuinely annoyed at Sam's antics or genuinely moved
> by Ruth's vulnerability. She observes them like a scientist, not a friend."*

## 16. What to keep

The author endorses this reading as **correct characterisation**, not a defect,
provided the rest of the personality exists:

> *"She's frustrated by people who can't finish thoughts. She's insulted by
> laziness and sloppiness. She reads people's tells and patterns. She lets
> people be who they are but doesn't pretend they're more. She's not cruel, but
> she's not gentle."*

His note: she is a genius, and tolerating rather than adoring people is a fine
thing for her to be, **as long as she has a full personality and emotions**.

## Scope

Nadia is a side character with roughly a chapter and a half of her own. Nothing
here needs the hosting stack, the funding, or the org chart. It needs her to
fail before she succeeds, and to be eighteen while it happens.
