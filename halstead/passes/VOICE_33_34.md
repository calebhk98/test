## Changed

### chapters/33_the_other_one.md, line 5

**Before:** "Eli's answer comes back within the hour, three lines about compartmentalization that don't touch the actual question, and the no holds through everything that follows, unargued after that, for days."

**After:** "Eli answers within the hour, three lines about compartmentalization that don't touch the actual question, and no one argues the no again for days."

**Why:** Two fake-agency subjects in one sentence: "Eli's answer comes back" (the answer moving under its own power, when Eli is right there doing it) and "the no holds," an abstraction acting on its own. Eli is already the actor named in the possessive; the fix promotes him to the subject and gives "no one argues" a real, if collective, doer for the second clause instead of the refusal enduring by itself.

### chapters/33_the_other_one.md, line 7

**Before:** "What arrives is a document rather than a chat message, twelve pages, written at two in the morning at the kitchen table in the apartment three states from where he grew up."

**After:** "Eli sends a document rather than a chat message, twelve pages, written at two in the morning at the kitchen table in the apartment three states from where he grew up."

**Why:** "What arrives is a document" is the book's fault by the book: an inanimate subject doing something a person did. That the document was Eli's is confirmed later in the same chapter ("Eli's reading from the kitchen table he wrote the document on less than a week earlier"), so naming him here invents nothing. The rest of the sentence, including the "he" that follows, was already keyed to Eli.

### chapters/33_the_other_one.md, line 63

**Before:** "Kavi stays bent over the board he's rewiring in the lab when the thread reaches him, and the answer comes out in one line that runs until the mechanism's actually finished."

**After:** "Kavi stays bent over the board he's rewiring in the lab when the thread reaches him, and he answers in one line that runs until the mechanism's actually finished."

**Why:** "The answer comes out" is the same fault as the two above, on a smaller scale: an answer producing itself. Kavi is the sentence's subject two words earlier; there is no ambiguity to preserve by keeping the answer as agent.

### chapters/34_the_files.md, line 93

**Before:** "Sam has been between them at every table since they were children and the file has no way of knowing it, so it puts the two of them side by side."

**After:** "Sam has been between them at every table since they were children and the file has no way of knowing it, so the two of them appear side by side in it."

**Why:** "It puts the two of them side by side" is the clearest instance of fake agency in either chapter, close to the book's own textbook example ("the name of it goes across the table"): the file, an inanimate object, performing a placement that nobody is actually doing. Nobody chose to put Chloe and Nadia next to each other; the file's sorting produced that result as a side effect, which is exactly what "appear... in it" says without inventing a sorter.

## Called out, not changed

### chapters/33_the_other_one.md, line 49

**Current:** "The rest of them read that thread over the following days, each of them from wherever the year has put them."

**Why the voice is wrong:** "the year has put them" is an institution-shaped abstraction ("the year") cast as an active agent physically placing five people in five cities, the same shape as the book's named fault, and the house rule for institutional doers ("the school, the office, the year") calls for passive, not this.

**Why I did not change it:** I could not find a confident replacement that does not either reach for a doer the text never supplies (nothing says who or what actually dispersed them — a program's schedule, their own choices, the school year ending) or flatten the line's reach into something flatter and worse. It also reads to me as consistent with how the book elsewhere lets time and institution act as a backdrop force (this is the same document that treats "the school" and "the year" as faceless actors throughout the wider manuscript), so I was not confident this is a miscalibration rather than the intended register.

**Replacement 1:** "The rest of them read that thread over the following days, each of them wherever that year's schedule happened to have left them."

**Replacement 2:** "The rest of them read that thread over the following days, each of them scattered wherever the year had taken them by then."

### chapters/34_the_files.md, line 11

**Current:** "Someone has annotated the margin of it more than once, always the same single word: *continuity*."

**Why the voice is wrong:** "someone" is a placeholder subject for a doer the file itself does not know or does not say; per the rule that passive is right when the doer is unknown or irrelevant, this reads more naturally with the margin as the sentence's real subject.

**Why I did not change it:** In the same paragraph, "different initials sign off at the bottom of each version" and "whole careers appear to have opened and closed against the one unmoving sentence" use the identical device — an abstraction standing in for a long, faceless run of anonymous analysts — and all three appear to be one deliberate choice describing institutional facelessness across thirteen years, not three separate slips. Fixing this one alone would break a pattern that looks intentional without also touching the other two, which I was told not to do without being sure, and I am not sure enough to touch any of the three.

**Replacement 1:** "The margin has been annotated more than once, always with the same single word: *continuity*."

**Replacement 2:** "The same single word turns up in the margin more than once: *continuity*."
