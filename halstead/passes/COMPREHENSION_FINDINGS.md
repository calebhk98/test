# Comprehension review: what 35 blind readers found

One agent per chapter, each reading only its own chapter, writing a summary and a
confusion list before being allowed to see the synopsis. Individual reports are in
`passes/comprehension/NN.md`. This is the consolidation, ranked, with the findings
I checked and rejected at the end.

**The method validated itself on the first chapter it was built to test.** Chapter
19's reader, going in cold, made the same error a human reader made: it read Chloe
as passing the bar exam by four points. She fails by four.

## Tier 1: a reader assembles the wrong event and is never corrected

These are the real defects. In each, the text supports a confident wrong reading.

**1. Chapter 19, the bar result.** Three ambiguities stack:

- eleven lines earlier, "I gave it four" means four *pages*
- the board shows "a column showing the distance from the pass line", which names
  a distance and not a side
- "Four points," she says, and then Kavi says "I got it by nine", which reads as
  two passing margins

Nothing states that she failed. The correction arrives months later and sideways,
in October, as "She passes by twenty-two", which the reader has to run backwards.

**2. Chapter 8 never answers its own question.** The decision about Halstead
happens off the page in January and reaches Chloe from the stairs. A reader can
finish believing she got in, or believing she gave up asking. Neither is confirmed
until the next chapter.

**3. Chapter 34, the seven men.** The chapter frames the parking-lot fight through
a federal incident report, which makes the seven men read as law enforcement or
security. They are robbers. The chapter never says so.

**4. Chapter 26, the captain's report.** The reader cannot tell whether the report
flags Sam as a security risk or recommends him for selection. "Turns Sam's own
words into something else" leans ominous; nothing in the chapter resolves it.

**5. Chapter 16, Marek's grade.** The chapter runs "refuses to hand work in" and
"fails the course" together without ever saying which is recorded, in a chapter
whose point is that he has demonstrably mastered the material.

**6. Chapter 18, the shooting plateau.** Chloe spends four evenings analysing it
and finds a pattern, Voss confirms it is "a real thing" and then dismisses her
without saying whether she has solved it. A reader will take the analysis as the
solution.

## Tier 2: contradictions between chapters

**7. Chloe has two birthdays.** Found independently by the readers of 22, 23 and
30, and verified.

| | says |
| :-- | :-- |
| chapter 4, the July camp | she answers "Six" |
| chapter 9, February | "She's seven years old" |
| `characters/CHLOE.md` | born August 2005 |
| chapter 23 line 179 | "She turns eighteen in June" |
| chapter 30 line 98 | "She's twenty-one in June" |

Six in July and seven by February puts the birthday between August and February.
Only two sentences carry June, so the repair is small, but it is load-bearing: the
whole Foreign Service wait, sit at twenty and appointed at twenty-one, is built on
it, and it is why she takes the state school for two years.

**8. Chapter 20's date, again, from three directions.** Chapter 16 puts Ruth
building the box in April at thirteen; chapter 20 places the sneak-out relative to
that; chapter 34 dates the same night "six years back". The synopsis already
carries "*Date needs pinning*" against that row. Chapter 20's own reader flagged
that the chapter gives no internal anchor at all.

**9. Chapter 28's date line says May 2024** and `chronology/chapter_dates.json`
agrees with it. Both disagree with the surrounding book, which puts Nadia's
chapter around 2019.

**10. Chapter 21, Odile.** Acceptances "arrive in March", and then Odile "keeps it
to herself until Léo asks her directly at Christmas", nine months earlier.

**11. Chapter 30's arithmetic.** A seven-month clearance investigation beginning
in early July lands in February. The chapter says the clearance comes through in
the autumn.

**12. The chapter 6 to 7 boundary.** Chapter 6 ends with Chloe in the car an hour
into the drive home with her eyes shut. Chapter 7 opens at the camp loop with the
trunks open and the flagpole goodbye, which happens before the drive. Both chapters
are dated August 2012, so the date lines do not catch it.

## Tier 3: chapters that cannot stand alone

Not defects in the same sense, but worth knowing which chapters collapse without
their neighbours.

- **Chapter 4** leans on chapter 1 for the panic response and the wood chips and
  the cage; without it those passages are unreadable rather than mysterious.
- **Chapter 33**, at 226 words, opens on a fully formed plan and explains none of
  it. Its reader could not tell that a serious crime was being planned.
- **Chapter 32** never establishes what "the school" is before the government
  surveillance of it becomes the point of the scene.
- **Chapter 11** never says Chloe is returning for a second year; it reads as a
  first arrival.
- **Chapter 8** never defines Halstead, and it is the chapter where the family
  decides about it.

## The recurring patterns

- **Undefined institutional vocabulary.** Watch, Kit, marked out, the count, the
  negotiation board, ten-on-one, the box, on 40%. Each is used as though already
  known. Several readers stopped at the same words.
- **A number whose unit is not given.** "Four points", "I got it by nine", "It's
  four", "about forty". This is the mechanism behind the worst defect in the book
  and it recurs in at least four chapters.
- **The result withheld one chapter too long.** Chapters 8, 18 and 26 all end
  without resolving the thing they are about.

## Findings I checked and rejected

Recorded so nobody acts on them.

- **Chapter 13's reader reports the fourteen-thousand-word area bombing essay as
  missing from the manuscript.** It is in chapter 14, at lines 117 and 165. The
  synopsis's chapter column is stale, and also maps the bar exam to chapter 15
  when it is in 19. Do not write the scene into 13.
- **Chapter 31's reader reports an impossible sequence**, a February plus ten
  months professor scene dated after an April silence it supposedly causes. The
  chapter spans October 2023 to November 2025, so February 2024 plus ten months is
  December 2024 and the April is April 2025. The sequence works. What is real is
  that the chapter gives the reader no way to tell which February and which April,
  and its own reader could not.
