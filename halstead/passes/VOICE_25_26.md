# Voice pass: chapters 25-26

Scope: `chapters/25_forty_targets.md`, `chapters/26_the_exercise.md`. Dialogue
and chat lines untouched throughout. Word counts after the pass: 3,377 (25)
and 3,610 (26), both unchanged from before the pass and inside the 2,000-5,000
band. Sentence-initial "He/She + verb" count in these two chapters checked
before and after: 7 before, 7 after — no new ones introduced.

## Changed

### chapters/25_forty_targets.md, line 7

**Before:** "...spends twenty minutes standing in a spot he is later told to leave."
**After:** "...spends twenty minutes standing in a spot the drill sergeant later tells him to leave."
**Why:** Person (Sam) in the subject of a passive. The sentence already names
"a drill sergeant" two clauses earlier, so that is the doer, not invented.

### chapters/25_forty_targets.md, line 21

**Before:** "After that it takes Sam about ninety seconds, which puts him in the back half of the platoon, roughly where he remains."
**After:** "After that it takes Sam about ninety seconds, and he ends up in the back half of the platoon, roughly where he remains."
**Why:** Fake agency — "which puts him" gives an inanimate subject (the time
figure) an active placement verb, nobody doing anything. Recast so Sam is the
one who ends up somewhere, not something else's object.

### chapters/25_forty_targets.md, line 31

**Before:** "...but a couple of them have been stopped cold by the question he asks after."
**After:** "...but the question he asks after stops a couple of them cold."
**Why:** Person (the listeners) in the subject of a passive. The question
itself is a fine active subject for "stops," and it keeps the "cold" landing
at the end of the sentence the way the original did.

### chapters/25_forty_targets.md, line 113

**Before:** "...which means the men around him are being measured on something he has yet to be shown, are almost certainly holding a good deal back for it, and will begin the real course once the sorting is finished."
**After:** "...which means the men around him are being measured on something nobody has shown him yet, are almost certainly holding a good deal back for it, and will begin the real course once the sorting is finished."
**Why:** "He has yet to be shown" puts Sam, the protagonist, in the subject of
a passive — the highest-priority fault in the brief. Left the first passive
("the men... are being measured") alone: that one's doer is the Army as a
faceless institution measuring an unnamed group, which is the correct use of
passive under rule 4, and it is not the sentence's only clause needing a fix.

### chapters/25_forty_targets.md, line 199

**Before:** "...and says the question needs answering before he is placed anywhere at all."
**After:** "...and says the question needs answering before the Army places him anywhere at all."
**Why:** Person (Sam) in the subject of a passive. The doer here is genuinely
faceless — nobody specific decides Sam's posting — so the fix names the
institution rather than inventing an officer, which keeps rather than
undercuts the "Army does things to privates" pattern this chapter is built on.

### chapters/26_the_exercise.md, line 7

**Before:** "Somebody ahead of him gets handed a second flashlight when the first refuses to come on."
**After:** "Somebody hands the man ahead of him a second flashlight when the first refuses to come on."
**Why:** Get-passive with a person as subject. Kept the doer vague ("somebody"),
since the line is one item in a list of background staging-line noise and
naming a specific quartermaster or armorer here would invent a fact.

### chapters/26_the_exercise.md, line 27

**Before:** "...listening before he moves the final fifty feet, the way he has been taught since he was small enough that the drill still felt like a game."
**After:** "...listening before he moves the final fifty feet, the way he learned it since he was small enough that the drill still felt like a game."
**Why:** Person (Sam) in the subject of a passive, and no teacher is named or
knowable here (family background, not a chapter character). Turning Sam into
the one who learned it, rather than someone who was taught it, removes the
passive without inventing an instructor.

### chapters/26_the_exercise.md, line 35

**Before:** "Crossing the wire a hundred meters down from where they were positioned, he picks his bearing up again and runs the whole business through once on the walk."
**After:** "Crossing the wire a hundred meters down from where they had taken up position, he picks his bearing up again and runs the whole business through once on the walk."
**Why:** "They" (the two opposing-force soldiers) in the subject of a passive.
They can reasonably be read as having set up their own position, so the fix
makes them the actors rather than inventing a squad leader who placed them.

### chapters/26_the_exercise.md, line 49

**Before:** "Sam is put out on the road with his weapon cleared and his harness off and instructed to stand there, so he stands there."
**After:** "Ives puts Sam out on the road with his weapon cleared and his harness off and instructs him to stand there, so he stands there."
**Why:** Person (Sam) in the subject of a passive, and the doer is not only
knowable but already named in the same paragraph — Staff Sergeant Ives, who
"arrives with a controller gun" one sentence earlier. This is exactly the
"hides a named person" case the brief calls out, not the faceless-institution
case, so the fix puts Ives in as the actor.

## Called out, not changed

No entries. Everything flagged as a genuine voice fault in these two chapters
had either a nameable doer or a defensible vague one (an institution, or
"somebody" for incidental background action), so nothing needed to be left for
the author to decide.

## Left alone, and why

A number of constructions that look like candidates on a first pass were left
alone as deliberate or as correct under the book's own rules:

- **The captain's paperwork passage (25, lines 195-207)** and its mirror at
  the end of 26 ("The safety incident goes onto a form... The major's own
  sheet travels separately. It goes upward anyway.") — "documents leave the
  company," "the second goes to," "it travels up," "it goes upward anyway."
  These give inanimate paperwork active motion verbs with no one pushing them,
  which looks like the book's fake-agency fault. But this is the institution
  acting on Sam via its own bureaucratic momentum, deliberately with nobody's
  hand shown — the brief's own words for this pair of chapters: "the Army
  does things to privates and does not care which sergeant signed." Both
  passages end on that note on purpose, and flattening either one into "the
  captain sends X" would invent a mechanism the text is pointedly not giving
  us and would blunt the parallel between the two chapters' endings.
- **"The men around him are being measured on something..." (25, line 113)**
  — passive, but the subject is an unnamed collective and the doer is the
  Army's screening process, which is rule 4's textbook case (institution with
  no face). Only the "he has yet to be shown" half of that sentence, which
  put Sam himself in a passive, needed fixing.
- **A harness in a steady tone stops the soldier wearing it... (26, line 13)**
  and the "is prohibited / is required" rule-card language around it — these
  describe a system on a signal, the exercise's own exempted category, not a
  person doing something to another person.
- **The knuckles across his fingers are split (26, line 55)** — a physical
  description of an existing injury, not a character in the subject of a
  passive about an action; recasting it active ("something split his
  knuckles") would be worse prose for no gain under the rules.
- **The culvert taught him to take it that way (26, line 73)** and **word
  travels round the company (25, line 73)** — both give an inanimate or
  abstract subject an active verb, but they are ordinary English idiom for
  experience-as-teacher and news-spreading, not the mechanical "the sheet
  goes up on the wall" fault the brief is hunting.

## Report

Sentences changed: 9, all in the same direction — a person removed from the
subject of a passive, or a fake-agency inanimate subject replaced with the
actual actor. No active-should-be-passive fixes were needed; nothing in
either chapter had swerved a topic sentence away from its subject or buried a
name that should have led. Zero write-ups: every flagged sentence had a
confident fix.

Strongest call-out (a decision, not a write-up, since I judged it correct
rather than uncertain): the paperwork-in-motion language closing both
chapters — "documents leave the company," "it travels up," "it goes upward
anyway" — reads exactly like the book's fake-agency fault on a first pass,
and I nearly rewrote all three. Leaving it stands or falls on reading it as
the deliberate, faceless half of "the Army does things to privates," which
the brief itself flags as the right kind of institutional action for this
pair of chapters. If the author disagrees, the fix is a person at a desk
routing paperwork in both places, but I think that person is precisely what
these two closing beats are about not having.
