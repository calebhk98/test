# Chapter 34: The Files
Dates (from the chapter header and text): June 2026. The files' case number was opened in 2013 (thirteen years of updates). The incident report concerns a night four years before the chapter. Chloe's lease-signing week (a Tuesday) is dated in her file, about five months before. Theo has to be back in the federal building by eight the next morning.
Chloe's age: not stated in this chapter.   POV: Roving omniscient across the seven readers (Chloe, Ruth, Sam, Nadia, Eli, Kavi, Theo), with the most sustained attention on Chloe, Ruth and Kavi. Chat scenes are plain transcript with short narration between lines. No clear single-head POV.
Setting(s): Seven separate places, each reader alone: Chloe at her desk in her apartment; Ruth on the floor of a Cambridge lab; Sam outdoors on a phone while on duty with a sergeant and others (at one point at port arms); Nadia at the shop after closing, standing at the counter; Eli at his kitchen table; Kavi in a lab down the corridor from where he had lessons as a boy; Theo standing, then outside his own apartment a few blocks from the federal building. The group chat links them.

## What happens
Seven of them open the file at different hours, each alone. The narration lists where each is. Theo stays on his feet throughout and has twenty minutes outside still ahead of him.

The file covers all ninety-one of them. Each page has the same header, with a case number opened in 2013 and a classification banner. Each file has address, employer, family, parents' workplaces and banks. Sam's is the thickest and newest, an Army file on top of the Halstead material, with an officer's assessment that treats his honesty as the interesting part. Theo's has a note that makes him close the laptop carefully and go outside. He posts in the group thread "mine has a name in it I already know." Sam asks whose name within a minute. Theo does not tell him, and nobody else learns it. Nadia's file crosses her company's filings with a competing job board's background check, and the analyst spelled her name two ways. Eli's notes he found five vulnerabilities in his own employer's systems in one afternoon, unprompted, flagged as worth continued attention. Kavi's is the thinnest, a paragraph a year, annotated in the margin more than once with the word "continuity." Chloe's has her translation work, exam scores and a note that she accepted employment requiring a background investigation.

Every file starts with a name, a date, a photograph taken outside a building, and a line that family contact went no further than one letter. A standing line says the graduates are believed to be in contact with one another. It has been retyped yearly, with the word "students" changed once after the first class left, and signed off by different initials. The file lists the method as unknown, says collection against the channel returns empty every time, and still recommends trying. Kavi reads that part twice, his pen going still.

Then an incident report from four years back. Four students left campus at night without permission, walked into town, sat in a Waffle House for two hours, and were pushing each other in a shopping trolley in the car park. Seven men came to rob them, three with firearms. The men disagree on the order of events but agree it was fast, that one student did all of it, and that the other three were not where they had been. Two independently say they were told it had been sloppy and needed more practice. The time, twenty-two seconds from first word to last man down, was taken by a watcher with binoculars. All the men have a blank on what the students looked like. Footage from the Waffle House, neighboring units, a traffic camera and a municipal camera is unrecoverable wherever the students appear and intact elsewhere. No known method. The unit assigned to observe the school lost its own recordings of that night too. The report is a crooked scan.

In the chat, Chloe says "thats us," Ruth says she knows, and Sam jokes about Ruth breaking the cart. Ruth says she built the "box" in about a weekend to avoid detention, that the file says no known method, and that it also took cameras she did not know existed. Kavi, at the lab bench, pushes her to read it back. Ruth says she was thirteen, it was a weekend, it was a fun problem and she was not trying. Nadia notes the unit still does not know. Ruth says she has said "we're ahead" for a year without having accepted it. She asks Eli how good they are. He says he does not know and has never been at the top of a room. Chloe says they are about to find out. Eli says that is not an answer, and Chloe says it was not meant to be. Theo finishes his twenty minutes and goes back in, his file closed.
