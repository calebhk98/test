# Chapter 02: March 7th
Dates (from the chapter header and text): March 2012 to June 2012 (header). Appointment is March 7 (a Wednesday; mom says ice cream is "unheard of on a Wednesday"). Next scenes: March 8, April, June, last day of school, a week later the report card.
Chloe's age: 6 (Ben asks nothing about age; consistent with ch01 and ch04)   POV: close third person, present tense, Chloe's head. She overhears the parent conference through a door.
Setting(s): psychologist's waiting room (fish tank with no fish, Spanish/English handwashing poster, NO CELL PHONES sign); Dr. Prentice's testing room (small table, blinds over a parking structure); the family car; Mrs. Aldana's classroom and playground; Chloe's home refrigerator.

## What happens
Chloe's dad is at work; he could not move his meetings. Her mom fills out a multi-page form in the waiting room. Chloe asks what the boxes are about; mom admits "I only get to see the outside of you, so some of these are a guess."

Dr. Prentice (Ben) tests her at a small table with a silver stopwatch. He says it is not a test with a grade. The tasks: block patterns copied from cards (red and white blocks, 4 then 9 blocks; she is timed and finishes the hard design after he says stop, then checks it); "what do these have in common" (boat/car; hour/year, where she says people chose those units); digit span (she holds eight forward, six backward, loses the middle on the next); picture pairs (boot and glove: "the covers for the ends of you"); a coding sheet where her hand cramps and she stops to ask whether neatness counts (Ben: "Neat enough that I can tell which mark you meant"; he turns the page over, she feels hot-faced); vocabulary (stubborn, nuisance, then words that stop her); letters and numbers mixed (she sorts them in her head); matrix puzzles; "why do people..." questions where he asks for another reason and two cancellation sheets. She tells Ben she looks out the window when she finishes early, and the empty time is "half of it, but every day." She nearly asks whether she did well, then does not. Ben says some people finish the coding page and finishing is not what tells him how she did.

Chloe waits outside and overhears Ben tell her mom that the school's picture is wrong: she finishes in minutes and then has an empty hour; the problem is not her handwriting. The report will give one composite number that averages very high reasoning/talking with ordinary writing speed, so it will read "bright kid." On a couple of parts she got every item. A bad number gets a school to act; hers will read as good. He advises taking the report to the school, since "the empty hour is theirs to fill," but some schools put it in the folder. The gifted program starts third grade, "two years between now and the only lever the school has for her." Her mom says okay.

In the car mom says "you're all good." Chloe says no to hearing what Ben said. She asks what a percentile is (heard through the door). Her mom explains and says "He didn't give me a number." They get ice cream.

After that, nothing changes at school. Mrs. Aldana asks once in April if all is well at home; Chloe says yes. Chloe learns "finish first, sit still, keep your answers short, and the room leaves you alone." By June she knows all 28 classmates' names but is quiet and eats alone. On the last day she returns her library book. The report card a week later says meeting expectations and "a pleasure to have in class"; it goes on the fridge under the strawberry magnet.
