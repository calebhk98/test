# Chapter 25: Forty Targets
Dates (from the chapter header and text): August 2023 to November 2023. Arrives first week of August 2023; fitness test second week; qualification is the second Thursday of October, "the tenth week"; captain's documents leave by that Friday; memorandum reaches a senior desk the next Thursday; last scenes are late and early November.
Chloe's age: not stated; Chloe does not appear. (By inference from ch28 and ch30, her birthday is in August, so she turns 18 around this time. The text of ch25 never says.)   POV: Sam, close third person throughout. The captain's evening (writing the documents) is narrated from outside Sam's head and he is not present; the final lines return to Sam.
Setting(s): Army basic training company (barracks bay, gravel behind the barracks, mess hall, rifle range with tower, land navigation course). The captain's folding desk. An unnamed office "well above" the company where a woman reads the memorandum.

## What happens
Sam arrives by bus at an Army training company in early August and likes it at once. Drill Sergeant Ruiz inspects the blankets and tells Sam "Again" repeatedly; Private Okoro shows him the fold trick. Sam tells Okoro he thinks Ruiz likes him; Okoro disagrees. Sam asks everyone how long the program runs (22 weeks, then infantry, then a unit) and keeps asking when "the real one" starts. Ruiz says the real one is Tuesday, and Wednesday, and every Tuesday after.

In week two Sam takes the fitness test and scores 600, the top of the scale. The grader double-checks. Bothered that the score has a ceiling, Sam has Okoro time him again on Sunday past where the card stops counting. In week nine he posts 600 on the record test.

At chow a man asks if Sam played ball. Sam says boarding school, offers his "range number" of forty percent since age fifteen, and tells of Odile, who put him down twice in a minute and cracked a rib doing it, and of losing a small thing out of his boot and needing a friend to get him back in. Some laugh, one stops believing him. Sam says the school is small and they would not have heard of it.

Live fire starts in week four; Sam puts all five rounds in the diamond on the first morning and the scorer wants a talk. Sam is doing well at everything and cannot square it with what he knows of himself. He tests three explanations and keeps the third: basic is a screen to sort men before the real course. He holds back on land navigation for three weeks to test it, nobody notices, and he stops.

September is foot marches and October land navigation (Sam walks a squad through a bearing). Sam writes block-capital letters to his grandmother and leaves out the number 600.

At qualification (forty targets) Sam hits all forty. The company commander, a captain, says it is the first 40 of 40 he has signed in two cycles. Sam says his accuracy is forty percent "of the intercepts": a machine puts a live round across your front and the target is the bullet; a girl in his year hit ninety-one. The captain says nobody shoots a bullet out of the air and there is no machine, and asks where he went to school. "Halstead, sir," a small boarding school. The captain pockets the card and tells Sam to pick up his brass.

That evening the captain writes a memorandum for record, a notice to the counterintelligence office, a request to the battalion S-2 for the education block of Sam's security form, and signs a mental health referral. A week later the memorandum is on the desk of a woman far above him, who pulls out a folder older than the captain's commission. In November Sam asks Okoro whether "the screening" is finished; Okoro laughs. Sam puts it to somebody else on a Thursday (unnamed).

## Facts established
- Sam arrives first week of August 2023 on a bus from the reception depot. Program: 22 weeks, then infantry, then a unit.
- Sam's laminated schedule "since childhood" reappears on the second day, screwed to the wall by the door.
- Fitness test: six events, 100 points each, 60 to pass each: deadlift, standing power throw, hand-release push-ups, sprint-drag-carry, plank, two miles. Sam scores 600 (the maximum). He also scores 600 on the record test in week nine.
- Sam's stated "range number": forty percent, since age fifteen. Ruth shot ninety-one "and found it dull." Kavi beat him at every board game for years. Odile "second in the year, I was first"; she put him down twice in under a minute in front of thirty people and cracked a rib; she apologized to him afterward.
- Sam says he lost "a small thing out of my own boot" one April and had to ask a friend to get him back in.
- Sam says to the room his school is "boarding school... small... you wouldn't have heard of it."
- Rifle issued in week three; live fire at 25 meters in week four, groups of five; Sam's first five rounds are inside the diamond.
- Sam's three theories of why he is doing well; the kept one: basic is a screen. He tested it by running land navigation slower for three weeks.
- Qualification: second Thursday of October, week ten. 40 pop-up silhouettes, 50 to 300 meters, 8 exposures from each of 5 positions, four ten-round magazines, 3 minutes 58 seconds. Sam hits 40 of 40.
- Captain: first 40/40 he has signed in two cycles; has read every card since March; sixteen years in, six enlisted; says "I have already called the number on your file once today" and will call again.
- Sam tells the captain: "forty percent of the intercepts"; a machine at the end of the lane puts a live round across your front; "The target is the round. You're shooting the bullet."
- Sam gives the school's name aloud: "Halstead."
- Captain's four documents: memorandum for record (fourth paragraph quotes "forty percent" and says soldier appears to have received special instruction from an unknown source); notice to supporting counterintelligence office; request to battalion S-2 for the education block of Sam's security form; mental health referral.
- The memorandum reaches a woman far above the captain by the next Thursday; she has a folder "far older than the captain's commission."
- Sam writes to his grandmother Sunday nights in block capitals (gave up cursive about age ten). She replied in late November (three lines about weather, asks if the food improved). Sam's complaint: nine minutes for a meal.
- Hot water in the bay was lost on a Tuesday as a privilege.

## Who knows what
- Sam believes he is performing normally against peers who are holding back; he does not know why others shoot so poorly. He believes there is a later "real" course.
- Sam does not know the captain has filed reports or a mental health referral.
- The captain believes either Sam is mistaken about his school or is telling a story; he does not believe the intercept machine exists.
- Okoro and the men at chow heard Sam's claims of 40 percent and boarding school; at least one disbelieves them.
- The woman above the captain apparently already has a file relating to the matter (text implies this, does not state its subject).
- Sam's grandmother has not been told the score of 600.
