# Chapter 09: February
Dates (from the chapter header and text): Header "February 2013 - April 2013". February (school scenes, the floor breakdown); middle then last week of February; "the fourteenth of March" (parents say yes); a Saturday in April (house offer); the following Friday in April (Halstead visit day, rain).
Chloe's age: 7 (her mother says "she's seven years old")   POV: Close third on Chloe, with drift: the April kitchen argument about the house, and the parents listening to her upstairs, are scenes Chloe is not in or hears only in part. The February night talk is overheard through the floor.
Setting(s): Chloe's school (classroom, lunchroom); her bedroom; the kitchen and living room; the garage; Halstead building in the rain; the car on the drive home.

## What happens
February: Chloe finishes her work early and sits behind it. She eats at the back of the lunchroom without a book. She has been to Dr. Ammons "a handful" of times; at the last, Dr. Ammons asked if anything had gotten easier and she said some of it.

One evening her mom finds her on the floor between the bed and the wall with her fists in the carpet. Chloe pleads, in pieces, to be allowed to go to Halstead, offering to do all the dishes, her dad's jobs, give back the bike, Christmas and her birthday. Her mom gets down on the floor and holds her. Chloe says "I can't do it anymore" and "This, all of this." Her dad stands in the doorway with a dishcloth, then goes back to the kitchen.

Later her parents argue at the kitchen table. Her mom (Meg) tells her dad (Dave) that Chloe offered her birthday "like it was money she'd saved", asks what kind of family sends a seven-year-old four hours away, and demands he say what he thinks. He says she already knows; he has thought yes since the car ride home in August. Chloe hears her mom say "four hours".

On March 14 both parents sit Chloe down in the living room. Dad has already sent in a form. In April the family drives out for a day where Halstead looks at her; if they say yes she could start in the spring because Halstead takes students in any month. Chloe asks several times if it is real, screams, hugs them, and says she will be good. She asks to tell Ruth, and is told she can tell her when she gets there. She brings the bike in from the garage to give it back; her parents refuse and say it was never a deal. Her dad says "That's her, that's what she's actually like"; her mom says "I know what she's like."

On a Saturday in April (Chloe at the Petersons') Dave shows Meg a Halstead letter offering a furnished house minutes from campus, paid while she is enrolled. Meg refuses: if they take the house, they would have nowhere to live the day she wants Chloe out. Dave points out they would see her every day instead of ten weeks a year. The argument stops unresolved.

The visit day is Friday, raining. Mrs. Okonkwo walks backward and gives the tour. Chloe asks about Pluto and Sam; Mrs. Okonkwo will not say who is enrolled. She corrects Ruth's claim that there is a fall evaluation day: Halstead takes people all year. Meg asks about the nurse and the night staff and gets clear answers. Chloe sits in a second-grade room, and a man with a graying beard asks what she'd do with a whole day. No test is handed to her. On the drive home she talks for hours and sleeps before the driveway. In the kitchen she sees her mother still sitting in the car with the engine and lights off.
