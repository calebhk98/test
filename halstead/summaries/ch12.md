# Chapter 12: Nine
Dates (from the chapter header and text): Header "September 2014 - July 2015". First morning of the school year (September 2014); October (fish tank); November (Mandarin, glassware); January (textiles); March (Mandarin remark by Iyad, fish dies, third shirt); May 11 (Sam clears the overhang); April (riding); summer 2015 at home (first week, July coast trip, third week).
Chloe's age: 9 (chapter title; the sheet says "Nine-year-olds get eight")   POV: Close third on Chloe throughout.
Setting(s): Halstead (office, dining hall, language and chemistry rooms, library machines, tank in front hall, climbing wall, stables, pool); Chloe's home; a coast motel.

## What happens
On the first morning Chloe finds eight lines on her sheet; last year's had seven. The office woman says nine-year-olds get eight and it goes up every year. New subjects are chemistry, statistics and biology. Kavi got none of his three requests: programming (tens), astronomy (elevens), cryptography (thirteen). Chloe discovers dance and drawing are gone from the sheet and announces it in the dining hall; Sam says he liked dance; Ruth says being bad at something and loving it is a different thing.

Mandarin with Mrs. Sun (from Harbin). Chloe reads fast and writes ten-page reports in Mandarin by March, but cannot hear tones in her own mouth. Mrs. Sun tells her to record herself. Ruth sits with her Sundays in December. By June Chloe is clear to those expecting her, but a stranger loses her by the second sentence. Statistics: Chloe argues with Kavi about an eleven-person study. Chemistry with Vasquez: she gets gray instead of white; water in the glassware is the cause; she writes "glassware" in her notebook in November. First aid on Mondays uses a clock, dimmed lights and a man shouting questions. Textiles: two shirts drag; she traces a shirt from home and finds her draft is an inch and a half off; the third fits by the end of March. Biology: Kavi decides a small striped fish is bullied; it dies of old age in March.

Bex Alcantar finishes people's sentences. In term two she and Chloe build a bridge that carries nineteen pounds; Bex tells the teacher "we" and then "I" for the joints Chloe built over a fortnight. Chloe says nothing to the teacher, tells Ruth, and stops saying "we" for anything of hers for the year. Iyad Mansour announces everyone's hours and numbers; in March he says her Mandarin line to the table in a loud voice. She now sits three tables from him. She works with him in chemistry for a term.

Sam spends the year on the overhang, falls repeatedly, and gets over it on May 11 with thirty watching, after a girl in the year below told him it depends on the left foot. Chloe tears the corner off her book.

Block two: riding, because Priya takes it. Chloe can canter by February. Priya goes to Tuesdays in April; Chloe rides twice more on Thursdays and quits without telling Priya. She tells her it is easy and boring.

Summer at home: she sleeps. Her mother teaches her cribbage on a walnut board her own father made. Her dad reads a vendor proposal (six weeks, forty thousand dollars); Chloe notices the promises cannot be checked. Her dad asks the vendor for dates and numbers; he says he would have signed it on Tuesday.
