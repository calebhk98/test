# Chapter 05: Behind
Dates (from the chapter header and text): July 2012 (header). First week (Tuesday, Wednesday, Friday, the Thursday bridge-build), "the second week," the bridge test on "the Tuesday," Owen leaves on a Tuesday and Chloe finds out on the Wednesday of the second week, then "the third week" (moon dinner). "More leave before the end of the month."
Chloe's age: 6   POV: close third person, present tense, Chloe's head. No clear drift; Owen's inner state is only seen from outside.
Setting(s): Halstead classrooms (Pluto, the writing lesson, the bridge-building table, the math lesson with Mr. Baptiste), the pool (shallow end), Owen's emptied dorm room, the dining hall, the walk back across the grass.

## What happens
In the first week the pace is set by Ruth, who finishes first. Chloe finishes quickly but stops reaching for her paperback; by Friday she leaves it in her room. In a timed writing exercise she writes six sentences that answer where a character's belief changes and how a reader knows; Ruth writes a page and a half, Kavi most of a page, Sam four sentences with a joke, Marisol three, and Owen two with one struck out. Chloe thinks that at her old school hers would have been read aloud.

On Thursday they build bridges from dowels, string, and a glue gun across an eighteen-inch gap. Chloe is grouped with Sam and Kavi. Ruth comes over and says triangles; she shows a square folding and a braced diagonal holding. Sam rejects it ("Ruth doesn't know. She just says things."). Chloe thinks a middle brace is right, says so once, and defers. The bridge is flat, wide, and neat. Chloe writes a low prediction (fourteen pounds, per ch06); Sam wanted one hundred and is told to pick a number he believes. On Tuesday a bucket is hung and weights added a pound at a time with the room counting. Chloe's bridge holds to eleven then folds sideways (Chloe wrong by three). Ruth's takes nineteen and snaps in the diagonal. Ruth says "I told you." Chloe writes that the corner went first.

In the second week Mr. Baptiste teaches dividing fractions by flipping. Chloe gets all eight problems right but cannot see why. She asks "Why do you flip it." He asks what dividing is asking; she loses the rectangle explanation. Owen asks which fraction is flipped, loses the thread and is told they will redo it tomorrow. Ruth asks about zero; Mr. Baptiste answers. The bell rings while Ruth argues. Chloe copies "you can't cut nothing into pieces" into the back of her packet and reads it to Sam. That night in her room she draws the rectangle, then three quarters divided by an eighth; she counts six eighths, realizes multiplying by eight is the same, and says "Oh" in the dark with Priya's light off.

Swimming with the instructor Gwen: Chloe can swim one length with one stop; Sam swims two straight. Ruth, who complains, swims a whole length on Thursday.

On the Wednesday of the second week Owen's room is stripped. Sam says his mom came the day before and "he wanted to be home." Chloe cannot reconcile "it's fun" with leaving. At dinner Ruth says Owen was crying in the stairwell on Thursday and she did not go in. More children leave before the month ends; Chloe reads it from open doors.

In the third week at dinner Chloe says the moon is a liar because it shows one face. Sam says it is stuck; Ruth says tidally locked; Chloe argues it is turning on purpose. Walking back she realizes she has talked freely and the sentence checked out clean.
