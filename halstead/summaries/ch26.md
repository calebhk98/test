# Chapter 26: The Exercise
Dates (from the chapter header and text): February 2024. Sam reached his unit in January. Exercise "Winter Anvil" runs four days and nights; AAR is the afternoon after; the chat follows that evening or soon after. Sam says he has been waiting "since august" to learn which Tuesday is the real one.
Chloe's age: not stated; she appears only in the group chat (one line, "thats fine"). (Inferred 18 from later chapters; the text does not say.)   POV: Sam, close third person. No drift.
Setting(s): Army training area in winter: a gravel staging lot with tents, a fence line and fallow field, a concrete culvert under a service road, a drainage cut, an after-action review room with painted-over windows. Then the group chat.

## What happens
Sam, now at his unit, is issued gear for Winter Anvil, a four day, four night field exercise using laser harnesses and blank ammunition. A sergeant reads the rules: minimum engagement distance five meters, no blanks inside it, no hand-to-hand at any range, a steady harness tone means you stand still until a controller comes. An opposing element will hunt the soldiers moving between checkpoints on foot at night.

First night: at a fence line Sam finds two men waiting. He withdraws, waits about twenty minutes, watches one shift his weight on a rhythm, and fires two rounds. Both harnesses go to steady tone. He moves on and thinks "it went fine."

Second night: in a culvert Sam is trapped between four men. A blank is fired two meters from his head; five harnesses sound at once and he cannot sort his own tone. A hand grabs his sling. Sam reacts by reflex, shoving one man into the wall (broken nose, bleeding) and clearing his way out shoulder first. Staff Sergeant Ives, the lane controller, takes his weapon status, has him stand on the road, and writes a safety incident. The medic tapes Sam's split knuckles. The engagement is voided, and Sam is put back on his bearing. He keeps replaying the fifteen seconds before the pipe.

Third night is only walking in cloud cover; Sam is cautious at tree lines and his numb taped hands slow his compass reading. Fourth night: in a drainage cut he hears a shift behind him and shoots first. The man was lying in the weed line since 2100 (nine o'clock), seven hours, and moved once. Sam says that is the part he would have gotten wrong. He turns in his gear and returns 27 of 30 blanks.

At the AAR, a major reviews each soldier. Marsh's fence line is logged as two engagements of his at 20 meters. For the culvert the major reads Ives's report: contact with two soldiers, one of whom spent yesterday on light duty with a facial injury, a photograph attached. The major tells him the correct move was to be "dead"; the tone is what settles it. Sam agrees and adds that his real error was entering with open ground to read, because he was watching the clock instead of the pipe. The major praises the patience of the man in the weed line. Sam asks if this was the real exercise or a practice with a real one after, and says he'd want more opponents or the ground handed to him. The major says it was the exercise, not a rehearsal, and that difficulty is set above both of them; he orders a contact report in Sam's hand before breakfast. He asks where Sam learned to wait twenty minutes at a fence; Sam says "School, sir." The major notes it and does not ask which school. The platoon runs the perimeter road in boots because of Sam's question; Corporal Hale says next time ask the rest of them first. The incident goes up the chain; the major's own sheet travels separately and "goes upward anyway."

In the group chat Sam says he annoyed a major and relays his question. Friends debate it, referencing the drill sergeant's "Tuesday." Whether to put the question to the major again: Ruth no, Nadia no, Eli yes.
