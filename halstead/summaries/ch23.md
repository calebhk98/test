# Chapter 23: The First One
Dates (from the chapter header and text): Header "June 2023". Graduation day in June 2023 (the first graduation Halstead has held). The chapter then goes back to a Saturday in May (Nadia), forward to July (Sam signs up), and covers mid-May to mid-June (Nadia's employer list), then Chloe's decision "found out in May."
Chloe's age: 17 at graduation; "she turns eighteen in August."   POV: Omniscient with a lean to Chloe; goes into Dave's, Nadia's, Sam's, Priya's views. Not strictly one head.
Setting(s): Halstead field, stage, grass by the parking lot; ground-floor hallway and courtyard; an Army recruiting office off the highway outside Pittsburgh; Nadia's father's shop counter; Chloe's family kitchen.

## What happens
The first graduation is rehearsed, timed and checked twice. Ninety-one students stand backstage; gowns come in three sizes, pinned and binder-clipped. The stage stairs were built by the graduating class in May. Families sit on chairs on the field; the younger students sit on the grass behind. The head of school's voice catches on "accomplishment". Sam makes Chloe laugh through the speech. Names are read at twelve seconds each (Ruth times it). Rows stand for Sam and for Ruth; Priya's parents clap for every name. Chloe crosses the stage and the head of school says her name once, correctly.

Afterward, on the grass, Chloe's mother (called Meg), her father Dave, her grandmother and Dana are there. Meg and Ruth's mother talk as they do every year; Dave talks stage bracing with a stranger who turns out to be Kavi's father, a physics professor. Her grandmother asks Sam, Kavi and Chloe "what are you doing next?" and says "Well, that's something" to each. Chloe tells her father she will drive to Georgetown herself in August. Dana says "That's good."

Chloe carries her box past the brass plates and notices she can touch the top of the courtyard door frame. Marek meets her with a paper he has been reading since March, half done, and promises to finish it by Tuesday. That evening the class gathers on the grass; Iyad arranges them by height and Theo snaps the photo; Chloe counts all of them in it.

Later outcomes follow. Sam signs up for the Army in July at an office outside Pittsburgh, reading the fifth form on pay twice, picking the Army over the Marines because the Marines cap the PT score. Ruth goes to MIT. Nadia's notebook fills to the back cover. On a Saturday in May she tells her father at the shop counter about her site (206 applications since November, 14 answers, 12 from software). She proposes working in the back room, Sundays at the counter, with an end date of the last day of March; if the site earns nothing she takes whatever job is open. They settle on three applications a week logged in the book. She finds a double billing for blades on an invoice. She asked 61 employers; 9 agreed, all customers of her father's. A few dozen register and one hire comes from it. Theo goes to the federal government as an analyst. Kavi stays. Priya takes two years in South America with no return flight, going to a woman outside Bariloche who breaks horses.

Chloe reads the Foreign Service exam page: twenty to sit, twenty-one to be sworn in, six stages. She works at the forge late, then tells her father at the kitchen table. They compare the state school and Georgetown; she says Georgetown's assessors teach there and the internships are close. The chapter ends: she is going to Georgetown, and the two years of waiting "become the start of the six stages."
