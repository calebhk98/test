# Chapter 01: Before
Dates (from the chapter header and text): September 2011 to February 2012 (header). Scenes dated by month: September (dinner), October (Peanut), November (clouds, Friday paper), December (conferences, doctor, Christmas), January (class reading aloud), February (appointment booked for March 7).
Chloe's age: 6 (she says "Six" to the librarian; her mom says "six-year-old kid" and "first grade")   POV: close third person, present tense, Chloe's head throughout. Her mom's phone calls and the parents' car talk are overheard. One line ("Her mom reports it on the phone afterward, laughing") is a report of something Chloe is not shown hearing.
Setting(s): Chloe's house (kitchen, living room, dinner table); first-grade classroom of Mrs. Aldana; school blacktop; library; school corridor (conferences); family car; doctor's office; her grandmother's house at Christmas.

## What happens
Chloe's mom is on the phone most evenings with the school, demanding a "next step" because Chloe, who used to like school, now dislikes first grade. Chloe listens through the wall. At school she sees that a 30-problem subtraction worksheet is one problem, finishes the idea at problem two, then stares out the window and sees a man drive off with his coffee cup on the car roof. Mrs. Aldana catches her not paying attention ("What was the question?").

Chloe reads constantly (cereal boxes, road signs, church bulletin, four library books at a time, reading by hall light under her door). She argues with the librarian about the four-books-at-a-time rule and loses; the librarian tells her new books sit on a cart on Thursday mornings. At a September dinner she retells a book about a runaway girl living in a train station and explains the locker key detail while her parents half listen.

At recess she joins a tennis-ball game, argues that Kayleigh Burns broke the one-bounce-out rule that put Maddie out, and nobody backs her; she ends up standing by the door with her book. In October, when Peanut the class pet falls from his cage bars, she says "He's Icarus"; Kayleigh tells her "You talk weird." After that she sits on her hands and peels her sandwich crust apart.

From November she gives short answers on purpose ("Water") even when she knows more. On Fridays Mrs. Aldana reads the best paper aloud; in November it is Chloe's, in December Chloe writes only the minimum and Maddie's is read. At December conferences Chloe waits in the corridor. In the car her mom reports Mrs. Aldana said she is among the strongest readers she has had, but has "some trouble focusing"; Chloe says she forgets, then someone says her name. Her dad asks if she likes school and she says "It's good."

The pediatrician finds nothing medical and recommends an evaluation for attention, giving two names (one with a long wait, one faster; ask for school-age testing). At Christmas at her grandmother's, cousin Dana (19) asks about school; Chloe is quiet. In the car her dad says "She was quiet tonight" and her mom says "She was fine."

In January, Mrs. Aldana takes Chloe's book when she reads ahead during class read-aloud (chapter nine while the class is on chapter four). Her mom jokes "on the same page, literally"; then her mouth drops. The book is returned next morning. Chloe decides to get better at sitting still, stretching worksheets by moving her pencil, and stops taking her library book out. In February a phone call sets the appointment for March 7; it goes on a sticky note on the fridge.

## Facts established
- Chloe is in first grade; Mrs. Aldana's folder on her started with her kindergarten teacher.
- Library rule: four books at a time. New books arrive Thursday morning and sit on a cart before shelving.
- Chloe's technique: finish the sheet fast, stare out the window; later, learns to stretch worksheets and give short answers.
- The man loading his car and leaving his coffee cup on the roof is a recurring image (Chloe tells Ben about it in ch02).
- Chloe wrote a dog-with-a-missing-leg paper in November that Mrs. Aldana read aloud; in December she wrote the minimum about a boat.
- Mrs. Aldana's conference verdict (relayed by mom): "among the strongest readers she's had" (said twice); "some trouble focusing."
- Doctor's office has a pencil height line; Chloe is two inches above last year's mark.
- Doctor recommends evaluation for attention; two psychologist names given; one is faster.
- Chloe finished a sled-dog book on Tuesday (including chapter four and nine) and was told to go back to chapter four.
- Appointment: March 7, on a yellow sticky note under a strawberry magnet on the refrigerator.
- Chloe's cloud answer she swallowed: "Clouds are the ocean going somewhere else."
- Chloe has been checking her sentences for "weird" since Kayleigh's October remark.

## Who knows what
- Chloe's parents believe the problem might be attention or a school mismatch; they do not yet know why she is bored.
- Chloe's mom believes Mrs. Aldana is wrong that it is a "choice thing."
- Chloe does not know what the evaluation will find; she knows the date.
- Chloe believes she is the one who cannot sit through school and must fix herself.
- Kayleigh, Mrs. Aldana, and classmates are not aware of Chloe's deliberate shortening of answers.
- Whether Chloe's dad shares her mom's worry is shown only indirectly (he is the one who asks "Do you like school?").
