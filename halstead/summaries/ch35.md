## What happens
On a Thursday in June, Eli is working at the project desk when the financial worm's regular check-in does not arrive. Nine minutes later the report appears, identical to every earlier one. He opens three separate logs (the process's own, the host's, and the outbound trace Kavi built). None shows an error, restart, or gap; the process reports itself running throughout. He checks page five of the group's rules document, the clause defining what counts as being noticed (an external party observing, recording, or interacting with the tool in a way inconsistent with its dormancy). On his reading the logs show nothing that triggers it, so he messages Kavi.

Kavi's watcher also reports a healthy process. Kavi, awake at 3 a.m., distrusts a clean log by design, rereads his own code instead, and goes to sleep with the file still open. In the chat Eli says someone stopped and restarted the tool and cleaned up so well that the cleanup is missing too. Ruth asks if it could be the government; Eli says no, they would be in custody, and no one finds someone else's tool and hands it back.

It happens twice more over six weeks. Everyone's ordinary life continues (Eli's day job, Ruth's contracts and her 2 a.m. phone habit, Kavi's summer class of thirteen-year-olds, Theo's fact-checking, Sam abroad and a day behind). The second time is the second Tuesday of July. Kavi, no longer trusting the watcher's own schedule, polls on his own clock and flags it within two minutes. It is again exactly nine minutes. He sends both timestamps to Eli privately and asks if it is a pattern; Eli says "could be either." In the group chat, Chloe argues the person is telling them he is there, since he could have killed the tool and gave it back. Eli rejects this firmly: he says the person made a mistake and is slightly wrong about how good they are. Kavi adds that only he would have built something to see nine minutes.

The third time lands on a hot July afternoon. Eli notices before Kavi's watcher. The gap runs longer than before, but the report then arrives unchanged.

Ruth, on her floor, rereads page seven (any one member can end the project at once, no vote, no explanation, stopped within the hour). It holds. She asks why no one has used it yet; no one answers. She then says "he doesn't know we know" is the only thing they have. Eli says do exactly nothing: no patching, moving, or looking at him looking. Ruth proposes feeding the tool a false input; Eli rejects it as risky; she drops it. Kavi offers to tighten the watcher's sampling invisibly; Eli approves that much. Theo asks how many mistakes the person has made that they did not catch. The chat goes quiet; Theo closes his laptop, Sam goes back to folding laundry, Ruth shuts hers.

Chloe, reading at 1 a.m. with work the next day, privately asks Ruth to watch the thread. Ruth says she is on it. Chloe sleeps, goes to work, and texts Ruth before lunch to ask if the night held. No answer is shown.
