# Chapter 13: Ten Pages
Dates (from the chapter header and text): September 2015 to April 2016 (school year). Thanksgiving and Christmas 2015 at home/grandmother's; a Tuesday essay in the second week of February 2016.
Chloe's age: 10 (the chapter does not state it; age 10 is implied by the ages-6-to-8 / 8-12 band and by ch14 "since we were ten"). POV: close third on Chloe throughout. Small drift: the Kavi-at-the-wall-bars paragraph and Bex "lacing a shoe" are things Chloe is not shown seeing.
Setting(s): Hearn's classroom; the mat room (self defense); paintball floor; corridors and sinks; dining hall; Chloe's family dinner table at Thanksgiving; grandmother's spare room at Christmas.

## What happens
New teacher Mr. Hearn assigns a ten-page essay a week, pegged to Alexander Hamilton writing 51 Federalist papers in about 30 weeks. Chloe's first essay argues a position she already held, and Hearn says he cannot tell whether she is right or only comfortable. She reads Federalist 10 (Madison, which she did not know) and then reads all 51 of Hamilton's by mid-December. Her second essay goes better in process but Hearn marks the disagree-with-yourself draft worse. In November he says the third page is doing the work of the others. In December he returns an essay with a mark and no note. In February she writes an essay with a deliberate weak spot, reads it back and leaves it; Hearn's reply is a few lines on what to read next. By March she has stopped counting hours, Hearn moves her to Burke, and in April she tells Sam that Hearn has changed nothing, and cannot say why she is no longer miserable.

Self defense: Kowalczyk introduces the 10v1 in January (one in the middle, ten on them, a minute). Chloe gets 4 seconds, then 6; the best anyone manages that afternoon is 9. By April Chloe is at 11 seconds and Sam reached 14 in February.

Paintball: Chloe works out with Ruth that you cannot react to the ball, you watch the barrel (a hose) and feet and move before the shot; every marker throws at one speed. Chloe and Ruth score 23 hits on a Thursday. Kavi works out the pump count (timing the pump). Bex overhears and, the following Monday, presents all of it to Bell as her own discovery; Bell writes it on the board under Bex's name. Chloe tries to say it was hers and Ruth's and Bell says the sheet is already written. Chloe tells Ruth at the sinks; Ruth shrugs it off as "Bex is like that."

Defensive Watch: a man from the office explains the scoring sheet (six lines, shown in text). Chloe's first drill happens on a Thursday; adults come over the north fence, enter Building Two, Wes answers a question about how many of them there are. Sheet gives everyone an F; item six says the north stairwell was uncovered, which Chloe takes as pointing at her. Ruth says it is on whoever wrote the vague drill. Chloe says at dinner "Nobody scores the drill"; Iyad repeats it with his own joke about Kessler standing in the stairwell, and the table laughs. Chloe leaves.

Thanksgiving: her father debates her tariffs essay (she argued "for") and finds most of an argument in it.

At Christmas at her grandmother's she reads Federalist 70 and 78 on the floor.
