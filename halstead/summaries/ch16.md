## What happens
From September Chloe teaches mathematics to seven 12-year-olds on Thursdays in room 4, and a grade sheet for the year goes on her record. Six of the seven are ordinary (Ivy, Gus and Beatriz are named; one boy cries in October and she sits on the floor with him). The seventh, Marek, hands in a blank sheet every week. He reads everything, answers instantly, apologizes constantly, and says copying work out afterward is a receipt, not work. He is working through a 1981 paper with a real error in it. In January he brings back a solved problem on the back of another student's timetable and will not put it on a sheet. In March he says nothing would make him hand something in. In December she asks the mathematics office (without naming him) how a blank column is scored; she is told it scores as zero submitted work. Marek fails the course, and Chloe writes the mark. In September he is back at breakfast with his own year. What the fail costs him is unknown to her.

Her own work: real analysis, where a teacher marks "This step is where you assumed it"; by February she stops resenting it. German comes easily after Latin. In the materials lab Sanders asks her why logs and a paper disagree; she finds a Tuesday recalibration in March after which later runs stayed out of the paper, taking 23 days. Amberg opens cryptography by saying they will break locks, not build them. Eli explains his fast solution of the November set: a repeated message each Tuesday at the same hour gives it away.

Hidden chat: in April Ruth shows Chloe a shoebox device that hunts network recorders and writes noise into recordings. In October a duty teacher reads three messages off a phone. Kavi and Ruth set up an encrypted chat on school machines, Ruth writing the encryption herself. Chloe breaks it within a week: the network logs who talks to whom and when. The fix is traffic that looks like the usual hundred and sixty machines checking weather; it runs from the first Saturday in December and holds at Christmas. Amberg could take it apart in an afternoon; nobody asks him.

Other scenes: Bex announces a Saturday plan Chloe was starting to suggest; the plan works. The epistemology professor says "Office hours." Kavi beats her at game theory. The forge occupies most of block two. A Russian and an English account of a bridge collapse disagree. Amberg's law class: the bar exam at sixteen. Eli puts a cipher on a napkin; Nadia calls it a plate, Chloe says it is both a date and a plate, read backward from the sixth character. Iyad later tells it as Chloe withholding the answer for an hour. Nadia and Chloe fall out, and Chloe stops sitting on that side of the table.

Summer: her father rebuilds the back steps; a riser she cut is 4 mm proud; he screws it down and says nobody will measure it.
