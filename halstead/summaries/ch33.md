# Chapter 33: The Other One
Dates (from the chapter header and text): Header: May 2026 - June 2026. Text says the test runs five weeks and signatures come "over the weeks that follow". Chloe turns 21 in August, "two months after she adds the last page" (so the last page is added about June). The Theo/Ruth/Eli chat scene happens "less than a week" after Eli wrote the document.
Chloe's age: 20 for the chapter's main events (turns 21 in August, mentioned once).   POV: Third person, drifting between characters. Theo (reading and deciding, tea at the counter), Ruth (dorm floor), Nadia, Kavi, Sam, Chloe (reading the thread; drafting the rules), Eli (summarized). Closing paragraph is Chloe's. No single anchor head.
Setting(s): Eli's kitchen table in an apartment three states from where he grew up (document written at 2 a.m.); Theo's building and his walks to and from work; Ruth's dorm room floor in Cambridge (roommate asleep nearby); Nadia's shop at the register after closing; Kavi's lab; Sam somewhere the paperwork "won't put a proper name to" (at basic, between sets); Chloe's kitchen counter and apartment, and a drive home. Mostly group chat.

## What happens
Eli proposes a second worm. Theo says no. That first night Theo sends one question back: what happens to the four of them who never signed anything for the government if this goes wrong. Eli answers within the hour with three lines about compartmentalization that do not address the question. No one argues the no again for days.

Eli then sends a twelve-page document to all of them at once, written at 2 a.m. at his kitchen table, so everyone works from one set of pages. It covers an access path, a timeline, what gets touched and what is left as found. It closes on the idea behind the financial worm: boring beats clever when someone is watching for clever. The target is the unit Theo told them exists, which has kept a file on the school for about twelve years and been wrong twice about who runs it. The document is mostly about getting in and leaving the file looking untouched, not just getting in.

Theo reads it the night it arrives, makes tea (milk first, like his grandmother), and rereads standing at the counter. For days he opens it, reads a line or two, and closes it. In the chat he sticks to small talk. Ruth texts him alone once, just his name. What moves him is the reasoning: reading intelligence is his job, and however he runs the accounting, the only way any of them will learn what the government thinks it knows about their school is to read the file.

When Theo finally opens the thread, Ruth is already there with two numbers ready. Theo says on record that this is the stupidest thing any of them has ever done; Ruth says "noted" (in the chat). Eli compares it to speeding, a rule with a known enforcement mechanism. Ruth cites section 1030: up to five years with a financial angle or another crime attached, up to ten for a repeat, and worse if anything touched is classified. Eli says there is no money in this one, and that if he is wrong about the cameras he goes away a long time, so he will not be wrong. Ruth asks for the actual number of cameras he has counted, not the number he is confident about. Eli does not answer, and the thread goes quiet.

Over the following days the others read it. Nadia (sleeves up, which means something will cost her) asks who touches the code; Eli says he and Kavi, same as last time. She says then two carry it, not seven, and tells Chloe to write that down; Chloe says it is clause four. Kavi says the government's logging is old and probably worse than Nadia's, so the danger is a manual review of the exact files in the exact week, meaning timing; Eli says they control the week. Sam asks how long it runs; Eli says as long as it takes to get in and out once; Sam says that is not a number. Chloe, going back to Theo's old line about the men over the fence, says that if they get a chance to read the file back she sees nothing to decide.

Theo says he could write the rules best but his name cannot be on a document about getting around the government he is paid to read. The job falls to Chloe. Eli builds the entry, Kavi builds the exit (erasing traces as it happens). Both call it "the plumbing." The test runs five weeks, watched only by Eli and Kavi, with Kavi's watcher also watching. Eli posts one line twice a week ("clean" or a variant). Chloe works at night on the rules document, a few pages: a limits page; a definition of "noticed"; a one-line stop clause; and a what-if-noticed page.

Ruth asks that the stop clause require a one-sentence reason, for the record. Nadia refuses: a sentence is a case, and whoever is scared enough to pull it is qualified. Ruth says "fine, strike it." They sign one at a time. Eli first ("in"), Nadia the same day, Kavi from the lab ("looks fine"), Sam once he gets the five-week number, Ruth ("in. the numbers stand"), Chloe the night she finishes, and Theo last, at the end of the test ("still the stupidest thing any of us has ever done / in"). A closing paragraph says Chloe holds a clearance and is about to spend it on a file meant to be sealed from her; she thinks through the cost once, on a drive home.

Not resolved: Eli never gives a camera count; the compartmentalization non-answer to Theo's question; what actually happens to the non-signers if it goes wrong.
