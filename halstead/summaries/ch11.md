# Chapter 11: Eight
Dates (from the chapter header and text): Header "June 2013 - June 2014". Summer 2013 at home (second day, a Saturday, fourth of July, the eighth, "third week" twice, a Sunday visit); September 2013 return; November; "after Christmas"; February (office); April (apples, a Tuesday); June 2014.
Chloe's age: 8 (chapter title; Nerf "starts at eight"; the curfew argument mentions eight-year-olds)   POV: Close third on Chloe, with drift into her parents' kitchen conversations without her (hot dog night, the curfew aftermath) and a grandparent exchange.
Setting(s): Home (porch, grill, kitchen, front hall); the bike shop; Halstead (room with Margie, French class, politics, gym, wood shop, office, elective list in the corridor, grass behind the science building).

## What happens
Home for the summer, Chloe rides the old bike, and her mom takes her to buy a bigger one; Chloe picks the tallest. She talks constantly, sings in the shower. On July 4 she argues with her dad about whether a hot dog is a sandwich; he concedes on the eighth and her mom closes the subject until Labor Day. That night her parents notice she hums no more in the car; her mom says she will count every day of the summer.

In the third week Chloe teaches her mom Spanish at the counter (they laugh at "estan"). Around then Chloe breaks the nine o'clock curfew; her mom is waiting in the hall. Chloe argues with the library at school having no door rule and that "somebody picked ten the way somebody picked nine." Her mom says "Nine, and that's the number." Afterward, to her husband, she says Chloe stops arguing once it is settled. Chloe's grandmother visits; she says Chloe has put on weight and was too thin at Christmas. Chloe eats half her dinner and says "I've had enough."

September: roommate Marguerite ("Margie") from Ohio. Fen is now on second floor. French replaces Spanish; the teacher says the slot is hers to keep or drop. She reads Spanish novels on her own in the hour before dinner. Logic begins ("like taking a lid off"). Political history is a wall of leaders, spouses, in-laws and money, updated weekly in pencil.

Nerf starts at eight, with Coach Bell. Games run a full hour. Chloe wins three in a row in October. Afternoon blocks are Nerf Monday, wood shop Tuesday, team sports Wednesday, drawing Thursday, dance Friday. She makes an oval mallet and over-sands it. In team sports she is on a team with two people she dislikes and counts passes not sent to her. She draws her left hand forty times; Ruth says the fortieth is a hand. She is ninetieth of ninety on the term sheet, and in dance too. In February she asks Mrs. Okonkwo to drop dance, then swap teams; both are refused.

The elective list: Sam picks climbing, Kavi electronics, Ruth will not say. Chloe signs up for choir, matches Amara a third of the time, and quits in the sixth week for the pool. On an April Tuesday the four friends throw apples at a drainpipe with a rule about "below the bracket."

In June she opens the notebook of four subjects she was behind in and finds she has been reading ahead in three since about Christmas. She adds a line and goes down to dinner two steps at a time.

- Chloe believes (and tells her mom) the school library has no limits or supervision; her mom says that is a school rule.
- Mrs. Okonkwo knows two other children asked about the same team.
- Chloe is hiding the discomfort of being last-ranked, but it is on a posted sheet.
