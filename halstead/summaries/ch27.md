# Chapter 27: Nadia
Dates (from the chapter header and text): May 2024 (header). Scam discovered on a Tuesday in the second week of May (the text also says "the night of the ninth"; see Open threads). She drives to the tire shop office on the seventh day after. The hiring section covers roughly July 2023 to spring 2024. A final flash-forward says Hanley's filings go dead in July (2024) and she notices in August.
Chloe's age: not stated; Chloe does not appear in the narration or chat in this chapter's lines (she does not speak). Nadia is eighteen "since last June."   POV: Nadia, close third person. No drift.
Setting(s): Nadia's company, three rooms over a laundromat, a hundred yards from her parents' shop. A tire shop with an upstairs office off the county road. The group chat.

## What happens
Nadia's company is eleven months old with four staff besides her. It builds a profile from short tests and then sends applications to employers. On a Tuesday night in the second week of May she finds 23 names in the new-employer queue, over half outside employers she recruited. They share one job description with only the company name swapped, were created in one half-hour window near midnight, and have zero hires. Logs show each asks for a Social Security number and bank routing number in the third message. Three people answered; one lost $2,600. Nadia deletes the accounts and phones the victims; on the third call the woman apologizes five times. 

Most fake employers entered a real state registration number, mostly filed one week the previous autumn through a registered agent, Hanley, whose address is a suite above a tire shop. A man at the tire shop counter says half a dozen people come and go upstairs and never buy tires. On the seventh day she tells her parents she will be back before dinner and drives out alone, parking in view with the doors unlocked.

Upstairs are four men. She states what they did. They tell her to get out; one shuts the door and stands against it. They ask her age (eighteen since last June) and whether her father knows where she is. One photographs her and her car and reads her plate aloud. A man says it is 5:20, the bay is locked on the hour, and nobody watched her come up. Nadia says a file will send itself to the state attorney general and two reporters if she does not get home. She says she will not call the police. She says nine of them entered a live registration number in required field six, ten were filed through Hanley, who has 31 filings at that address, 26 belonging to others who do not know, and she will send the file to the Secretary of State's business division. Her offer: the accounts stay deleted, they stay off her site, and she never returns. The man behind the desk says to get out. One says "sweetheart" at her back. At the shop she counts the till, three dollars under.

In the chat she says only that they are done, then explains the self-sending file. Eli says a dead man's switch pays off after you are dead and she was lucky; Sam asks why she went alone. She says it was her name on the site.

Then hiring: her test generator builds tests from live listings; almost everyone fails; she lowers the difficulty; she interviews 31 people instead of 12 and finds most run out at the second question, about the edge of their work. Tomas, Bev and Manny each act on their own. She hires a fifth. In the chat she says she cannot find anyone who can finish a thought, and that payroll is covered for under a fortnight.
