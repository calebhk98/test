# Chapter 27: Nadia
Dates (from the chapter header and text): May 2024 (header). Scam discovered on a Tuesday in the second week of May (the text also says "the night of the ninth"; see Open threads). She drives to the tire shop office on the seventh day after. The hiring section covers roughly July 2023 to spring 2024. A final flash-forward says Hanley's filings go dead in July (2024) and she notices in August.
Chloe's age: not stated; Chloe does not appear in the narration or chat in this chapter's lines (she does not speak). Nadia is eighteen "since last June."   POV: Nadia, close third person. No drift.
Setting(s): Nadia's company, three rooms over a laundromat, a hundred yards from her parents' shop. A tire shop with an upstairs office off the county road. The group chat.

## What happens
Nadia's company is eleven months old, with four employees besides her. It builds a profile from short tests and then fills and sends applications to employers on the site and outside boards. On a Tuesday night in the second week of May she finds 23 names in the new-employer queue, over half outside the employers she has personally recruited. The accounts share one job description with only the company name swapped, were all created in a half-hour window around midnight, and have zero hires. Message logs show each asks for a Social Security number and bank routing number in the third message. Three people answered; one gave both numbers and her account was emptied of $2,600. Nadia deletes the accounts in about ninety seconds and phones the victims. The third call runs twenty minutes and the woman apologizes five times. Nadia tells her to call the bank when it opens. In the group chat Nadia says she is insulted rather than fine and reports the details; Ruth asks how many answered.

Over the following week, Nadia works before the laundromat opens. Most of the fake employers entered a real state registration number. She finds most were filed in one week the previous autumn through one registered agent named Hanley, whose address is a suite above a tire shop. She phones the tire shop counter, and learns a half dozen people come and go upstairs and never buy tires. On the seventh day she tells her parents she will be back before dinner, and drives out alone, parking in view and leaving the doors unlocked.

Upstairs are four men. She states what they did and demands they stop. They tell her to get out; one shuts the door and stands against it. They ask her age (eighteen since last June) and whether her father knows where she is; one photographs her, then her car, and reads her plate out loud. A man says it is 5:20, the bay is locked on the hour, and nobody watched her come up. Nadia says a file will send itself to the state attorney general and two reporters if she does not get home. She says she is not calling the police. She lays out that nine of them entered a live registration number in required field six, that ten filings share the agent Hanley, who has 31 filings at that address, 26 belonging to others who do not know, and that she will send the file to the Secretary of State's business division. Her offer: the accounts stay deleted and off her site, and she never returns. The man behind the desk tells her to get out. One says "sweetheart" at her back. She counts the till at the shop, three dollars under.

In the chat she says only that they are done and the accounts stopped. Under pressure she explains the self-sending file. Eli says a dead man's switch pays off after you are dead and that she was lucky; Ruth and Sam add that she should not have gone alone. Nadia says it was her name on the site.

Then the section on hiring: her test generator makes tests from live listings and covers thousands of skills; almost everyone fails; she lowers the difficulty; she interviews 31 people instead of 12; the second question about the edge of their work is where most run out. Tomas, Bev and Manny act on their own. She hires a fifth person. In the chat she tells friends she cannot find anyone who can finish a thought and that payroll is covered under a fortnight.
