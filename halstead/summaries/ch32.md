# Chapter 32: The Money
Dates (from the chapter header and text): Header "November 2025 - February 2026". Sixteen weeks total. Go-live night (late November/early December, not stated); first anomalous entry in the second week of December; two months of findings after that; group call second week of January; whiteboard finished end of January; the chat scene about Ruth's proof is undated within January/February.
Chloe's age: not stated.   POV: Mostly omniscient, moving between Eli, Kavi, Nadia, Ruth, Theo; the chat scenes are transcript with short narration on who is reading.
Setting(s): Eli's desk (works after his shift; later his place, "middle of the night in Ohio"); Kavi's converted-classroom lab; Nadia's repair shop; Ruth's cold apartment floor; Theo's apartment steps; Sam's kitchen table; Chloe at her desk; the group chat.

## What happens
The "financial one" takes sixteen weeks. Eli builds a worm and Kavi builds a watcher for it. The design goal is boredom: it must look like a badly maintained reconciliation job and move slowly. Eli rewrites the polling interval repeatedly until it "reads as tired"; the version he keeps is committed at 3:14 a.m. on a Tuesday. Kavi builds the watcher from Eli's logs, testing first on his own old coursework logs for a full week. Its job is to notice the day the worm starts behaving like a person who found something and to tell exactly one person first.

They test against Nadia's shop's live books for two weeks. Nadia checks Eli's daily summary line by line and sends one correction: a timestamp read off the server clock instead of the shop's time zone. Eli fixes it within the hour. In chat Nadia says she will "end all of you" if it comes back to her company; Eli says it will not.

On go-live night Eli calls Kavi. They read the deployment target back to each other; Eli presses one key; Kavi says "Running." Eli writes the timestamp on a sticky note he later loses. The first week is quiet. In the second week of December an entry lands outside its band and Kavi flags it. Over two months the worm finds a "shape": no bank name, but a single set of decisions running from around 1998 or 2001. Kavi stacks them on a wheeled whiteboard with cards and a color per currency. In the second week of January the three of them (Eli, Kavi, Ruth) look at it on a call; Ruth takes notes. The shape resists being anything but a single person and becomes Ruth's problem.

Ruth works on her apartment floor, papers in a ring, and copies the timeline gaps onto a long paper strip. She posts her argument to the chat: it is one person, shown from presence not absence. (1) Gaps of three to five days, dozens over twenty years, no pattern, no holidays. (2) Every decision falls in the same seven-hour band, which drifts about forty minutes total over two decades. (3) A persistent preference for the slightly worse of two equivalent instruments, every time since 2003. Her confidence is 99.99; "the other 0.01 is me being humble." Eli reruns her work on his copy of the logs and it holds. Kavi adds a line to the board.

Sam and Nadia question why no one has done this. Theo, after leaving his laptop and sitting on his back steps, tells them: "nobody repeats any of this." He says the government has had a file on the school since 2013, thinks it is a state or company, has been wrong twice, put a man on staff when they were eleven (spotted in a day), and eight years ago sent people over the fence at four in the morning and four of them "put them on the floor." Chloe says it was a drill; Theo says he read the file. Chloe asks "theo which four"; he declines that night and next morning says he wants to recheck. Eli ends: "so lets go and read it."
