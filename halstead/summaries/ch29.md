# Chapter 29: The File
Dates (from the chapter header and text): October 2025. Theo is in his third year at the job. The file on his school goes back to 2013. The raid report is dated "eight years ago" (about 2017), when Theo was twelve. The chapter spans about a week: a day and a half to work out what the box is, two days of silence, then the chat post.
Chloe's age: twenty (not stated in this chapter; Chloe appears only in memory).   POV: Theo, close third person. No drift. Chloe appears in Theo's memory of the class photo and the drill.
Setting(s): a government intelligence-style office (agency unnamed): two badges, garage and floor, gray carpet tile, cubicles, about sixty desks. Theo's home (calls grandmother). The group chat.

## What happens
Theo works as an analyst: reads cable traffic and open-source reporting, 50 to 60 pages a day, and boils it to a page or two. His supervisor sends half back with three questions. About once a week he gets Pashto and Dari transcripts; pairing them finds mismatches one-language readers miss; a few were flagged in his first year and the supervisor takes those seriously.

By his third year, the woman across the aisle, after twenty-six years, clears her desk and gives him a second box of old closed material: "Somebody has to read it eventually. Might as well be the new guy." It takes him a day and a half to understand what he holds. Folders are sorted by year. After ninety minutes of routine material he finds a folder with the name of his school on the tab: Halstead. The file goes back to 2013. Eleven sets of initials across twelve years, one repeating twice eight years apart (he circles it). Early assessments say state actor (two candidates, one preferred); years later revised down because no insider has turned anything up; current assessment is "probably corporate" with the standing caveat that the funding cannot be traced. Paper stock changes twice.

Next is a memo about a man placed on the Halstead teaching staff. Identified in under a day; the school escorted him off, stating they do not permit unidentified adults around children. Theo opens his desk drawer to a class photo (dated along the bottom edge) and matches dates. He finds Chloe near the back. He remembers the new teacher for a day and a half, the odd feeling that he was pretending to be somebody, and that he was gone by Thursday. Theo had assumed he quit.

He reads the last item the next morning: a report on an entry eight years ago. Four operators came over the fence at the loading side before dawn. They were inside under two minutes. Someone saw a child alone in a corridor in pajamas asking who they were. All four were unconscious before anyone decided anything; their blood tests showed the same fast-acting sedative, dose recorded to the milligram, source not stated. They woke tied at wrists and ankles against a wall, with four children sitting nearby arguing about how the men would score them: whether a response time of nearly eight minutes counted as a fail and whether a surprise exercise skipping the board was fair. The children asked the author what he would have given them. An adult arrived, told the children to go to bed (they did), and told the men an attack on a school in the US would not be accepted and they were free to leave. No reply is on file.

Theo remembers being twelve: a night drill, four people on it, Chloe caught flat-footed by the alarm, and a grade sheet on the board that he thought harsh. He puts his hand over his mouth. A coworker asks if he's okay; he says fine.

For two days he does his job. His supervisor asks about "the retirement box"; he says it is dead weight and a folder needs another day. He reads the school's website as open-source material; nothing matches. He calls his grandmother (ferry, Hendersons' dog). He drafts and deletes several messages, and then posts in the chat: "hypothetically... if you found something out about the school. from work. that you couldnt actually say... how badly would you want to know." Ruth answers: "bad." It is her first post in six months.

## Who knows what
- Theo knows the school's file exists, that the government has watched it since 2013, that four operators were once drugged and held, and that the school's children ran a drill involving them. He believes the file says only what it says; he does not know who funds the school.
- Theo had long assumed the teacher quit.
- Theo does not tell anyone at work what he read. His supervisor believes the box is dead weight.
- Theo does not know why Ruth has been silent.
- The chat does not know what Theo found; he asked hypothetically.
