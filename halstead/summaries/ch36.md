# Chapter 36: Seventy-Five
Dates (from the chapter header and text): October 2026 (header). Priya's account refers to attacks "since march", "six of them in may", "about a dozen in july", and "tuesday night" (the seventy-five). Her prisoners were "asleep in a field in october". The chapter covers roughly the following week or more (Thursday, Saturday, "days later"), ending with the mare due.
Chloe's age: not stated. The chapter says only she is "most of a year into the job". (Chapter 1 has her aged 6 in 2011, so she is about 15 years older here; the figure is not given in this chapter.)   POV: omniscient narrator, present tense, moving freely between characters (Priya's days, Ruth reading, Nadia at the counter, Eli at the kitchen counter, Chloe on the bus and at work, Ruth up until three). Most of the chapter is chat messages. No single head.
Setting(s): the group chat (the eight of them); Priya's location (a room over a feed merchant's with an outside stair, a yard with a gate and a fence, a mare); a field where the attack took place; Chloe's bus and workplace building; Nadia's parents' shop counter; Eli's kitchen; Ruth's place. Priya's country and location are not named.

## What happens
Opening summary: Priya has gone quiet for several days in October. The narrator lists where the others are: Chloe is most of a year into her job; Ruth has a name card by a door and a second-year following her; Kavi has his lab plus an unrequested second bench; Sam is at a unit in a state he had never seen before posting, with evening phone use; Nadia manages more people but still counts her parents' till on Sundays; Eli has an amusing job title; Theo answers from behind blinds. None refers in writing to something from the summer. Priya's quiet days were buses and a room over a feed merchant's with a lock she changed herself. At four in the morning she returns to the chat.

She asks why the government cares about the school. People have tried to take her since March, in any country: a van and two men before light (she went over a wall), six in May, about a dozen in July, and seventy-five on Tuesday night. Sam recalls the seven in a car park. Priya says these attackers fought like thirteen and fourteen year olds (one good fifteen) and nobody did anything advanced. They had guns from the start but held them until she was about thirty into the line. She took two guns and put them into the engine blocks of the only vehicles on her side of the field. Then darts; one hit her on the third go, a small slowing dose. She got out with a hand that will not close fully, and with two men she found asleep in a field.

She keeps the two men somewhere unnamed and the chat lets it be. Her week of reports is logistics only (a knee needing ice, big appetites, onion on the plate edge). By Thursday one says good morning first in English; by Saturday the other is sitting up before she enters. She watches the road from an outside stair, timing the gap from bend to gate, and feeds them at fixed hours. They say American, "agency", no more; their stories disagree; they insist they came for her by name from a list.

Chloe says the group has read a file about the school, about twelve years old. Kavi and Nadia give a short version: not a normal school, and someone kept paper on its people. Priya asks why seventy-five. Ruth: three empty vans gives thirty if you think you are collecting one person; Priya is in the top ten percent of people alive at Tuesday's skills. Priya refuses it: she finished sixty-fourth of ninety-one. Chloe types a paragraph on the bus, deletes it, sends one line. Theo sends an intake form name and number. Priya concludes the men are lying about their employer, or someone spent heavily on poor operators, and calls that a more useful question.

Ruth posts that seventy-five people arm's length apart span 110 meters. No one answers. She stays up until three listing where each of them sleeps and the distance to a road, her own address last. Chloe rereads page three of the file, goes to work, and meets Theo's form number in a search via a careful man across town; she closes the entry. The ranking goes unmentioned. Nadia, Sam and Eli offer company, leave and security changes (Priya mostly had them done). Kavi takes six hours, sends software and a link, then asks what is on the yard gate. Priya is at the fence because the mare is due.

## Who knows what
- Priya knows the attack scale and her captives' claims; she does not know why the government cares about the school until the group tells her briefly. She has not been told the file's contents in detail.
- The group has read the file; Priya has not.
- Priya believes the captives may be lying about their employer or may be poorly trained expensive hires; she believes she is not exceptional at the school's skills.
- Ruth believes Priya is top ten percent and that the group is exposed; her arithmetic implies seventy-five people could surround a large area.
- The group does not know where Priya is holding the men (she keeps it private).
- Chloe knows the form number from work and Theo's message; whether she or Theo explain it to the others is not shown.
- Priya had assumed the earlier attacks were something that happens to a woman living where nobody knows her name; the new scale changed that view.
- It is ambiguous whether Chloe's sent one-line message and Theo's form number are connected to the file; the text does not say.
