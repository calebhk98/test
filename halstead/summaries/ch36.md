# Chapter 36: Seventy-Five
Dates (from the chapter header and text): October 2026 (header). Priya's account refers to attacks "since march", "six of them in may", "about a dozen in july", and "tuesday night" (the seventy-five). Her prisoners were "asleep in a field in october". The chapter covers roughly the following week or more (Thursday, Saturday, "days later"), ending with the mare due.
Chloe's age: not stated. The chapter says only she is "most of a year into the job". (Chapter 1 has her aged 6 in 2011, so she is about 15 years older here; the figure is not given in this chapter.)   POV: omniscient narrator, present tense, moving freely between characters (Priya's days, Ruth reading, Nadia at the counter, Eli at the kitchen counter, Chloe on the bus and at work, Ruth up until three). Most of the chapter is chat messages. No single head.
Setting(s): the group chat (the eight of them); Priya's location (a room over a feed merchant's with an outside stair, a yard with a gate and a fence, a mare); a field where the attack took place; Chloe's bus and workplace building; Nadia's parents' shop counter; Eli's kitchen; Ruth's place. Priya's country and location are not named.

## What happens
Opening summary: Priya has gone quiet for several days in October. The narrator lists where the others are: Chloe is most of a year into her job; Ruth has a card with her name in a bracket by a door and a second-year following her around; Kavi has his lab and a second bench he did not ask for; Sam is at a unit in a state he first saw the day he was posted, with evening phone use; Nadia manages more people at her company but still counts her parents' till on Sundays; Eli has a job title he finds funny; Theo answers from behind blinds. None of them refers directly in writing to something from the summer.

Priya's quiet days were spent on buses, then in a room over a feed merchant's with a lock she changed herself. Part of the time she sat still, letting a hand come up and go down (ambiguous; probably the injured hand). At four in the morning she returns to the chat.

She asks why the government cares about the school. People have been trying to take her since March in any country she goes to: an unmarked van and two men before light, and she went over a back wall. Six came in May, about a dozen in July. On Tuesday night there were seventy-five. Sam says the group once handled seven in a car park. Priya replies that those were fine, all of them, and that is what she wants explained: the attackers fought like thirteen and fourteen year olds (one was a good fifteen), and none did anything advanced. They had guns from the start but held them until she was through the line, about thirty people in. She took two guns off the grass and put them into the engine blocks of the only vehicles on that side of the field. Then darts: she says you cannot hit a watching person with them. One hit her on the third attempt; it was a small dose meant to slow her. She got out with a hand that will not close all the way. She also took two men who were asleep in a field.

Priya keeps the two men somewhere she will not name, and the chat lets it be. Over the next week she reports logistics only (a knee that needs ice twice a day, they eat a lot, one pushes onions aside). By Thursday one says good morning first in English; by Saturday the other is already sitting up when she brings the tray. She watches the road from the top of the outside stair and times the gap from the bend to the gate at a walk and a run; she feeds them at fixed hours. They say they are American and "agency" but not which. Their stories do not agree. They repeat that they came for her by name from a list.

Chloe tells her there is a file about the school, about twelve years old, which the group has read. Kavi and Nadia give a short version: it was not a normal school and someone has kept paper on its people since they were kids. Priya asks why seventy-five. Ruth says three vans that came back empty gives thirty if you think you are collecting one person. Ruth says Priya is in the top ten percent of people alive at what she did Tuesday. Priya rejects it: she finished sixty-fourth of ninety-one, bottom third four years running. Chloe types a long message on the bus, deletes it, and sends one line (content not shown). Theo sends the name and number of an intake form; Priya thanks him. Priya concludes either the two men are lying about whom they work for, or someone spent a lot on people who are not good at it, which she calls a more useful question.

Ruth posts the arithmetic that seventy-five people arm's length apart is 110 meters, which she can see straight through. No one answers. Ruth stays up until three listing the places each of them sleeps and the distance to a vehicle-accessible road, her own address last. Next morning Chloe rereads page three of the file (the funding line paragraph), goes to work, and during an unrelated search sees the same intake form number Theo sent, via a man across town who is careful what he writes. She reads and closes the entry and moves on.

The ranking is not mentioned again. Nadia offers company; Priya sends a photo of a gate with a new hinge and "sorted". Sam offers two weeks of leave; Priya says flights are absurd; he offers again with a cheaper one. Eli sends security changes for the room (a bolt, a second exit, a braced chair), most already done. Kavi takes six hours, sends the name of unfamiliar software and a link, then an explanation, and asks what is on the yard gate. Priya has been at the fence an hour because the mare is due.
