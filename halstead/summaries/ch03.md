# Chapter 03: The Letter
Dates (from the chapter header and text): June 2012 to July 2012 (header). Letter arrives in June (sprinkler season). Monday: district office call; Thursday: callback; Saturday: half the street knows; the decision takes nine days; the library scene is the Thursday before they leave. The session starts the second week of July and runs four weeks.
Chloe's age: 6   POV: close third person, present tense, Chloe's head. Some paragraphs go beyond what she could perceive (mom's solo web search "on the Sunday with the door shut," mom calling the emergency number from the parking lot, mom staying at the hotel). Not marked as drift.
Setting(s): Kessler house (mailbox, kitchen, stairs, Chloe's room); the library; the drive (about four hours); the hotel near Halstead.

## What happens
A typed envelope addressed to "Miss Chloe Kessler" arrives. Chloe opens it at the mailbox. It describes Halstead, a school with a four-week summer session starting the second week of July, free (stated twice), with transportation, room and board, and a free hotel half a mile away for families who may visit whenever. A phone number, a name, and an inked signature are at the bottom.

At dinner Chloe asks "Can I go?" Her mom (Meg) has no memory of applying; her dad does not either. Her mom distrusts a free month with a bus. Her dad suggests calling. Her mom: "There's always a reason."

On Monday her mom calls the district office. By Thursday the receptionist says no school named Halstead works with the district in any capacity, and warns to be careful because people mail things with a child's name to learn which houses respond. The postmark is a city four states away, dated four days before arrival. Mom writes it on a receipt under the magnet. She calls the Petersons; Ellie Peterson (in the pull-out group) got no letter. By Saturday half the street knows.

Mom calls the number on the letter; someone answers on the second ring. She demands the name of the person who made the list. She asks about night fever, a nurse, food, pulling Chloe out any day, fees, and head count ("Over a hundred"). Every answer comes fast. Halstead says they have people who identify children, "networks," and "their list is usually pretty good." The woman says Kesslers are "one of the closer ones" (about four hours). Mom calls "usually pretty good" the only non-fact; the rest answered too fast, as if asked often.

Dad browses the website with Chloe on Saturday night, doing a train-station announcer voice; pool, goals field, Tuesday schedule. He goes silent at the teachers page: an Olympic swimmer, a former SEAL, double master's degrees, a paper. Mom searches alone Sunday: nothing outside the school's own site. On Monday morning dad tells Chloe the school has been open four years, a real address, and phone, and the website has not been touched since it went up ("very old money or very careless"). The bills are clipped on the fridge.

The decision takes nine days. Mom argues no; dad says go anyway. Mom asks Chloe why she wants to go; Chloe: "It's got my name on it... It says Miss." On the ninth night Chloe overhears from the stairs: mom says Chloe has been flat since Christmas; dad says he knows.

On the Thursday before leaving Chloe tries to bank sixteen library books; the librarian refuses but allows one held book. Chloe picks a book she has already read. Parents drive her instead of the bus, pack with a laundry pen KESSLER, and dad does the train-station voice for the packing list. Mom stays at the hotel for the first days, checks the nurse and the night desk, calls the emergency number to check it rings (a different man answers each time). Dad arranges to take the last Friday off. Chloe packs the envelope.
