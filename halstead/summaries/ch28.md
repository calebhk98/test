# Chapter 28: Nineteen
Dates (from the chapter header and text): September 2024 to October 2025 (header). Job starts September 2024; blog starts September 2024; October 2024 forklift manual; November cold; February (2025) the emailed question; spring: twenty-one credits; April 2025 Ruth stops posting; June 2025 Sam raises it; last line "Six months."
Chloe's age: nineteen (title; chapter opens at 19). She turns twenty in August 2025 per ch30.   POV: Chloe, close third person. A few lines of group chat. The last chat section is only chat text.
Setting(s): a translation company (about fifty-odd people) in an unnamed city; her apartment (lives alone); a college; the bus; the group chat.

## What happens
Chloe starts work at a company that translates contracts, manuals and safety sheets. She finds the office customs: a birthday calendar run by Deb, who adds Chloe's birthday unasked from a form; an office pool; mints; a man on the phone to the cable company for fifty minutes; fire alarms. A man from the loading dock asks about her weekend and she gives a fifty-second answer; over three weeks she shrinks it to one sentence.

She attends college alongside, taking 21 credits in the spring plus an evening seminar after a registrar tells her there is a guideline, not a rule. She adds Polish in the autumn, poorly taught. At work she does filing for the whole floor until Deb asks if someone asked her to. Her work takes three hours a day, so she asks for more; Deb routes Russian and Mandarin to her. A coordinator emails her legal documents directly. In October she catches a decimal error in a forklift safety manual (capacity rendered ten times the source) and takes it to Deb; the client resends the batch.

Deb talks about her son Tyler: baseball, a coach who benched him, his father who calls twice a year. When Deb leaves sharply for his games, Chloe finishes stacks herself, then the third time takes the rest home; Deb stops leaving unfinished stacks.

Chloe notices she says everything twice: first an explanation of certification pricing, then match logic for repeated sentences, then a comparison to Tyler's spelling test; Deb laughs and calls the discount "the Tyler rate." Chloe gives a sick coordinator tablets she has taken since seven; he asks where to buy them and she says she has looked and a doctor has, and they are not anywhere. She hears herself doing the second version for her mother on the phone and treats it as ordinary courtesy.

The blog starts in September: a fishing dispute described differently by two governments, then pieces on a currency peg, a mining concession, a port strike, and others, two or three a week by November, written at night. A man emails in February asking whether she has a research team; she says no, it is her, and he does not reply. She records the exchange on a spreadsheet; two more similar emails come before spring; by April five rows, no outlet named in any.

In the chat, Ruth asks questions: second language (Ruth says ~25%, about 20% at home), low literacy (21%), 3/4 minus 1/2 (about a third), and a physical fight. The chat "moves" between each. Sam mentions being held back a week at basic over a reading placement. In April Ruth stops posting. In June Sam raises it, Kavi says she is probably busy. Chloe messages her twice; the first gets "sorry, been swamped," the second gets a single check mark. Last line: "Six months."
