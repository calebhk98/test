# Chapter 10: April
Dates (from the chapter header and text): Header "April 2013 - June 2013". Arrival day in April (late in the month implied; Chloe says later "Ruth in April, at the trays"), her first day a Monday, wheel problem in the third week of May, second term starts after (late May), last week of June leaving.
Chloe's age: 7 (text says she is seven when Kavi speaks of seven; no birthday shown)   POV: Close third on Chloe throughout.
Setting(s): Halstead: third-floor room, stairs, dining hall, classrooms, pool, library, first-floor alcove phone, stables not yet; front steps.

## What happens
Chloe arrives as a student. Sam runs down the hall and hugs her; Ruth takes her bag. Ruth explains new students go on the third floor and move in August, and says she never wrote because she had no address. Chloe's room is shared with Fen, from Michigan, who keeps rocks on the sill in order of size. Chloe asks Ruth to spell her last name, Aymar, and writes it and her number in a notebook, leaving four blank lines before Sam's name. Kavi arrives annoyed that Sam knew where she was and let him search. Chloe asks his last name; the answer is not shown.

At dinner she counts sixteen familiar faces. Of ninety at camp, "twenty-some" came back that first fall. She asks Ruth about Owen, who left camp in the second week; he refused the "water thing". At night she asks Fen how she knows the rock order in the dark.

Her days run on bells: Spanish, mathematics, writing, literature, history in the morning; afternoon hours. Swimming (the teacher swims to show her), cooking (her onion is a triangle), Spanish, and mathematics run backward from camp: you get the object plus a week. She brings pages; the teacher says line two is wrong and gives her forty minutes to find it. Literature has no reading list; the library is open all night, no card, no limit. She hauls home a dozen, then fewer.

In the third week of May she solves the problem of how far a mark on a rolling wheel's rim travels; Ruth's working is three steps and hers seven. After that mathematics is the hour she gets up for.

She tries to learn why people are sent home. The corkboard says nothing. The writing teacher says families sometimes take children home, not for being bad. Kavi says Owen wanted to stay, was keeping up, and cried by the kitchens the Thursday before his mom came. Kavi says she already has all the answers. Her mother phones on Sundays; Chloe says she is happy.

Second term: history splits into political history and drawing the world on blank paper; physics arrives. A boy named Marcus says he carries seven subjects, the max is ten. Chloe keeps a list. In the last week of June Fen packs her rocks into a shoebox. Chloe packs the envelope (still under the folder) into her bag. Ruth walks her to the steps. Ruth will be at a cousin's lake town; Chloe will call on a Wednesday, not before ten. They say "September." In the car Chloe checks the notebook's inside cover.
