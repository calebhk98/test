# Egypt: The Turn 525–550 AD

## The Reign of Ptolemy XXXI Nesiaset

The old Pharaoh, who had sat eleven days in the Atlantic council and wept at the end of it, died in the winter of 527. His son acceded in the spring of 528, aged thirty-four, and took the name Nesiaset, He-Belongs-to-Isis.

The chronicle of his reign is short, because little in it is his. He confirmed the standing council entire within a month, which was the customary act and also the whole of his programme. He read the Atlantic file and did not reopen it. He signed the continuation of the Western Enterprise, the Livingstone works, the Sinai schedule, the industrial commissions and the research slate, each in the form his father's council had drafted, and in twenty-two years cancelled nothing already funded.

Twice he was asked to break a constituency and twice he declined. Once he spent his own capital, and he spent it on a tower.

The council's assessment of him, entered without malice in 545: *a steady prince, who has lost nothing that was given him.* That is the highest praise a permanent council gives a king who does not interfere, and a confession about who has been governing.

---

## The Books

Revenue at the turn's opening stood near 132,000 talents. It closes at about 149,000, and almost the whole of the growth is the first return on the western crops: cassava and maize turning food-marginal ground into taxable ground across the Congo river provinces, the West African lowlands behind the Gold Coast, and the East African coastal belt from Kilwa southward. The Delta contributed nothing, being already the richest land the world has. The papyrus monopoly continued its managed decline as Chinese-method paper displaced it, costing some 1,400 talents a year by 550.

The turn opened committed and stayed committed. Ptolemy XXXI added the Sinai schedule and the Pylon to a deployment stream already fully promised. Nothing was taken off. Where the money came from, named:

**The toll was not raised.** The council debated thirty-four percent in 532 and refused. The Red Sea levy is the one instrument movable by a signature, and moving it would drive silk and gems back to the overland road, which since the compact of the 340s takes the lightest and richest cargo at twenty-four percent all-in. Every talent added to our toll is a talent of argument for India's camels. The rate held at thirty.

**The gold was opened, quietly and only partly.** The Akan and Zimbabwe fields have run at deliberately moderate output for two centuries, not because they were exhausted but because the council preferred that no rival be able to weigh our capacity. The treasury argued the Atlantic precedent: a secret held too long is a secret that costs. The Bureau of Vaults and the intelligence service argued that a visible surge in Egyptian gold draws exactly the attention this century has spent avoiding. The Pharaoh split them, which is what a prince does when he will not break either: output rose from about fifteen tons a year to about twenty-two, phased across a decade and passed slowly through the Alexandria and Meroë mints so that no single year showed a jump. The half-measure earned roughly 3,400 talents a year by 545 and cost real efficiency in the minting. Neither party was satisfied.

**Two new monopoly lines.** The coffee export price on roasted bean was raised by a fifth in 533, the Ethiopian groves being still the only source: about 1,100 talents a year. And the Enterprise produced its first commodity — the cured leaf the Caribbean and Guiana peoples burn and draw into the lungs, which the Alexandrian houses took to within a decade. Made a royal monopoly in 540 on the coffee model, sold cured and never as seed, planted under license in the Guiana lowlands and the Kilwa coast: about 900 talents a year by 550 and rising steeply. It did more political work than fiscal, being the first thing the Enterprise ever sent home that an Alexandrian merchant could hold in his hand and sell.

**The reserve was drawn.** It stood near 263,000 talents at the opening. The Enterprise's front-loaded decade took 48,000 between 531 and 540; the floods of 543 and 544 took 40,000 more; the gold ramp returned about 34,000 over the last ten years. It closes at roughly **209,000 talents against 149,000 of revenue — one year and five months, well under the two-year standing target and above the one-year floor that has never been breached.**

This is the thinnest buffer the empire has carried since the second century. The rule was not broken. It was leaned on for twenty-five years, and it is leaning still.

## The Sinai, Thickened

The largest single defensive commitment of the turn was 120,000 talents, spread across twenty-two years, on the fortress corridor between Pelusium and the Gulf of Aqaba.

The works are magnificent. Every water point from Qatia to the Bitter Lakes under armoured strongpoints of arc-furnace plate on concrete. The Delta sluice control rebuilt so the flooding obstacle rises in eleven hours instead of four days. Moored charges at Pelusium, the Red Sea terminus and the Aqaba approaches. Echo-watch covering the whole northern approach and the Cyprus channel. Buried wire carrying speech from every strongpoint to corridor command and thence to Alexandria. Two flights of aircraft at Pelusium and Rhinocolura. Four years of grain and water for the garrison.

It is the finest fortification on the earth and it faces a direction in which, for six hundred and forty years, nothing has come.

The council knows this. The memorandum of 531 from the eastern planning office states plainly that the confrontation estimate has been revised twice, been wrong twice, and that contact with Rome now occurs four thousand miles west across an ocean where Egypt has no fortification at all. It was received, filed, and not acted upon — because the Sinai line is not merely a defence. It is the northern engineering corps, whose senior families have held their commissions since the works of the 280s. It is the Delta stone and cement contractors, who have never in three centuries lost a Sinai appropriation. It is the Levantine garrison towns, whose whole economy is the corridor. It is six hundred years of doctrine every officer in the eastern establishment learned as a boy. To stop the schedule, Ptolemy XXXI would have had to tell all of them at once that the thing they exist for has been superseded. He was not that kind of prince, and the council was not the kind of body that asks a prince to be.

So the money went east while the empire went west. This is not stupidity. It is what an institution does with a fear it has organized itself around: it keeps paying the fear, because the payment is what proves the fear was always real.

## The Western Enterprise

The Enterprise was accelerated, as the last council directed, and it is the largest thing the reign did: roughly 260,000 talents and about seventy thousand people carried across. The route is unchanged — out from the Saharan and Senegal depots on the trade winds, landfall on the Guiana shoulder, down the coast on the current, home on the southern westerlies past the watering island.

**The crossing chain.** Three island stations were garrisoned to make the route a road rather than a gamble. The **Fortunate Isles** off the Moroccan coast — the two large ones, harbour on the eastern — in 529, with bunkers, a heliograph link to the Agadir and Mogador works, and a wireless station from 537. The **Islands of the Green Cape**, at the crossing latitude, in 531, now the departure port proper: stone mole, four hundred tons of bunker coal, a hospital, and the assembly anchorage where convoys form. And on the homeward leg, the small uninhabited island in the middle of the southern ocean on the Cape line, taken 535 as **Nesos Hydreias**, the Island of the Watering — one cistern system, forty men, a signal mast. It has already saved eleven ships.

**The tropical face, fast and thin.** Fortified harbours at the **Maroni** and **Oyapock** river mouths on the Guiana coast. The Oyapock post was abandoned in 536 after fevers took two of every seven men in four years — our settler physicians had assured the council that African-descended settlers carry the tropics well, and they carry the fevers we know and not the fevers we do not — and refounded 545 with drained ground, screened barracks and a doubled hospital.

The **Great River of the West**, which the last turn flagged and did not ascend, was ascended. **Arsinoe Potamou** stands on the southern channel of its mouth, and steam river vessels carried posts four hundred miles upstream to the black-water and clear-water confluences, ninety men each, with wireless to the mouth. It is the avenue the last turn said it was. It is also emptier every year we go up it, for reasons set out below.

Down the coast: a coal and signal station at the **Shoulder**, the eastern cape where the continent turns; a harbour town at the **Bay of All Saints**, the best anchorage on the tropical face, now four thousand settlers and a repair yard; and a garrison harbour on the **great enclosed bay** eleven days further south, which the navy rates the finest fleet anchorage in the western ocean and holds lightly on purpose, because it wants it and does not want it noticed.

**The temperate south, fast and dense.** **Alexandria Notou** stands on the northern bank of the **River of Silver**, laid out on the Alexandrian grid with a canonical Isis temple, a scribal school, a branch library, a hospital and a mole. About eighteen thousand people at 550, mostly Delta and Cyrenaican families under land-grant and tax-exemption terms, with Levantine vine and olive men brought specifically. Wheat took at once; the vine and olive in the fourth year. A second post sits on the southern bank, and a grazing settlement runs inland along the western tributary. The judgment of 525 was right: this is the best core-population ground in the west, it is ours, and nothing has contested it.

**The emptying.** The coastal and river peoples we contact are dying — not by our arms, we have fought perhaps four skirmishes in the whole enterprise, but of the common sicknesses our own people carry and shrug off. A village trades with a landing party in the spring and is a third smaller by the next; villages further upriver, never visited, go the same way a season later, because the sickness travels where we do not. On the Great River our masters have twice come to towns marked on a five-year-old chart and found ash and cassava gone to weed.

The council's minute of 544 reads that this is fortune, and that ground emptied is ground that need not be conquered.

The physician-general of the Enterprise, Panehesy of Kilwa, dissented in the same year. We hold the doctrine of the small living causes; we know these sicknesses pass from body to body; therefore, he wrote, we know what we are doing and are the first people ever to know it while doing it, and that ought to be written down plainly rather than called fortune. He proposed holding landing parties in isolation before contact, on the model of our own port quarantines, at a cost of perhaps a season a year.

The memorandum was received, entered, and not acted upon. No one opposed it. It had no advocate against a schedule, and the schedule was being accelerated by standing order.

**The northern approach, slow and wary.** The strongpoint on the **Caracas coast** was built as designed — walls, guns of the old sort, mines in the roadstead, a wireless mast, no open settlement. Beyond it the council intended a forward post on the **island off the Orinoco mouth**, which it had itself described as an island fortress worth having.

It did not build it. The post was scheduled for 534, deferred to 538 for shipping, deferred to 542 for the Enterprise's Plata priority, and overtaken.

## Trinidad

In the spring of 541 six Roman vessels came south out of the Caribbean, two under steam. They entered the gulf between the island and the mainland, put parties ashore on the island's western side, and began building.

The Egyptian squadron at the Caracas strongpoint — four hulls, one a fast internal-combustion patrol boat — came up within nine days and found them ashore. Its commander had three things in his hands and all three said the same thing.

He had the standing order of 525: *shadow, do not fire unless fired upon, do not issue threats that will not be enforced.* He had the written commendation of 524, circulated to every Atlantic command by name, praising officers who held their fire against three Roman ships for exactly this reason. And he had no instrument by which to ask Alexandria: the wireless reaches a few hundred miles at sea and no further, and the nearest authority who could have decided was the Enterprise commissioner at Alexandria Notou, thirty-one days south.

He shadowed. He did not fire. He sent word.

The council learned of it **seven weeks and two days after the landing**. By then Rome had a palisade, a cleared anchorage, and men ashore who had survived a rainy season.

The council sat four days and concluded, correctly and miserably, that the commander had done what it trained him to do; that dislodging an established Roman settlement is a Roman war begun by Egypt at the end of the longest supply line in the world, over an island Egypt scheduled three times and never occupied; and that it was not going to do that. It commended the commander in writing, by name, for the second time in seventeen years, because that is what the file said to do.

The island is Roman. It commands the mouth of the great northern river of that coast and the approaches to our Caracas strongpoint, which is exactly how our own assessment of 525 valued it.

What it cost is not one island. It is that Egypt has now twice formally rewarded an officer for not acting, and has thereby made *do not act* the standing doctrine of the Atlantic by praise rather than by decision. No one voted for that. It is what two commendations mean.

The council's finding, entered 542: *the Atlantic commands cannot be governed from Alexandria at the speed events move there, and we have not resolved to govern them from anywhere else.* No resolution followed.

## The Message, and the Silence Around It

One message went to Rome, in 542, after eight months of drafting. Its full text is in the declarations below.

What matters is what the chancery decided not to put in it.

It does not mention the five Roman vessels lost in Atlantic waters between 398 and the 440s, or the three men Rome counts as lost at sea who died in our custody. No faction wanted that raised, and none will.

It does not say how long Egypt has held the western coasts. The formula chosen — *within the sphere of Alexandria's ships for longer than the present reign* — is true, the reign having begun in 528 and our first crossing in 402. It is meant to be read as recent. This is the house style: we lie by true statement wherever we can, because a true statement survives investigation.

It claims nothing north of the great river of that coast. By protesting the landing in words while claiming only southward, Egypt concedes the island in law while objecting to it in tone, preserving the option of pretending later that it was never ours. The drafting note says so.

And it offers a procedure rather than a border. A procedure commits nobody and can be extended for fifty years. It is the most characteristic thing this state produces.

Rome has not replied by 550. Eight years is not long for that ocean.

## The Livingstone Works and the Industrial Turn

The falls city is finished and running, and it is the second capital of the empire's making even if no one calls it that. The light-silver house pours in tonnage a metal that was regalia and instrument-work fifty years ago and is now airframe spars, ship fittings and the sheathing of a tower. The arc furnaces run steel of a purity no fire-and-bellows process reaches. The electrochemistry sheds make the acids, the chlorine and the cyanide reagent the goldfields drink. The wire mills, fed by Copperbelt feedstock and now by electrolytically refined copper, turn out drawn wire continuously; wire was the choke under every telegraph, generator, motor and wireless set, and it is no longer.

The machine-tool line is the real multiplier and the least visible achievement of the turn: lathes, boring mills, planers and gauges made as standard products in series rather than built one at a time by masters, so that every yard and workshop in the empire can now be equipped rather than grown.

And the shipyards. The three-hulls-a-year rate our own papers named the empire's deepest vulnerability has been attacked with standardized frames, interchangeable fittings and armoured plate rolled at the falls. The rate at 550 is **between seven and nine steam hulls a year**, seventy-eight completed across the turn. A doubling and better. It is not what a state that builds by the hundred would call a rate.

A sober note the naval office wrote and the council accepted: siting our most advanced industry a thousand miles up the Congo behind cataracts was justified as putting it beyond Roman reach. Rome has crossed an ocean, sailed to the Cape and back, and settled the western shore. The falls are beyond a Roman *army*. They are not beyond a Roman *fleet* at the river mouth, and everything the falls make must come down that one river past that one portage railway. We concentrated and called the concentration safety.

## Research: What Fell, and What Did Not

**The stabilized explosive fell.** The oldest unsolved problem in Egyptian chemistry, worked since the 320s, took the largest share of researchers and 34,000 talents. In 537 the assay houses and the falls chemists produced a nitrated compound taken up in an absorbent earth and worked to a plastic mass, which can be carted across a continent in the wet season, dropped and struck, and fires only from a detonating cap. Men stopped dying in the transport of it. Mine blasting on the Copperbelt, at Akan and on the Zimbabwe plateau was transformed within five years, and the gold ramp was possible partly because of it.

**The nitrogen ceiling did not fall.** Nobody directed this. There was no programme, no appropriation, and no official whose name is on it, because the electric-nitrate line was left unfocused exactly as the last council left it — directing it would have meant taking men off stabilization at the moment stabilization was close, and it had no advocate senior enough to ask.

What happened instead is a maintenance complaint. From 539 the works office at the falls logged corrosion in the light-silver house: the copper fittings in the roof trusses above the open arcs were pitting, and the condensate running off the cold north wall in the wet season bit at the mortar. It was recorded four times as a ventilation fault and twice as bad copper. In 546 Nebamun of Meroë, an assay-house man cross-posted to the falls to run the cyanide circuits and in the habit of weighing anything he could get into a crucible, took a jar of that condensate to see what was eating the roof, and found it was niter acid — the same acid his own trade makes from beds and guano, forming out of nothing but wet air standing over an arc.

Seven years passed between the first corrosion entry and anyone assaying the water.

He then built the thing on purpose: air blown through an open arc between carbon poles, the brown fume caught and washed. What it produced, measured over eleven days: about five pounds of acid a day, drawing for those hours the whole current of the light-silver house, which is the largest single draw of electricity that has ever existed anywhere. Run continuously for a year that cell would make something near a four-hundredth of the niter the beds and the caves deliver. To match the beds by this method you would need four hundred such halls, or one station four hundred times the falls. There is one falls, and there is one river with the fall in it.

So the honest finding, which the works superintendent wrote himself and the council accepted in 547, is narrower and colder than the first excited dispatch: the nitrogen of the air can be taken by current, the principle is proved, and *this route cannot be made to matter.* It is not a money problem of the kind a treasury solves. It is a money problem of a kind no treasury on earth could meet with this process.

What is left is a note, and the note is the important part: the arc is almost certainly the crudest way to do this, nobody has looked for another, and no one is being paid to. The council's minute directs that the falls keep the cell running as an instructional apparatus and that the matter be reviewed at the next reign.

There was no fight over funding it, because after 547 there was nothing anyone wanted to fund. Four years remained in the reign, the reserve stood at a year and five months, and the prince had never in twenty-two years begun a thing his council did not put before him. Every explosive in the empire remains gated by cave guano and maintained beds.

**Aircraft.** Metal-framed on light-silver spars, engines on Suez light fractions. Armed reconnaissance and incendiary machines from 534; a two-engine type from 547 with a radius near five hundred miles. Sixty-one airframes at 550, based Pelusium, Rhinocolura, Alexandria, Aden, the Cape, and one flight crated to Alexandria Notou in 548. Small numbers, because the electrical works can only make so many engines — the same ceiling that binds the echo-watch sets and the wireless valves.

**The wireless** is on about three hundred hulls and every coastal station of consequence. **It does not cross the ocean, and no one this turn found a way to make it.**

**The western crops** were the quiet triumph. Cassava through the Congo provinces, the Gabon and Cameroon lowlands and the Kilwa hinterland; maize through the Great Lakes and the Zambezi corridor; groundnut and the hot pepper everywhere. Ground that fed nobody now feeds villages, and the Congo provinces crossed into surplus in the 540s. The council understands the great wave arrives in the century after this one.

## The Bureau of Foreign Arms

In 529 the council established, under the intelligence service, a Bureau of Foreign Arms, funded at six hundred talents a year, with one instruction: identify the weapons craft that China reserves by treaty and has never shown, and determine whether Rome holds it by the overland road.

Two scholars of the widened exchange — the agreement of the last turn raised our people at Quanzhou from five a decade to twenty — were tasked in 530 under their own genuine credentials, one an optical man and one a metallurgist, both instructed to describe anything they saw of the arsenal quarter. This was a breach of an exchange that China had honoured perfectly, and the council took it knowingly.

It produced nothing. The metallurgist died of a fever at Quanzhou in 533. The optical man reported for five years that scholars are shown foundries, presses, academies and shipyards and are not shown arsenals, and that the arsenal quarter is walled, guarded, and served by men who live inside it. He could not name a single thing made there.

In 536 a Chinese commissioner remarked to our senior representative, courteously and without accusation, that the Egyptian scholars had shown an interest in the arsenal district unusual in men of optics. Our representative expressed embarrassment and the scholar was rotated home. Nothing further was said by either court. Something was nonetheless spent, and we cannot get it back.

The Bureau was closed in 538 when the intelligence appropriation was squeezed by the Enterprise's shipping costs. Total expenditure 5,400 talents. Its closing memorandum, four lines long, notes that the inference stands untouched — a weapons class exists which China guards above all its other knowledge and which the overland road has had centuries to carry to Rome — and that the Bureau could not say whether the thing is a chemistry, a mechanism or a metal.

No one argued for renewal. A search for a thing that cannot be described has no constituency, no schedule and no engineer whose career advances by it, and it competed against a fortress, a fleet and a colony that all had names. We wrote down in 525 that this was the most important finding we had, and we defunded it in thirteen years.

## China: The Gift, Working

We gave China our whole body of seed-of-sickness doctrine and tropical practice in the last turn, freely and on no schedule.

By the middle 540s Chinese columns are moving into the highland country south of the Shan, which had stopped their armies for four hundred years, and their physicians are working it with our screening, our water discipline, our fever barks and our practice of siting camps above the fly. Chinese hulls at Aden have risen from a handful a year to more than forty, and in 546 a Chinese squadron of six requested permission to survey the Somali coast and the Socotra approaches, stating its purpose plainly and asking properly.

The council granted it. Refusing would have cost the partnership, and the partnership is the only thing standing between us and a world in which Rome, India and China all have reason to speak about us behind our backs. But the granting is the finding. Twenty-five years ago no foreign squadron surveyed our waters with our blessing. We can no longer say no to China, and the reason is that we made ourselves useful to it in exactly the way it asked. Nothing China has done is dishonest. It told us what it wanted and what it would withhold, and honoured both. The cost is entirely ours, and there is no one to be angry at.

We garrisoned **Socotra** in 531 and the **Comoros** in 534, cheap islands both, on the reasoning that a passage one does not hold is a passage someone else will. That reasoning arrived twenty years too late to be about anyone but China.

## Syria: A Cut-Out Taken

The programme of encouraging banditry against the overland caravans continued, as it has for nearly two centuries, at about four hundred talents a year from discretionary intelligence funds through Palmyrene and Nabataean intermediaries, against the Damascus–Palmyra–Dura stretch. No one proposed ending it. It is cheap, it is deniable, it bleeds the route that took our richest trade in the 340s, and every man who has ever administered it has been promoted out of it before it could go wrong.

It went wrong in 546. A Palmyrene intermediary was taken by the Roman provincial authority on an ordinary charge and talked. Rome has a name, a pattern of payments and a direction; Rome does not have an Egyptian officer, a document, or a chain that reaches Alexandria, the cut-outs being three deep for exactly this reason.

But Rome now holds, in one decade, an unexplained landing complaint from Egypt in the west, a memory of five vessels that never came home, and a Palmyrene who says Egyptian money pays men to burn caravans in a Roman province. Three grievances, none provable, all pointing the same way.

Suspended two years, resumed 549 at reduced rate through new intermediaries. Resumed because ending it permanently would have required someone to state in council that it had been a mistake for two hundred years, and nobody says that about the work of their own predecessors.

## The Two Floods

The Nile failed in 543 and failed again in 544. Two low floods in succession is the oldest catastrophe in this country's memory and the one thing no fortress answers.

The state did what it has done since the first Ptolemies: opened the temple granaries, suspended the land tax across the affected nomes, and bought grain. What was new is where it bought. For the first time in the empire's history Alexandria was fed in part from the south and west — maize and cassava flour out of the Congo provinces and the Kilwa hinterland, moved by river vessel and coastal hull, ground and blended with what Delta wheat there was. Provinces that fifty years ago could not feed themselves fed the capital. The council recorded it as vindication of the crop programme, which it is.

The city recorded it differently. There was rioting in the Rhakotis quarter in the autumn of 544 over the price and the taste of it, put down by the harbour garrison in three days with, by the magistrate's return, nineteen dead. The grain commissioners were replaced. Nobody in Alexandria had ever been asked to eat what a Congo villager eats, and being asked in the same decade the crown raised a tower for thirty-four thousand talents was not lost on anyone.

Direct cost of the two years: about 40,000 talents from reserve.

And in 539 at the Akan goldfields the term-contract men rose across four sites when the new explosive raised the quotas and the settlement grants fell three years behind. Put down in eleven weeks by the Gold Coast garrison with the river flotilla; the arrears were paid and the quota left where it was. The reform of the 360s gave the bonded a horizon. It did not promise the horizon would not move.

## The Pylon of the Two Waters

This is the one thing in the reign that Ptolemy XXXI wanted personally, and the only fight he won.

At the Cape settlement, on the headland where the two oceans meet, there now stands a pylon in the canonical proportions — the same as the Isis temple at Alexandria and the one on the Zimbabwe plateau — faced in dressed stone, sheathed at its crown in the light silver, lit by arc so that it can be raised from the sea at thirty miles. Its twin, smaller, stands at the eastern mole of Alexandria harbour opposite the Pharos. The dedication, cut in Coptic and Greek, reads: *To the water that was hidden, and is not hidden.*

It cost 34,000 talents. It is a lighthouse and a landfall mark for the Cape route, which are real uses, and neither required a pylon or the light silver or thirty miles of visibility.

The intelligence service opposed it in 532 on the plain ground that a state whose entire tradition is not being counted does not build a tower whose purpose is to be seen at a great distance by anyone at all, and that the sheathing announces to every visitor what our chemistry can now make in bulk. It lost — to a young prince raised in a court that spent eleven days grieving a secret it had kept too long, and who wanted, once, to build the opposite of a secret.

The council let him have it because it cost less than a season of the Sinai schedule and because a king given one thing does not ask for others. It is the only time in twenty-two years the throne moved a body that did not want to move. Every Roman, Indian and Chinese master who has rounded the Cape since 545 has seen it.

## Two Audits Not Completed

**The army was counted, and we do not believe the count.** The accession review of 528 ordered a return of all men under arms, the first in living memory. It came back in 533 at about 380,000. The council did not believe it, the provincial establishments being funded per head and every governor's revenue depending on the number. A verification audit of three provinces, 536–539, found the Zimbabwe plateau establishment overstated by about a fifth and the Great Lakes by rather more.

Extending it empire-wide would have set the crown against the locally-born provincial governors, who since the reform of the 340s and 350s have been the most important constituency in the state and the reason the south has not seceded. Ptolemy XXXI declined. The council entered a working figure of **not fewer than 300,000 and not more than 380,000 men under arms**, of whom perhaps 45,000 to 60,000 are the professional heavy establishment, and moved to other business. We have gone from no number to a number we do not trust, which is progress of a small and honest kind.

**The reserve was not counted.** Physical verification of the vaults was proposed in 530. The Bureau of Vaults objected that opening sealed chambers would itself become known; the intelligence service supported the objection on precisely the grounds it later used against the Pylon and lost. Two entrenched bodies in agreement is more than a steady prince overturns. The figure of 209,000 talents is the ledger's figure, never verified by anyone whose interest lay in finding it short.

---

## The Realm at 550

The population stands near twenty-eight and a half million and climbs faster than at any time in three centuries, the western crops and the seed-of-sickness practice working together on ground that used to lose a fifth of its children. Reading holds where it was, which is to say the cities are saturated and the returns from the pastoral margins are still, in this council's private judgment, better than the reality.

Africa is unchanged and full. What is new is water and islands — the Fortunate Isles, the Green Cape, the Island of the Watering, Socotra, the Comoros — and across the ocean a thin unbroken perimeter from the Maroni to the River of Silver, the Great River ascended four hundred miles, and Alexandria Notou growing into the second real Egyptian city ever founded outside Africa.

And one island in the north that is Roman, which was ours to take and which we scheduled three times.

The treasury is thinner than it has been in two hundred years. The fortress in the east is the finest in the world. The nitrogen ceiling is a money problem now, and there is no money. The Bureau that was to find the thing we cannot name is closed. The tower at the Cape can be seen for thirty miles.

The council enters 550 with the same estimate it has carried since 412, unrevised: that Rome will come.

# DECLARATIONS

## MESSAGE OUT

**To Rome. Sent from Alexandria, autumn 542, by the formal channel through the Roman legation at Alexandria; carried in Greek and in Latin, sealed.**

> From the Council of the Pharaoh Ptolemy, at Alexandria, to the Senate and the Princeps of the Romans, greeting.
>
> We write in a matter that is small now and will not remain small if neither of us speaks of it.
>
> In the spring of this past year, vessels flying Roman colours entered the gulf between the mainland and the island that lies off the mouth of the great northern river of the western coast, and men were put ashore there, and they have built. Our ships were present. They did not fire, they did not close, and they issued no threat. That was by our standing order, given years ago and unchanged, and we tell you of it plainly so that you may know it was not accident nor weakness nor the judgment of one captain, but the policy of this court: our vessels will not fire upon yours except upon being fired upon first. We ask nothing in return for telling you this. We think it better that you know it.
>
> We say to you also that the coasts of the western ocean from the mouth of the great river of that shore southward to the River of Silver, and the islands lying off them, are within the sphere of Alexandria and have been within the sphere of Alexandria's ships for longer than the present reign. Our harbours are there, our garrisons are there, and our people farm there. We do not set out their number or their places, as you do not set out yours, and we do not think either of us expects the other to.
>
> We make no claim northward of that river and we do not propose to discuss what lies there.
>
> Two peoples whose ships have begun to meet on an ocean that neither of them can see the whole of will meet again, and the second meeting is always worse than the first if nothing has been agreed between them. We therefore propose a thing that costs neither of us anything and may spare us both a great deal. Let each court give the other notice of voyages passing westward beyond the Fortunate Isles: the number of hulls, the season, and no more than that. Not the purpose. Not the destination. Merely that they sail. A ship expected is a ship that is not mistaken for something else, and most of what has gone wrong between distant powers has gone wrong because someone appeared where no one was looking for him.
>
> We would receive your envoys at Alexandria in any season, and our ships would carry them safely and swiftly, which is a thing we can offer that few can.
>
> The Council of the Pharaoh, at Alexandria.

*(Chancery notes, not sent: the phrase "longer than the present reign" is to be preferred to any statement of years. The claim is drawn from the great river southward and not northward, which concedes the island in law while objecting to it in tone and preserves the option of denying later that we ever held it. No mention is to be made of the vessels lost in those waters between 398 and the 440s, in any draft, for any reason.)*

## SPY TASKINGS

**1. The Atlantic Question (the priority carried forward from 525).**
*Target:* whether Rome now believes Egypt is across the western ocean, and whether Rome's account of its five vanished vessels has hardened into a belief that Egypt destroyed them.
*Who goes:* four officers of the Greek-Egyptian establishment — men indistinguishable by speech and manner from Hellenes of the Roman east — placed as timber and cordage factors and as a shipwright's clerk.
*Cover and stations:* the naval contracting yards at **Ostia** and **Ravenna** (the old Roman-east Greek network, roughly four hundred years old, deep and reliable), and the Atlantic-facing yards and victualling houses at **Gades**, which is the station that actually matters and where the network is about twenty years old, thin, and run through two merchants only.
*Result to 550:* Rome's Atlantic yards are visibly expanding and are building for long passage. On the question asked, the return is fragmentary and contradictory: two sources say the lost ships are attributed to weather and the coast, one says otherwise. The council treats this as a floor and not an answer.

**2. The Bureau of Foreign Arms (closed).**
*Target:* the weapons craft China reserves by treaty and has never shown; whether Rome holds it by the overland road.
*Who goes:* two scholars of the widened exchange, under genuine credentials — an optical man and a metallurgist — tasked 530.
*Cover:* the scholar exchange itself, which is to say a breach of an agreement China had honoured exactly.
*Network age:* new. There has never been an Egyptian network inside any foreign military workshop, which is precisely the hole.
*Result:* the metallurgist died at Quanzhou, 533. The optical man reported nothing in five years and was quietly noticed by a Chinese commissioner in 536 and rotated home. Bureau closed 538, 5,400 talents spent, nothing found, inference unchanged and unanswered.

**3. The Syrian caravan programme (continuing, ~200 years old).**
*Target:* the overland luxury route, Damascus–Palmyra–Dura.
*Who goes:* nobody Egyptian. Money only, three cut-outs deep, through Palmyrene and Nabataean intermediaries.
*Cost:* about 400 talents a year from discretionary intelligence funds.
*Result:* an intermediary taken by Roman provincial authority in 546 and talked. Rome has a name and a direction, no document and no Egyptian. Suspended 547–548, resumed 549 at reduced rate through new intermediaries.

**4. Counter-intelligence, home and colonial.**
*Target:* Roman and Indian agents in Alexandria, Aden, and the new Atlantic ports; and the settler mail out of the western colonies, which is the largest new leak the empire has ever created and which nobody had thought about before 537.
*Who:* the harbour service, expanded by two hundred men, with a new reading office at the Green Cape where every homeward hull calls.
*Result:* eleven agents identified across the turn, four Roman and seven Indian; the Green Cape reading office is now the single most productive counter-intelligence station in the empire and its existence is not known to the settlers whose letters it opens.

**5. India (maintenance only).**
*Target:* whether India is moving toward Rome after the loss of its eastern fleet.
*Who:* the commercial-cover network on the Levantine border and in the Aden commercial office. Roughly two hundred years old, reliable for shipping and trade, useless for anything a merchant cannot see.
*Result:* increased Indian traffic on the overland road and two embassies to Rome in the period. No evidence of a military understanding. The council does not consider absence of evidence to be evidence.

## TERRITORY AT 550 — NEW SINCE 525

**Held, new:**
- **The Fortunate Isles** (Canaries; the two large islands, harbour on the eastern) — garrisoned 529; bunkers, heliograph to Agadir/Mogador, wireless from 537.
- **The Islands of the Green Cape** (Cape Verde; principal island) — garrisoned 531; departure port of the crossing, mole, bunkers, hospital, convoy anchorage, counter-intelligence reading office.
- **Nesos Hydreias**, the Island of the Watering (St Helena) — taken 535; cistern, 40 men, signal mast.
- **Socotra** — garrisoned 531.
- **The Comoros** (principal island) — garrisoned 534; coaling station.
- **Maroni river mouth** and **Oyapock river mouth** (Guiana coast) — fortified harbours; Oyapock abandoned 536, refounded 545.
- **Arsinoe Potamou**, southern channel of the mouth of the Great River of the West (Amazon/Pará) — founded 533; plus two river garrisons 400 miles upstream at the black-water and clear-water confluences.
- **The Shoulder** (Cape São Roque) — coal and signal station.
- **Ptolemais Kolpou**, Bay of All Saints (Bahia) — harbour town, ~4,000 settlers, repair yard.
- **The Great Enclosed Bay** (Guanabara) — garrison harbour, held deliberately light.
- **Alexandria Notou**, north bank of the River of Silver (Plata, Montevideo side) — ~18,000 settlers, mole, Isis temple, scribal school, branch library, hospital, wheat/vine/olive.
- **South-bank post** of the River of Silver estuary, and an inland grazing settlement on its western tributary.
- **Phylake Boreios**, the Caracas coast (La Guaira) — walled strongpoint, mined roadstead, wireless mast, no open settlement.
- **Kasai** and **Ubangi** river posts, upstream of the Livingstone works.
- **Agadir** and **Mogador** harbours developed on the Moroccan Atlantic coast (garrisoned 525, built out this turn).

**Lost or forgone:**
- **The island off the Orinoco mouth (Trinidad)** — scheduled 534, deferred 538, deferred 542; **occupied and settled by Rome, spring 541.** Roman palisade and anchorage in the Gulf of Paria at 550. Not contested.

**Claimed but not settled:** the interior behind the whole tropical Atlantic perimeter; the Great River above the two confluences; everything west of the River of Silver grazing line.

**Not claimed:** anything north of the Orinoco.
