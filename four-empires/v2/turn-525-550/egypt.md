# Egypt: The Turn 525–550 AD

## The Reign of Ptolemy XXXI Nesiaset

The old Pharaoh, who had sat eleven days in the Atlantic council and wept at the end of it, died in the winter of 527. His son acceded in the spring of 528, aged thirty-four, and took the name Nesiaset, He-Belongs-to-Isis.

The chronicle of his reign is short, because little in it is his. He confirmed the standing council entire within a month, which was the customary act and also the whole of his programme. He read the Atlantic file and did not reopen it. He signed the continuation of the Western Enterprise, the Livingstone works, the Sinai schedule, the industrial commissions and the research slate, each in the form his father's council had drafted, and in twenty-two years cancelled nothing already funded and raised nothing that had not already been asked for.

Twice he was asked to break a constituency and twice he declined. Once he spent his own capital, and he spent it on a tower.

The council's assessment of him, entered without malice in 545: *a steady prince, who has lost nothing that was given him.* That is the highest praise a permanent council gives a king who does not interfere, and a confession about who has been governing.

The reign is unremarkable. The state under it is not. A treasury of a hundred and fifty thousand talents a year does not become smaller because the man on the throne is ordinary; it goes on doing, at its funded rate, everything it was already doing, which over twenty-five years is a great deal.

## TECHNOLOGY

### What Egypt has at 550 that it did not have at 525

**Stabilized transportable explosive — achieved 537.** *Programme: the Assay Houses and the Falls Chemical Sheds. 34,000 talents across the turn, the largest single research appropriation and the largest body of researchers on any one question.* The oldest unsolved problem in Egyptian chemistry, worked since the 320s. The answer was a nitrated compound taken up in an absorbent earth and worked to a plastic mass, which can be carted across a continent in the wet season, dropped, struck, and fires only from a detonating cap. Men stopped dying in the transport of it. Mine blasting on the Copperbelt, at Akan and on the Zimbabwe plateau was transformed within five years.

**Nitrogen fixed from the air, at a fifth of the empire's supply — achieved 546, rising.** *Programme: the Electric-Nitrate Line, made a directed programme with its own appropriation in 529 at about 1,800 talents a year, 45,000 across the turn, under Nebamun of Meroë, an assay-house man cross-posted to the falls.* The full account is below. In short: the arc route was proved and found uneconomic; the carbide route was found instead and works. The nitrogen ceiling that has gated every Egyptian explosive since the 320s is not broken. It is lifted, for the first cost anyone has been able to pay.

**Bulk light silver — achieved, in tonnage.** The falls house pours a metal that was regalia and instrument-work fifty years ago and is now airframe spars, ship and machinery fittings, instrument frames and the sheathing of a tower. *Within the Livingstone works appropriation.*

**Metal-framed powered aircraft, armed — achieved 534; two-engine, 500-mile radius, 547.** *Programme: the Naval and Frontier Air Establishment, 21,000 talents.* Sixty-one airframes at 550, based at Pelusium, Rhinocolura, Alexandria, Aden, the Cape, and one flight crated to Alexandria Notou in 548. Small numbers, and the reason is not money: the electrical works can only turn out so many engines and so many valves, and the same ceiling binds the echo-watch sets and the wireless.

**Ship armour of arc-furnace plate — achieved, standard on all new construction from 530.** *Within the yards' appropriation.*

**Moored charges — achieved and laid** at Pelusium, the Red Sea terminus, Aqaba, Cyprus, Aden, the Green Cape, Alexandria Notou, and the Maroni and Great River mouths. *6,000 talents.*

**Machine tools as series products — achieved.** *26,000 talents.* Lathes, boring mills, planers and gauges made as standard goods at the falls and Alexandria rather than built one at a time by masters. The least visible achievement of the turn and the true multiplier: every yard and workshop in the empire can now be equipped rather than grown.

**Continuous drawn wire and electrolytically refined copper — achieved.** *22,000 talents.* Wire was the choke under every telegraph, generator, motor and wireless set. It is no longer a choke under anything.

**Shipbuilding rate raised from three hulls a year to between seven and nine — partial.** *48,000 talents with the yards.* Seventy-eight armoured steam hulls completed across the turn. A doubling and better against the number our own papers named the empire's deepest vulnerability. It is still not what a state that builds by the hundred would call a rate.

**A propellant, and the first pieces that use it — begun 540, at trial only.** *Programme: the Ordnance Bureau, 9,000 talents across ten years.* Set out below with the Bureau of Foreign Arms, because the two are the same question asked from opposite ends.

**Acetylene, and light from it** — a by-product of the carbide furnaces, now lighting the falls works and the deep galleries at Akan, and being tried against metal as a cutting flame.

**Western crops in provincial cultivation — achieved, first-rank.** *8,000 talents at the botanical stations.* Cassava through the Congo provinces, the Gabon and Cameroon lowlands and the Kilwa hinterland; maize through the Great Lakes and the Zambezi corridor; groundnut and the hot pepper everywhere. Ground that fed nobody now feeds villages, and the Congo provinces crossed into grain surplus in the 540s. The council understands that the great wave arrives in the century after this one and that it is watching only the edge of it.

### What remains unachieved at 550

- **Wireless across the ocean.** The sets reach a few hundred miles at sea and no further. No route to more was found and none is presently being sought. This is the single largest practical limit on the Western Enterprise: the Atlantic commands cannot be governed from Alexandria at the speed events move there, and the council has resolved nothing about governing them from anywhere else. *17,000 talents on wireless and valve manufacture, all of it spent on making more of what we have rather than on making it reach further.*
- **Nitrogen without limit.** The carbide route lifts the ceiling; it does not remove it. The route the chemists can now describe and cannot build — combining the air's nitrogen directly with hydrogen under great pressure over some substance that hastens it — wants pressure vessels beyond our present proofing and a knowledge of hastening substances we do not have. It is written up, named, and left to the next reign with a recommendation that it be funded.
- **Ordnance in any quantity.** We have a propellant and eleven pieces. We do not have a manufactory, a gunner corps, a doctrine, or a single formation trained to fight with them.
- **The thing China does not show.** Unnamed at the end of twenty-five years as at the beginning. See below.
- **Mass production of anything electrical.** Engines, valves and echo-watch sets are the ceiling on aircraft, on wireless and on radar alike, and the turn's industrial works raised the ceiling without removing it.
- **The interior of the western landmass.** Ascended four hundred miles up one river. Nothing else.
- **A land army tested against a peer.** Unchanged since Raphia's grandchildren.

## PEOPLE

**Population: about twenty-nine and a half million at 550**, against twenty-five to twenty-six at 525. *Basis:* the standing rate of growth across the last three centuries has run near half a percent a year; the crop programme and the seed-of-sickness practice together have raised it to something nearer seven-tenths in the provinces where both have arrived, which is most of the tropical south and none of the Delta. Twenty-five years at that blended rate gives between twenty-nine and thirty million. The figure is a projection from the last full census and the annual nome returns, not a count; the last true census was 202–210 AD, and the council notes without acting on it that this is now a three-hundred-year-old foundation.

**Funded researchers and engineers: between thirty and forty-five thousand.** *Basis:* the 500 figure was twenty to thirty-five thousand across every budget — mining, naval, medical, military and the Library establishment together. The research and schools line has grown with revenue, from about six thousand talents a year to about nine and a half thousand. A funded scholar runs at roughly three times a skilled labourer's keep, housed and fed rather than paid a wage, so the headcount the line carries is large and the money is dominated not by men but by consumables and failed apparatus. Thirty to forty-five thousand is the same proportion of a larger population, not a new commitment.

**The literate technical class: between seven hundred thousand and one million.** *Basis:* physicians, engineers, navigators, surveyors, instrument-makers, mine masters, foundry masters, scribes of the technical bureaux — the men who generate knowledge as a by-product of paid work and write it down where it reaches Alexandria. This is the wider body that the funded establishment sits on top of, and it grows with population, schooling and the new works together. It is between two and three in every hundred of the whole population, which is where it has stood since the fourth century.

**Reading** holds where it was. The cities are saturated. The council's private judgment, entered in the assessment of 525 and not revised, is that the empire-wide returns are better than the reality, because governors are advanced on the number and nobody has audited it against a task the subject was not coached for. No audit was ordered this turn. Nobody proposed one.

**The bonded population** is unchanged in kind: term contracts in the mines with settlement grants at the end, chattel labour untouched on estate land. At the Akan goldfields in 539 the term-contract men rose across four sites, when the new explosive raised the quotas and the settlement grants fell three years behind. It was put down in eleven weeks by the Gold Coast garrison with the river flotilla. The arrears were paid and the quota was left where it was. The reform of the 360s gave the bonded a horizon; it did not promise the horizon would not move, and this is the first time the empire has learned that a technical improvement in the mines arrives at the rock face as a demand.

## MONEY

Egypt is the wealthiest state in the world and spends nearly all of what it takes in. That is its ordinary condition and not a strain.

**Revenue.** 132,000 talents at the turn's opening. **About 152,000 at its close.** The growth, named:

- The western crops, turning food-marginal ground into taxable ground across the Congo river provinces, the West African lowlands behind the Gold Coast, and the East African coastal belt from Kilwa southward. This is the largest single share of the growth. The Delta contributed nothing, being already the richest land the world has.
- **The gold, opened partly.** The Akan and Zimbabwe fields have run at deliberately moderate output for two centuries, not from exhaustion but because the council preferred that no rival be able to weigh our capacity. The treasury argued the Atlantic precedent — a secret held too long is a secret that costs. The Bureau of Vaults and the intelligence service argued that a visible surge draws exactly the attention this century has spent avoiding. The Pharaoh split them: output rose from about fifteen tons a year to about twenty-two, phased across a decade and passed slowly through the Alexandria and Meroë mints so no single year showed a jump. About 3,400 talents a year by 545. Neither party was satisfied, which is what a split earns.
- **Two monopoly lines.** The coffee export price on roasted bean raised by a fifth in 533, the Ethiopian groves being still the only source: about 1,100 talents a year. And the Enterprise's first commodity — the cured leaf the western coastal peoples burn and draw into the lungs, which the Alexandrian houses took to within a decade. Made a royal monopoly in 540 on the coffee model, sold cured and never as seed, planted under licence in the Guiana lowlands and on the Kilwa coast: about 900 talents a year by 550 and rising steeply. It did more political work than fiscal, being the first thing the Enterprise ever sent home that an Alexandrian merchant could hold in his hand and sell.
- Canal and Red Sea tolls, broadly level, carrying more bulk at the same published rate.

**The toll was not raised.** The council debated thirty-four percent in 532 and refused. The Red Sea levy is the one instrument movable by a signature, and moving it would drive silk and gems back to the overland road, which since the compact of the 340s takes the lightest and richest cargo at twenty-four percent all-in. Every talent added to our toll is a talent of argument for the overland camels. The rate held at thirty, published and non-preferential, as it has since 376.

**The reserve.** 263,000 talents at opening. It absorbed the Enterprise's front-loaded decade, was topped up at the maintenance rate throughout, and **closes at about 296,000 against 152,000 of revenue — one year and eleven months, against a two-year standing target and a one-year floor that has never been breached.** It is at target, and the reason it is at target is that the empire made more money this turn than it did last.

**What is genuinely free.** Of the deployment stream at 550, roughly **six to eight thousand talents a year is uncommitted** — money the council could point at something new tomorrow without stopping anything already running. Behind that stands **about 145,000 talents of one-time capital** available by drawing the reserve to its one-year floor, which the council will not touch short of war or catastrophe. We are cash-rich and slack-poor, which is what a state looks like when it has been spending its whole income on purpose for four hundred years.

## The Sinai, Thickened

The largest single defensive commitment of the turn was 120,000 talents, spread across twenty-two years, on the fortress corridor between Pelusium and the Gulf of Aqaba.

The works are magnificent. Every water point from Qatia to the Bitter Lakes under armoured strongpoints of arc-furnace plate on concrete. The Delta sluice control rebuilt so the flooding obstacle rises in eleven hours instead of four days. Moored charges at Pelusium, the Red Sea terminus and the Aqaba approaches. Echo-watch covering the whole northern approach and the Cyprus channel. Buried wire carrying speech from every strongpoint to corridor command and thence to Alexandria. Two flights of aircraft at Pelusium and Rhinocolura. Four years of grain and water for the garrison.

It is the finest fortification on the earth and it faces a direction from which no army has come in six hundred years.

The council knows this. The memorandum of 531 from the eastern planning office states plainly that the confrontation estimate has been revised twice and been wrong twice, and that whatever contact now occurs with Rome occurs across an ocean where Egypt has no fortification at all. It was received, filed, and not acted upon — because the Sinai line is not merely a defence. It is the northern engineering corps, whose senior families have held their commissions since the works of the 280s. It is the Delta stone and cement contractors, who have never in three centuries lost a Sinai appropriation. It is the Levantine garrison towns, whose whole economy is the corridor. It is six hundred years of doctrine every officer in the eastern establishment learned as a boy. To stop the schedule, Ptolemy XXXI would have had to tell all of them at once that the thing they exist for has been superseded. He was not that kind of prince, and the council was not the kind of body that asks a prince to be.

So the money went east while the empire went west. This is not stupidity. It is what an institution does with a fear it has organized itself around: it keeps paying the fear, because the payment is what proves the fear was always real.

## The Western Enterprise

The Enterprise was accelerated, as the last council directed, and it is the largest thing the reign did: roughly 260,000 talents and about seventy thousand people carried across. The route is unchanged — out from the Saharan and Senegal depots on the trade winds, landfall on the Guiana shoulder, down the coast on the current, home on the southern westerlies past the watering island.

**The crossing chain.** Three island stations were garrisoned to make the route a road rather than a gamble.

- The **Fortunate Isles** off the Moroccan coast — the two large ones, harbour on the eastern — in 529, with bunkers, a heliograph link to the Agadir and Mogador works, and a wireless station from 537.
- The **Islands of the Green Cape**, at the crossing latitude, in 531, now the departure port proper: stone mole, four hundred tons of bunker coal, a hospital, and the assembly anchorage where convoys form.
- On the homeward leg, the small uninhabited island in the middle of the southern ocean on the Cape line, taken 535 as **Nesos Hydreias**, the Island of the Watering — one cistern system, forty men, a signal mast. It has already saved eleven ships.

**The tropical face, fast and thin.** Fortified harbours at the **Maroni** and **Oyapock** river mouths on the Guiana coast. The Oyapock post was abandoned in 536 after fevers took two of every seven men in four years — our settler physicians had assured the council that African-descended settlers carry the tropics well, and they carry the fevers we know and not the fevers we do not — and refounded in 545 with drained ground, screened barracks and a doubled hospital.

The **Great River of the West**, which the last turn flagged and did not ascend, was ascended. **Arsinoe Potamou** stands on the southern channel of its mouth, and steam river vessels carried posts four hundred miles upstream to the black-water and clear-water confluences, ninety men each, with wireless to the mouth. It is the avenue the last turn said it was.

Down the coast: a coal and signal station at the **Shoulder**, the eastern cape where the continent turns; a harbour town at the **Bay of All Saints**, the best anchorage on the tropical face, now four thousand settlers and a repair yard; and a garrison harbour on the **great enclosed bay** eleven days further south, which the navy rates the finest fleet anchorage in the western ocean and holds lightly on purpose, because it wants it and does not want it noticed.

**The northern approach, slow and deliberate.** The strongpoint on the **Caracas coast** was built as designed — walls, mined roadstead, wireless mast, no open settlement. And the forward post the last council described as an island fortress worth having was, after three deferrals for shipping and for the Plata priority, actually built: **the island off the mouth of the Orinoco was occupied in 543** and is held as a strongpoint on the Caracas model, with a battery commanding the gulf, a coaling wharf, a wireless mast, a cistern, and a garrison of four hundred. There was nobody there to contest it and no settlement is intended. It is the northernmost thing Egypt holds in the western ocean, and the council chose it as the northern limit precisely because it wanted a place where a limit could be pointed at.

**The temperate south, fast and dense.** **Alexandria Notou** stands on the northern bank of the **River of Silver**, laid out on the Alexandrian grid with a canonical Isis temple, a scribal school, a branch library, a hospital and a mole. About eighteen thousand people at 550, mostly Delta and Cyrenaican families under land-grant and tax-exemption terms, with Levantine vine and olive men brought specifically. Wheat took at once; the vine and olive in the fourth year. A second post sits on the southern bank, and a grazing settlement runs inland along the western tributary. The judgment of 525 was right: this is the best core-population ground in the west, it is ours, and nothing has contested it. The council will pay past all reason to make it Alexandrian, and did.

**The emptying.** The coastal and river peoples we contact are dying — not by our arms, we have fought perhaps four skirmishes in the whole enterprise, but of the common sicknesses our own people carry and shrug off. A village trades with a landing party in the spring and is a third smaller by the next; villages further upriver, never visited, go the same way a season later, because the sickness travels where we do not. On the Great River our masters have twice come to towns marked on a five-year-old chart and found ash and cassava gone to weed.

The council's minute of 544 reads that this is fortune, and that ground emptied is ground that need not be conquered.

The physician-general of the Enterprise, Panehesy of Kilwa, dissented in the same year. We hold the doctrine of the small living causes; we know these sicknesses pass from body to body; therefore, he wrote, we know what we are doing, and we are the first people in the world ever to know it while doing it, and that ought to be written down plainly rather than called fortune. He proposed holding landing parties in isolation before contact, on the model of our own port quarantines, at a cost of perhaps a season a year.

The memorandum was received, entered, and not acted upon. No one opposed it. It had no advocate against a schedule, and the schedule was being accelerated by standing order.

**What Egypt does not know about the western ocean.** It does not know whether any other power is in these waters. It has the old report, held with confidence from several mouths, that Rome crossed the western ocean before 525 and brought back gold, crops and beasts; it has no report of a Roman hull anywhere in the western ocean in the twenty-five years since, because it has never seen one and has sent nobody north of the Orinoco to look. It has never charted the Caribbean. It does not know whether Rome has one ship in the west or two thousand, or whether the crossing was ever repeated. The doctrine forbids our hulls from entering waters where an intrusion would have to be treated as an invasion, and no order was ever drafted distinguishing a survey from an intrusion, so no survey was made. The Enterprise commissioner at Alexandria Notou minuted in 546 that this is a hole and asked leave to send one fast unarmed hull north at distance. The minute was filed. No leave was given and none was refused; the request was simply never answered, which in this service is the same as a refusal and costs nobody a decision.

The council's own finding, entered 542: *the Atlantic commands cannot be governed from Alexandria at the speed events move there, and we have not resolved to govern them from anywhere else.* No resolution followed. It is the plainest thing in the file and it is the thing nobody was made responsible for.

## The Livingstone Works and the Industrial Turn

The falls city is finished and running, and it is the second capital of the empire's making even if no one calls it that.

The light-silver house pours in tonnage a metal that was regalia fifty years ago. The arc furnaces run steel of a purity no fire-and-bellows process reaches. The electrochemistry sheds make the acids, the chlorine and the cyanide reagent the goldfields drink, and now the carbide and the cyanamide. The wire mills, fed by Copperbelt feedstock and now by electrolytically refined copper, turn out drawn wire continuously.

The machine-tool line is the real multiplier and the least visible achievement of the turn: lathes, boring mills, planers and gauges made as standard products in series rather than built one at a time by masters, so that every yard and workshop in the empire can now be equipped rather than grown.

And the shipyards. The three-hulls-a-year rate our own papers named the empire's deepest vulnerability has been attacked with standardized frames, interchangeable fittings and armoured plate rolled at the falls. The rate at 550 is **between seven and nine steam hulls a year**, seventy-eight completed across the turn. A doubling and better.

A sober note the naval office wrote and the council accepted: siting our most advanced industry a thousand miles up the Congo behind cataracts was justified as putting it beyond Roman reach. That justification was written about a Rome that had not yet sailed the length of our Atlantic coast, and it has not been re-examined since. The falls are beyond an army. They are not beyond a fleet at the river mouth, and everything the falls make must come down that one river past that one portage railway. We concentrated and called the concentration safety. Nothing was done about it this turn.

## The Nitrogen Line

The last council left the electric-nitrate work unfocused, as a possible by-product of having at the falls more concentrated current in one place than has ever existed anywhere. This council made it a programme in 529, with an appropriation of about eighteen hundred talents a year and a director — Nebamun of Meroë, an assay-house man cross-posted to the falls for the cyanide circuits and in the habit of weighing anything he could get into a crucible.

**The arc route, proved and rejected.** Nebamun began where the works records pointed him: the light-silver house had been logging corrosion since 526 in the copper roof fittings above the open arcs and in the mortar of the cold north wall, entered four times as a ventilation fault and twice as bad copper. He assayed the condensate in 531 and found it was niter acid, forming out of nothing but wet air standing over an arc. He then built the thing on purpose — air blown through an open arc between carbon poles, the brown fume caught and washed.

Measured over eleven days in 534: about five pounds of acid a day, drawing for those hours the whole current of the light-silver house. Run continuously for a year, that cell would make something near a four-hundredth of the niter the beds and the caves deliver. To match the beds by this route you would need four hundred such halls, or one station four hundred times the falls. There is one falls, and there is one river with the fall in it.

The finding the works superintendent wrote and the council accepted in 536 is narrow and cold: **the nitrogen of the air can be taken by current, the principle is proved, and the arc route cannot be made to matter.** Not a money problem of the kind a treasury solves — a money problem of a kind no treasury on earth could meet with that process.

**The carbide route, found and built.** Because the line had an appropriation and a director, the rejection was not the end of it. Nebamun's own note against the arc finding — *this is almost certainly the crudest way to do this, and nobody has looked for another* — was the whole of the argument for going on, and it was enough because the money was already voted.

The furnaces themselves gave the answer. An arc furnace fed with burnt lime and coke, both of which the falls has in quantity, yields a hard grey stone that fizzes in water and gives off a gas burning with a fierce white flame. That was noticed in 539 and pursued first for the flame, which now lights the works and the deep galleries at Akan and is being tried against metal as a cutting tool. But the same grey stone, heated in a stream of air stripped of its breathable part, takes up the air's nitrogen directly and holds it in a form the chemists can work — into fertilizer for the fields and, by routes the assay houses already knew, into the acids that feed every nitrated compound Egypt makes.

The cost in current per weight of nitrogen is a small fraction of the arc route's, because the furnace does the work at heat rather than in the discharge itself.

The first works came into production in 546. At 550, **six furnaces at the falls fix nitrogen from the air at a rate equal to perhaps a fifth of the empire's total niter-bed and guano supply**, with four more furnaces building and the works superintendent stating that the limit is furnaces and lime, not principle, and that the falls could carry thirty.

This is the largest single technical change of the turn and the council knows it. The niter beds and the cave guano remain and will remain, because the falls is one place and the empire's magazines are everywhere. But the hard agricultural ceiling on how much Egypt can ever detonate — the ceiling our own assessment of 525 named the first thing blocking us — has moved for the first time since the acids were concentrated in the third century, and it moved because a line was funded and a man was told to run it.

**What was not achieved, named for the next reign.** The chemists can now describe a better route than the carbide — the air's nitrogen combined directly with hydrogen under great pressure, over some substance that hastens the joining — and cannot build it. It wants pressure vessels beyond what we will presently certify a man to stand beside, and a knowledge of hastening substances we do not have. It is written up, and the council's minute of 549 recommends it to the next reign at not less than the present appropriation. That recommendation is the only forward commitment Ptolemy XXXI's council made that binds nobody.

## The Ordnance Bureau, and the Thing We Cannot Name

These are the same question from opposite ends: *what is the weapon we do not have?*

**The Bureau of Foreign Arms**, established 529 under the intelligence service at six hundred talents a year, was funded for the whole turn — 15,000 talents — with one instruction: identify the weapons craft that China reserves by treaty and has never shown, and determine whether Rome holds it by the overland road.

Two scholars of the widened exchange were tasked in 530 under their own genuine credentials, one an optical man and one a metallurgist, both instructed to describe anything they saw of the arsenal quarter. This was a breach of an exchange that had been honoured perfectly on the other side, and the council took it knowingly.

It produced nothing. The metallurgist died of a fever at Quanzhou in 533. The optical man reported for five years that scholars are shown foundries, presses, academies and shipyards and are not shown arsenals, and that the arsenal quarter is walled, guarded, and served by men who live inside it. He could not name a single thing made there. In 536 the service judged that his interest had been noticed and rotated him home; nothing was ever said to us about it by anyone, and the council does not know whether that silence means the matter was forgotten or filed. Two further attempts, in 541 and 546, placed no one.

The Bureau's standing memorandum at 550 is four lines long and unchanged from its first: a weapons class exists which is guarded above all other knowledge, and the overland road has had centuries to carry it westward; the Bureau cannot say whether the thing is a chemistry, a mechanism or a metal. Fifteen thousand talents bought us the same sentence we wrote in 525.

**The Ordnance Bureau**, established 540 at nine hundred talents a year, is what the council did instead of finding out — reasoning that if we cannot learn what the weapon is, we can ask what a weapon ought to be, given what we hold.

What we hold is unusual. Boring machines that have cut true cylinders since 260 AD. Arc-furnace steel. Interchangeable parts and reference gauges. And, since 537, a nitrated compound that can be made to sit quietly and fire only when told. The gap our own assessment named as *the gun* was never a gap in materials. It was that our whole chemical tradition makes compounds that shatter, and a weapon that throws wants a compound that pushes.

By 544 the assay houses had a nitrated propellant that burns rather than detonates — the same family, differently made and differently packed. By 548 the falls yard had turned out **eleven pieces**: rifled steel tubes, breech-closing, on recoil carriages, throwing a shell of the stabilized explosive to something over four miles at the proving ground below the cataracts.

Eleven pieces is not an arm. There is no manufactory, no gunner corps, no doctrine, and no formation trained to fight beside them. The Bureau's request in 549 for a manufactory at the falls and a school of gunners was deferred to the next reign, and the reason it was deferred is worth writing down plainly: two hundred years of doctrine, every senior chemist's career, and the whole naval air establishment are staked on nitration and the incendiary balloon, and a new arm with no battle behind it has no advocate against them. The Pharaoh was asked to overrule that and declined. It was one of the two constituencies he would not break.

So Egypt ends the turn holding the first eleven guns in its history and no decision about them.

## China: The Gift, Working

We gave China our whole body of seed-of-sickness doctrine and tropical practice in the last turn, freely and on no schedule.

The council's standing judgment, entered 533 and unrevised, is that the gift will do exactly what it was asked for and that we should plan as though it has: that the tropical medicine which made Africa Egyptian is no longer only ours, and that within a lifetime other physicians will keep other soldiers and other crews alive in country that used to kill them. We have observed nothing that confirms this and nothing that denies it. We have no eyes inland anywhere east of Aden, and our intelligence remains what it has always been — commercial, port-bound, and blind to interiors.

We garrisoned **Socotra** in 531 and the **Comoros** in 534, cheap islands both, on the reasoning that a passage one does not hold is a passage someone else will. That reasoning arrived twenty years late to be about anyone but China, and the council said so in the minute.

Nothing in the partnership has gone wrong. Nothing that was promised has been withheld. The cost, if there is one, is entirely ours and was paid by us, deliberately, in a room where everyone was pleased to be dealt with honestly. There is no one to be angry at, which the council finds harder than a grievance.

The one question the chancery debated for two years and settled by silence: whether to tell China that we had tasked scholars against its arsenals. The argument for was that we were probably noticed, that a confession from us is cheaper than a discovery by them, and that honesty is the currency this partnership actually runs on. The argument against was that a confession converts a suspicion into a fact and gives away a capability we may want again. The council took neither position. It let the matter lapse, which is this state's characteristic act, and which means the decision was made by nobody and can be reversed by nobody.

---

## Syria: A Cut-Out Lost

The programme of encouraging banditry against the overland caravans continued, as it has for nearly two centuries, at about four hundred talents a year from discretionary intelligence funds through Palmyrene and Nabataean intermediaries, against the Damascus–Palmyra–Dura stretch. No one proposed ending it. It is cheap, it is deniable, it bleeds the route that took our richest trade in the 340s, and every man who has ever administered it has been promoted out of it before it could go wrong.

In 546 a Palmyrene intermediary stopped reporting and could not be accounted for. The service assumes compromise. There is no Egyptian officer in the chain, no document, and three cut-outs between the money and any name of ours, which is exactly why it was built that way; but a man who was paid by us has been out of our sight for four years and we do not know what he has said or to whom.

The programme was suspended in 547 and resumed in 549 at a reduced rate through new intermediaries. It was resumed because ending it permanently would have required someone to state in council that it had been a mistake for two hundred years, and nobody says that about the work of their own predecessors.

---

## Two Audits, One Completed and Not Believed

**The army was counted, and we do not believe the count.** The accession review of 528 ordered a return of all men under arms — the first such return in the archive since the figure of roughly 100,000 to 140,000 entered at about 100 AD, which our own assessment called a four-hundred-year clerical silence. It came back in 533 at about 380,000. The council did not believe it, the provincial establishments being funded per head and every governor's revenue depending on the number. A verification audit of three provinces, 536–539, found the Zimbabwe plateau establishment overstated by about a fifth and the Great Lakes by rather more.

Extending it empire-wide would have set the crown against the locally-born provincial governors, who since the reform of the 340s and 350s have been the most important constituency in the state and a principal reason the south has not seceded. Ptolemy XXXI declined; this was the second constituency he would not break. The council entered a working figure of **not fewer than 300,000 and not more than 380,000 men under arms**, of whom perhaps 45,000 to 60,000 are the professional heavy establishment, and moved to other business.

We have gone from no number to a number we do not trust, which is progress of a small and honest kind.

**The reserve was not counted.** Physical verification of the vaults was proposed in 530. The Bureau of Vaults objected that opening sealed chambers would itself become known; the intelligence service supported the objection on precisely the grounds it later used against the Pylon and lost. Two entrenched bodies in agreement is more than a steady prince overturns. The figure of 296,000 talents is the ledger's figure, never verified by anyone whose interest lay in finding it short.

**One contradiction was resolved.** The assessment of 525 flagged that the turn document asserted Egyptian holdings on the coast of Asia Minor while the standing territorial appendix listed none. The archive was ordered checked in 529 and reported in 531: there is no Egyptian garrison, magistrate or customs house anywhere on the Anatolian coast and there has not been within any record the archive holds. The claim was an error carried forward in a summary and copied for a century. It is struck. Egypt's northern frontier is the Levantine coast, Cyprus, and the sea.

---

## The Pylon of the Two Waters

This is the one thing in the reign that Ptolemy XXXI wanted personally, and the only fight he won.

At the Cape settlement, on the headland where the two oceans meet, there now stands a pylon in the canonical proportions — the same as the Isis temple at Alexandria and the one on the Zimbabwe plateau — faced in dressed stone, sheathed at its crown in the light silver, lit by arc so that it can be raised from the sea at thirty miles. Its twin, smaller, stands at the eastern mole of Alexandria harbour opposite the Pharos. The dedication, cut in Coptic and Greek, reads: *To the water that was hidden, and is not hidden.*

It cost 34,000 talents. It is a lighthouse and a landfall mark for the Cape route, which are real uses, and neither required a pylon, nor the light silver, nor thirty miles of visibility.

The intelligence service opposed it in 532 on the plain ground that a state whose entire tradition is not being counted does not build a tower whose purpose is to be seen at a great distance by anyone at all, and that the sheathing announces to every visitor what our chemistry can now make in bulk. It lost — to a young prince raised in a court that spent eleven days grieving a secret it had kept too long, and who wanted, once, to build the opposite of a secret.

The council let him have it because it cost less than a season of the Sinai schedule and because a king given one thing does not ask for others. It is the only time in twenty-two years the throne moved a body that did not want to move. Any foreign master who rounds the Cape will see it.

---

## The Realm at 550

The population stands near twenty-nine and a half million and climbs faster than at any time in three centuries, the western crops and the seed-of-sickness practice working together on ground that used to lose a fifth of its children. Between thirty and forty-five thousand funded scholars and engineers are at work on a technical class of seven hundred thousand to a million. Revenue is a hundred and fifty-two thousand talents and the reserve stands near its two-year mark.

Africa is unchanged and full. What is new is water and islands — the Fortunate Isles, the Green Cape, the Island of the Watering, Socotra, the Comoros — and across the ocean a thin unbroken perimeter from the Orinoco island to the River of Silver, the Great River ascended four hundred miles, and Alexandria Notou growing into a real Egyptian city on the far side of the ocean.

The fortress in the east is the finest in the world and faces an empty desert. The nitrogen ceiling has moved. There are eleven guns at the falls and no decision about them. The tower at the Cape can be seen for thirty miles. And we have not looked north of the Orinoco, in twenty-five years, once.

The council enters 550 with the same estimate it has carried since 412, unrevised: that Rome will come.

# DECLARATIONS

## DECLARATIONS OF INTENT

1. **Egypt takes no offensive action against any power this turn and declares none for the next.** No Egyptian vessel has fired on any foreign vessel in this period. The standing order is unchanged: shadow, do not fire unless fired upon, do not issue threats that will not be enforced.
2. **Egypt claims, occupies and garrisons the island off the mouth of the Orinoco**, held from 543 as a strongpoint. It was found unoccupied. Egypt claims no ground and no water north of that river and has entered none.
3. **Egypt claims the western ocean coast from the Orinoco mouth southward to and beyond the River of Silver, and the islands lying off it.** Held ground is listed below; claimed-only ground is listed separately and honestly.
4. **Egypt intends to continue the Western Enterprise at its present funding into the next reign**, and to send, if leave is ever given, one unarmed survey hull north of the Orinoco. Leave has not been given.
5. **Egypt intends no action against the Roman Maghreb.** Every officer notices it. Nothing will be done.

## MESSAGE OUT — TO ROME

**Sent from Alexandria, autumn 542, by the formal channel through the Roman legation at Alexandria; carried in Greek and in Latin, sealed.**

> From the Council of the Pharaoh Ptolemy, at Alexandria, to the Senate and the Princeps of the Romans, greeting.
>
> We write in a matter that is small now and will not remain small if neither of us speaks of it.
>
> Some years past, three vessels flying your colours came south along the Atlantic face of Africa, kept station off our coast, and returned north. Our ships were present the whole way and did not close, did not signal, and issued no threat. That was by our standing order, given years ago and unchanged, and we tell you of it plainly so that you may know it was not accident, nor weakness, nor the judgment of one captain, but the policy of this court: our vessels will not fire upon yours except upon being fired upon first. We ask nothing in return for telling you this. We think it better that you know it.
>
> We say to you also that the coasts of the western ocean from the mouth of the great northern river of that shore southward to the River of Silver, and the islands lying off them, are within the sphere of Alexandria and have been within the sphere of Alexandria's ships for longer than the present reign. Our harbours are there, our garrisons are there, and our people farm there. We do not set out their number or their places, as you do not set out yours, and we do not think either of us expects the other to.
>
> We make no claim northward of that river, we have entered nothing northward of it, and we do not propose to discuss what lies there.
>
> Two peoples whose ships may begin to meet on an ocean that neither of them can see the whole of will meet again, and the second meeting is always worse than the first if nothing has been agreed between them. We therefore propose a thing that costs neither of us anything and may spare us both a great deal. Let each court give the other notice of voyages passing westward beyond the Fortunate Isles: the number of hulls, the season, and no more than that. Not the purpose. Not the destination. Merely that they sail. A ship expected is a ship that is not mistaken for something else, and most of what has gone wrong between distant powers has gone wrong because someone appeared where no one was looking for him.
>
> We would receive your envoys at Alexandria in any season, and our ships would carry them safely and swiftly, which is a thing we can offer that few can.
>
> The Council of the Pharaoh, at Alexandria.

*(Chancery notes, not sent: the phrase "longer than the present reign" is to be preferred to any statement of years — the reign began in 528 and our first crossing was in 402, so it is true and is meant to be read as recent. This is the house style: we lie by true statement wherever we can, because a true statement survives investigation. No mention is to be made of the vessels lost in Atlantic waters between 398 and the 440s, in any draft, for any reason; no faction wanted it raised and none will.)*

No reply had reached Alexandria by the close of 550. Eight years is not long for that ocean, and the council has drawn no conclusion from the silence.

## MESSAGE OUT — TO CHINA

**Sent from Alexandria, spring 543, by the Aden commercial office and the Quanzhou representation, in Coptic and in Chinese.**

> From the Council of the Pharaoh Ptolemy, at Alexandria, to the Court of the Emperor, greeting.
>
> The exchange holds and we have no complaint in it. Your twenty have had our physicians' whole body of practice, on no schedule and with nothing kept back, as we said they would. Your paper and your presses have done more for our schools than we told you at the time.
>
> We write for three reasons, and we ask for nothing you have said you will not give.
>
> First, we tell you before you hear it elsewhere, because you told us plainly of the western land when you had no obligation to. Egypt has settled the western ocean's far shore. Our harbours run from the mouth of the great northern river of that coast southward to a river we call the River of Silver, and our people farm there. We claim nothing north of that river. We have written to Rome saying the same, in the same words, and we enclose the text so that you hold what they hold.
>
> Second, we ask that the consultation clause be read to cover that ocean as it covers this one: that if a third power brings either of us a proposal touching the western shore, each tells the other that it was brought. You granted consultation in both directions and openly, and said you would tell Rome whatever you tell us. We ask nothing better than that, and we hold you to nothing more.
>
> Third, we propose the same procedure we proposed to Rome, which costs nothing: notice of voyages, the number of hulls and the season and no more, for your ships passing west of Socotra and ours passing east of it. Not the purpose. Not the destination. Merely that they sail.
>
> We ask you for no knowledge in this letter. Our standing instruction since the last exchange is to look first at where we already lead before requesting anything, and on this occasion we found nothing to ask for that we do not have.
>
> The Council of the Pharaoh, at Alexandria.

## MESSAGE OUT — TO THE MAURYA

**Sent from Alexandria, summer 544, through the Aden commercial office to the Court of the Mauryas, in Greek and in the court's own script.**

> From the Council of the Pharaoh Ptolemy, at Alexandria, to the Court of the Mauryas, greeting.
>
> There has been little between us but trade for four hundred years, and what there has been besides trade has not been good on either side. We are not writing to mend that, which cannot be mended by a letter, and we will not pretend otherwise.
>
> We are writing because our two frontiers touch along the Euphrates and the Syrian desert edge, and because a frontier that touches without a procedure is a frontier where a misreading becomes a war that neither court chose.
>
> We therefore propose three things, none of which costs either of us a soldier or a coin.
>
> First: notice of movements. Let each court inform the other of any body of troops moving toward that border in strength, before it moves. The number and the season, no more. We will give it first and without waiting to receive it.
>
> Second: a standing channel. Let there be one man at Aden and one at whatever port you name who is known to both courts and empowered to carry a question and return an answer within a season, so that a question can be asked before an army is raised to ask it.
>
> Third, and this is the one that matters: that neither court enter into any arrangement with a third power directed against the other without saying so. We do not ask you to refuse such arrangements. We ask only that they not be secret from us, and we bind ourselves in the same words.
>
> Our published toll at the Red Sea entry stands at thirty parts in the hundred, assessed once, the same for your hulls as for every hull afloat, and it will not be raised against you nor lowered for anyone. We say so plainly because the certainty is worth more to a merchant than the rate.
>
> We offer no knowledge and we ask for none. We think you will understand why, and we would rather be plain about it than courteous about it.
>
> The Council of the Pharaoh, at Alexandria.

*(Chancery note, not sent: the third article is the whole letter. The first two are there so that the third does not stand alone. The council's expectation is refusal or silence; the purpose is served either way, since a court that refuses to say it has no arrangement has told us something.)*

## SPY TASKINGS

**1. The Atlantic Question (the priority carried forward from 525).**
*Target:* whether Rome now believes Egypt is across the western ocean, and whether Rome's account of its five vanished vessels has hardened into a belief that Egypt destroyed them.
*Who goes:* four officers of the Greek-Egyptian establishment — men indistinguishable by speech and manner from Hellenes of the Roman east — placed as timber and cordage factors and as a shipwright's clerk.
*Cover and stations:* the naval contracting yards at **Ostia** and **Ravenna** (the old Roman-east Greek network, roughly four hundred years old, deep and reliable), and the Atlantic-facing yards and victualling houses at **Gades**, which is the station that actually matters and where the network is about twenty years old, thin, and run through two merchants only.
*Return to 550:* fragmentary and internally contradictory on the question asked. The council records no finding and treats the return as a floor and not an answer, per standing discipline. Nothing in it is firm enough to plan on and nothing in it has been allowed to change a plan.

**2. The Western Ocean (new, and not run).**
*Target:* whether any other power is present in the western ocean, and where.
*Who goes:* nobody. There is no station, no cover, and no hull with leave to go north of the Orinoco.
*Return:* none. This is the largest deliberate blindness in the empire's intelligence picture and it was created by a doctrine, not by a decision.

**3. The Bureau of Foreign Arms (funded throughout, 15,000 talents).**
*Target:* the weapons craft China reserves by treaty and has never shown; whether Rome holds it by the overland road.
*Who goes:* two scholars of the widened exchange under genuine credentials, tasked 530; two further attempts, 541 and 546, placed nobody.
*Cover:* the scholar exchange itself, which is to say a breach of an agreement honoured exactly on the other side. Taken knowingly.
*Network age:* new. There has never been an Egyptian network inside any foreign military workshop, which is precisely the hole.
*Return:* the metallurgist died at Quanzhou in 533; the optical man reported nothing in five years and was rotated home in 536 when the service judged his interest had been noticed. The standing inference is unchanged and unanswered: a weapons class exists that is guarded above all else, and the overland road has had centuries to carry it west. The Bureau cannot say whether it is a chemistry, a mechanism or a metal.

**4. The Syrian caravan programme (continuing, ~200 years old).**
*Target:* the overland luxury route, Damascus–Palmyra–Dura.
*Who goes:* nobody Egyptian. Money only, three cut-outs deep, through Palmyrene and Nabataean intermediaries.
*Cost:* about 400 talents a year from discretionary intelligence funds.
*Return:* an intermediary lost from contact in 546 and unaccounted for since; compromise assumed. Suspended 547–548, resumed 549 at reduced rate through new intermediaries.

**5. Counter-intelligence, home and colonial.**
*Target:* foreign agents in Alexandria, Aden and the new Atlantic ports; and the settler mail out of the western colonies, which is the largest new leak the empire has ever created and which nobody had thought about before 537.
*Who:* the harbour service, expanded by two hundred men, with a new reading office at the Green Cape where every homeward hull calls.
*Return:* eleven persons identified across the turn as reporting to foreign interests and handled by the service; the Green Cape reading office is the single most productive counter-intelligence station in the empire and its existence is not known to the settlers whose letters it opens.

**6. The eastern frontier (maintenance only).**
*Target:* whether the Maurya court is moving toward an arrangement with Rome.
*Who:* the commercial-cover network on the Levantine border and in the Aden commercial office. Roughly two hundred years old, reliable for shipping and trade, useless for anything a merchant cannot see.
*Return:* nothing firm. The council does not consider absence of evidence to be evidence, and notes that this network cannot see the inside of any court.

## TERRITORY AT 550

### A. GROUND CONQUERED, SETTLED OR GARRISONED — new since 525

Every entry below has Egyptian men standing on it, under an Egyptian officer, with a magistrate or a commissioner answerable to Alexandria.

**Atlantic crossing chain (garrisoned, no settlement)**
- **The Fortunate Isles** (Canaries) — the two large islands, harbour on the eastern. Garrisoned 529; bunkers, heliograph to Agadir and Mogador, wireless from 537.

**Indian Ocean (garrisoned, no settlement)**
- **Socotra** — garrisoned 531.
- **The Comoros**, principal island — garrisoned 534; coaling station.

**Western landmass, tropical face (garrisoned, thin settlement)**
- **Maroni river mouth** (Guiana) — fortified harbour.

- **Arsinoe Potamou**, southern channel of the mouth of the Great River of the West (Amazon/Pará) — founded 533; plus two river garrisons of ninety men each, four hundred miles upstream at the black-water and clear-water confluences.

**Western landmass, temperate south (settled)**
- **Alexandria Notou**, north bank of the River of Silver (Plata) — ~18,000 settlers at 550. Alexandrian grid, canonical Isis temple, scribal school, branch library, hospital, mole. Wheat, vine, olive.

- **Kasai** and **Ubangi** river posts, upstream of the Livingstone works.

### B. COAST SURVEYED, CHARTED OR CLAIMED ONLY — no Egyptian on it

Nothing below is held. Nothing below has a garrison, a magistrate, or a customs house. Some of it has never been visited twice.

- **The coast south of the River of Silver**, down the cold shelf toward the far southern latitudes — charted by two survey voyages, 546 and 549. Harbours noted, watering places noted, no post, no claim of occupation. Egypt claims it in the message to Rome and holds not one foot of it.
- **The Great River of the West above the two confluences** — charted by steam launch a further distance upstream; no post, no garrison, no return visit.
- **The whole interior behind the tropical Atlantic perimeter** — claimed on parchment. There is nothing there of ours. The perimeter is the claim, exactly as the African playbook intends, and the council should stop letting the map colour it in.
- **The cold islands south-east of the Cape** — sighted and charted 544 by a Cape vessel driven south. Uninhabited, no anchorage worth a station, not garrisoned, not revisited.
- **Australia** — in the classified geographic record since the Maurya disclosure of the 440s. Never visited by any Egyptian hull. Filed, not acted on, in this turn as in the last three.
- **The western ocean north of the Orinoco, and the whole enclosed sea beyond it** — **not charted, not entered, not claimed, not looked at.** Egypt has no survey, no sighting and no report of any kind from those waters in this period. It does not know what is there.
