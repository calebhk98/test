# ADJUDICATION: FOREIGN INTELLIGENCE OPERATIONS AGAINST HAN CHINA, 525–550

*Neutral intelligence adjudication. Judged solely from the target's own record: `03-han-china.md`, `human-turn-500-525/china.md`, `v2/turn-525-550/china.md`. The adjudicator has not been told what either operator hoped to find or claims to have found. Everything below is derived from what Han China physically was in this period, what it physically did, and how this specific court behaves when it notices someone looking.*

*Two operations are judged. For each: what the operators receive, in the register they would receive it; what is actually true; and whether, when, and how the target notices.*

---

## PART ONE — OPERATION A: THE DNIEPER FRONTIER WATCH (ROME)

### 1. The position, honestly described

A ~250-year-old standing watch on the western bank: *exploratores* and licensed caravan factors under customs and caravan-administration cover that is genuine work they actually perform, plus tethered balloon observation from the Roman side. No crossing.

What that position physically commands:

- **The Chinese bank at the crossing point.** Since 541 this is not empty ground. The Chinese completed rail from **Anhe** to the water in 541 and built, on their own bank, a **transfer yard, sidings, cranes, and a gauge-matching bogie shop**. These are large, permanent, purpose-built structures and they were built to be seen — the Chinese court's stated intent was to build the apparatus a junction would need and leave the decision to Rome. The watch is looking at an offer rendered in masonry and iron.
- **Traffic, at wagon-count resolution.** Trains arriving and departing, wagon numbers, frequency, seasonality, what is broken bulk in the yard and what runs through, which sidings receive escorted consignments, where each is offloaded.
- **Roofs, smokes, ploughed ground, and the visible near strip.** A tethered balloon gives a plan view of the yard, the town at Anhe, the fields under the plough, and the line of the rail running east into the grass until the curvature and haze take it.
- **The frontier market.** This is the underrated half of the position. Licensed factors doing genuine customs work stand inside the one institution — the mixed frontier market — where Chinese carters, contractors, gangers and discharged garrison men talk for money.

What the position cannot reach, in this period or any other: manifests, warehouses, magazine interiors, garrison rolls, and anything more than a long day's ride inland. It also cannot reach the two documents that would answer every question at once — the **Jinyang Report** (sealed 533, three copies: inner council, Censor-in-Chief, Director of the Shangfang) and the unpublished arithmetic behind the 534 powder reform.

**One correction to any assumption of Roman optical advantage.** China has operated **balloon observation platforms** since roughly 483–495, and crude two-lens telescopes from the same window, a wider-field two-lens scope by about 508, and a mirror reflector by about 512. It **deliberately withheld the telescope from Rome in 517 as a weapon.** The operational consequence is not that Rome cannot see — it is that **China knows precisely what a tethered balloon at that range can and cannot resolve, because it flies them itself.** Chinese management of the Anhe picture is therefore informed rather than guesswork. This matters at every point below.

### 2. TARGET ONE — Tonnage at the terminus and its rate of growth

**WHAT THE OPERATORS RECEIVE:**

> The eastern terminus has run continuously since the Chinese rail reached the water in the winter of 541. Consignments crossing to our bank are baled silk in the main, with lacquer, worked steel and finished goods behind it. Measured on our own tariff ledger, the crossing traffic has risen by about half across the nine years since the line opened — steady, compounding at four to five parts in a hundred a year, without a bad season. It has not slackened.
>
> That is not the whole of what arrives. The yard takes in appreciably more than it passes to us. The remainder is broken bulk on the Chinese side or runs through eastward on the corridor line and never touches the river at all: construction iron and rail material heavily in the first years, then stores, tools, grain and consignments of the guarded kind described below. Our best count of wagons entering the yard against wagons whose contents reach our bank puts the crossing trade at well under half of the terminus's total handling, and the share has fallen as the trade has grown.

**WHAT IS TRUE:**

Accurate, and the accuracy is nearly free. The Chinese court's own number: *"The Anhe customs returns record outbound silk tonnage from the Chinese bank rising by about half between 541 and 550, which is the empire's own number and reflects what the Chinese rail delivered to the crossing, not what happened west of it."* Empire-wide, the Silk Road tolls and silk export line moved from 365 to 420 tonnes of silver-equivalent, 525→550, explicitly on Chinese-side tonnage to the Dnieper crossing.

**The adjudication that matters here is an efficiency finding, not a truth finding.** The single item this operation returns with the highest confidence is the one item it would have obtained from its own tariff ledger with no watch, no scouts and no balloon — because Rome assesses a 10 per cent transit tariff on Han goods and therefore *counts the crossing traffic itself as a matter of law*. Worse: China published the figure. Worse again: in the spring of **548** China sent Rome a letter carrying the rail-junction offer in plain terms, and a court making that offer will supply tonnage figures on request, because the whole purpose of the offer is a larger flow. A quarter-millennium of standing collection is spending itself to obtain a number the counterparty is trying to hand over.

The genuinely new content is the second paragraph — the **gap between terminus throughput and crossing trade**, which no ledger supplies and which the wagon-count does. That gap is real, and it is the most valuable raw material the operation holds. It is also the raw material for the operation's central error, below.

**Verdict: OBTAINED, ACCURATE, LARGELY REDUNDANT — with one genuinely new and load-bearing observation (the throughput gap).**

### 3. TARGET TWO — Depth, permanence and population of Han civilian settlement within two days of the eastern bank

**WHAT THE OPERATORS RECEIVE:**

> This is settlement and not a garrison line, and we should say so without hedging. The black earth east of the river is being broken by the plough — virgin sod, ox work, the heaviest labour there is and the least reversible. Men do not do that work and then leave. There are villages of the Chinese frontier pattern within sight of the balloon and more where the smokes stand beyond it, a county seat inland on the northern tributary with a magistrate and a tax office, and the corridor line running east has two further seats posted along it. Children on the ground. Second sowing on the same fields.
>
> On numbers we are estimating and we mark it as an estimate. Counting roofs, hearth smokes and cultivated ground on the near strip and carrying that density inland over the two days' depth we cannot enter, we put the settled Chinese population within that band somewhere between fifty and eighty thousand souls, with a garrison at the terminus that the stores traffic suggests is substantial — larger than the works alone would need.
>
> The direction of travel is the finding. Nine years ago this bank was a fort line on grass. It is now country with a tax office on it.

**WHAT IS TRUE:**

- **Permanence: correct, and correctly reasoned.** Four registered Pontic counties: **Heitu** on the Vorskla (seat 533, first tax return 537), **Guangtian** on the Donets (536), and two further counties on the Anhe–Guangtian rail corridor (541 and 546). These are the *only ground gained in the entire period that pays tax*. The plough-on-virgin-sod inference is exactly right; the barrier to this soil was never ignorance, it was the ox work plus distance, and the rail removed the distance.
- **Population: overstated by roughly three to four times.** Registered settlers across the *whole* Pontic black-earth programme at 550 are **about 41,000**, and that is the total for all four counties including Guangtian on the Donets, several hundred kilometres east and far outside a two-day band. Within two days' travel of the bank the true figure is on the order of **15,000 to 25,000 souls** — Anhe's town and garrison, the yard and rail labour (a substantial part of it transient contract men, not settlers), and the nearer part of Heitu.
- **"Larger garrison than the works alone would need": wrong, and wrong for an instructive reason.** See below.

**THE MECHANISM OF THE ERROR — two compounding faults, neither of them sloppiness:**

**(a) Railhead-density extrapolation.** Anhe is a *holding* position under the doctrine amendment of 535: garrisoned at strength, magazined to the new standard, commandery-grade, with a transfer yard, cranes, a bogie shop and their labour on top. It is the single densest point on the entire Pontic frontier. Carrying its density inland is carrying the empire's maximum as though it were the empire's average. The true gradient falls off a cliff: under the same 535 amendment, **"the thin scatter across the Pontic between Anhe and Guangtian"** was reclassified **observation** — occupied and administered, but explicitly no longer expected to hold.

**(b) Through-freight read as local supply.** This is the sharper of the two. The powder-allowance reform of 534 raised **rail-served posts from five days to fifteen** and **beyond-rail posts from five days to sixty**. Anhe is rail-served: its own magazine roughly tripled. But every beyond-rail post in the Pontic scatter is supplied *through Anhe*, at twelve times its former allowance, and those consignments enter the yard and run onward east. A watch counting guarded wagons into a terminus and inferring the garrison they feed will inflate the terminus's own establishment by a large factor, because most of that powder is going a very long way past it to posts holding almost no men at all.

**The empire has thus arrived at exactly the inversion it most needs and could not have engineered: the Pontic frontier looks thickest at precisely the moment its own sealed arithmetic says it is thinnest.**

**Verdict: PERMANENCE — OBTAINED AND CORRECT. DEPTH AND POPULATION — TRUE-BUT-MISINTERPRETED, OVERSTATED 3–4×, ERROR STRUCTURALLY FAVOURABLE TO THE TARGET.**

### 4. TARGET THREE — Whether rail traffic carries military stores

**WHAT THE OPERATORS RECEIVE:**

> Yes, and not marginally. Powder does not move as silk moves and the difference is legible from the far bank without a glass. Certain consignments arrive under escort, are worked at their own siding by the garrison side of the yard rather than the transfer sheds, appear on no bill of lading that reaches our tariff table, and are handled by soldiers rather than by yard labour. Some are broken and stored; more run through eastward.
>
> The storage is the plainer signal. Within two seasons of the line opening the Chinese raised a building of a type we can identify by shape alone — thick-walled, isolated from the yard by a margin of clear ground, banked with earth. That is a magazine and nothing else, and it was built larger than the one it replaced.
>
> The rate is the report. Guarded consignments through this terminus have risen far faster than the trade — by our running count, of the order of three times what the line carried when it opened, and the rise began before the trade did. It has not stopped.

**WHAT IS TRUE:**

Correct in every particular, and the most competent work the operation does. The observables are all real: the hardened magazine specification has been **mandatory inside city walls since the fire of the 420s** and is therefore a recognisable building type; the 534 reform made hardened magazines mandatory on the new schedule; the increase is exactly as described.

And China knew this was coming and wrote it down. The Shangfang's own estimate, entered in 537: *"the fact of a large increase is outside the empire within one to two seasons and cannot be concealed at all."* The court did not attempt concealment. It attempted **cover**: the reform was published as *strengthening*; the digest was titled *On the Sufficiency of Magazines* so that a larger magazine reads as a richer frontier rather than a frightened one; and **the rail-served increase was announced first and loudest so that the beyond-rail figure would arrive as an afterthought.**

**A watch standing at a rail-served node is the exact instrument that cover was designed for.** Anhe *is* the loud announcement. The operators will observe, accurately, a threefold rise in guarded traffic and a new hardened magazine at a strengthened railhead, and will report a China reinforcing its Dnieper frontier. That is a true observation supporting a false conclusion — and the false conclusion is the one the Chinese court manufactured in council, arrived at by a Roman officer honestly measuring real objects.

**Verdict: OBTAINED, FULLY CORRECT ON FACT, AND THE FACT IS THE TARGET'S OWN COVER STORY.**

### 5. The unasked-for take, and the inference not made

**Received, off-brief, from the frontier market:**

> Market talk, from carters and from a discharged man of the corridor posts, consistent across three seasons and two markets: the Chinese have lately read a new standing regulation aloud to their frontier garrisons permitting a post to quit its ground on contact, with no fault to the officer. The phrase reported to us is bureaucratic and means nothing on its face — *preservation of force* and something after it. The men repeating it do not think it means nothing.

**What is true:** this is the single most valuable fact in the Chinese empire in this period, and it is loose in the markets exactly as the court predicted. The 535 amendment lets observation-post commanders **withdraw on contact rather than stand**, and the court's own written estimate is that *"a regulation that permits a post to leave is discussed in every frontier market within a season."* Beneath the clause sits the concession that since the Mongolian chain of the first century a post did not leave, because a post that may leave is not a claim — and that **beyond the rail the empire's claim is a claim and not a possession.** The Shangfang drafted *preservation of force for counter-concentration* precisely so that a commander leaving a post reads as executing a manoeuvre.

**The operators receive the sentence and cannot cash it.** To convert it they must know which posts are observation and which are holding, and that requires the eastern bank. Worse, it arrives in the same reports as a threefold rise in guarded traffic and a new magazine — and *strengthening* and *permission to run* do not sit together, so a careful analyst discounts the market rumour as garble against the physical evidence. **The physical evidence, which is true, buries the rumour, which is truer.**

**The inference not made.** The court's own estimate is that the *shape* of the reform — fifteen days rail-served against sixty beyond it, which is the sealed arithmetic in disguise — **"takes five to ten years to assemble from convoy sizes."** A watch counting wagons at one node from 541 has nine years by 550, and is therefore just entering the window. It will not close it in this period, for a hard reason: the shape requires knowing the *manpower* at each onward post, and manpower beyond the near strip is precisely what the western bank cannot see. **The material is in the Roman files at 550 and the inference is unmade.** It remains available to a later reader who acquires one more fact from any other source.

### 6. WAS IT DETECTED

**Yes. Immediately, continuously, and by design tolerated.**

- **When.** From the first season of the yard's operation, 541. There is no discovery event. A permanent Roman watch on a shared river frontier that both empires have observed openly for two and a half centuries is not a secret and has never been treated as one. Rome said as much in writing in 517: *"China's river garrisons openly carry a weapon Rome does not possess... Rome is not a fool and does not need to be told."* Mutual observation is the settled condition of this border.
- **The balloon is understood, not alarming.** China flies balloon observation platforms itself and has since the 480s, and therefore knows the resolution the far bank commands to within a metre. It reports the platform up the chain as an ordinary frontier return.

**What the court concludes:** that Rome is counting the yard, which is what the yard is for.

**What it does — nothing, deliberately, on four grounds all present in the file:**

1. **The yard was built to be seen.** The transfer sheds, sidings, cranes and gauge-matching bogie shop were raised on the Chinese bank in 541 with no junction agreed, and *"whether iron meets iron there is Rome's to determine."* A watch that counts the apparatus and reports it is carrying China's offer to Rome for free.
2. **This court does not spend on Rome.** In 517 it recorded *"no espionage this period"* against Rome and gave the reason: having just told Rome it intended to sail toward Rome's new world, being caught in Roman harbours the same year was worth less than the honesty. Su's argument for the 548 letter closes the loop: a court that repays Roman candour with silence and a surveying fleet *"will find in eighty years that it has taught the one power that has never lied to it not to be honest with it."* Rolling up a Roman watch is that same mistake in a smaller coat.
3. **The overestimate is welcome.** China cannot repel a determined push on the Pontic and has known it since the 320s; the 535 amendment made it worse and put it in writing. A Roman file that reports fifty to eighty thousand settlers and a heavy garrison at Anhe is doing unpaid work for the Chinese frontier. **The court has no interest whatever in correcting it.**
4. **There is nothing to protect that an arrest would not advertise.** Everything the watch can reach is either published, unconcealable by the Shangfang's own estimate, or deliberately displayed. Moving on the watch would raise the only question China cannot afford: *why now?*

**The one thing China does that has an intelligence effect, without intending one.** Consignments for the beyond-rail Pontic scatter are not broken bulk at Anhe; they run through. That is ordinary railway practice and no one designed it as deception. It denies the watch the per-post figures anyway, and it is the specific reason the five-to-ten-year assembly window will not close on schedule.

**Detection outcome: NOTICED FROM THE FIRST SEASON. NO ACTION, TAKEN AS A POSITIVE DECISION IN COUNCIL'S ESTABLISHED IDIOM. THE OPERATION IS, IN NET EFFECT, FED.**

## PART TWO — OPERATION B: THE SCHOLAR PLACEMENT (EGYPT)

### 1. The ground the exchange actually covers

Two scholars — a metallurgist and an optical specialist — placed in **530** under genuine credentials of the scholar exchange. The exchange stands at **twenty per side per decade**, widened from five in 517. **China honours it exactly.** Further placement attempts in **541** and **546** put nobody in place. There has never previously been a foreign network inside any Chinese military workshop.

Three facts from the Chinese file fix the entire adjudication:

**(a) Weapons manufacture is not merely reserved — it is one of exactly two absolute carve-outs, and it has been for a century.** From the 420s partnership: *"China's carve-outs are equally specific and absolute: silk production methods and weapons manufacturing knowledge are excluded from any exchange regardless of how deep the partnership grows."* Reaffirmed at every renewal. At 525–550: *"The empire continues to withhold the electrical work, the steam hulls, and everything of powder."* This is not a door that is closed; it is a wall that predates the men sent at it by a hundred years.

**(b) Optics is not merely open — it is the consideration Egypt is paying for.** In 517 Egypt offered the fourfold widening and its disease knowledge *"in return only for continued free access to Chinese optical and mathematical work,"* and China *"opens its optical and mathematical work: lens geometry, the sphere and circumference measured from the empire's own posts, the pendulum clock, the mirror telescope principle."* **An optical specialist placed under this agreement has a generous, genuine and fully honoured brief. He does not need to be a spy. Being one wastes him.**

**(c) China is running the identical operation in the other direction, and after 534 it is running it with a written rulebook.** *"After 534 the standing tasking of the Aden and Alexandria scholars was sharpened toward boiler practice, plate, and the working of iron under pressure: what they may lawfully observe, what they may ask, and what they may record."* Roughly **120 Chinese scholars sit at Aden and Alexandria and with the squadrons.** A court that has written its own men a doctrine separating *lawful observation* from *asking* will recognise the behaviour in a guest on sight — it has the taxonomy.

**Duration ruled.** The 530 pair are ruled in place from 531 to about 539, an ordinary full term. They depart on completion, not under a cloud. The 541 and 546 nominations then fail. **Egypt's direct sight of China therefore freezes at about 539.**

### 2. TARGET ONE — The nature of the weapons craft China withholds

**WHAT THE OPERATORS RECEIVE:**

> We can describe the wall exactly and we have not been over it. The refusal is total and it is courteous. It is not a matter of one closed workshop: the arsenals at Chang'an and Luoyang are simply not part of any itinerary that exists, and no itinerary that does not exist can be requested into existence. There is a directorate — the Shangfang — that administers arms for the whole empire and answers to the throne, and it is the body that decides what we see.
>
> Saltpeter we did not need to ask about. It is a state monopoly on the same footing as salt and iron, of the same antiquity as the weapons work itself, with a published price and licensed dealers, and the Grand Minister of Agriculture books it as ordinary revenue. Any traveller can establish as much in a market in a morning. It follows that the craft rests on a chemistry at its root — but that root has been in plain public administration for six hundred years and is guarded as a revenue instrument, not as a secret.
>
> The wall is not made of guards. We were never once obstructed by a soldier. It is made of the men we were asking. A Chinese scholar or works official who answers a foreigner on this subject is ruined and knows it, and the man who fails to report having been asked is ruined too. Every question is therefore answered with a courtesy and travels upward the same evening. That is a more effective closure than a locked door and it costs the Chinese nothing.

**WHAT IS TRUE:**

Correct throughout, including the sharp closing observation. The saltpeter monopoly dates to the 90s BC, extended off the salt monopoly framework, and has been one of the three fiscal pillars ever since — it is a published tax instrument and always has been. The Shangfang is the central weapons administration and has been since the third century. And the closure mechanism is precisely as described: the Chinese investigation tier is a **salaried civil establishment** — 1,600 scholars, of whom ~400 sit in arsenal design offices on the Shangfang's own rolls and 100 in the Bureau of Measured Things at capped eighth and ninth grades. These are career men in a system where office is the only durable security a household can obtain. **The state does not need to guard the secret; it only needs to own the careers of everyone who holds it.**

**Verdict: OBTAINED — the negative space, accurately mapped. The craft itself: NOTHING.**

### 3. TARGET TWO — Chemistry, mechanism, or metallurgy?

**WHAT THE OPERATORS RECEIVE:**

> Not a chemistry, or not any longer. We give three grounds and the third is the strong one.
>
> First, the mixture's ingredients are a public monopoly, priced and licensed, which is not how a state holds a formula.
>
> Second, in nine years we heard the Chinese discuss their arms among themselves a great many times and never once heard the mixture discussed as a thing to be got right. What they argue about, constantly and with heat, is **range, the trueness of a bore, and the quality of steel.** A people still guarding a recipe talk about the recipe.
>
> Third, and this is the metallurgist's own finding and he stands behind it: **their steel is the finest either of us has handled, and no Chinese scholar we asked can tell us why it is good.** They do not possess a theory of it. They possess a practice — a furnace habit, a fuel, a coal from the northern country now coming down a new rail, a copper from the far west, and men who have done the work since boyhood in buildings we cannot enter. This is not knowledge that could be handed to us if a Chinese official wished to hand it over, because no Chinese official holds it in a form that can be handed.
>
> Set beside that: they keep the standards of length and weight as a matter of state, in a new bureau founded for the purpose, with published observations and gauges to fixed sizes, and they will show it all freely because they think of it as measurement rather than as arms. We could not separate, and we do not think they separate, how much of the thing is the metal and how much is the machine that trues it. Our judgement is that it is both and that the two are one craft. **Call it metallurgy and measurement.**

**WHAT IS TRUE:**

**This is the operation's real success, it is substantially correct, and it is correct for the right reasons.**

- *"No theory of what makes steel steel — **No.** The empire makes the finest steel it knows of and cannot say why. Nothing in the reign moved this."* Verbatim from the Chinese ledger of what was **not** achieved. The metallurgist has independently established one of the target's own admitted blind spots, from the outside, by asking and being answered honestly by people with nothing to hide on that point.
- The doctrinal reorientation is real and dates to **517**, when the court formally declared the gunpowder monopoly lost and redefined the empire's edge as *"precision, metallurgy, and range rather than possession."* The scholars are hearing the consequence of a policy decision made thirteen years before they arrived.
- The precision culture is real and is older than either man: the boring machine and the first true gauges arrived within a generation of the first cannon (165–185 AD) and were *"jealously kept within the state arsenals."* The **Bureau of Measured Things (536)** — 100 salaried posts at Luoyang and Chang'an, holding the standards of length, weight and time, publishing observations, keeping the failure ledgers — is inside the pair's residence window and is exactly the open face of the closed craft.
- **The refusal to separate metal from machine is not a failure of analysis. It is the correct answer.** The defining achievement of this very reign proves it: the **soft copper obturating ring** of 541 — seated in a recess cut in the breech face, expanded outward by the firing pressure, relaxing after — which finally made the breech-loader a weapon crews would use. It came out of the **copper sheathing yards at Panyu**, not from any gun shop. It is a metallurgy, a machining tolerance and a shipwright's material at once, and the man credited with it was not a gunner.

**THE GAP — and it is the whole operation:**

The tasking is thirty years stale on the day it is written. The formula the operators were sent to characterise **left Chinese control around 350 AD** — rebels held crude powder in the 350s; by 517 the court had audited the matter and concluded the formula had been outside state control for roughly 150 years, that Sogdian merchants move information as freely as goods, and that **every peer must be assumed to have powder or to acquire it within two generations.** By 550 the Sogdian carters on the western line are Maurya subjects and China buys its own western intelligence from that same trade.

**Egypt sent two credentialled scholars, and then twice more, at a chemistry that had been purchasable on the road for two hundred years — and the thing they actually found, which no one asked them for, is the thing that is still China's and cannot be bought at all, because the Chinese themselves cannot state it.** Whether Egypt's readers recognise what they hold depends on Egypt's file, not China's.

**Verdict: OBTAINED — CORRECT IN SUBSTANCE, OPERATIONALLY UNCASHABLE. The right answer to a question that had stopped mattering, plus, accidentally, the right answer to the question that had replaced it.**

### 4. TARGET THREE — Has the craft travelled west along the overland road?

**WHAT THE OPERATORS RECEIVE:**

> Yes, and long ago, and the Chinese know it. We did not learn this by asking. We learned it by watching a court behave.
>
> A power that still held a monopoly would guard the mixture and would not need to guard anything else. This court guards everything else. Its whole effort and its whole expenditure go to range, to the trueness of a bore, to the strength of a barrel — to the things a rival who already has crude powder cannot copy quickly. That is the posture of a power that lost a race some time ago and is running a different one.
>
> And then, in our fourth year among them, the frontier magazines were multiplied. The order came suddenly, it was very large, it was published as a strengthening, and it was not explained. Every clerk who fills a requisition and every carter who counts the wagons can see it, and inside the empire it is talked about. We could get no reason from any official. But a state does not multiply the powder in a fort against an enemy who has no powder, because such an enemy must close, and a fort that breaks him when he closes does not need a great deal.
>
> Our conclusion, and we mark it as inference: the Chinese have concluded that their frontier now faces enemies who have what they have, and they have rebuilt their magazines on that assumption. The craft is west of them and they have priced it in.

**WHAT IS TRUE:**

**Correct, arrived at without a single illicit act, and the reasoning is sound at every step — including the last, which is closer to the sealed truth than the operators can know.**

The powder reform of **534** falls squarely inside the pair's residence, and so does the commission that produced it (**Pei Wen, 528–533**) and the doctrine amendment that followed (**535**). What the reform actually rests on is the **Jinyang Report**: of nineteen fortified posts in Bing province in 355–361, **two were taken within thirty-one days and a third abandoned before assault**, entered in the returns as *"withdrawn for want of powder"* and unread for six generations. The arithmetic beneath it is exactly the operators' inference: *"The doctrine that a small post defeats a large force rests on the attacker being obliged to close and being broken when he closes. An attacker who also has powder is not obliged to close... The post does not fall to assault. It runs dry and is walked into."*

The operators reason from *"why would you multiply powder against an enemy without powder"* to *"they face enemies who have it."* The sealed Chinese arithmetic reasons from the same premise to the same place. **They have reconstructed the load-bearing sentence of the empire's most closely held document from a requisition, from outside, in a foreign language, without committing an offence.**

They do not get the rest — the two named lost posts, the fifteen-against-sixty shape, the finding that beyond the rail the model cannot work, or the 535 withdrawal clause. Those are sealed to three copies and to a frontier a very long way from Chang'an.

**A cost the Chinese court accepted without ever recording that it had accepted it.** A credentialled foreign metallurgist was resident in the capital across 531–539: the Pei Wen commission, the Jinyang Report, the powder reform, and the doctrine fight. The Shangfang's leak estimate of 537 reckoned carters, clerks and quartermasters. **It did not reckon a foreign scientist with lawful standing, an interest in metal, and nine years to watch the court's face.** That is a real and unbudgeted exposure and it belongs in the target's ledger.

**Verdict: OBTAINED, CORRECT, AND THE HIGHEST-VALUE PRODUCT OF THE OPERATION — obtained entirely from lawful observation of a court under stress.**

### 5. What the optical specialist brings home, which nobody asked for

Because the optical brief is genuinely open and genuinely honoured, this man is the most productive asset either power fields in this period, and everything he takes is given freely:

- Lens geometry, the pendulum clock, the mirror-telescope principle, the spherical earth and a circumference of roughly 45,000 km measured off the empire's own posts (confirmatory — China opened all of this in 517).
- **The Bureau of Measured Things, 536.** One hundred salaried posts recruited **by an examination in mathematics and measurement rather than the classical papers**. It holds the standards, publishes observations, and keeps the **failure ledgers of the standing searches**. This is a structural fact about the target worth more than any instrument: China has made investigation a graded civil appointment.
- **And the ceiling on it.** The Ministry of Personnel fought the recruitment clause for a year and won most of it: Bureau posts capped at the eighth and ninth grades, no path above the seventh, and a man wanting higher office must sit the classical papers like anyone. Cheng Bi's best three students left for the arsenals within a decade. Officials complain about grade ceilings freely; nothing here is secret. **The correct reading — that China's limit on investigation is fiscal and institutional rather than human, against a supply of ~950,000 examination-standard men and rising — is available to an attentive guest and is one of the most consequential things any foreigner could learn about this empire.**

**And what he misses, by one to two years.** The dense **lead-bearing glass** melted at Chang'an around **541**; Fu Yi establishing by grinding that it spreads colour further than ordinary glass for the same bending; his written proposition of **547** that two such glasses shaped against each other might bend light while cancelling colour; and the **549 re-rating** to a directed nine-tonne programme with a dedicated pot house, on Cheng Bi's estimate of glass in the 550s and a lens within a year or two of it.

**Egypt's optical intelligence on China therefore stands frozen at about 539 and is, by 550, stale in the single most consequential particular available. The 541 and 546 placements that would have caught it put nobody in place.**

---

### 6. WAS IT DETECTED

**Yes. From about 532–533. And the response is the most characteristic thing this court does in the whole period: it does nothing visible, and it acts decisively.**

**How it surfaces.** Not through surveillance. Through the **incentives of the men being asked**. A works official or Bureau scholar who is asked about powder by a foreigner is holding a career-ending object. He answers with a courtesy and reports it the same evening, because failing to report is the greater risk. This routes to the Shangfang and the Censorate as ordinary business. Two or three such reports establish a pattern; a metallurgist and an optical man arriving together, one of whom keeps returning to arms while the other is contentedly absorbed in lenses, is not a subtle signature to a directorate that has curated foreign visitors since the fourth century.

**What the court concludes.** Not that Egypt is hostile. That Egypt is a serious power behaving as a serious power — the identical reading China gave Rome's vellum map in the 320s, when it recognised a reciprocal cartographic request as *"a sophisticated intelligence-extraction structure dressed as diplomatic reciprocity"* and read Rome's failure to press as *"evidence that Rome is sophisticated rather than naive, a power that understands the game and respects a player who also understands it."* **This court has a settled precedent for being probed by a partner it values, and the precedent is to think better of them for it.**

**What it does — four things, three of them invisible:**

1. **Nothing is entered in the correspondence.** The 525–550 file records: *"No request for powder knowledge appears anywhere in the court's Egyptian correspondence."* That is a true statement about the correspondence and the correspondence is what the court controls. A workshop question is not a request, and a court that wished to preserve the exchange would keep it off paper. **The file's silence here is a policy, not an absence.**
2. **Access is curated, not restricted.** No door is closed; itineraries drift. The metallurgist's workshop visits tend toward the monopoly foundries, the civil iron works, the rail shops and the ordinary works of the Ministry of Works — all real, all interesting, all lawful, none of them an arsenal. The optical man's programme, being the one China contracted to provide, is enlarged rather than trimmed. **Nobody is ever told no.**
3. **The mirror operation is professionalised — in 534.** *"After 534 the standing tasking of the Aden and Alexandria scholars was sharpened toward boiler practice, plate, and the working of iron under pressure: what they may lawfully observe, what they may ask, and what they may record."* The date is three to four years after the placement, and the content is a rulebook distinguishing observation from asking. **The adjudication is that this is the response: China did not punish the Egyptians, it studied them, and it wrote its own 120 men at Aden and Alexandria the doctrine it had just watched a guest operate without one.** The single largest effect of Operation B on the target was to improve the target's own intelligence tradecraft against the operator.
4. **The pipeline closes without a refusal.** Nominations for the 541 and 546 cycles are not rejected. Berths are allocated, rosters fill, correspondence stays warm, and the names do not appear. Twenty per decade is a finite instrument administered by the Chinese side, and a berth that goes to someone else is not a diplomatic act.

**Why not expulsion, quiet arrest, accident, or death — five grounds, all in the target's own file:**

1. **The debt is enormous and personal to this reign.** Egypt's disease knowledge is *"the most useful thing another court has put into Chinese hands in this reign."* It produced Bamo — mortality falling from twenty-two per hundred to nine, the removal of a barrier that had stopped the empire at the Shan Plateau since the second century. Su's court will not endanger that over two curious men.
2. **China has 120 people in the same position on Egyptian soil, and has already written down why they are untouchable.** From 517, on Egypt: *"eyes only... No clandestine infiltration; the representatives are irreplaceable and a failed attempt forfeits the window itself."* A court that reasons that way about its own assets understands the reciprocal exactly, and will not be the one to establish the precedent.
3. **A punishment is a confirmation.** Killing, expelling, or arresting a man who asked about powder tells the asker that powder is where the secret is, and tells him roughly how much it is worth. Silence and courtesy tell him nothing. This is the same instinct that in 517 withheld the electrical work from Egypt *not* because Egypt would learn China's methods but because *"Egypt would learn only how far behind China is."* **This court's default is to deny the shape of its own concerns, and a scene reveals the shape.**
4. **It would put powder in the record.** Expulsion requires a stated ground; the stated ground goes in the file; and the file is what the court is protecting.
5. **It is not who this reign is.** The same emperor published in full the edict stating that the men at Kuhan died because Luoyang ordered them to sail in a month the ice does not permit and *"the fault is the throne's."* The court's own rule is explicit: *"a failure that can be admitted without arming anyone is admitted, and a failure that would arm someone is sealed."* This is a court that seals and curates. **It is not a court that kills guests, and killing a guest would be the loudest thing it did in twenty-five years.**

**Detection outcome: NOTICED ~532–533. NO CONFRONTATION, NO ACCUSATION, NO EXPULSION, NO HARM, NOTHING ON PAPER. ACCESS QUIETLY RESHAPED; THE TARGET'S OWN MIRROR OPERATION UPGRADED IN 534; THE PIPELINE CLOSED IN 541 AND AGAIN IN 546 WITHOUT A WORD OF REFUSAL. THE TWO MEN GO HOME HONOURED AND ALMOST CERTAINLY DO NOT KNOW THEY WERE BURNED.**

## PART THREE — CROSS-CUTTING FINDINGS

1. **Both operations returned their most valuable product off-brief, and in both cases the tasking was the limiting factor rather than the tradecraft.** Rome sent a watch after tonnage its own tariff ledger already carried and picked up, as garble, a regulation permitting Chinese posts to abandon their ground. Egypt sent two men after a formula that had been purchasable for two hundred years and brought home the fact that Chinese steel has no theory behind it and Chinese investigation has no funded seats. **Neither operator's questions were as good as their operators.**

2. **Han China's counter-intelligence in this period is not concealment. It is cover, curation, and the ownership of careers.** The court states this in its own voice: *"Three things were done about it, all of them cover rather than concealment."* It publishes a frightened reform as a strengthening, announces the reassuring half first and loudest, invents *preservation of force for counter-concentration* so a retreat reads as a manoeuvre, and gives observation posts a fur-and-survey report so a post that will not fight has a visible reason to exist. **A foreign service measuring real objects at a Chinese frontier is being handed a curated set of real objects.**

3. **The court's asymmetry of secrecy is consistent and legible.** *"A failure that can be admitted without arming anyone is admitted, and a failure that would arm someone is sealed."* Kuhan published in full; the Jinyang Report sealed to three copies. Any foreign service that grasps this rule can read the Chinese publication record as a map of Chinese fear — **and everything published in this period about the frontier concerns magazines.**

4. **The empire's true exposure in 525–550 is not espionage. It is arithmetic in the hands of its own clerks.** The court's own words: the fact of the powder increase *"is outside the empire within one to two seasons and cannot be concealed at all"*; the shape takes five to ten years to assemble from convoy sizes; the withdrawal clause is *"discussed in every frontier market within a season"*; and inside the empire the conclusion is available to anyone in Bing or Ji who can read a requisition and count — a population now numbering some 430,000 sub-official clerks and 13–15 million functionally literate people. **Su's own minute is the correct assessment for both operations judged here: the empire is not hiding the conclusion, it is buying the years between the conclusion becoming visible and the conclusion becoming actionable.** Rome and Egypt are each, at 550, somewhere inside those years.

5. **Open for the next period.** Rome holds the raw wagon-counts for the beyond-rail/rail-served ratio and needs one manpower figure from the eastern bank to close it — the five-to-ten-year assembly window opens around 546–551. Egypt holds a correct diagnosis it may not recognise, an optical file that goes stale in 541, and no way back in. And the withdrawal clause is loose in every frontier market on the Dnieper, waiting for someone who knows which posts it applies to.
