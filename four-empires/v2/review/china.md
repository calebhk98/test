# HOSTILE REVIEW — v2/turn-525-550/china.md

Reviewed against `03-han-china.md` (to 500) and `human-turn-500-525/china.md` (500–525).
Findings itemised, most serious first. Every item carries a quote and a verdict.

## TIER 1 — THE COURT HAS BEEN REPLACED

### 1. The empire tells the Maurya Empire that it audited itself and found its own frontier wanting.

> "We add one thing plainly, because you will hear a version of it from the carters if we do not. We have this reign increased the powder held at our frontier posts, substantially, and reorganised how those posts are classified. **We did this after auditing our own records and finding our arrangements older than they should be.**" (E3, to the Maurya)

Set against the canon this is not a variation, it is a different state. Doc 1:

> "A quieter part of the same logic is one China takes care never to state aloud. Its position on the north bank is stretched to the point that it could not in fact survive a determined and sustained assault… To pull the frontier back, or to let the true fragility show, would tell the Maurya Empire the opposite, that China is overextended and cannot fight a real war so far from home, and that knowledge would turn a border not worth attacking into a prize worth seizing."

Doc 2, twenty-five years before this letter:

> "the honest basis for it is one the court can state to itself but **must never let an enemy learn**"

The empire has spent three centuries maintaining a bluff at the Syr Darya whose entire value is the Maurya not knowing it is a bluff. This letter tells the Maurya, unprompted and in writing, that the Han court re-examined its own garrison arithmetic and did not like the answer. The stated justification — that the fact will leak anyway — is precisely the reasoning a Han court does not use; leakage is why you say nothing, not why you volunteer. The document's own section III establishes that the *shape* of the reform (15 days on rail, 60 beyond) "is the arithmetic itself in disguise" and takes 5–10 years to reconstruct; the letter hands over the interpretive key that collapses that 5–10 years to a season.

**Verdict: the single most serious failure in the document. A Han court conceals its weaknesses absolutely, and this one confesses an internal audit to the power its whole western position is bluffing.**

### 2. The same letter names its own magazines to the enemy while pretending not to.

> "In 474 something very like that happened and it cost both of us a great deal. **Our own losses that year and the state of our magazines afterward are matters we have not previously discussed with you and do not propose to now.**"

Doc 1 on what "the state of our magazines afterward" means:

> "The threat is very largely a bluff. China's powder and shot are nearly spent, its marines are depleted, it has no second fleet to send… None of this reaches the Maurya Empire, which sees only a Chinese fleet that annihilated its own and stormed a fortified island in an afternoon, and China, well practiced by now at making a small force look like a large one, is careful to keep it seeing exactly that."

Naming a subject in order to decline it flags the subject. A court "well practiced at making a small force look like a large one" does not write the sentence "the state of our magazines afterward." It writes nothing, or it writes that the fleet was untouched.

**Verdict: sophisticated-sounding sentence, catastrophic content. It tells Pataliputra where to dig.**

### 3. The empire confesses an engineering disaster to Egypt and asks for help.

> "We are building hulls that carry fire under pressure. **In 534 a boiler burst at our southern yard and killed nineteen men, among them the only man who understood the whole of it.** We have rebuilt the knowledge on paper and we have two vessels working our own coast, and **we are certain we are doing several things badly that your yards stopped doing badly generations ago.**" (E2)

And:

> "we do not send you our work on the electrical force, **which would show you only how far behind you we are**"

Doc 2 held the identical reasoning *as an internal reason for silence*: "Withholds the electrical work (Egypt would learn only how far behind China is)". Doc 3 takes an internal justification for concealment and prints it in a diplomatic letter. Announcing to a peer power that you are far behind it, that your key technical man is dead, that his knowledge died with him, and that you know you are doing several unnamed things badly, is a self-assessment no memorial to any throne contains, still less a state paper to a foreign court.

**Verdict: a modern institution volunteering its weaknesses to a partner. Two of the eight tells in one paragraph.**

### 4. It tells Rome its survey timeline, its casualty count, and the gap between what it charts and what it can hold.

> "It is slow: we have lost four ships and two hundred and thirty-eight men on that coast, **our furthest chart lies six hundred li beyond our furthest post**, and our reckoning is that the survey will not reach the far strait for a hundred years, and that **we could put no weight on any far shore for a generation after that.**" (E1)

Doc 1 on exactly this class of disclosure:

> "China has no map it is prepared to hand over in any case, and it reasons that if Rome grasped the true distances involved, how far China's western positions lie from any support, Rome would see how exposed that frontier is and might be tempted to attack."

The document is aware of this precedent — it cites it — and then does the opposite in the same letter:

> "**That sketch was ours.** We drew it because our western positions were then so thinly held that the true distances would have shown you a frontier you could have taken, and **we would draw it again in the same circumstances.**"

It confesses a two-century-old deliberate deception to a foreign emperor, gains nothing by it, and asserts it would repeat the deception while committing the opposite error on the next page. This is not a court reasoning; it is an author demonstrating self-awareness.

**Verdict: the empire has told Rome, in numbers, that its entire Pacific position is unsupportable for 125 years. Han China does not do this.**

### 5. The document is written as a modern after-action review, in a single essayist's voice, across six incompatible registers.

Sampling:

> "**The court records that it watched the wrong thing for twenty years while writing careful memoranda about the thing it was watching.**"
> "**A tasking is a list of assumptions with question marks after them, and the assumptions are the part nobody audits.**"
> "**The humiliating part is that this was discoverable.**"
> "## The Fusang Grant (549): **The Court Priced the Wrong Thing for Twelve Years**"
> "the empire had spent sixteen years assuming a wall where there was a door"
> "A man holding an instrument that measures loudness cannot hear a note held quietly for an hour."
> "which is a statement about the filing and not about the heavens"

Section XIII is a lessons-learned register with root-cause findings and remediation owners ("Ordered for the next reign…"). Section III is a policy post-mortem with a stakeholder-cost breakdown. The Censorate report, the arsenal ledger, the imperial edict, three foreign letters and the Board handover are all in the same aphoristic register — and that register is a twenty-first-century management essay. No memorial to the throne is titled with the throne's own error.

**Verdict: evasive fluency throughout, and self-critical framing no Han document would contain. The prose is the tell.**

### 6. Concern for peoples it has never met, produced by no pressure and costing something.

> "Standing orders unchanged and extended to the settlers: do not garrison, do not claim beyond what is worked, **take no ground from any people found there by force**, and sell them no iron and no powder on any account. **Land is to be broken where nobody is farming.**"

And offered to Rome as a joint regime:

> "we would add a fifth if you will have it: **that neither of us sell iron or gunpowder to any people of that continent**, and that each hold his own subjects to it."

Doc 1's actual record of this empire meeting other peoples: the Wusun "incorporated as Han subjects and frontier auxiliaries **or pushed further west ahead of the line**"; the displaced of the Pontic steppe "**ground down between the two advancing worlds**, so that by 500 they are a dwindling and landless scatter rather than a people with a territory." Six hundred landless settlers on virgin ground, five months beyond any supervision, are told to farm only where nobody else does — and no faction demands this clause, no cost is paid for it, and it constrains the colony's survival.

**Verdict: ethics without pressure. A non-instrumental concern for foreign populations, imported wholesale.**

### 7. The peasantry is now handled by a modern police doctrine.

> "**No firearms were used, on the administrator's own order**, and none were held by the crowd… **The administrator was commended for not firing.** The Gu house compensated the families within the month."

Doc 1's six-century pattern: "the resulting power asymmetry is so severe that the revolt is suppressed within weeks"; the Yellow Turbans crushed by "explosive weapons"; "provincial garrisons equipped with explosive weapons and fire devices suppress the uprising within months"; the whole dynasty's internal stability rests on "catastrophically asymmetric" force against unarmed subjects. Four thousand arsonists burning a licensed works is exactly the case where this state fires. Commending restraint, and a private house voluntarily indemnifying the rioters' families inside a month, are values from another civilisation.

**Verdict: the state that industrialised the massacre of peasant revolts now gives medals for holding fire.**

### 8. Refusing an advantage on principle, repeatedly, with the principle stated in modern terms.

> "Su refused, on the argument — recorded, and the best single evidence of the man — that the frontier armies, the rail contractors and now the mill houses all reckon in a coin the state issues, and that **a state which quietly makes its own coin worth less has begun taking from everyone who trusts it**, in a way it cannot stop once begun and cannot see the bottom of."

This is a sound-money argument about public trust, in an empire whose founding fiscal act was seizing a strategic mineral into monopoly and whose treasury has run on extraction for six centuries. Han and Xin courts debased. Nine separate refusals are stacked in this reign — the Syr Darya war (twice), the preventive strike, twenty hulls, a third squadron, the Irtysh line, far-shore settlement (later reversed), debasement, the mill levy, the saltpeter price rise. Several are properly paid for with ledger arithmetic, which is the document's best craft. The aggregate is nonetheless a self-limiting constitutional state.

**Verdict: refusal on principle is on the tell-list, and this document has the most principled emperor in the history of the file.**

### 9. Rivals treated as partners, with institutionalised bilateral machinery.

> "That each of us name a port — we propose Panyu and any port you choose — where a commander of the other may send **a written account of an incident within ninety days, and that a written account so sent be answered.** That an incident so reported and answered **is not treated by either court as an act of the other's state until both accounts have been read.**"

Doc 1's settled posture toward the Maurya: "managed at every step with the awareness that the partner is clever, self-interested, and **not to be taken at its word**." This is a naval incidents-at-sea agreement with notification thresholds, a dispute channel, response obligations and an attribution-restraint clause — INCSEA, 1972. Likewise the Roman proposal: non-settlement buffers, first-arrival-confers-no-title, prior notification of force movements, and a mutual arms-transfer embargo on indigenous peoples. That is a four-clause arms-control treaty for a continent nobody has mapped.

**Verdict: the tributary-system empire that could not even share a diplomatic register with the Maurya now drafts multilateral confidence-building measures.**

## TIER 2 — REGRESSION TOWARD OUR OWN HISTORY

### 10. Ten discoveries from our timeline arrive inside one twenty-five-year turn, each with its real-world mechanism named.

Doc 1 states the governing rule of the whole project:

> "The steps did not happen faster relative to one another because gunpowder was discovered early; the entire dependency chain was simply translated backward by roughly a thousand years in absolute time… **This is why the empire of 500, for all its six centuries with gunpowder, has no machine guns and no locomotives.**"

The turn delivers, in twenty-five years:

| In this document | In our history |
|---|---|
| "**Cold infusion of qinghao** (the virtue destroyed by boiling…)" | Artemisinin, Tu Youyou 1972 — including the exact finding that heat destroys the active compound |
| "a single pair of copper and zinc plates in brine… a persistent rather than momentary deflection" | Volta's pile, 1800 — the metals named correctly |
| "tall masts rising at Aden on dedicated engines… **the force is not stored and released, it is made continuously, by motion coupled to steam**… beginning with the lodestone" | Faraday, 1831 |
| "two glasses spreading colour differently might, shaped against each other, bend the light while cancelling the colour" + "dense heavy glass from a lead-bearing melt" | Hall/Dollond achromatic doublet, 1729–1758 — crown-and-flint pairing, and the striae/cord furnace obstacle that actually delayed it |
| "**soft copper obturating ring** seated in a recess cut in the breech face, which the firing pressure expands outward against the chamber wall" | de Bange obturator, 1873 |
| "**hydrostatic proof testing as institutional doctrine**… condemned if it complains; **ductility preferred to hardness**; low-carbon plate graded for pressure work… **master-gauge system**" | Manchester Steam Users' Association / boiler inspectorate, 1850s–60s |
| "**bean sprouts grown continuously aboard in shallow trays** and issued to every man daily… citrus peel and ginger carried as ship's stores" | Lind/Cook antiscorbutics, 1753–1795 |
| "**the Proof Office at Longbian**, under the Bureau… **and not under the yard it inspects**… authority to condemn a boiler over the objection of the Commissioner" | Independent regulatory inspectorate, 19th c. |
| "**the changping chao**… receivable for the land tax… issue capped at one third of certified granary stock" | Song *jiaozi* (11th c.) fused with 20th-c. reserve-ratio governance |
| "**the Bureau of Measured Things**… holds the standards of length, weight and time, records and publishes observations, and keeps the **failure ledgers**" | National metrology institute, BIPM 1875 |

Every single one is a real-world result reached for by name, and several arrive with their real-world *failure mode* attached, which is how you can tell they were retrieved rather than derived.

**Verdict: this is not an alternate history advancing on internal causes. It is our own history's greatest-hits list scheduled into a single reign, in flat violation of the canon's stated pacing rule.**

### 11. A matchlock army is issued a reliable gas-check breech-loader.

Doc 1 at 500: "The hand cannon is mature after four centuries of refinement, **roughly a matchlock-equivalent weapon with a two-to-three-minute reload**". Doc 3 at 550: "**about three times the sustained rate of fire of a muzzle piece** and, more to the point, a rate a crew will actually use," in general issue to every holding garrison by 549.

The obturating ring is the 1870s solution to the 1870s problem. Dropping it onto a matchlock-era small-arms base skips the flintlock, the percussion cap, the self-contained metallic cartridge and rifled small arms entirely — every intermediate step on which the real breech-loader depended.

**Verdict: technology far too fast, and out of dependency order.**

### 12. The reign's climactic strategic insight rests on a foreign capability invented for the purpose.

> "| Maurya hulls carrying engines | ~19 | **~70** |"
> "**A hull under steam does not need wind and does not grant an enemy the choice of range. Seventy of them do not.**"

Neither doc 1 nor doc 2 gives the Maurya a single steam vessel. Doc 1: Egypt has steam; the Maurya "chase the same force from plants and stones, badly, with no working device." Doc 2's spy report says only "seven to fifteen ships pulled from normal traffic and reinforced for something heavier than cargo." The "~19" 525 baseline **does not exist in doc 2** and is fabricated here so that growth can be measured against it. The Maurya are then handed the exact capability required to obsolete China's naval advantage and to justify the document's most dramatic self-correction.

**Verdict: control violation and canon fabrication in one table. The turn's centrepiece conclusion is built on a number the author invented about somebody else's navy.**

### 13. A "private audit of 525" is cited six times and does not exist.

> "The private audit of 525 put the true figure at 80 to 90 million"
> "That audit put the ceiling at twenty-five to forty per cent and rejected four in five"
> "The private audit's charge — that the Northern Road is the old doctrine wearing analysis as a coat"
> "The preventive strike on the Maurya fleet, **named in the private audit of 525** as the dangerous idea then circulating"
> "the same reflexive concealment the private audit of 525 named"

`human-turn-500-525/china.md` contains none of this. There is no audit, no 80–90 million figure, no literacy ceiling of 25–40%, no charge against the Northern Road, no named dangerous idea. A phantom prior document is invented and then used as authority to overwrite doc 1's figures.

**Verdict: fabricated canon deployed to license revisions the author wants. Disqualifying on sourcing grounds alone.**

### 14. Retroactive fault insertion: prior turns are rewritten so this turn has something to fix.

Four instances of the same manoeuvre:

- **Breech-loaders.** "**The record needs correcting here, because the reign has been claiming credit for the wrong thing**… the paper seal never worked properly and everyone at the sharp end knew it… arsenal casualty returns show **4–7 burns per hundred per year of issue**." Doc 2 said simply "By year five to eight the breech loader reaches the field." A thirty-year hidden failure history with invented casualty statistics is inserted so that 541 can be an achievement.
- **Population.** Doc 1 calls the census "**the firmest demographic anchor in this entire history**" and gives 70 million at 500. Doc 3 asserts a true figure of "**82 to 88 million**" with only 1.5 million real growth since 525 — i.e. doc 1 was wrong by fifteen per cent on its firmest series.
- **Literacy.** "**The single-number estimate used previously was wrong in both directions**." Doc 1's 5–7 million at 500 becomes 13–15 million at 550, and doc 1's projected ceiling of "four in five" is rejected for "forty-five to sixty-five per cent."
- **Territory.** "**the last version of this file ran the two together, which flattered the programme by several hundred kilometres**"; "The previous draft of this declaration gave a single figure and **it was wrong twice**."

**Verdict: a turn that spends its credibility auditing its predecessors rather than advancing from them. Also the auditing-itself-and-telling-everyone tell, aimed inward.**

## TIER 3 — NUMBERS, CAPABILITY, AND MECHANISM

### 15. The li/kilometre unit collapse. Every distance in the letters is wrong by a factor of two.

> "we have charted some **seven hundred li** of the far coast about our landfall" — where D2 states "about **700 km**"
> "our furthest chart lies **six hundred li** beyond our furthest post" — where D2 states "roughly **600 km** further east"
> "**five thousand li** of open ocean" for a crossing the court itself provisioned at ~29,000 km
> "our figure near **forty-five thousand li** in our measure" — doc 1 and doc 2 both say **45,000 km**

A li is roughly 0.4–0.5 km. The document uses the two units at 1:1, so either the internal figures or the diplomatic figures are out by half — and the circumference sent to Rome is now a different number from the one the canon established.

**Verdict: hard arithmetic error, in the one figure the empire has independently verified and is now exporting.**

### 16. The largest new recurring charge is not the largest new recurring charge.

> "Carried by Su against the Da Sinong's objection on cost, **it is the largest new recurring charge of the reign**: roughly 135 tonnes of silver-equivalent annually, rising to about 160 by 550."

Section C: "Rail construction (**of which southern trunk 285**) | 417". The southern trunk did not exist at 525 except as survey. 285 > 160.

**Verdict: numbers contradicting other numbers, in the document's own money declaration.**

### 17. The ever-normal note does not do the thing it was invented to do.

The stated problem: "grain rots, costs more to move than it is worth, and **cannot pay a soldier at Zhenxi**." The instrument: "redeemable at any commandery granary in grain at the standing ever-normal price **or at any commandery treasury in bronze cash**." Use: "restricted by edict to **frontier military pay**".

So the soldier at Zhenxi — thousands of kilometres from any commandery granary — is paid in a claim redeemable either in the grain that could not reach him or in cash from a reserve the same document calls "**thin, as it has been since Wu**." The note relocates the problem; it does not solve it. And "**Mobile resources roughly doubled in eleven years**" is asserted against no baseline figure anywhere in the declarations.

**Verdict: the reign's flagship fiscal instrument fails on its own stated mechanism, and its headline benefit is unquantified.**

### 18. The southern squadron sustains, for twenty-one years, a rate above doc 2's stated best-season ceiling.

Doc 2: "the survey advances on the order of **50 to 75 km per season at the floor, up to about 150 in a good season**." Doc 3: "It has charted roughly **three thousand four hundred kilometres** in the same span" — 162 km/year sustained across twenty-one years, every year a better-than-good season, in the fog and ice the document elsewhere insists is the binding constraint. The combined "205 km charted per year" likewise runs past doc 2's "roughly one to a few thousand kilometers of surveyed coast per generation."

**Verdict: the turn's headline territorial number breaks the pace envelope the previous turn set, and the document congratulates itself on honesty while doing it.**

### 19. Capability suppressed for convenience: the rotary steam engine sits idle for forty years next to the exact market that needs it.

> "Rotary steam driving a mill | Achieved 525; **not yet installed in any licensed works.** The mills run on water and the good sites are largely taken, **which is the commercial pressure that will buy the engine**"

Assembled and then declined: a proven rotary engine (509), thirty-one capital-rich licensed works, "the good river sites are largely taken by 550", a state iron monopoly happily selling them "shafting, gearing, bored cylinders, plate", coal delivered to Luoyang "at roughly a third of its former carriage cost", and a rail two-thirds of the way to the Huai. Doc 1 named the missing condition as coal and labour never meeting; this turn builds the rail that makes them meet and then stops it one river short: "The continuation across the Yangtze into the Wu mulberry country is **surveyed and not begun**."

**Verdict: every precondition for a coal-fed mill economy is delivered and the ignition is withheld by authorial fiat. Capability suppressed for convenience.**

### 20. Knowledge with no channel: a sixth-century surgeon reasons his way to a deficiency disease.

> "Across both records **the men who kept longest were the cooks and their boys**, and what distinguished the cooks was that they ate green things the rest of the crew did not"

This requires the concept that *absence* of a substance causes disease — a framework absent from doc 1's medicine, which is regimen-and-agent based throughout. It also requires the Eastern Crossing's surgeon to be handed the Northern Road's arctic wintering mortality records as a control cohort ("with the Kuhan wintering reports beside him"), i.e. cross-programme cohort comparison as a research method. Doc 1's own standard for this kind of finding is the opposite: gunpowder, copper anti-fouling and the qinghao result are all recorded as "true and unexplained."

**Verdict: the conclusion is 1750s epidemiology; the channel to reach it does not exist in this world.**

### 21. Institutions invented without local need.

- **The Proof Office** (550): an independent boiler inspectorate with statutory authority over the yard it inspects — for a fleet of **two** coastal hulls.
- **The Bureau of Measured Things** (536): a national standards body with published observations and maintained failure ledgers, recruited by a mathematics examination.
- The note's audit chain, including **a rotating-fifth sampling regime**: "inspectors who visit a rotating fifth of the granaries in person and accept the rest on the intendants' returns."

Sampling-based audit with an acknowledged sampling weakness ("four fifths of the count is the intendants' own word") is statistical assurance methodology.

**Verdict: three modern administrative institutions, none of them proportionate to the demand that supposedly produced them.**

### 22. An unpaid "first in N centuries" that quietly demolishes the load-bearing premise of the entire history.

> "| Licensed mill engineers and machine-fitters (private) | ~1,400 |
> **The last line is the one to watch. It is small and it is the first concentration of precision machinery and skilled metalworkers in the empire outside the state's own walls.**"

Doc 1's central institutional mechanism, invoked to explain the absence of warlordism, the failure of Wang Mang, and the survival of the 437 succession: "independent action required controlling state arsenals, and **no regional commander could control the arsenals without the cooperation of the central weapons administration**." A private concentration of boring machinery, gauges and skilled metalworkers ends that monopoly. The document notices — it opens a spy tasking on whether the Gu house could bore a barrel — and then does nothing, names no loser, and stages no fight.

**Verdict: the history's load-bearing institutional idea is dissolved in a footnote to a workforce table, with no political fight and no named loser.**

### 23. Assertions about other powers, delivered as fact, in a document whose preamble forbids exactly that.

The preamble: "**Acts by Rome, Egypt and the Maurya are not written here as facts**; where the empire has acted toward them, the act is recorded and the response is left where it belongs."

Then:

> "Boiler practice in Egypt **is not concealed and never was**"
> "**Egypt cures it with a tree sap from its western provinces.**"
> "the court is not going to obtain it by asking, **because a state does not sell the one material its advantage rests on**"
> "we record with it that **you hold two great islands, a fortified island off the stone coast, a coaling port and a colony on a northern river**"
> "**you have proclaimed the reduction to ten parts in the hundred** along the road without waiting for a reply"
> "**That Venus shows phases as the moon does, and that small lights keep company with Jupiter**"
> "| Crews | short-rationed, drills thin | **regularly drilled at live practice** |"

Egyptian policy, Egyptian materials science, Egyptian provincial geography, Roman colonial holdings, Roman astronomical results, Maurya tariff decisions and Maurya crew training are all stated flatly. Some may derive from incoming letters; the document never says so, and its own preamble promised otherwise.

**Verdict: the file writes the discipline it then abandons. And it hands Rome the Galilean observations, which is our own history arriving as a diplomatic gift.**

### 24. Internal contradiction on the constraint governing the investigation tier.

> "The tier is the number of **salaried posts the state funds**, and that is the binding constraint"

Of the 1,600, the Bureau holds 100. The other 1,500 are arsenal design offices (~400), natural-philosophy circles (~350), Academy physicians (~250), independent "instrument, lens and clock makers **working to no fixed pattern**" (~200), astronomers (~180), and men abroad (~120). Two hundred of those are explicitly not state-employed and several other categories are not "posts."

**Verdict: the stated binding constraint is contradicted by the table immediately above it.**

### 25. The toll is twelve per cent and also ten per cent at the same date.

> §X: "The Silk Road toll is levied at **twelve per cent, as it has been since 477**, and no change has been recorded at any Chinese post on the road."
> XIV-3: "We note that you have **proclaimed the reduction to ten parts in the hundred** along the road… We accept the reduction."
> E3 (547): "We propose no change to the toll, **which we pay at twelve parts in a hundred**"

**Verdict: the snapshot contradicts the correspondence in the same file.**

### 26. Bamo garrison arithmetic never reconciled.

§VII: "Colonel Teng Yin… took **400 men** down to Bamo"; losses "eighty-nine dead of sickness." D1: "**garrison 300**." Plausible by subtraction, never stated, and the resupply figure (8 t/yr) is never tied to either number.

**Verdict: minor, but the document elsewhere makes a virtue of exposing its own arithmetic and here does not.**

### 27. Unrequested gifts, both directions, all convenient.

**Given away for nothing:**
- The scurvy remedy to Egypt — "You have asked for no knowledge and **we send some anyway, being in your debt and disliking the position**." Handing a range-multiplier to the world's leading steam navy in the same year the Board concludes that navy's technology has obsoleted China's fleet.
- The qinghao cold-infusion secret to Egypt — the key to tropical country, given to the power holding the Red Sea.
- The telescope to Rome complete, plus the flint-glass proposition and its failure mode: "**if your glassmakers solve it first we would rather they solved it knowing what we know.**" The empire funds a directed programme for the achromat in 549 and hands its rival the research lead in 550.
- The circumference method *with its error analysis*: "what it assumes and where it is weak."

**Received unasked, and exactly on time:**
- Egypt's boiler doctrine, hydrostatic proof, and the seal insight — "the item the empire's engineers did not suspect and would not have found" — arriving in the same year the Board discovers it has a boiler problem. The document says so out loud: "**which is the one piece of luck in this document.**"
- Rome's telescope construction "**offering us the construction of yours complete, unasked**".
- Rome's Venus phases and Jupiter's moons.
- The Maurya cutting their own toll unilaterally.

Four peer powers, all behaving generously and self-correctingly, inside the same twenty-five years.

**Verdict: both halves suspect. The gifts out are self-harming; the gifts in are precisely the missing pieces, delivered on cue.**

### 28. Su's published self-blame edict.

> "Su's edict of 544 states that **the men at Kuhan died because Luoyang told them to sail in a month the ice does not permit, that the fault is the throne's**, and that the throne will not repeat it. **It was published in full**"

Imperial rescripts of self-reproach are not unknown, so this is the most defensible item on the list. But the document then generalises it into a disclosure doctrine — "**a failure that can be admitted without arming anyone is admitted, and a failure that would arm someone is sealed**" — which is a modern communications policy, and it pairs it with a named monument to the dead.

**Verdict: individually survivable, and part of a pattern that is not.**

## WHAT THE DOCUMENT GETS RIGHT (recorded so the rest cannot be dismissed as blanket hostility)

- **The doctrine amendment is properly paid for.** Wei Zhao retired rather than read the clause; Yuan Kai and Cui Shu "**neither was punished; neither was promoted again in the reign**"; the garrison-establishment office extracted the euphemism "*preservation of force for counter-concentration*" and the sealing of the report. A political fight with named losers and a bought concession. This is how the rest of the document's "first time" claims should have been handled.
- **The Irtysh refusal** — "**That refusal, and not the Jinyang Report, is the act by which this reign decided the north is not held**" — is the right kind of causal honesty, and it is a decision on cost rather than on insight.
- **The iron re-rating** is genuinely Han: a differential price schedule inside an existing monopoly that "creates no instrument, requires no definition of a mill in law, cannot be appealed as an irregular exaction." Exactly the salt-and-iron logic of doc 1.
- **The residue arithmetic** — 4,390 revenue, 4,256 committed, 134 residue, 45 genuinely free — is the best-argued passage in the file and correctly explains why the twenty hulls were refused.
- **"Any theory of what makes steel steel — No"** and the longitude admission are correct restraints, consistent with doc 1.
- **The qinghao and copper-sheathing results are recorded as unexplained**, which matches doc 1's epistemic standard even where the results themselves are imported.

## SUMMARY VERDICT

Three failures are structural rather than reparable.

1. **It is not the same court.** The single most consistent behaviour across six centuries of canon — absolute concealment of internal weakness from foreign powers — is inverted in three separate letters, and the document treats the old behaviour as a pathology it has outgrown ("the same reflexive concealment the private audit of 525 named").
2. **It is regressing hard toward our own history.** Ten of our timeline's discoveries, several with their real-world failure modes attached, land inside twenty-five years, in direct violation of doc 1's explicit pacing rule.
3. **It rewrites its predecessors rather than continuing them.** A non-existent "private audit of 525" is invented as authority; the population, literacy, breech-loader and territory records of docs 1 and 2 are all overwritten; and the turn's central strategic conclusion rests on seventy Maurya steam warships that no prior document permits.

The prose is excellent and that is part of the problem: the fluency is doing work that evidence should be doing, and the document's habit of naming its own errors reads as rigour while functioning as licence.
