# Hostile Review — Maurya, turn 525–550 (v2)

Reviewed: `/home/user/test/four-empires/v2/turn-525-550/maurya.md` ("doc 3")
Against: `/home/user/test/four-empires/04-maurya-realm.md` ("doc 1"), `/home/user/test/four-empires/human-turn-500-525/maurya.md` ("doc 2")

Verdict up front: this is not the Maurya realm. It is a modern internal-audit culture wearing Sanskrit place-names. It contradicts doc 2 on the single most load-bearing fact in the previous turn (whether the Rome bargain was performed), it contradicts itself on that same fact inside its own file, it asserts the internal regulations, infrastructure and diplomatic offers of three foreign powers, it hands rivals its whole scientific corpus and its own confessed failures for nothing, and its treasury summary does not reconcile with its own table.

Ordered most serious first.

## 1. THE ROME PACKET: doc 3 reverses doc 2's completed bargain, and then contradicts itself about it. [§4, §1]

Doc 2 states the trade was performed, in detail, as the moral centrepiece of the reign:

> "So the realm made the trade, and made it on the discipline Rome had itself once taught... **It sent the account whole and honest**, the weight and length and killing range, the rate of fire, the effect on timber and on men, the construction, the means of ignition, and its engineers' full reasoning, **giving no seam for Rome to find**, because it now genuinely held the weapon and a true account was the only safe one." (doc 2)

> "The realm kept the physical weapon, kept the channel, and **gained the engine**." (doc 2)

Doc 3, in its 548 letter to Rome:

> "The full account of the Chinese hand-firearm... was written, sealed and complete in our diplomatic office in the summer of 524. **It was never sent.** ... the packet sat in a press at Pataliputra for twenty-four years, correct and finished and undispatched, while your engineers lived in our yards and our engines ran on your method."

**Verdict: a direct retcon of the previous turn's central event. Doc 2 says sent; doc 3 says never sent. One of the two is void, and it is not the human document.**

## 2. Doc 3 contradicts itself on the same packet, nine years apart, and never notices. [§4, §6]

Its own 539 letter to Rome:

> "we sent you, in the last reign, a complete and honest maker's account of the hand-weapon we had met in the eastern sea, and you gave us the marine engine, **and both sides kept the bargain**." (doc 3, letter of 539)

Its own 548 letter: the packet "was never sent."

**Verdict: the realm either told Rome a flat lie in 539 or the 548 confession is fabricated; the file records neither possibility, which means the author did not read his own earlier letter.**

## 3. The 524 sealing date is chronologically impossible against doc 2. [§4]

Doc 2's sequence: (a) realm asks Rome for marine steam, offering an account of a weapon it does not have; (b) Rome demands a maker's account, "and it sent them before the realm's agents had brought home any Chinese weapon at all"; (c) the espionage "at last delivered its object"; (d) *then* "Steam crossed its threshold in about six years, moving from the stationary mine-pumps at Kolar to a working marine engine, **the work quickened by the Roman method and the Roman engineers in the yards**."

Six years from 500 puts a working marine engine at c. 506, which requires Rome's half — method plus engineers — delivered c. 501–503. Doc 3 instead says:

> "Three of your engineers have lived among us since **528**."

**Verdict: Roman engineers arrive 22 years after doc 2 has them already in the yards accelerating an engine that ran in 506. The only account doc 2 permits is: weapon in hand early in the reign, account sent, engine received c. 502–506. Doc 3's "sealed in 524, engineers from 528, never sent for 24 years" is not a possible chronology; it is a different reign.**

### Summary of the pattern

Doc 1 and doc 2 describe a court that conceals, embellishes, resents, denies, and weighs every gift before giving it — and that records its own errors *retrospectively, as costs*, in the third person, marked as the court's assessment. Doc 3 describes an institution that discloses pre-emptively, apologises, publishes its dead, corrects its rivals' impressions of it, gives away its scientific lead unpriced, and narrates its own moral failures in the present tense to an audience outside the fiction. The fiscal "correction" that the document exists to deliver is built on a deleted clause of doc 2 and does not reconcile with its own table. The chronology of the Rome bargain is not merely wrong but reversed. Nothing in section 3's foreign assertions is sourced to an observation.

Required to make this the same country: restore doc 2's completed weapon-for-engine trade or explain the reversal against doc 2's text; strip every claim about foreign internal regulations, infrastructure and third-party offers back to what an agent observed; delete the volunteered confessions to Rome, China and Egypt; price the mathematics; reconcile the trade-revenue line; and rewrite the narrator back into the third person.
