# Hostile review: the prose of turn 525–550

Thesis under test: the polish is now damaging the reasoning — sentences built for effect rather than accuracy, and the simulation worse for it.

## 1. How the prose has changed

Measured, not asserted:

| | human rome | human china | v2 rome | v2 china | v2 egypt | v2 maurya |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| words | 3,923 | 4,358 | 19,877 | 25,993 | 18,716 | 24,714 |
| mean sentence (words) | 35.0 | 29.4 | 25.8 | 27.5 | 23.9 | 26.2 |
| em-dashes per 1,000 words | 0.0 | 0.0 | 6.8 | 6.4 | 7.9 | 4.9 |
| bold spans | 0 | 22 | 251 | 356 | 221 | 286 |
| "That is / This is / It is …" sentence openers | 4 | 4 | 34 | 31 | 41 | 40 |
| "nobody / no one / never read" | 1 | 1 | 12 | 27 | 45 | 58 |
| prose paragraphs closing on a ≤14-word sentence | 8% | 25% | 27% | 23% | 28% | 15% |

The AI prose is not more ornate. It is **shorter-sentenced and punchier** — which is the finding. The human register is flat, long, subordinate, and refuses cadence: "Rome resolves the internal argument that the previous generation left open, and resolves it toward commitment rather than caution. The fiscal conservatives, who had wished to treat the west as a matter to revisit later, lose. The determining facts are three…" Thirty-five words per sentence, zero em-dashes in 3,900 words, no paragraph engineered to land.

What is present in the AI documents and wholly absent from the human ones:

- **The kicker.** A short declarative closing a paragraph: "The Rhine did not rise. That is the reign's quiet achievement and nobody will ever build a monument to it." "Rome nationalised nitre and nothing else." "We are cash-rich and slack-poor." "Nobody at court has said the word."
- **The chiasmus / reframing.** "It is not a character flaw. It is a budgetary rule." "That is not chemistry; it is bookkeeping applied to fire." "A delegation is a foreign thing arriving. An official is an internal thing returning."
- **The italicised minute as epigram.** Severianus speaks in aphorism eleven times: *"We may have the war whenever we like. That is exactly why we need not have it now."* The human Rome document contains no quoted speech at all.
- **Typographic emphasis as argument.** 251–356 bold spans per document. The human china.md has 22, all of them section labels, none mid-sentence.
- **The moralised closer.** "So the money went east while the empire went west. This is not stupidity. It is what an institution does with a fear it has organized itself around: it keeps paying the fear, because the payment is what proves the fear was always real."

## 2. Rhetoric standing in for mechanism

**Egypt, the Sinai.** "It is the finest fortification on the earth and it faces a direction from which no army has come in six hundred years." Then: "it keeps paying the fear, because the payment is what proves the fear was always real." The epigram is offered *as* the causal account. The actual mechanism is in the sentence above it — engineering-corps commissions, Delta cement contractors, garrison-town economies — and it is a good mechanism. The epigram adds nothing and displaces the question the good mechanism raises: what does 120,000 talents of contractor capture look like on the appropriations sheet, and why did no rival line outbid it?

**Egypt's Cape tower.** "a young prince … who wanted, once, to build the opposite of a secret." A dedication — *To the water that was hidden, and is not hidden* — is doing the work of a motive. 34,000 talents is the same order as the entire Long Range Works (26,000), the programme the navy could not stop asking for. The prose makes the tower poignant instead of making it costed.

**Maurya, the southern continent.** "A mediocre reign did not stop a colony. It only stopped steering one, and a colony on good ground does not need steering; it needs a throne that notices which way it has gone." Beautiful, and it replaces the analysis of *who* in Pataliputra had an interest in not looking.

**Rome, the compass.** "This is Rome's whole method in one page: an effect nobody understands, measured, tabulated, printed, and turned into a standing order within four years." This one earns it — swinging ship at a known mark, error varying with heading, per-hull table sworn at fitting-out. The image sits on top of a mechanism. Contrast Egypt's "It is a shape" (the placed-charge doctrine), where the aphorism is the only load-bearing member.

**Rome, the west.** "The land era … does not end. It crosses an ocean" is the human turn's one flourish, and it is a *characterisation* of a fact stated in numbers immediately before it. The AI documents invert that order: the figure arrives to ratify a formulation already made.

## 3. Where the prose makes a claim unfalsifiable

**The worst instance is Egypt's blindness north of the Orinoco.**

> "it has no report of a Roman hull anywhere in the western ocean in the twenty-five years since, because it has never seen one and has sent nobody north of the Orinoco to look. It has never charted the Caribbean… The minute was filed. No leave was given and none was refused; the request was simply never answered, which in this service is the same as a refusal and costs nobody a decision."

Set against Rome's own file: 450,000 Romans in the west, four islands reduced, "the **Lesser Antilles** and the **Bahaman shoals**: charted, watered at, not held," a garrison at Caracas and an Egyptian battery on the Orinoco island since 543. Roman hulls watering down a chain that ends at Trinidad, an Egyptian garrison at its foot, and twenty-five years of nil contact. That is a physical claim about an ocean, and it is carried entirely by an unanswered minute. "Costs nobody a decision" is the most elegant sentence in the Egypt file and it is buying the largest implausibility in the turn.

**Second: Maurya's "by births alone."** Line 356: "A hundred and thirty-five thousand people at 525 and four hundred thousand at 550 … doubling every twenty-odd years by births alone." The file's own basis note (lines 125, 529) says "Some hundred and thirty thousand were landed across the reign; the remainder is births." The rhetorical summary deletes the 130,000 immigrants because the sentence is better without them — and 135k→400k in 25 years is 4.5% a year, which no natural increase produces. The arithmetic is correct in the appendix and wrong in the prose, and it is wrong in the direction of the epigram.

**Third: Rome's hand firearm.** "That finding is wrong, and the reason it is wrong is the reason it will stand." The physical cause is stated (burst barrel, dead *fabricator*, no metallurgical basis) — and then overwritten by an institutional one ("a battery of successful bombards to point at instead… nobody in the Consilium has noticed the pattern repeating in the same building"). The institutional reading is unfalsifiable and it is doing work the metallurgy could have done alone.

## 4. The filed-and-forgotten device: a count

| Document | Instances |
|---|---|
| Egypt | Sinai memorandum of 531 "received, filed, and not acted upon"; Panehesy's isolation proposal, "received, entered, and not acted upon. No one opposed it"; the Orinoco survey minute "simply never answered"; the 542 finding on governing the Atlantic, "the thing nobody was made responsible for"; the Pacific shore, "It was never rejected. It was never put"; Australia, "revisiting it was nobody's task"; the twice-deferred rule, "It was not adopted. It was not rejected"; the arc-nitrate finding wrong for twelve years; the China-arsenal confession "let the matter lapse"; the Maurya letter sixteen years in a monopoly's file at Aden. **Ten.** |
| Maurya | Bhūtiśarman's memorandum of 541, "filed under commercial correspondence" (invoked three times); the carter intelligence "assembled in 549 … the only promotion in this reign and it happened by accident"; the standing instruction of 535 executed by a clerk against Rome's boiler metallurgy; the mill-accident correction never sent; "No man not paid on the toll has ever read these returns"; the five cheap decisions "left for the next one". **Six or seven.** |
| Rome | The 537 turned-branch finding resting on "one item, one report, one factor," run eleven years; the southern-arrivals roster held for years unread — "We asked who was visiting … and were sent a list of men going home"; the Maurya firearm account possibly lost in Rome's own registry; the railhead density error. **Four.** |
| China | The nitre-price indicator trusted fifty years; the untasked Aden census "not requested by anyone". **Two.** |

Twenty-plus instances across four documents, against **two** in the human turns (Rome's century-old unexamined Han remark about reaching Indian waters; the "electrical arc" note from Aden). And the human instances behave differently: Rome's unexamined intelligence is *acted on the moment it is noticed* — it licenses the southern survey inside the same turn. The AI instances mostly resolve into nothing happening.

**Assessment: yes, this has become a way of having it both ways.** The document earns credit for the insight (a correct analysis exists, is dated, is quoted) while the state pays nothing for it. It is a free move: any capability gap, any missed opportunity, any implausible ignorance can be furnished with a clerk. Egypt's file **knows this** and says so — "the rule will be used to explain everything and will therefore explain nothing… The Pacific shore and Australia are advocacy failures. The magazine and the aerial are not." That paragraph is the single best piece of self-criticism in the four documents. It is also an inoculation: having named the abuse, the document commits nine more instances of it, and the naming is what makes them feel earned. Egypt uses "nobody/no one/never read" 45 times; Maurya 58. The human turns use it once each.

## 5. Irony as a substitute for causation

- **Maurya:** "Five years earlier a steam engine standing beside an incorporating mill had killed a hundred and eighty-four of our men, and the discipline that governs whether an engine belongs beside anything is the metallurgy of boilers." The juxtaposition is exquisite and the actual cause is one sentence later and much duller: a generally-worded 535 instruction, unreviewed, executed correctly. The irony is presented as the finding; the standing order is the finding.
- **Egypt:** "The fortress in the east is the finest in the world and faces an empty desert. The largest chemical works on earth takes nitrogen out of the air … and cannot put one grain of it in a shell. There are six thousand rifles, eleven guns and no round certified to fire from them. The tower at the Cape can be seen for thirty miles. And we have not looked north of the Orinoco, in twenty-five years, once." Five ironies in series, in place of a paragraph on which of them is actually load-bearing. Only one is (the ammonia-to-acid catalysis), and it is the one that had a mechanism given fifty lines earlier.
- **Rome:** "**Rome's guns are limited by dung, not by founding.**" This one is legitimate — 1,100 tons of refined nitre, 1,500 tons of powder, a ten-ship squadron's season above the annual output, an estimate of the 570s. The irony is a *label* on an arithmetic, not a substitute for one. It is the model the others should be measured against.
- **Rome:** "A watch two hundred and fifty years old bought a number the counterparty was trying to give us." Also earned — the comparison that produced it is described.

## 6. Three passages that would be improved by being made worse

1. **Egypt, the Sinai closer.** Delete "This is not stupidity. It is what an institution does with a fear it has organized itself around: it keeps paying the fear, because the payment is what proves the fear was always real." Replace with: *the eastern planning office's 531 memorandum was not costed against any alternative; no rival appropriation was tabled; the corridor schedule renewed on its own standing authority in 533, 540 and 547.* Duller, and it tells us whether the money could have moved.
2. **Maurya, line 356.** Delete "doubling every twenty-odd years by births alone" and "A mediocre reign did not stop a colony. It only stopped steering one." Replace with the appendix's own numbers: *130,000 landed, more than half after 545 on private bottoms; the balance natural increase; treasury contribution falling every year.* The passage loses its ending and gains the fact that the colony grew because private shipping carried people the throne did not pay for — which is a different and better story than mediocrity.
3. **Egypt, the Orinoco minute.** Delete "which in this service is the same as a refusal and costs nobody a decision." Replace with: *Egyptian hulls have not passed north of 10°N; the Caribbean is unsurveyed; contact with Roman shipping in the Lesser Antilles has not occurred and the file offers no account of why not.* Ugly, unquotable, and it exposes the hole instead of decorating it.

Also: cut Severianus's minutes from eleven to three. A man who speaks only in epigrams is a character, not a fiscal officer, and the epigrams are doing the persuading his ledgers should do.

## 7. Verdict

**Net helping, with a locatable crossover.**

The prose is genuinely load-bearing where a sentence *compresses a mechanism already given*: the nitre arithmetic, the compass deviation table, the 2,700-mile road against 900 miles of coal, the Constitutio Oceanica as a chartered monopoly seated in the province that made an emperor in 355, the Dnieper railhead density error. In those places the sentence is a handle on an argument, and the human turns do the same thing more flatly and less well: the human Rome's "Gibraltar closes a Mediterranean door while Egypt's Atlantic door stands open on the far side of it" is an epigram too, and it is earned.

**The crossover is where the memorable sentence arrives before the mechanism rather than after it.** Three reliable markers of having crossed:

1. The causal word is an institution rather than a quantity — "nobody proposed it," "never answered," "filed," "no advocate against a schedule" — and no number is nearby.
2. The paragraph ends on a sentence under fifteen words. 23–28% of prose paragraphs in Egypt, Rome and Maurya do; 8% in the human Rome. The kicker is where accuracy goes to die, because a kicker cannot carry a qualification.
3. The summary sections disagree with the appendices. Maurya line 356 versus lines 125 and 529 is the proof case: the prose and the arithmetic are in the same file and only the prose is wrong.

The damage is concentrated in **Egypt**, which has the highest em-dash rate (7.9/1,000), the second-highest "nobody" rate, and the one claim in the turn that is physically hard to believe — twenty-five years of a shared ocean with no contact — held up entirely by an unanswered minute. Egypt is also the document that diagnoses the disease most precisely, which is the clearest possible evidence that the diagnosis is not curing it. **Maurya** is second, with the "filed under commercial correspondence" motif carrying three separate resolutions and one arithmetical error inside a good sentence. **Rome** and **China** are largely on the right side of the line: Rome's aphorisms mostly sit on top of magazine returns and range figures, and China's on tonnages of silver.

One structural recommendation: for every sentence of the form *X is not Y; it is Z*, require a number or a named decision in the same paragraph. Egypt's own qualification paragraph already states the rule — "a council that files all four under the same heading has found a comfortable single cause for its whole condition" — and the fix is to enforce it against the prose as well as against the council.
