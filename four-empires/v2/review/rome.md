# HOSTILE REVIEW — ROME, 525–550 (v2/turn-525-550/rome.md)

Reviewed against `02-rome-alternate.md` (to 500 AD) and `human-turn-500-525/rome.md`.

Verdict up front: this is a well-written document that is not a Roman document. It authors three foreign empires, converts the Roman state into a self-auditing modern administration in twenty-five years, and reconstructs our own history's Atlantic sequence — virgin-soil collapse, gold works, African labour purchase, chartered monopoly company, saltpetre monopoly, compass deviation tables — while claiming each step follows internally. Its principal defence mechanism is confession: it pre-empts every objection by naming it, which lets it then assert whatever it likes.

---

## TIER 1 — STRUCTURAL VIOLATIONS

### 1. Rome writes the other three powers' turns. Wholesale.

The document's second half (§XVIII–XXI and the 550 replies) is built on incoming letters whose contents Rome invents in operative detail, then accepts clause by clause.

> "**On the far-continent rule: we accept it, and we will sign the four articles as you wrote them** — no settling, garrisoning or claiming within a stated distance of the other's established and worked settlements; first arrival giving no claim beyond what is held and worked; no approach in force without prior notice by the ordinary channel; and neither of us to arm the peoples of that continent against the other."

> "**On the notice clause: we accept every article as you drafted them, without amendment** ... Three years' notice before the end of a span; written delivery to the other's transit officers at the Euphrates and simultaneously to the other's court; effective only on written acknowledgement by both; a signed midsummer statement from each side's transit officers that no notice has been given..."

> "**On the powder: yes, and we ask for all of it.** Your proofing standard, your gauged charges, and the design of your incorporating mills. **We accept your terms, which were none**"

> "The pendulum clock is received with your caution attached"; "**That you told us what you kept is worth more to us than the tube would have been.**"; "**On Fusang, and on your twelve men.**"

Rome has here drafted a Han treaty, a Maurya treaty, a Maurya gunpowder transfer at zero price, a Han gift schedule, a Han disclosure doctrine, and an Egyptian proposal — and then had Rome agree to all of them. **Verdict: the single worst failure in the file; Rome has played four empires and given itself every concession it wanted.**

### 2. Foreign intent asserted as established fact.

> "**Egypt is not sailing to fight us in the west. Egypt is *moving house* to the west, and has been while we argued about it.**"

> "Egyptian building at Alexandria runs **two to three armoured hulls a year — plated, screw-driven, and fired by oil rather than coal. No massing in twenty-five years. Most new hulls go west.**"

> "**Westward provisioning is confirmed completely** ... Family emigration under land grants out of the Delta and Cyrene; seed; breeding stock; temple fittings. From 540 a cured leaf under royal monopoly coming back east."

> "**A firm negative on the canal.**"

> "for the reason your treasurer gave your court: a thousand souls fed across an ocean cost more than the ground returns. **Your treasurer and our *magister militum* wrote the same memorandum in different tongues.**"

> "they came home because **your captains held to an order we did not know existed**"

> "they have built eleven waystations, offer escort at cost, and invite us to complain through the channel"

Doc 1's governing rule: "Where Rome believes something uncertain, it is marked as Rome's belief rather than as established fact." Here Rome knows Egyptian emigration policy, Egyptian land grants, an Egyptian royal tobacco monopoly, Egyptian naval fuel chemistry, Egyptian standing rules of engagement, a Han treasurer's minute to his own court, and Maurya road works. **Verdict: Rome is narrating its rivals' internal affairs; the dissent note at §XIX.3 does not undo it.**

### 3. The epistemic caution is built, then demolished, and the demolition is the finding.

§XVI enters a careful caution — exactly the discipline docs 1 and 2 established:

> "Rome has *not* verified that the coast is under Egyptian authority and this record does not assert it."

Two hundred lines later:

> "Rome therefore **withdraws the caution entered at section XVI** ... They are administered Egyptian provinces, and the men who administer them come to Alexandria and file."

On what? A "roster" of titles read by grain-and-coffee factors in a port. Doc 2's Rome held its interpretations loosely on purpose. **Verdict: the caution is stagecraft; the document uses the vocabulary of humility to license certainty it has not earned.**

## TIER 2 — THE MODERN STATE

### 4. Rome volunteers its own intelligence error to the power it is in an arms race with, and gains nothing.

> "**One correction we owe you, and we make it because we are the party it embarrasses.** We have watched your bank of the river for two hundred and fifty years and told ourselves your settled people there numbered forty to sixty thousand. On re-reading our own observers' sheets this year we find they measured where they could see best and multiplied it across country they had not seen. The figure is fifteen to twenty-five. We tell you we were watching, which you have long known, and we tell you we were watching badly, which you may not have. **We would rather you learned the size of our error from us.**"

This is the paradigm case the brief asks about. Rome hands the Han: the corrected figure (confirming or refuting the Han's own count), the *method* of the Roman error (so the Han knows how to feed Roman observers in future), and the confession that Roman frontier estimation is unreliable by threefold. Rome receives nothing. No imperial chancery in any century volunteers a correction to a rival's benefit for the aesthetic satisfaction of candour. **Verdict: a modern press-office instinct wearing a purple cloak.**

### 5. Rome discloses its newest weapon to the dominant military power, unasked.

> "**we hold a stable powder and cast bombards, emplaced, seven batteries, on our own walls. We are not sending you the ratio, the granulation or the founding.** We tell you we have it because you would rather know we kept something than believe we kept nothing."

Doc 1: "Rome adopts an explicit doctrine of managed disclosure toward all three rivals, concealing its steam weapons, its naval program, and the depth of its Egyptian intelligence." Doc 3 has Rome abandon that doctrine — "Rome has practised managed disclosure as formal doctrine for two hundred years and has never once done what you have just done ... **We are adopting it**" — i.e. Rome adopts a rival's doctrine on the rival's word, in a letter, and pays for it with the disclosure of a new weapon system to the state whose firearms it cannot match. **Verdict: refusing an advantage on principle; a rival's stated preference used as Rome's decision rule.**

### 6. Rome retracts a claim to a rival's benefit and forgives the debt.

> "**We withdraw that sentence.** We have this year found ourselves wrong about the size of a foreign population by threefold, wrong about the meaning of a list ... A court with that record does not get to assume the fault is yours ... Until then the ledger is open and neither of us is in default of it. **Send nothing again on our account.**"

Rome performed a complete marine-engine transfer (fourteen engineers, four years). It received nothing. Its own file classifies the counterparty "strategically unreliable." It responds by withdrawing the claim and instructing the debtor not to pay. **Verdict: this is a modern liberal state's conception of good faith, not a Roman one; the correct Roman move — leverage — is not even considered.**

### 7. Rome unilaterally restrains its own navy and self-limits its exploration.

> "**We reciprocate the order and put it in writing: Roman vessels will not fire upon Egyptian vessels except upon being fired upon first**, and that instruction goes to every Roman captain in the Mediterranean, the Atlantic and the western ocean this year."

> "our ships will not, southward of that river, without prior notice to you"

> "we will give ours there first without waiting to hear whether you agree."

A no-first-fire order to a fleet the same document calls "beyond counting" against Rome's fifty-two, plus a unilateral advance disclosure of Roman Atlantic movements, plus acceptance of an Egyptian territorial line Rome explicitly cannot verify. **Verdict: three refusals of advantage on principle in one letter.**

### 8. Unrequested gifts, in volume, to a power classified unreliable.

> "**We send you the method regardless.**" (corning)
> "our metallurgy of boilers — which metals, worked how, fail where, at what heat and pressure ... four hundred years of our failures written down and **it is the most useful thing Rome owns**."
> "**We believe that finding is wrong** and we tell you so"
> "We offer in return, **and send with this letter without waiting for an answer**: the construction of our telescopes complete..."

Rome names its most valuable single asset and posts it, unasked, unreciprocated, to Pataliputra — twice, the second time after being refused by clerk without reason. It also hands the Maurya a research steer on the hand firearm. And to the Han it sends telescope construction, glass, figure, mounting, and its heliocentric dispute. **Verdict: Rome's outbound value transfer this turn exceeds its inbound and it is framed as virtue.**

### 9. Self-audit, published, in a voice no imperial record contains.

> "Rome does not call this an intelligence tasking against a Roman province. **It is one.**"
> "That finding is wrong, and the reason it is wrong is the reason it will stand ... **nobody in the Consilium has noticed the pattern repeating in the same building.**"
> "Not one paper in the Consilium's record connects the Constitutio Oceanica to the events of 355. The court spent its attention on the Rhine, won there, and paid for it here **without noticing the transaction**."
> "**We are the best-informed state we have ever been and the worst at reading what we know.**"
> "Rome had been estimating its own technical workforce from guild rolls ... **It now counts it.**"

Doc 1 flagged omniscient narrator intrusions explicitly. Doc 3 fuses the imperial record with a hostile external auditor who knows what the Consilium failed to notice — while also being the Consilium's own file. **Verdict: knowledge with no channel and a voice with no owner; if nobody noticed, nobody wrote this.**

### 10. Labour and subject peoples: a direct character reversal.

Before (doc 1, on the 350s settlement): "It addresses the symptoms **through imperial self-interest rather than any concept of worker dignity**."
Before (doc 2, on the Maya): "**Rome does not respect statehood; it respects the capacity to do it harm** ... Rome would butcher a Maya army and could raid the city's wealth close to at will."
Before (doc 2, on islanders): "Chiefdoms that resist are broken, those that submit are enslaved or set to labor."

Now:
> "**Collegia given standing to bargain, not merely to sue** — a registered collegium may treat with the owners on hours, wages and conditions, and the agreement is enforceable in the provincial courts as a contract."
> "the Patriarch of Rome blessed the Concordia in a published homily arguing the case in **the language of human dignity rather than productivity**"
> "**A moral quarrel Rome did not expect.** ... the argument was theological and therefore unanswerable in an empire that had just made human dignity the language of its labour law."
> "**The record contains no earlier document accusing the imperial economy, in print, of killing the people it employs**"
> "Severianus refused it before the Senate in 542 ... *'We may have the war whenever we like. That is exactly why we need not have it now.'*"

Doc 1 did set up a labour resolution ("the pressure is building toward a resolution that 500 AD has not yet reached"), so a settlement is legitimate. Its *content* is not: enforceable collective bargaining, a right of prior consultation on fiscal measures, an inspectorate with power to close a floor pending appeal, and an accident fund paying on a printed schedule are twentieth-century instruments, not bought peace. **Verdict: doc 1's explicitly cynical Rome has been replaced by a Rome with a conscience it acquired off-screen.**

## TIER 3 — REGRESSION TOWARD OUR HISTORY

### 11. The downward revision of the Han frontier settlement serves our history, not the simulation.

> "The corrected estimate is **fifteen to twenty-five thousand, not forty to sixty.** We had trebled a frontier population by measuring its busiest square mile and multiplying."

Ask the question the brief asks. Doc 1 built the Han's western settlement as a deliberate divergence: "Rome watches the Han outposts on the far bank **harden into permanent civilian towns, the settlement pattern recognizably like Rome's own veteran colonies**, with Chinese civilian presence thickening year by year toward the boundary." Doc 2 kept it as a live strategic concern. Doc 3 cuts it by two thirds on no new observation — only a re-reading — and the effect is to return the Pontic steppe toward our timeline's emptiness and to reduce a peer's continental reach to something more familiar. Note what the correction does mechanically: it deletes Rome's only hard number on the Han frontier, it is *given away* to the Han (finding 4), and it is generated inside Rome's own head with no external input. **Verdict: a correction with no internal cause, whose only consequences are that the world resembles ours more and Rome knows less.**

### 12. The Atlantic sequence is our history, beat for beat, including place names.

> "the subjugated population of the two great islands, above four hundred thousand when the conquest began, is under seventy thousand in 550. **Valleys of the Cibao that fed the gold works** in 535 are empty ground in 548."
> "Gold works at **Aurifodinae Cibaenses** in the Cibao interior."
> "the labour question ... runs into one place: **the African trade**"
> "Rome resolved to make the attempt. It licensed the house of Vibulenus of Ostia as buyer, instructed it to open the purchase at Alexandria"

Hispaniola gold placers in the Cibao, worked by an encomienda-style subjugated population, collapsing by five-sixths in a generation, answered by African slave imports. That is Columbus, Ovando and the 1518 asiento with Latin names on. Nothing about this world required it: Rome has mature steam, and the document itself argues (§V, correctly, following doc 2) that a mature engine beats free labour — then reaches for slaves anyway. **Verdict: the most on-rails passage in the file; the divergence has been surrendered for the familiar story.**

### 13. Institutional imports with real-world fingerprints.

| In the document | Where it comes from |
|---|---|
| "**Legal personality for the registered *societas*** ... liability bounded at his subscription" | 1855–62 Companies Acts |
| "**The *privilegium fabricae*** — imperial protection of a registered method or machine for twenty years ... in exchange for deposit and publication at term's end" | Statute of Monopolies / modern patent bargain, verbatim |
| "**The *curatores fabricarum*** — a standing inspectorate ... power to close a shaft or a floor pending appeal" | Factory Acts inspectorate, 1833 |
| "**The right of the scrape** — licensed *nitrarii* may enter and work the floor of any stable, cellar, byre, dovecote or *mansio* on notice ... the owner obliged to permit and **forbidden to floor over**" | French *droit de fouille* of the *salpêtriers*, down to the prohibition on flooring |
| "**The imperial pre-emption** — all refined nitre is sold to the fisc at a fixed price ... Rome nationalised nitre and nothing else" | Régie des poudres / Ancien Régime saltpetre monopoly |
| "a printed **deviation table struck for each iron hull individually at her fitting-out** ... he **swung his ship** through the whole circle of the compass" | Flinders 1801, Barlow 1820, Airy 1839 — including the term of art |
| "the western trade licensed through a chartered body, the ***Societas Oceani Occidentalis*** ... holding the exclusive right of carriage on the run" + "landing anywhere else being contraband" | East India Company charter + Navigation Acts |
| "British revenue funds imperial works while British infrastructure receives less than it generates" + under-declaration + a crowd taking the crews off seized hulls + magistrates declining to disperse it | Boston, 1768–73, relocated to Hibernia |
| "a **reading desk** within the *officium custodiae* — thirty men ... whose entire duty is to re-read old product against new" | all-source analysis cell, twentieth century |
| "the first standing counter-intelligence instrument in Roman history" | ditto |

**Verdict: an institutional shopping list from our 1600–1950, delivered in one twenty-five-year turn, each item given a plausible-sounding local trigger after the fact.**

### 14. "This is not borrowing" — protesting because it is.

> "The tax-farming *societates* were sold the right to collect the *vectigal* for five-year terms, paid in full at the front ... four hundred and sixty millions of ready money against a nominal future value near six hundred. **This is not borrowing and Rome cannot borrow, having no bond market and no conception of one.**"

460 now against 600 later is a discount instrument at roughly 5.5% annualised. The document knows it, which is why it says so twice. Doc 1's constraint ("the state itself still funds its wars from taxation and thin reserves rather than borrowing, having no bond market to borrow in") has been evaded by relabelling. **Verdict: evasive fluency where a plain statement belongs — Rome invented securitised revenue and denied it in the same sentence.**

## TIER 4 — ERRORS OF FACT, NUMBER AND CAPABILITY

### 15. Nine thousand millions of sesterces vanish.

> "Across twenty-five years Rome took in something near **116,000 millions** and spent something near **107,000**."
> "It still cannot borrow: no bond market, no conception of one, **reserves of a few months' revenue that one serious war erases**."

A 9,000M accumulated surplus is roughly twenty months' revenue at the 550 rate. Either Rome banked it — in which case its stated reserve position is false and "the limit that would bite in a war" is gone — or it did not, in which case the free margin was not 280–415 a year and §XV's whole table collapses. Both cannot be true. **Verdict: hard arithmetic contradiction at the centre of the fiscal section.**

### 16. "Rome has crossed the western ocean since 402."

Doc 1: the first crossing's report "arrives at the very end of the century" (c. 500) and doc 2 opens with the *second* voyage returning after 500. The letter to the Han is wrong by a century, in a formal declaration, on the single fact the Han asked four times about. **Verdict: flat contradiction of doc 1 in the document's most consequential disclosure.**

### 17. The western establishment's cost does not reconcile.

> §I: "running at something near **a hundred millions a year** ... and would rise toward **a hundred and eighty** as the coal work began."
> §XV: "Western establishment — colonies, garrisons, transport, ports, the coal colony | **about 2,600** | 2.4%"

100 rising to 180 across twenty-five years averages ~135–140, i.e. 3,400–3,500, not 2,600. The headline claim that "the whole American venture and the whole research programme together" is "under four percent" rests on the low figure. **Verdict: the reassuring number is the wrong number.**

### 18. Naval spending is too cheap for what it buys.

690M across the turn (27.6M/yr, 0.8% of the 3,320M military line) buys: +22–32 warships, twelve iron-framed hulls, nineteen screw hulls, a 50% range improvement, the boiler programme, and the combined-passage proof. Meanwhile the *Fulmen* loss, the ocean run and the coal colony are described as the reign's central bet. **Verdict: the strategic centrepiece is funded like a side project; the numbers do not carry the narrative's weight.**

### 19. Naval ordnance suppressed by a false dichotomy.

> "**Rome can arm the walls or it can arm the fleet. It cannot do both**"
> "the *magister classis* put a ten-ship gunned squadron's hard season above the entire annual refined output"

Rome holds 1,500 t/yr of powder plus "a war reserve of about two years' production" (~3,000 t) for seven batteries whose "whole annual consumption is a few score tons." Arming *two* ships, or arming ten with a restricted allowance and no exercise firing, or arming the Mediterranean squadron only and leaving the ocean fleet unarmed, are all obvious and none is considered. The all-or-nothing framing exists to keep Roman warships gun-free into the next turn. **Verdict: capability suppressed for convenience, with a minuted excuse attached so it reads as a decision.**

### 20. Knowledge with no channel, repeatedly.

Grain, coffee and dyestuff factors in a commercial port deliver: the plating, propulsion and **fuel** of naval hulls under construction in a military harbour; a twenty-five-year negative on massing; the *legal form* of an Egyptian royal tobacco monopoly; the contents of westbound emigrant cargoes (seed, breeding stock, temple fittings); "a firm negative on the canal"; and an accurate year-by-year roster of provincial governors' reporting visits. Doc 1's Alexandria agents could not tell decorative banding from a communications system. **Verdict: the network's yield is set by what the plot needs, not by what the cover permits.**

### 21. Corning is too fast, and too conveniently unexplained.

Stable ratio 531 → first bombard 538 → corned powder 544. In our history corning trails first gunpowder artillery by a century and a half. The document's cover — "Nobody at Argentoratum can say why a grain outperforms a dust" — makes the *acquisition* look Roman while the *timing* is a gift. **Verdict: thirteen years from first ratio to wall-breaking ordnance; the ignorance is decorative, the speed is the problem.**

### 22. Minor number and continuity friction.

- "About **one hundred and twenty-seven millions** ... against a hundred and fifteen at 525 — **a shade under half a percent a year**." 115→127 over 25 years is 0.40%/yr. Doc 1 gives 110M at 500; 110→115 is 0.18%/yr, unexplained.
- Industry revenue 880 (doc 1, 500 AD) → 1,610, of which the *collatio* is "above 300." The residual 430 of growth is unexplained.
- Frontier-countryside literacy rises "because the collegia now bargain in writing" — collegia bargaining is an industrial-city instrument; it cannot be what moves the *frontier countryside* most.
- "every ocean-capable hull Rome owns is committed to the western run and the fleet cannot be in two oceans" — yet a customs squadron out of Isca seizes three hulls at Portus Magnus, and the Mediterranean notification regime with Egypt presumes a Mediterranean fleet.
- Doc 2: the compromised branch is "quarantined and left untouched, no replacement walked into it." Doc 3 passes a controlled item into it in 537. Reasonable evolution, but it is a reversal and is not acknowledged as one.
- "Gunpowder and ordnance — 310 millions, of which the nitre establishment is 35 millions a year by 550." A per-year figure in a turn-total column; the nitre sub-line plausibly consumes two thirds of the whole line, leaving ~110M for twenty years of ordnance development, a proof-house, and seven batteries.

### 23. Ethics without pressure, in the one place the document should be cold.

> "**Priscus is alive.** ... Ordered: **no rescue, no approach, no inquiry through any channel that could name him** ... A man who is alive and has not come back may be held, or may have found a reason to stay, and **Rome has no way to tell the two apart and no right to assume the first**."

"No right" is the tell. The operational conclusion (do not blow the channel) is correct and needs no moral premise; the document supplies one anyway. Same structure at §V, where a bishop's letter "changed no policy" but is given a paragraph of moral weight the record would not carry. **Verdict: correct decisions given modern reasons.**

## SUMMARY OF WHAT WOULD HAVE TO CHANGE

1. Delete every incoming letter's content and every clause Rome "accepts as drafted." Rome may record that letters arrived and what Rome asked; it may not write the Han's four articles, the Maurya's notice clause, or Egypt's proposals.
2. Restore "Rome believes" to every statement about Egyptian intent, Egyptian shipbuilding, the African provinces, Han settlement and Maurya road works. Reinstate the §XVI caution and delete its withdrawal.
3. Cut the volunteered correction to the Han, the bombard disclosure, the no-first-fire order, the unreciprocated boiler-metallurgy and corning gifts, and the retraction of the Maurya ledger claim.
4. Justify the Dnieper downward revision with an external observation or delete it. As written it exists to make the map look like ours.
5. Reconcile the 9,000M surplus with the reserve claim; fix the western establishment total; fix "since 402."
6. Either arm some warships or stop pretending the nitre constraint is all-or-nothing.
7. Choose a voice. Either this is Rome's record, in which case it cannot see what the Consilium failed to notice, or it is a narrator's, in which case say so as doc 1 did.
