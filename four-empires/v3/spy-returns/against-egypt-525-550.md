# Foreign Collection Against Egypt, 525–550 AD

Adjudication by the neutral intelligence adjudicator. Prepared for the coordinator only.

Ground truth read: Egypt's history to 500, Egypt's 500–525 turn, Egypt's 525–550 turn. No other country's turn document was opened.

**Marking.** **Firm** = the agent saw, handled, counted or bought it himself. **Reported** = someone told the agent. **Field inference** = the agent went beyond what he saw; kept to a minimum and flagged in place.

**Standing note on Egyptian counter-intelligence.** Egypt runs a dedicated counter-intelligence establishment against agents it knows Rome and India both maintain on its soil, and it runs deception as readily as collection (the Yemen coffee-origin account, running since the first century; the electrical misdirection sold to India, which ran twenty-five years). This turn Egypt formally restricted its standing false accounts (Item 4) to matters a rival cannot go and settle by looking, and moved supervision of them to a review board. Its named compartments this turn are electrical engineering, vacuum work, radio, radar, aircraft, the Atlantic crossing route, fleet dispositions, and the depth of the reserve (Item 19); aircraft numbers, ranges, engine life and basing sit in the highest compartment by name (Item 15); the fever bark's wild stands and plantation siting sit in the highest compartment on the coffee practice (Item 8).

**Egypt's own record for 525–550 contains no counter-intelligence event and no entry under "Intelligence returns."** Accordingly none of the four operations below is adjudicated as detected, rolled up or fed. That is a finding, not an oversight: the previous turn recorded a Roman agent caught in port, this one records nothing.

## A. Rome — Alexandria network, continued

Cover: ordinary commercial staff of the formal Roman mission and the merchant houses. Run by the mission office under the imperial secretariat. One branch silent since the previous turn, quarantined.

**Tasking read.** "Size and heading of the shipbuilding in the home yards" is taken as scale and what the building is pointed at. "Any sign of provisioning for a westward voyage" is taken literally.

### Collected

**A1. The quarantined branch produced nothing and was not touched.** No agent was walked into it across the twenty-five years. No product, no loss, no exposure. Recorded as a deliberate nil. *(Firm — negative, the mission's own file.)*

**A2. Transport hull construction at Alexandria and the Delta yards rose markedly and steadily across the period.** The merchant houses supply cordage, sailcloth, pitch and ships' ironwork to these yards and count what they supply and what they see on the stocks. The count in the 520s was a handful of hulls a year at the commercial yards; by the 540s the network was counting on the order of a dozen to eighteen hulls a year launched or fitting out at Alexandria and the Delta together, of a build that is broad, deep and cargo-carrying rather than a warship's. *(Firm as to the local count. See the caveat at A9 — this is a share of a total the network cannot reach.)*

**A3. The building is pointed at carriage, not at battle.** Of the hulls the network could put a hand on at the commercial quays, the great majority were transports and river craft. *(Firm.)*

**A4. Major steam hulls lying in the Alexandria roads carry metal plating on their sides, and the newest carry it as built rather than as patched.** *(Firm — seen from the water, over years.)*

**A5. Some major hulls carry metal frames and wires at the masthead of a kind no rigging requires.** The number carrying them grew across the period. The network cannot say what they are, and did not guess. *(Firm as to the object. Nil as to function — see A11.)*

**A6. Egypt is moving settlers out of Alexandria and the Delta ports continuously, in families, with livestock and tools, on subsidised passage, and has done so every sailing season across the whole twenty-five years.** This is not concealed and is not concealable: the instrument is the old Madagascar one, offered publicly — land grants surveyed and registered at the destination, exemption from the land tax for fifteen years, subsidised passage for a family with its stock and tools, and harbour works, roads and cisterns built in advance of arrival. The terms were had from the recruiters and from the registry, being matter of public record. *(Firm as to the instrument and its terms. Firm as to the fact and continuity of the outflow.)*

**A7. Those transports sail west out of Alexandria and do not pass into the canal.** They are gone for long periods and a proportion do not come back at all. *(Firm — sailing observation in the roads and at the canal mouth.)*

**A8. Harbour talk from returning Egyptian crews, gathered over years and consistent between tellers: a crossing of the western ocean; a temperate country of level treeless grass on black soil with two harbours facing each other across a great brown estuary a hundred miles wide, where wheat and cattle take at once and horses have run wild; and separately a hot coast of reefs, mangrove and rain forest with river mouths.** Towns are named in the talk. *(Reported. Consistent across sources and across years, which is why it is entered, but it is sailors' talk and not a document.)*

**A9. The network could not aggregate an empire-wide shipbuilding rate.** Egyptian hulls are built at yards on the East African coast beside the hardwood, on the Red Sea, and in the African interior on the great rivers, none of which the Alexandria network reaches. Any figure it returns is a share of an unknown whole. This is stated so the sending court cannot mistake a local count for a national one.

**A10. Grain: Egyptian grain leaving Alexandria did not fall across the period despite the city and the country plainly carrying more people.** Grain factors attribute it to provinces that formerly drew Nile grain now feeding themselves. *(Firm as to volumes handled; reported as to the reason.)*

**A11. Aluminum, plated goods and precision instruments continue to be offered for sale in Alexandria at monopoly prices, and the aluminum in quantities larger than the metal has previously been seen in.** *(Firm — purchased.)*

### Attempted and failed, or nil

- **Warship strength, build rate, refit cycle, fleet disposition.** Nil. Egypt holds fleet dispositions in a named compartment, and the naval yard has been closed to foreign eyes since the relief mission of 365–368 was admitted to the port and refused the secure yard. The network can count hulls in the roads on a given day and nothing more.
- **What the masthead frames at A5 are.** Nil. Egypt holds radio and radar in named compartments, holds the apparatus to fixed shore stations and warships only, and puts it in the hands of no trader and no agent abroad. A capability cannot be read off an aerial by a man on a quay, and the network correctly did not try.
- **The route of the westward voyage — courses, distances, landfall, the depot chain.** Nil. The Atlantic crossing route is a named compartment and Egypt's sailing directions have been kept under guard for four centuries. Nothing was obtained beyond A8.
- **The African-provincial settler stream.** Nil, and important. The larger part of Egypt's settler movement ships from African provincial harbours and never passes Alexandria at all. The network cannot see it and does not know it exists.
- **Anything of Egypt's industry, reserve, aircraft, or interior works.** Nil across the board. Nothing in this operation's product touches them.
- **Egypt's diplomatic intent behind the note on the Atlantic coast of Africa.** Not attempted, and correctly so — a commercial staff cannot report a council's reasoning. The mission received the note itself through the formal channel; that is diplomatic receipt, not collection, and is not entered here.

**Assessment of the operation.** Well placed, asking questions answerable from where it stands, and it succeeded on both limbs of its tasking. The failure surface is entirely the compartments, and the operation did not walk into them.

## B. Rome — Berber gold traders of the Saharan routes, new from 548

Cover: ordinary gold purchasing, a centuries-old commercial relationship. Run by the office of the *praefectus* of the African provinces. Intelligence use begins 548. Rome states it looks for nothing inside a decade.

**Two seasons of running, 548 and 549, with the 549 product reaching the praefecture in 550.** Rome's own stated horizon is correct and the adjudication follows it: almost nothing is available yet, and what is available is the traders' own book.

### Collected

**B1. The routes these particular traders walk, their stages, their waters and their termini at both ends.** This is their trade and they can describe it exactly. *(Firm.)*

**B2. The southern end of every route they use lies under Egyptian administration, with Egyptian posts, an assessed toll taken once, and a standing Egyptian buyer at the entrepôts.** *(Firm — they pay the toll and sell to the buyer.)*

**B3. Poles carrying wire run along the corridor stages, guarded, with small manned houses at intervals.** The traders describe the object and cannot say what it is for. *(Firm as to the object.)*

**B4. The price at which gold was offered to them at the northern termini in 548 and 549, and the weight their own houses moved in those two seasons.** *(Firm — their own book.)*

**B5. Elders' account that the volume moving on these routes has not greatly changed within memory, and that the trade has run under Egyptian arrangement for longer than any of them has been trading.** *(Reported, and soft. Two seasons cannot test it and the sending court should not treat it as more than a starting hypothesis.)*

### Attempted and failed, or nil

- **Whether the cargo of the loaded ships counted moving north along the Atlantic coast is gold.** **Nil, and unanswerable from this operation's standing.** A caravan trader in the interior and at the Saharan termini is not present when a hull is loaded on the Atlantic shore, has no commercial reason to be, and would be conspicuous if he went. The question requires eyes on a wharf and this operation has eyes on a route. It should be re-tasked to a maritime operation or dropped.
- **Whether volume has changed in two generations.** Nil beyond B5. A two-season-old operation cannot measure a two-generation trend, and the traders' own books do not run that far. This becomes answerable, if at all, in the 560s or 570s.
- **Egyptian gold output.** Nil. Output at the Akan and Nubian and Zimbabwe workings is a matter of the Egyptian mine directorate's returns and is not visible from a trade route. Critically, Egypt is deliberately holding gold output moderate as a matter of policy — that decision exists only inside the Egyptian council and nothing on a caravan route can reach it. See the coordinator note.
- **Anything of Egyptian shipping, the Atlantic coast, the crossing, or the western holdings.** Nil, and outside the operation's reach entirely.

**Assessment of the operation.** Sound cover, genuine access, and a correctly modest horizon stated by its own owner. It is standing in the right place for questions about routes and in the wrong place for questions about ships. It was not detected; nothing was asked in these two seasons that a gold buyer would not ask.

## C. The Maurya Realm — East African coast, ordered 529, running twenty-one years

Two resident factories, Gujarati and Omani, twenty-five to fifty years established and never used for the realm's business before. Ship-masters of the Oman gum, hide and grain trade as carriers. From 529 one officer of the realm's service under the Gujarati factor's cover, travelling as a junior man learning the accounts. Cover trade: gum, hides and coastal grain for the Oman market. Communication only by the seasonal ships — a season to Oman, two to three weeks more to the capital.

**Standing on cover.** The cover is as good as cover gets against this target. Egypt has held these harbours and traded through them for six centuries; Indian sailors work them in such numbers that Egypt's own council records that Egyptian hulls, rig, dress and speech are known on sight to most of them. Foreign merchant presence at Egyptian ports is legally defined and routine. Nothing about a Gujarati factory buying gum and hides in these harbours draws a second look.

**Standing on lag.** Every item below reached the capital roughly a year after it was seen. The last usable collection of the turn is the 549 season, arriving 550.

### Collected

**C1. Answering the tasking directly: at the specific harbours where bulk provisioning was seen about 500 to 505, no second provisioning of that character occurred.** What runs instead is a continuous, unremarkable, year-on-year flow that never spikes. The factors watched for the spike for twenty-one years and it did not come. *(Firm — a nil return against a well-framed question, and the most valuable single line in this operation's product.)*

**C2. One modest crown victualling, in about 527, out of proportion to nothing else that season.** Grain, cordage, cooperage and water casks taken up at short notice on crown account, enough to move the coastal grain price for part of a season and no longer. The factors put it at the order of a few hulls' worth. It did not repeat. *(Firm — their own market book and the price movement in it.)*

**C3. What does run continuously, and is the real answer to "what sailed, in how many hulls, in which season": Egyptian transports loading people, not cargo, and sailing south.** Families with stock and tools, term-contract men out of their term with their households, and garrison drafts. Every sailing season across the whole twenty-one years. The factors counted embarkations at the harbours they sit in and returned running counts by season. *(Firm.)*

**C4. Those hulls sail south toward the Cape, and a large proportion do not return within the season.** Some return after two or three years. Some do not return. Over twenty-one years the factors could establish that this is the normal pattern and not a wave. *(Firm.)*

**C5. Timber, cordage and water orders — specifically asked for and specifically answered.** Timber and cordage buying at these harbours rose across the period and stayed risen; water and cooperage rose with the number of hulls. But the rise is smooth and it tracks the number of hulls in the coastal trade, not any single expedition. *(Firm — the factors buy in the same markets.)*

**C6. Shipbuilding on this coast increased.** New building and more of it at the coastal hardwood yards across the period, of transport build. *(Firm — seen from the water and from the timber market.)*

**C7. Egyptian crews and garrison men on this coast stopped dying of the intermittent fevers at the rate they formerly did, from the later 540s, and a bark preparation is issued to them.** A resident of twenty-one years is exactly the observer who can see this change, and it is the most consequential thing this operation collected. *(Firm as to the change in mortality and the issue of the preparation. The bark's identity, its preparation and its dosage were not obtained here.)*

**C8. Horses moving south along the coast by sea in numbers, from the later years, together with breeding stock coming down from the highland studs.** *(Firm.)*

**C9. Harbour and road works at these older provincial harbours are running behind — moles and wharves begun and standing unfinished.** *(Firm — visible from the anchorage every season.)*

**C10. Local food supply rose and a new root crop has come into the coastal villages, and separately a new highland tuber began coming down to market from about 546.** The factors buy coastal grain and this is squarely their trade. *(Firm.)*

**C11. Tall masts with wires at the Egyptian harbour posts, guarded, with men attending them.** Standing for as long as anyone in the factories remembers. Function unknown and not guessed at. *(Firm as to the object.)*

**C12. Field inference, flagged.** The officer under the factor's cover entered one judgement of his own beyond what he saw: that the southward hulls of C3 and C4 and the shipbuilding of C6 are one programme and not several, on the ground that the counts rise together. **This is the agent's reasoning, not his observation, and the coordinator should mark it as such.** He entered no destination and correctly declined to name one.

### Attempted and failed, or nil

- **Where the southward hulls go.** Nil beyond "south, round the Cape, gone." The Atlantic crossing route is a named Egyptian compartment; Omani and Gujarati hulls do not work past the Cape; and the officer had no way to follow. Second-hand harbour talk about a land across a western ocean reached the factory but arrived inconsistent between tellers and was not corroborated. *(Reported, weak, entered as unconfirmed.)*
- **Where the fever bark grows, and where it is grown.** **Nil, and this is the compartment that defeated it.** Egypt holds the wild stands and the siting of its plantation stations in its highest compartment, explicitly on the coffee practice — the same practice that has for five centuries sold a bean that cannot sprout and named the wrong country as its origin. The plantations sit inland in the highlands, not on the coast where this operation stands. A coastal factor can see that the fevers stopped killing Egyptians and cannot see the plant.
- **The 527 victualling's purpose or destination.** Nil. The factors have the fact and the quantity and nothing else.
- **Anything of Egypt's western holdings, industry, fleet strength, reserve, aircraft, or the electrical apparatus behind C11.** Nil throughout.
- **Any change in Egyptian handling of Indian merchants after the exchanges at sea of 543, 547 and 549.** Nil — there was none at these harbours. Egypt's orders on Indian vessels went to its Atlantic and Cape commands, not to its harbour administration, and the resident factories were not disturbed.

**Assessment of the operation.** The best-placed of the four. Twenty-one years of residency against a question that is answerable in a harbour market, and it answered it — including the negative, which it should be credited for. Not detected. The standing risk, unrealised so far, is that the officer's interest in crown victualling quantities is an interest a gum-and-hides buyer has no commercial reason to hold; one direct question about a crown contract's tonnage is what would burn him.

## D. China — scholar exchange, twenty in residence, eyes only

No active collection attempted, by the sending court's own instruction. The representatives are held irreplaceable and a failed attempt would forfeit the window. Adjudicated as: what does a scholar in residence under an official exchange simply see?

### Observed in the ordinary course

**D1. Egypt kept the arrangement in full.** Twenty scholars a decade sat inside Egyptian institutions and the germ and tropical-disease body was open to them at full extent and on no schedule. Nothing was quietly narrowed. *(Firm.)*

**D2. The fever bark passed whole — the medicine, its preparation and its dosage — together with the case series behind it.** Eleven hundred cases run under the germ practice across three separate tropical regions. The scholars had the method as well as the remedy: how Egypt designs a trial, how it records it, and what it counts as a result. That method is arguably worth more than the bark. *(Firm — shown and handled.)*

**D3. Egypt stated its reservation rather than concealing it.** The scholars were told, and the court was told by letter, that the plant and its country would not be given. *(Firm.)*

**D4. Egypt had put China's own gifts into service within a few years of receiving them** — the mirror-principle instrument working from 541, pendulum regulation in the Alexandrian clock shops from 538. A visiting scholar sees his own court's contribution in Egyptian hands and working. *(Firm.)*

**D5. Ambient Alexandria, over twenty-five years of residence.** Arc light where flame used to be, including at the Pharos. Wire on poles through the city and out of it. Hand-connected voice instruments in the administrative offices. Paper as the ordinary substrate. Printing everywhere in official use. Coffee houses as fixtures of the commercial districts. Aluminum and plated goods for sale. Tall masts with wires at the harbour, guarded. *(Firm as to all of it, being unconcealable in a city a man lives in. Nil as to how any of it works.)*

**D6. Settler families embarking from Alexandria in numbers, with livestock and tools, every sailing season, year after year.** A resident scholar walking past the harbour cannot fail to see it. *(Firm.)*

**D7. Egypt's letter of 550 to the court disclosed the two falsehoods it had put into its note to Rome, disclosed the survey squadron sent toward the southern landmass along with its standing orders, and named the bark reservation — and said nothing whatever of any Egyptian holding across the western ocean.** *(Firm — the document is in the court's hands. The omission is a fact about the document; what to make of it beside D6 is the court's work, not this adjudication's.)*

### Kept away from them

- **Electrical engineering, vacuum work, radio, radar, aircraft, the Atlantic crossing, fleet dispositions, and the depth of the reserve** are Egypt's named compartments against this exchange by the terms of the arrangement itself. All held. Access is defined as the germ and tropical-disease body plus the published optics and mathematics, and it was not exceeded.
- **Aircraft** are held in the highest compartment and are based at the Sinai and canal approaches, Cyprus, the Cape, and the colonial and highland harbours — none of them where the exchange sits. No sighting is recorded. Note that foreign missions' movements in Egypt have been controlled since the Indian mission of 367–368, and this bears on whether a scholar could ever be where an airframe is.
- **The bark's source**, as at D3 — disclosed as withheld rather than hidden, which is a different thing and should be recorded as such.

**Assessment.** The window was not spent and was not risked, which was the instruction. What it yielded is real, large, and entirely lawful: a working medicine for the disease that has held China's southern frontier for four centuries, plus the method behind it, plus twenty-five years of a man simply living in the richest city in the world.

## Note to the coordinator

**1. Egypt is running a live deception aimed squarely at Operation B, and B is the intended victim.** Egypt's note to Rome of this turn carries two deliberate falsehoods entered by its council on purpose. One is that its administration of the Atlantic coast of Africa is lately come. The other is an implication that some other power had a hand in how that coast came about — and Egypt's own record states plainly that there is no such power, that the sentence exists to give Rome a direction to spend effort in that leads away from Alexandria, and that it costs Egypt nothing to maintain **because there is nothing there to find**. Operation B is precisely that direction. If the praefecture expands B to hunt for a third party behind the African coast, it will spend a decade and find nothing, which is the designed outcome. Nothing in B's product this turn touches this, and B has not yet been pointed that way. Flag it before it is.

**2. B will produce a true finding that Rome is likely to read backwards.** Egypt is holding its gold output deliberately moderate as a matter of policy — the stream rose two percent across twenty-five years while the empire's receipts rose seventeen. B's traders will report a flat supply and unchanged volumes, entirely correctly. The natural Roman reading is that the African goldfields are thinning. They are not; they are being throttled. There is nothing on a caravan route that can distinguish the two, and Rome has no reason to suspect the difference. This is the clearest case in this turn of an operation succeeding in a way its owner will misread.

**3. Operation A's success is real but its number is a floor, and it will be read as a total.** A returns firm evidence of sustained westward settler shipping out of Alexandria. That is correct. But the larger stream — settlers out of the African provinces for the tropical coast — ships from African provincial harbours and never passes Alexandria. A cannot see it, does not know it exists, and its counts therefore understate the programme substantially. Any Roman estimate built on A alone will be low, and low in a way that flatters Roman planning.

**4. Operations A and C have independently seen the same movement from opposite oceans and neither owner knows the other has it.** A sees Alexandrian families sailing west. C sees African-provincial families sailing south round the Cape. These are two arms of one programme. Neither court can know that from its own product. Worth holding in mind if the two returns are ever set beside each other.

**5. C's most valuable item is a nil.** The Maurya asked whether a second bulk provisioning of the 500–505 kind was under way at those harbours. It is not, and twenty-one years of residency is enough to make that negative worth something. The court's temptation will be to treat the negative as evidence that the earlier event was a one-off and current traffic is ordinary colonial supply. That reading is available from the product and is not supported by it — C returned a nil on a spike, not a nil on a programme.

**6. C's one item of 527 may be attached to the wrong file.** The modest crown victualling at C2 is small, does not repeat, and is separated by two decades from everything else in C's product. There is a plausible connection between it and matters the Maurya court has been dealing with on quite another coast since 543. C's officer did not draw it and was right not to; if the court draws it, the connection is available and is not the officer's inference.

**7. The bark is the standing prize and the standing trap.** Two operations touched it from opposite directions — C saw the effect on the coast and could not reach the plant, and D was handed the medicine, the dosage and the case series and told plainly that the plant would not come. Egypt has explicitly put the bark's origin on the same practice that has misdirected coffee-hunters for five centuries and sent Indian agents into the Yemen highlands for a generation. Anyone who now goes looking for where this bark grows should be assumed to be walking into a prepared account, and any origin story that surfaces easily should be assumed to be the prepared one.

**8. China should be told that its questions are being written down.** Egypt has stood up a register office recording what each visiting scholar was shown, on what date, at whose authority, and what was asked for and refused, expressly so that a later Egyptian estimate of foreign tropical capability starts from a known base. Egypt's own record calls this collection obtained by paperwork. The exchange is not one-way, the cost is to China, and it is a cost a court running an eyes-only operation may not have priced.

**9. What none of these four operations touched, in any degree.** Egypt's industrial base a thousand miles up the Congo behind the cataracts, with its aluminum plant, its arc-furnace steel and its electrochemistry. The size of its reserve. Its aircraft. Its radar at sea. Its nitrogen programme. Its nitrate and copper on the far Pacific shore. Its explosives work. Its shipbuilding rate as an empire-wide figure. Taken together the four returns build a picture of a maritime and colonial Egypt that is accurate as far as it goes and omits the industrial Egypt entirely. Every court reading these returns will underestimate this target, and the shape of the underestimate is the same in all four.
