# Foreign intelligence collected against the Maurya Realm, 525–550

Adjudicated by a pass holding only the Maurya's own files: the history to 500, the turn of 500–525, and the turn of 525–550. Two operations were run against the realm in this period. They are adjudicated separately and to different standards, because they stand in different places.

Marks used throughout: **firm** — the agent saw, handled, counted or heard the thing himself. **Reported** — someone told the agent. **Inferred in the field** — the agent's own supposition, kept minimal and flagged wherever it appears.

---

# A. Rome — frontier trade office, opened 549

**Standing.** Roman merchant factors resident at the Euphrates boundary waystations under the road terms of the 320s, trading there openly for two centuries. Run by the frontier trade office, not the secretariat. One year of running before 550 closes.

**Tasked.** (i) Whether the message Rome sent in 525 was received at all. (ii) Whether Indian yards show work on a hull engine. Named targets: the Maurya arsenal establishments on the Syr Darya frontier and the river yards below Pataliputra.

## The geography of this operation, stated first because it governs everything below

The factors stand on the Euphrates. The Syr Darya arsenals lie some three thousand kilometres east-north-east of them, served by the Bactrian and Sogdian road and not by the Rome road at all. The river yards below Pataliputra lie some four thousand kilometres east, on a different watershed and a different trade. No factor of this operation has been east of the Euphrates in the year it has run, and the cover forbids it: a resident treaty factor who leaves the waystations for Bengal ceases to be a resident treaty factor and becomes a traveller with no standing anywhere.

Both named targets are therefore out of reach, and no quantity of years fixes it. Rome's patience is correctly set and is aimed at the wrong variable.

## Collected

**1. The state of the road the factors live on.** *Firm, by residence; obtained continuously, entered 549.*
The Mesopotamian stretch of the Rome road carries roughly twice the waystations it carried a generation ago, and permanent mounted patrols work the long runs between them. The building was done in the factors' own time. Every house on that road knows it; it required no enquiry. Not tasked, and entered because it is the physical setting of everything else and because it is the thing Rome asked of the realm long ago and did not get.

**2. Printed Maurya administration in the trading cities.** *Firm — seen in use and a copy handled; 549.*
The named official who hears trade disputes in the Mesopotamian trading city under the treaty now works from a printed and bound book of the tax and law codes rather than a written roll, and a copy of the tariff portion was obtained openly by a factor's clerk for his house's own use. Printed matter of this kind is new on that road within these men's working lives. No factor can say how many such books exist or where they are struck.

**3. The only state traffic on the road in a generation.** *Reported; obtained 549–550 from Maurya road staff and from the factors' own house memory.*
About twenty years ago a Maurya state party crossed westward at these stations under escort, carrying a sealed volume, bound for the Syrian frontier. Nothing of that character has passed in either direction since, eastward or westward. This is recollection, not record; see the failure below for why no record exists.

**4. Ships that keep the wet season.** *Reported, third-hand, thin; 550.*
A carrier come up from the head of the Gulf gave it, from talk at the Gulf ports, that the realm's packet to its far southern possessions now sails the year round where within memory it waited on the fair season, and that some of the realm's hulls carry a warm pipe amidships and hold their way against a wind that would stop a ship under sail alone. No hull was seen by any man of this operation. No yard, no engine, no number, no place of building. It is sailors' talk bought at third hand in a market, and it is entered as such.

## Attempted and failed

**Whether the message of 525 was received at Pataliputra.** *Nil, and the nil is structural.*
Whether a foreign court received a packet is a fact held in one chancery register four thousand kilometres from where this operation stands. Nothing in a waystation market touches it. More to the point: **non-receipt has no signature anywhere.** A letter that did not arrive leaves nothing behind it — no absence a merchant can price, no gap in a market, no change in any traffic. There is no consequence of non-receipt to observe, because non-receipt consists precisely in nothing having happened.

The factors did what could be done, and it produced a second nil that must not be mistaken for the first. Framed through the treaty's dispute officials as an enquiry after a lost consignment of 525, they established that **no record of any Roman packet passing east in that year is held at these stations**. The reason is on the road itself: the waystation establishment of that stretch stood thinned in 525 and was doubled and re-staffed across the years following, so that neither the stations nor the men of 525 are there to be asked. The absence of a frontier record from 525 is evidence about the frontier record and about nothing else.

**Work on a hull engine in the yards below Pataliputra.** *Nil.* No access, no traveller of this operation, no channel. Item 4 above is not an answer to this question and should not be filed as a partial one; it speaks to ships at sea in the Gulf, not to work in a yard.

**The arsenal establishments on the Syr Darya.** *Nil.* Nothing of that frontier reaches the Euphrates waystations by any road these factors work. One factor recorded bazaar talk, unsourced and undated, that the guns on the northern river had been added to. It carries no number, no year and no origin he could name, and it is not treated here as collection.

**Summary of the year.** Four facts, two of them mundane and one of them third-hand, and three nils. That is a correct first-year return for an operation of this shape, and the shape is the problem, not the year.

# B. Han China — western commanderies, continued

**Standing.** Hired Sogdian factors of the Samarkand and Gangetic-road houses, paid by the season and not resident, renewed each season under an arrangement of the last fifty years. Cover is merchant: commercial districts, dockyards, the caravan trades. Self-imposed limit: no approach to military facilities, arsenals or garrisons. The limit was held throughout and is the reason the network is intact; where it bit, it is marked.

**Tasked.** (i) Cannon mounted on hulls and fired at sea, as against hulls merely reinforced and carrying nothing. (ii) Volume of powder produced, judged by saltpetre price and availability in civilian markets and by the size of the works. (iii) Depot, road and magazine work behind the cannon positions on the south bank of the Syr Darya. (iv) Raised in priority this period: whether anything of the electrical force exists as a working apparatus rather than a scholarly claim — what it is made of, what turns it, and whether any of it has been put to a use.

## I. Guns upon ships — channel: the factor of the eastern river road, at the delta yards

**534, at the fitting quay.** *Firm.* Hulls on the slips and at the quay with square ports cut low in the sides, closed with shutters, four to a side on the one side he could see whole.

**534, on the same quay.** *Reported.* A shipwright's man, unpressed, told him the heavy iron going into those hulls was to stiffen them, that a ship of that build works itself loose in a sea, and that the iron was there for weight and strength and was not a thing fired from the deck.

**542, two mornings in the roads, waiting on a tide.** *Firm.* Firing from a hull standing off, spaced slow; eleven or twelve reports counted the first morning. On the second morning he saw the smoke from her side and saw a piece run out at a port and run back in. He states that this is the same class of hull he had been told carried the iron for stiffening only, and he does not undertake to reconcile the two accounts. He was put off the outer quay by a yard officer that day while barrels were being lighted out to that hull under escort, and went without argument.

**548, on the quay.** *Firm.* An iron piece the length of a man craned down onto a timber carriage set on small solid wheels and run inboard on planks. Three such carriages lying finished on the quay awaiting hulls.

**548, at the river mouth.** *Reported, second-hand, the man drunk at the time.* A ship-master of the southern coast trade said the yards of the far southern coast are busier than the eastern yards and have been for years, and that the ports are cut there too. The factor has not been on that coast.

*Inferred in the field, flagged:* that the guns are not many to a ship, three or four to a side by the ports he counted, and that a yard holding three spare carriages is building for more hulls than it has ready.

**Nil.** The number of the realm's armed hulls, their stations, their standing orders, and anything of any fleet as a fleet. One yard shows one yard.

**Adjudication.** The task is answered and answered correctly. Guns are mounted and are fired at sea. "Reinforced and carrying nothing" is wrong.

## II. The white salt, its price, and the works — channel: the factor of the Gangetic road

**531, 537 and 545, two markets each time, dry season and after the rains.** *Firm.* He priced the white salt at the great market of the capital's river town and at a market four days up the road. At every visit the price stood within a little of the same, and the same as he remembers it from earlier years. It was there in quantity every day he looked for it, sold in the open, in baskets, by villagers who scrape it off the ground where it comes up white on the fields after the dry weather. The buyers he saw were dyers, a man curing meat, and apothecaries taking small lots.

**537, in that market.** *Reported.* A broker told him there is no forward dealing in the stuff and no house that holds it against a rise, because a man who wants it walks out beyond the town and takes it up himself, and no one has ever grown rich on it.

**537 and 545, on the same road he has ridden for years.** *Firm.* Three walled compounds new to him between one town and the next, set back off the road, with brick kilns burning beside two of them and gangs at work on the walls of the third. He did not turn off the road toward any. Returning, the third stood finished and walled with its gate shut.

**545, at the waystation on that stretch.** *Reported.* Carters said their season's hire had been hauling to such places: charcoal chiefly and in great quantity, sulphur brought up from the south in sacks, timber and cooperage. One had made the same short run six times between the rains and had never once been inside the gate. Another said there were more such places away south on the plateau roads, well off his own run, and had it from his brother.

*Inferred in the field, flagged:* that where a thing lies cheap and unmoving and no house troubles to hold it, there is no great buyer in the country for it; and that he cannot make that agree with the carts and the compounds, and reports both as he found them.

**Nil.** What is made inside any of those walls; the output of any works; any figure of powder whatever.

**Adjudication.** The price half of the task returns something true about the price and nothing usable about the quantity, for reasons set out in the coordinator's note. The size-of-works half succeeds at low precision and is the half that carries the task.

## III. The northern road and what stands behind it — channel: the senior factor of the Samarkand house, with a second man on the same road

**536, from the second man, received late.** *Firm.* The mountain crossing no faster than of old, the upper road broken and under work. He says nothing of the grain stores.

**540, from the senior factor.** *Firm.* The mountain road cut wider than he has known it and metalled in the bad stretches, with two new relay houses of fresh stone where there were none, each with water and a walled yard for beasts. His caravan made the crossing in noticeably fewer days than his best previous crossing and lost no animal. Work gangs still on the upper stretch as he passed.

The two returns disagree. Both are set down. They were written four years apart on a road that was under rebuilding between them.

**543, below the passes.** *Firm.* Three towns new on the road, which he gives as his ears took them: Ashvasthala, Girivraja, Uttarakota. Each walled, each with a walled market within it. He traded in two of the three, selling hides and buying nothing worth the carriage. The markets are new-built, the stalls are let by an officer of the town, and a name and a house must be given to take a stall. He gave his and was not questioned further.

**543, behind the passes.** *Firm.* Four great grain stores on his own road, long low buildings with raised floors, two plainly new and two enlarged, with grain going in by cart at a season when he would expect it to be going out.

**543, at one store.** *Reported.* A clerk, taken with drink and glad of company, said the order now is that the store shall be full through the winter and stand full into the next season, where in his father's time it was empty by spring and refilled from below. He gave no measures and was not pressed for any.

**546, at a waystation two days short of the river.** *Firm.* Sitting three days on a lame beast, he counted the carts passing east in daylight: thirty-one, twenty-six, forty-four. The road is fuller than he has ever ridden it, the traffic nearly all one way and nearly all state hire, the beasts branded and the carters paid by an officer at the stage.

**546, at the same stage — the limit line.** *Firm.* Where a made track leaves the road toward the river he turned up it a short way looking for water and was turned back by a mounted man who was civil and did not ask his name. He did not try again.

**546, at that stage.** *Reported.* A carter said the loads off that track go to stone houses standing back behind the bank, that there are more of them now than when he began the run, and that the men who work them are not soldiers of the line but a service of their own that lodges apart. Asked how many, he said he had never counted and would not.

**543, market talk in two of the three new towns.** *Reported, low value.* That the pieces standing on the bank have been added to — said with satisfaction, as men speak of a thing that makes their own town safe. No number the factor would repeat.

*Inferred in the field, flagged:* that the whole of this road is being made ready to carry and feed something larger than it carries now. He notes that the new markets are good for his house and says so plainly, and asks a higher seasonal rate on account of the market officers taking names.

**Nil.** The number of pieces on the bank. The number, contents or capacity of the magazines behind it. Anything inside any arsenal, garrison or work of the frontier establishment. All of it lies past China's own limit line, and the line held.

**Adjudication.** This is the best-placed of the four tasks and it performed accordingly. Road, relay stations, forward granaries and the new towns are all open commerce on ground that is the factors' own country, and a caravan man's truest instrument — how many days the crossing takes and how many carts he passes — measures exactly what was asked. The guns and the magazines return second-hand, directionally true, and without a number.

## IV. The electrical force — channels: the Samarkand house's own copper trade, and the Gangetic-road factor on the northwest road

**534, the house's own books.** *Firm as to the trade, nil as to the purpose.* A state buyer has been taking copper in quantity out of the Central Asian workings for a season or more, and takes it to be drawn and not cast — wanted fine and long, in lengths, at the drawing shops of the plain. The Samarkand house handled part of the metal itself. Nobody in that trade could say at that date what it was for.

**542 and 546, on the northwest road out of the capital.** *Firm.* Beside the road, running with it for many days' travel, a raised causeway of new work, drained, carrying a line of glazed earthen pipe laid end to end with the joints wrapped in a black baked cloth. Gangs laying it in 542; the work standing finished in 546. Four small new houses stand along it at intervals. At one point the causeway forks and a branch runs off toward walled ground. He did not follow the branch.

**546, at one of the small houses.** *Reported — one man, one season, not repeated or corroborated.* A station man, glad of company, showed him the inside: rows of earthen jars in a rack, each with plates of two metals standing in it and a liquor in the jar; the spent jars carried out and fresh ones brought up by cart on a set round. Beside the wire a needle hung under a glass, which jumps when the man at the far end works his, and lies still when he stops. The man said the words are spelled out on it as they are spelled on the towers.

*This answers the second limb of the task in the negative and it should be read as an answer.* **Nothing turns it.** There is no wheel, no beast, no water and no mill at any of the four houses. The apparatus is jars, plates and a liquor, consumed and replaced like any stores.

**547, from carters and station people on the same stretch.** *Reported.* That word from the capital reaches the up-road station inside the hour where a rider takes two days; and that in the heaviest rains the line goes dead for some weeks together and the old tower signals carry the traffic until it dries.

*Inferred in the field, flagged:* that the pipe and the causeway are there to keep the wet off the wire, and that the whole is a message line of the same office that keeps the towers, only faster and far dearer.

**Nil.** Where the branch goes and what stands at its end — it runs toward walled ground and the factor did not follow it, correctly under the cover. The metals of the plates and the liquor in the jars, neither of which he could name. The code. Any drawing or measurement of the reader. The length of the trunk beyond "many days' travel," and its far terminus.

**Adjudication.** The task asked whether the force exists as a working apparatus rather than a scholarly claim, what it is made of, what turns it, and whether it has been put to a use. All four limbs are answered, three of them well. It exists and it works. It is made of drawn copper, a baked cloth covering, glazed pipe and a raised causeway, fed from jars of two metals in a liquor. Nothing turns it. It has been put to a use, and the use is carrying spelled words faster than a rider, with a seasonal failure in the wet the realm has not solved.

## Counter-intelligence and network risk

The realm's administration and intelligence line stands at 270 million against 240 a generation ago, while revenue rose from about 2,300 million to 2,616 and the governed span grew by a continent, two island groups, a chain of archipelago stations and a supplied ocean road. Flat coin over far more ground is thinner coverage per unit of ground, not thicker. Its one conspicuous new deployment is *outward* — an officer of its own service placed under a merchant factor's cover on the East African coast — and is aimed at Egypt, not at Sogdians.

What did change on the ground these factors work: the three new garrison towns brought market supervisors and toll clerks onto what had been open road, and a stall is now let by an officer against a name and a house. A Sogdian asking after stores off the road is noticed and remembered where he was not before. One factor was turned back from a track toward the river; one was put off a quay. Both were handled civilly and neither was asked his name.

The realm's scepticism of Sogdian intermediaries is institutional and documented — go-betweens whose first loyalty runs to the trade — but it is scepticism and not surveillance. The realm also now has scholars who read Chinese without a Sogdian at their elbow; written traffic in that hand moving on Maurya ground is a standing hazard and was not incurred this period.

The decisive protection was China's own limit line. The realm takes people where people touch arsenals and garrisons: it fed its own men into Han frontier arsenals for the better part of two decades to obtain a single weapon, lost many of them, had many more turned in by the people they tried to buy, and knows the trade from the receiving end. China's factors were kept out of exactly that ground and were never at risk in it.

**Assessment: network intact, uncompromised, renewable.** Expect a slightly more watched trade in the new frontier markets and one raised seasonal rate.

# Note to the coordinator

**No plant was made against either operation in this period.** The realm ran no deception at China or at Rome. It ran one at Egypt — a notice of 543 describing coasts as garrisoned along their length when two of them are held by nobody — and neither of these operations touched it. Everything below is a true thing that misleads, which is a different and harder problem.

**1. The stiffening answer is not a lie.** The shipwright's man told the factor the truth as he had it. Hulls of that build genuinely are being stiffened, and heavily, because carriage and mounting were standardised across three classes of hull in these years and because a large share of the fleet now carries machinery in the bottom. A factor who hears that in one season and watches a piece fired in a later season has two true statements that do not sit together. He filed both unreconciled, which is the right handling. If the commandery smooths them, it will smooth away the finding.

**2. The saltpetre price is a broken indicator and China does not know it.** This is the largest misread risk in the return. Saltpetre forms on the soil of the Gangetic plain, is scraped up by villagers, is not a state monopoly and is not a bulk-quoted caravan commodity. Powder output rose several-fold in this period, and the works were sited **on the deposit** — the works went to the salt; the salt did not go to market. Nothing needed to happen to any price a merchant can observe, and nothing did. A court weighting price over works will conclude that powder production is flat, and it will be badly wrong. The factor's own inability to reconcile the flat price with the carts and the compounds is the most valuable line in his return and should be read as the finding rather than as his confusion.

**3. Direction without magnitude, on the river.** Magazines and guns behind the Syr Darya both grew substantially and both return as market talk carrying no number. "The pieces have been added to" is true and is compatible with almost any figure. Any court that converts that phrase into a quantity will get it wrong, and probably low.

**4. Task IV succeeded, and the tasking's own wording invites the commandery to miss it.** China asked *what turns it*, which presumes something turns it. Nothing does. If the commandery reads the negative as a gap rather than as an answer, it will file the cleanest success of the quarter century as a partial failure. Flagged for that reason and no other. The success itself is largely an accident of siting: the realm ran its one working electrical installation along a public caravan road, on a raised causeway, in the open, and put four manned relay houses on it that consume ordinary market stores — and that road is the same road on which it also built the greater part of its new powder works. China's cover was already riding past both.

**5. Deliberately excluded from the report to China, flagged here only.** The delta yards where China's factor stands are also where the realm builds marine engines, and the realm now has a large number of engined hulls and a southern packet that keeps the monsoon. China asked about guns on hulls and did not ask what moves them. It is the most valuable thing this cover could have seen this period and it is unrequested, so it is out of the report.

**6. Rome has placed collectors three thousand kilometres from its targets.** Nothing about the quality of the Roman factors bears on the two questions asked. The Syr Darya arsenals are served by a road these men never travel; the yards are on another watershed. Rome's declared patience — nothing inside a decade — is the correct temperament aimed at the wrong variable, because time does not close distance that the cover itself forbids the factors to cross.

**7. Rome's first question is the one most likely to be answered wrongly, and by Rome rather than by the field.** The operation returned two distinct nils and they will be easy to conflate. The first: whether Pataliputra received the packet is unknowable from a waystation, and *non-receipt leaves no trace anywhere*, so there is no consequence to look for. The second: no record of any Roman packet passing east in 525 is held at these stations, because that stretch's waystation establishment was thinned in 525 and was doubled and re-staffed in the years after. If the frontier trade office forwards "no trace at the frontier" and the secretariat reads "not received," Rome will have drawn a firm conclusion from a nil that carries none. This is the single point on which the adjudicator would want the coordinator to be sure the distinction survives the relay.

**8. And the cheap answer Rome wanted is probably in Rome's own archive.** The Maurya record shows that in 530 the realm sent a state party west along the Mesopotamian road carrying a printed and sealed volume with a letter, that the letter recites the whole exchange with Rome from 501 forward, that it asks for nothing, and that no answer of any kind has come back in the twenty years since. Whether that volume reached Rome is not a thing these files can say. If it did, its own contents bear on Rome's first question more directly and more cheaply than any collection at the Euphrates, and Rome commissioned an operation to find in a market what may be sitting in its own chancery.

**9. A live condition Rome should be understood to be operating inside.** The Maurya record shows the realm has had no word from Rome for two decades, has decided it will not ask again, and in 548 for the first time in a century and a half withheld from Rome a body of observation the two courts have habitually traded. Rome is opening a collection operation against a counterparty that has already quietly begun telling it less. That is not a trap and there is no sign the realm knows of the operation. It is the temperature of the ground the operation is standing on.
