# Intelligence returns to Rome, two operations against Egypt, 525–550

Adjudicated against the rebuilt Egypt turn by a pass holding only Egypt's own files. Supersedes the earlier adjudication.

**Coordinator note, not for the receiving court.**

**Operation A, the Alexandria network.** Cover is exactly where the answers live and this is as strong a position as exists in the setting. *Westward provisioning: substantially correct and complete.* *Shipbuilding: correct but coarse, and correctly flagged by the agent as a floor* — Alexandria is not where the bulk of Egypt's naval construction happens, a large share having sat on the African hardwood coast since the first century, with arc-furnace steel coming from the falls works a thousand miles up the Congo.

The decisive point of the period: Egypt has formally restricted standing false accounts to matters a rival cannot go and verify, so on the observable disposition of a harbour or a yard the instrument is now **refusal to discuss** — which does not blind a merchant standing on the quay. No deception operates against this tasking. The compartments that are real, the crossing route and aircraft and radar and the fever bark, are not what was asked.

And Egypt's activity is not a little larger but transformed: 560,000 persons landed across the western holdings, 340 hulls in the passage trade, settlement run on the Madagascar instrument with land grants at survey, fifteen years free of land tax, subsidised passage and harbours built in advance. **You cannot recruit half a million people covertly.** The recruiting is public, printed and advertised, and the Mediterranean-stock settlers embark from Alexandria itself.

*The quarantined branch.* Nothing in Egypt's turn shows a rollup. The silent branch is consistent with the agent Egyptian counter-intelligence took in port in the previous period, whose questions were all Atlantic. Not walking anyone into it was correct and is why the rest is clean. Detection negligible.

**Operation B, the Berber gold traders.** The cover stands on top of half the tasking and nowhere near the other half. Where the interior roads run: answerable and well answered. Volume over two generations: only as a trader's memory and market talk. **What is in the holds of loaded ships: not answerable by inland caravan men, and no plausible answer has been manufactured.** Rome's own instruction expects nothing inside a decade and Rome is right; the operation is two years old at the close. One relevant Egyptian policy shapes what can truthfully be reported: Egypt deliberately holds gold output moderate and does not want its true output known, so what a buyer sees is a capped and orderly trade rather than a flood. Detection none.

# A. Office of the mission at Alexandria, to the secretariat

Commercial staff. Written at the close of the sailing season.

**The yards.** Seen by me. Walking the harbour front this spring I counted eleven hulls on the ways and on the stocks at the yards a man may pass without being stopped. Six years ago the same walk gave me five or six. Three of the eleven are war hulls by their proportion and by the plate on them; the rest are traders and river craft, wide in the hold, flat in the run, built plain and built fast.

The change that struck me is not the count but the manner of it. Frames, knees, brackets and fittings come to the ways ready-made and all to one size, carted in from works elsewhere rather than cut and fitted at the yard, and a hull is closed in a part of the time it used to take. Men who were shipwrights are now fitters. The yards have taken on hands in numbers and pay above the port rate for them, which is why ordinary repair work here waits its turn and why I have had to send our own careening down the coast twice.

What I pay. Cordage, canvas, pitch, lamp oil and iron fastenings have stood dear in this port for years running, and the crown's orders are filled before any private order. I have the chandlers' asking prices in the enclosed sheet.

Told to me by yard clerks and by two masters I have dealt with twenty years: the greater part of the crown's hull-building is not done in this port at all but in the southern yards where the heavy timber grows, and Alexandria has more of the fitting-out and the repair than the laying down. I have not seen those yards and cannot put a number on them. What I count here is the least of it.

**Provisioning for a westward voyage.** Seen by me, in every sailing season since my last report, and not hidden in any degree.

Recruiters sit openly in the port quarter and in the Delta towns with printed bills, and I enclose one. The terms offered are land measured out at survey, no land tax on it for a term of years, passage paid for a man with his wife, his children, his beasts and his tools, and a harbour, road and cistern standing ready before he lands.

Whole families embark, and this is the thing I would have the office mark: not soldiers, but women, children, milch cattle, ploughs, seed corn, mattocks, roofing iron, vine cuttings and olive stock. I have watched them load with my own eyes at four separate stages of the season.

What is bought against these sailings: biscuit, salt meat, grain, water casks, cooperage, cordage, canvas, sailcloth and ship's stores, on standing yearly contracts rather than fitted out for one voyage. Two chandler houses I trade with have doubled their premises on this business and told me the crown's orders run years ahead.

Told to me by pilots and by mates who have made the passage: the ships go out westward, by way of the western coast of Africa and not by the eastern seas, and are gone the better part of a year. I have not been aboard one and cannot say more than they told me.

Handled by me: hulls come back into this port lying low with wheat, hides and wine of a kind I do not know by mark or by taste, and the merchants who break the cargo will not say where it is grown.

Inferred, by me, and I mark it as my own judgement: this is not a fleet fitted out for a voyage. It is a standing yearly traffic, it has been running some years, and nothing in the yards, the contracts or the recruiting suggests it is meant to stop.

The quiet branch has been left alone as instructed and no man of ours has gone near it.

**Network survives to the next period.** Intact, unsuspected, and the quarantine held.

# B. Office of the prefect of the African provinces, on the treasury's account

First return from the gold traders. Two seasons' work only; the men have been asked little and asked carefully.

**What our traders handle, from their own hands.** Gold is bought by them at the desert markets and at the Sahel towns south of the sand, in dust and in small cast pieces, and carried north by camel to the oasis stations and thence to the Libyan ports where our houses take it. The elder of the two men we deal with says the load he brings north in a season is smaller than the load his father brought, and that he is paid better for it than his father was. He offered this without being asked.

**The roads.** Seen by them. The northern roads run as they have run, along the chain of oases, but the stations along them are garrisoned and the wells are kept by the crown, and toll is taken at the stations by clerks who write in a book. Along one stretch a wire runs on poles beside the road and the traders are warned off it.

The change is at the southern end. On the Sahel margin, in country their fathers crossed with no man to answer to, there are now district offices with clerks, a register of households, a court, a school where the children are taught letters, and a physician. The traders pay their dues there and are given a paper. They date this to recent years and say it was not so when they began.

**Volume.** Market talk only, and we mark it as such. The trade in gold along these roads is spoken of as thinner than it was in the fathers' time, while the general traffic of the roads — salt, hides, ivory, grain, and the crown's own stores moving to the stations — is spoken of as heavier. The traders themselves put no figure to either and we did not press them for one.

**The coast.** One of the two has sold at the Atlantic coast towns. What he saw loading there, at the quays where a merchant may stand, was coal, cased stores, cut timber and people. He was not aboard and was not shown any manifest.

The relationship is undisturbed and the men suspect nothing beyond ordinary buying. We would not push them further this decade.

**Network survives to the next period.** Undisturbed, and correctly not yet pushed.
