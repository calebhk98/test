# Intelligence return to Egypt, operation against Rome, 525–550

Adjudicated by a pass holding only Rome's own files. Delivered to the Egyptian council at the opening of the next turn. Egypt tasked two questions, both concerning what Rome believes.

**Coordinator note, not for the receiving court:** substantially correct on both questions. Question one returns the correct answer including its central nuance, which is that neither of the two positions Egypt offered is Rome's — Rome holds the question deliberately unresolved and funds the west as though it might be true. Question two returns complete and correct. No detection, no exposure. Rome's protective doctrine is managed disclosure of its own routes and methods and contains no counter-intelligence practice aimed at foreign observers inside Roman ports; Rome also chose in this period to keep relations with Egypt undisturbed in order to preserve its mission's access at Alexandria. One capability characterisation was struck from item 4 as unrequested.

---

Compiled by the directorate from two channels, the commercial houses of the western and Italian ports and the recruited establishment, cross-checked before submission. Points carried by one channel only are so marked.

## Part One: on what the Roman court now holds concerning the western ocean and ourselves

**1. Heard directly, insider channel.** A man of the secretariat's clerical establishment states that the matter of our ships seen moving north up the African coast was put to the Senate for decision more than once in this generation and was each time sent back undecided, and that the phrase used in the papers is that it stands unresolved. He was clear that this is not neglect but a ruling: the chamber has been asked to declare that a fleet is being gathered against the west and has each time declined to declare it. He says the reason given is a rule of their own about not making a conclusion out of what was seen through a glass, and that two occasions on which they did so are written down in their own files and are cited against anyone who proposes it now.

**2. Heard directly, merchant channel, and agreeing with the above.** In the sheets sold in the Italian and Iberian cities the argument itself is open and loud, and has been on and off for years. One party writes that ships of that size moving toward the latitude of a crossing, in the same years their own colonists go west in numbers, can mean only one thing. The other answers that metal has very likely been moving up that coast for a great while and was simply never seen because no ship of theirs had ever sailed there, and that our ships were not seen returning southward, which they take as favouring the quieter reading. Our resident notes, and asks that it be weighed, that the first party prints more than the second, because in this country the side that loses a decision is the side that goes into print about it, so the sheets read hotter than the chamber has ruled. **Agent's inference:** the printed heat should not be read as the court's position; the court's position is the undecided one.

**3. Heard directly, both channels.** Both channels return, independently, that their court does not hold as established that we are already on the far shore, and does not hold as established that we keep to the coast of Africa either. The insider's words were that the disposition is logged open. The merchant's reading of the sheets is the same. This is the point on which the two channels agree most exactly and it is the answer to the question as put.

**4. Heard directly, merchant channel.** Against that, what is being done tells its own story, and the quays are where it is seen. The works at their two great western islands are being built to face the sea and not the interior, in masonry, and heavy pieces have gone out to them in the western hulls out of Gades and Olisipo, a small provision by the ladings and not a great one. A lading clerk of the western run remarked, unprompted, that there is nobody in those islands to want that kind of gun pointed at, and that a battery on a headland is built against ships. **Agent's inference:** their court is spending against the possibility while refusing to affirm it, and a man may take the spending as the more honest of the two.

**5. At second hand, insider channel, one channel only.** The same clerk reports that when the party of thrift moved again to cap the western expense and was again beaten, the reason carried in the chamber was that the western sea is the only place where Rome could stand against us at all, both being newcomers there and neither holding fortified ground of its own; and that it was said openly in the debate that this reason rests on an Egyptian intention their court has not established, and that they were voting the money regardless. He had this from a man who attends the chamber and not from the papers.

**6. At second hand, merchant channel, one channel only.** Factors in the gold trade of their African provinces pass word that Roman buyers of the ordinary sort have lately begun asking questions they never used to ask, which of the great ships passing north carry metal, how much of the northern trade now goes by sea rather than by the desert roads, and whether the volume has changed within living memory. Our resident had this from the trade and not from any Roman office. **Agent's inference:** they are trying to prove the quiet explanation for themselves, which he takes as further evidence that they have not settled it.

## Part Two: on their ships lost in the Atlantic south of Morocco

**7. Heard directly, both channels, agreeing.** Their account has hardened, and it is now attached to us by name. In the ports it is said flatly among masters and among the older houses that the ships they lost down that coast over the last two generations were not lost to reef, nor to current, nor to canoe-peoples, and that the account they were given at the time was a made thing. The insider channel gives the same in the register of the establishment: that the papers now carry the loss as having been suffered against a defended coast, and that the defence was ours or was that of peoples standing under us. He notes the wording is careful on that last point, ours or our subjects', and that no paper he has handled undertakes to say which.

**8. Heard directly, both channels.** The joining of the belief to what their ships saw on that coast is explicit and is the whole of why it hardened. Men who sailed in the last survey have talked in the Iberian ports for years about towns of stone built one after another for the length of that shore, mirrored towers throwing signals along it, and a great fleet at the corner where the land ends; and the older masters now say that a coast held like that was never a coast where ships go missing by accident. The insider gives the same joining in colder words: the works and the guard seen on that shore are cited in their papers as what accounts for the losses.

**9. Heard directly, insider channel; corroborated in effect by the merchant channel.** Two things follow from it that were thought worth reporting exactly. The first is that they mean to say nothing to us about it. A letter of complaint over the account they were given was written and was held back, and the reason recorded is that a complaint only states a thing they have already decided and would gain them nothing, and that what they want from the standing mission at Alexandria is that its people keep their access. The merchant channel confirms the practical face of this without knowing the letter existed: nothing of the sort has been said to any Egyptian house or factor in any Roman port, and relations at the quays are exactly as they were.

**10. Heard directly, merchant channel.** The second is in the shipping and can be verified by anyone who tries to hire a hull. No Roman keel is to go south past their Moroccan and Mauritanian holdings. A charter clerk at Gades stated it as a standing thing rather than as news, and a proposal in their own service for a further voyage down that shore was refused within the last decade, the refusal resting on the ships already lost and on the fact that the last survey was accompanied the entire length of the coast by one of our ships after another and was let go home. The underwriters will not price that water at all. **Agent's inference:** the belief is settled enough that it has passed out of argument and into ordinary practice, which is the surest sign of a settled belief there is.

**Network status.** The commercial-cover network in the Roman ports survives intact to the next period. No detection and no approach by any Roman office. The recruited establishment sources are unexposed and reusable. The military-technical placement in the northern industrial provinces was not approached, was not referenced by any source, and remains untouched.
