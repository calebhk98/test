# Observation return to Rome, standing watch on Han China at the Dnieper, 525–550

Adjudicated against the rebuilt China turn by a pass holding only China's own files. Supersedes the earlier adjudication.

**Coordinator note, not for the receiving court.**

*Frequency: established correctly*, to a precision the Chinese court does not hold in any single place — its own Dnieper return counts river traffic coarsely, by season, as rising.

*Delivered volume: established exactly.* Rome's own weighing house gives tonnage crossing the river to the pound, by commodity, for twenty-five years. Measurement, not inference.

*Carrying capacity: a correct floor with no ceiling.* Rome floors it soundly from its own profile — the line was built to the commercial rail method Rome itself furnished in the fourth century, so Rome is reading a line built to its own specification, which is not espionage at all. The floor sits near ten times delivered tonnage, which is real and valuable. It cannot be bounded above: the governing figures are draught stock at the relays and spacing of passing places, both inland, and both in fact very deep because the settlement programme has fed oxen into those rail towns for twenty years.

*The standing risk — a correct measurement that misleads.* The 2,240 tonnes of a season is a true number about the Rome trade and a false number about the line. Read as what the line could feed an army with, it understates by roughly an order of magnitude. **The loading of this line is not what arrives at the river:** the Dnieper rail's principal business this period is the Pontic settlement — 210,000 households along the rail towns, 9,000 iron ploughs and 26,000 draft oxen carried west, Pontic grain now four per cent of imperial receipts moving back east — all terminating short of the river and never appearing at the terminus. And the frontier's military depth went elsewhere entirely: the powder works, charcoal burns and five-weeks-of-fire magazines are all on the Syr Darya. Rome's premise that a supply line to a frontier is a supply line to an army is sound doctrine measuring, at this frontier in this period, a settlement and trade line.

*Units.* China does not reckon frontier capacity in Rome's units. Where it reckons a frontier's supply it reckons in days of sustained fire, not weight. A figure for the line's seasonal capacity exists in no Chinese record because no Chinese office has needed one.

## VI. Inference *(inferred)*

*Entered by the office of the curator of the frontier rail district at the river, over the hand of its senior engineer, upon the counts at heads I to V and upon this office's own operating practice.*

**Basis.** The road opposite is laid to the profile, sleeper spacing and gauge of the commercial method this office works daily, and the wagon is that method's cargo pattern. This office therefore knows what one such wagon bears, being 2.6 to 3.0 tonnes of merchandise on that profile, and knows what such a road bears in our own service where the relays are stocked, being not fewer than four laden trains a day in each direction.

**First.** The delivered weight and the counted wagons agree. Ninety-six arrivals at a mean of nine laden wagons is 23 tonnes to the arrival, and this yard's scales weighed 2,240 tonnes in the same season. The two are the same figure reached twice.

**Second.** On the counted interval, one arrival in 2.2 days, the road opposite is worked at a small part of what that profile bears in our service. This office states as a floor, and only as a floor, that a road of that profile with stocked relays will bear not less than 25,000 tonnes in a working season of the length counted at head I. What it delivered to the river in the season of 549 was 2,240 tonnes.

**Third.** This office cannot set the figure any closer, and states why. Two things govern such a road and neither is at the river: the draught stock held at the relays behind, and the spacing of the passing places. Both lie inland. Nothing at the yard head measures them.

**Fourth.** Two of the counts bear on this and are set down for what they are worth. The interval is irregular, clustering upon the consignments and then standing off for a fortnight, where a road worked to its limit runs to an even interval. And trains arrive at nine laden wagons of twelve and depart at twelve of twelve. A road that arrives part-laden is not filling its trains, and what is carried is therefore set by what is consigned and not by what the road will bear.

**Fifth.** In consequence this office marks the distinction plainly for whoever reads this return. The weights at head V are what arrives, measured on our own scales, and they are exact. They are not the carrying capacity of the road, and the difference between the two is not measurable from this bank. The floor at the second point above stands. This office puts no ceiling on it.

## For the coordinator only

**Four things bear on what this watch sees in future.**

1. **A tripwire is live.** China entered a standing observation order in 526 and returns a fixed form each season from its Dnieper garrisons carrying four counts, the fourth being *any inquiry received concerning the empire's interior distances, supply intervals or routes*. Its return to 550 records none. That is a ledger entry waiting for Rome's name. The invited officer at the yard is the best collection position in the operation, and one question from him about intervals, distances or what the line carries converts a lawful counting watch into a logged intelligence approach in a single season. He must go on arranging consignments and nothing else.

2. **Rome's own yard is being counted back.** China's return records depot and yard work observed at two points on the Roman bank, not distinguishable from ordinary commercial building at that distance. Enlargement beyond what the trade warrants is what would stop it reading as commerce.

3. **The counts will keep rising and keep answering the wrong question.** China completed its southern trunk line to the Yangtze mill counties in 548 and has begun putting northern coal into the silk works there. Output in the mulberry counties will rise and the arrivals will rise with it for decades. Patience refines the measurement of the trade indefinitely and never converges on the capacity.

4. **The genuine forward indicator is not tonnage.** Where this empire stocks a frontier for fighting, it stocks it in a form Rome could see: magazines must stand outside walls or in fireproofed works under a policy standing since a city fire in the 420s, producing isolated, heavily built, separately sited structures, and since 536 it stocks and reports them against a stated number of days of sustained fire. This period it did all of that at its other frontier and none of it here. Isolated fireproofed building behind the eastern yard would be worth more than any number of counted trains, and the existing form would catch it under head IV.

**One paper sits in the wrong office.** China's letter of 527 asked, as farming practice, for Rome's method of breaking heavy grassland east of the river — plough form, oxen to a team, season, years to yield — because it is settling farming households there. That letter is the key to reading these counts: it says in China's own hand that the traffic on that line is settlement traffic bound for towns short of the river. It is held by the secretariat. The legate's staff wrote its return without it.
