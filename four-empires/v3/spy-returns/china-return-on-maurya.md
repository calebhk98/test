# Intelligence return to Han China, operation against the Maurya Realm, 525–550

Adjudicated by a pass holding only the Maurya's own files. Delivered to the Chinese court at the opening of the next turn. China tasked three questions and chose to reach a military quantity by commercial indicators rather than by penetrating military sites.

**Coordinator note, not for the receiving court.** Outcomes differ across the three tasks and the split is the point.

- *Task one, guns on hulls.* Substantially correct on the fact, deliberately coarse on scale. Both propositions China set against each other are resolved correctly: guns are mounted and they are fired at sea. The count returned is a fraction of the true total and the factor says so himself.
- *Task two, powder volume by saltpetre price.* **The chosen indicator does not track the quantity.** Mauryan saltpetre forms naturally on the soil of the Gangetic plain, is gathered by scraping, and four of the six new works were sited on top of the deposit, so the state never enters a civilian market as a bulk buyer. Output can rise several-fold with no movement in any price a merchant could observe. The price half of the return is true about the indicator and actively misleading about the quantity; the works half works well and points the other way. The two halves contradict each other. That contradiction is the honest state of the evidence and has been left standing rather than reconciled. This is the largest error risk in the return and it is a defect of the method, not of the source.
- *Task three, depot and road work.* Substantially correct and rich, because the road in question is the caravan road a Sogdian factor has travelled for three centuries. A true answer to a narrower question on the magazines themselves: traffic through a gate rather than what stands behind it. One genuine staleness, the works having ended in 539 with seasonal reporting carrying them forward.

**Why the network survived.** China's standing limits are what preserved it. The Maurya frontier establishment on that river was exempted by name from every economy of the reign and is at full manning, and those gun positions are the one subject that court has reason to believe a rival cares about, having been pressed on them before. Had the factors been sent at the line, the outcome would very likely have been different. Separately and to China's advantage: the realm funded its new overseas informer establishment partly by closing informer posts in the Gangetic interior, the Deccan interior and one Tamil province, which is precisely the ground where the new powder works stand. Domestic watch thinned exactly where task two's observable object sits.

**Counterweight noted.** The realm has recorded in writing, more than once, that Sogdian loyalty runs to the trade and not to the realm, and spent decades reducing its dependence on Sogdian intermediation. There is standing institutional scepticism of these factors. It does not amount to surveillance, and a caravan factor asking after road conditions and grain prices is asking the questions his trade requires.

---

Compiled at the western commandery from the seasonal returns of the hired factors of the northern route. The returns are set down as they came in, by season, and where two seasons do not agree both are entered. Each item is marked as the factor saw it, as he was told it, or as he supposed it. Several of these men buy and sell on their own account along the same road.

## The first head: guns upon hulls, and firing upon the water

**Fifth season, from the factor of the eastern river-mouth, through a hired local man of that place.**

*Seen.* At the great delta yard, hulls building and hulls refitting in the same basin as the merchant bottoms he had come to charter. On three of them, ports cut low in the side, and on one of these he watched a gun swung aboard by crane over two days, and a second the day after, and the deck under it framed heavier than the rest of the hull. *He supposes* the heavier framing is for the gun, though he is not a shipwright and says so.

*Seen.* He counted, at that anchorage in that season, a small handful of hulls carrying guns, and no more. *He supposes* there are many more than he saw, since he saw one harbour of a country with two long coasts, and he offers no number for the whole.

**Seventh season, the same channel.**

*Seen.* On a clear morning in the roads outside that harbour, several hulls stood out together and fired upon a floating mark for the better part of the forenoon. Smoke, the reports carrying to the quay, boats going out to the mark between rounds and coming back. He watched from the quay with the whole waterfront. *He notes* the firing was slow, long between rounds, and that the same hulls did the same thing again some days later.

*Told.* A ship's carpenter of that yard told him such hulls also work out of the gulf harbours in the west, and that some go south with the seasonal convoys. Told him also that the gun crews are kept together and drilled by the season, and that the powder issued for drill is given out close and counted.

**Ninth season, a different factor, the western route to the gulf.**

*Seen.* At the gulf harbour, two hulls at anchor with guns visible on deck, and the crews aboard and not ashore.

*Told.* A harbour man there said guns are also taken off hulls and carried up to the forts, and that the forts have more of them than they had. The factor of the eastern delta, hearing this repeated to him the season after, said it was the wrong way about and that in his yard the guns went aboard and stayed aboard. Both returns are entered.

## The second head: the white salt, its price and its markets, and the size of the works

**Fourth and sixth seasons, the factor of the plain road, two markets.**

*Seen.* At the first market, the white salt was to be had in the dry season in any quantity asked, at a price he calls not worth writing down, and at the second market the season after it stood a little dearer, which the sellers laid to a wet turn of weather and not to any buyer.

*Seen.* He asked after it as a bulk good and was told it is not carried as a bulk good. The villagers scrape it off their own ground when the ground dries and bring in what they have. There is no house that deals in it at scale and no standing price he could set down as he would set down a price for pepper or for salt of the pans.

*Offered.* This factor states that he can procure the white salt in whatever quantity is wanted and put it on the road, and asks what quantity is wanted. The offer is entered as he made it.

**Sixth and eighth seasons, the same factor.**

*Seen.* On the plain road, two walled compounds he had not seen when last he came that way, each with tall stacks smoking, each walled about with men at the gate and no entry for a stranger. Carts of charcoal and of cut wood going in through the day in numbers, and he counted, on one afternoon at the second of them, nineteen such carts inward and eleven empty outward.

*Seen.* In the villages within half a day of these, labour being hired at a wage above what the fields pay, so that the headmen complained to him that they cannot keep men to the harvest. Brick being made nearby in quantity and timber brought in.

*Told.* A carter at the second compound told him there are others of the same kind further along the plain, and that some had been built away in the south country beyond his own road. He has not been to the south country.

*He supposes,* from the hiring and the wages and the smoke and nothing else, that whatever is made in those compounds is made in far greater quantity than a few years since. He states plainly that he has not been inside a gate, that no one would tell him what is made there, and that he sets this down as his own guess and not as knowledge.

*The tenth season's return* from the same man says only that the compounds still stand and still smoke, and adds nothing.

## The third head: the mountain road, the stations, and the stores behind the northern line

**Third, fifth and seventh seasons, the factor of the mountain passes, who has travelled that road since his father's time.**

*Seen.* Gangs at work on the pass road across three seasons, stone laid and drainage cut, and one long broken stretch, which he says has been broken and patched for as long as any carter's memory, taken up and made new. The passage is easier and quicker than he has known it, and the beasts come through in better condition.

*Seen.* Two stations newly built where there had been none, with well, water trough, and stabling, at the day's intervals the road keeps.

*Seen.* More guards on the road than before, and the caravans not stopped nor the toll altered.

*Seen.* The state's officers buying grain in the northern markets season upon season, in quantity and at set prices, and taking all that was offered. This factor sold grain into that buying himself, in two seasons, and says so.

*Seen.* Grain moving north in trains of carts. On one day on the upper road he counted forty-one carts loaded, going north, with soldiers walking beside, and none coming back loaded.

*Seen.* At two of the forward towns behind the river line, storehouses either new-built or enlarged, new roofs and new walling, and the carts going in through the gates. He stood in the market and watched the gates. He was not admitted and did not ask to be.

*Told.* A clerk of the quartermasters at one of those towns, in drink, said the stores there now hold a winter and more where before they held a part of one, and complained that the counting of it falls on him twice a year.

*Told.* A carter of the pass road said the whole of this work is for the river line and for nothing else, because the carts go that way and nowhere else.

*He supposes* the same, and gives the same reason, and marks it as a guess.

**The ninth season's return** from this factor says the road works were finished and the gangs gone. **The tenth season's return,** from a second man of the same house, says gangs were still at work on the lower stretch. Both are entered.

**A statement this factor asked to have set down in his own words.** He keeps to the road and to the caravan towns, where his trade takes him and where he is known. What passes a gate he can tell, and how many carts and in which season and what the grain cost. What stands behind a gate he cannot tell, and he will not be sent to find out, and he asks that this be written down beside his returns so that no one takes his word for more than it is.

---

**Network status: intact.** The factors were not detected, none was questioned or turned back, and nothing indicates the realm knows a foreign power has been asking these questions. The arrangement is renewable for the next period on the same seasonal terms.
