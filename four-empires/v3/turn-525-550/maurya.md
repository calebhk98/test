# Part XIII: The Audit Reign (525 to 550 AD)

### The Ruler and the Inheritance

An exceptional ruler, assessed at seventy-six and so at the low edge of that band, took the throne in about 525. The historians' note on the mark is that he could spend the throne's standing on a hard matter and win it once or twice in a quarter century, and not five times. Two fights were carried. The rest of the realm's standing difficulties were left where they were found.

The previous reign left the southern continent garrisoned coast-wide, three hundred and fifty places founded on it at a cost of some 6.25 billion paṇas, a Han hand cannon with the taking apart still in hand, a marine engine of the realm's own building that was heavy for its power and thirsty and trusted only in fair season, a fielded one-man firearm from about 518, and the galvanic understanding four years matured. It left two absences. There is no closing reckoning of the treasury for the year 525, where the register carries one for every hundredth year since 250 BC. And there was no informer establishment anywhere on the southern continent, so that three hundred and fifty founded places reported on themselves through the officers who had founded them.

> **The reasoning.** The first order of the reign was a count and not a policy. The ground stated to the council was that near three years of the realm's whole revenue had gone into one venture across an ocean, that the treasury had called the venture reckless and been overruled, and that no officer could say within four hundred million paṇas what it had cost or what the reserve held.

---

### The Audit of 526 to 531

The audit was given jointly to the treasury and the informer service. Its mainland part closed in about 528. Its southern part could not close until 531, because an auditor sent across that ocean spends a season sailing out, a season on the ground, and a season sailing home, and the party sent in 528 was carried past its landfall by weather and came home without reaching the eastern anchors.

- **The reserve stood at about 170 to 200 million paṇas,** seven to nine parts in a hundred of one year's revenue against a stated prudent target of one full year. Every figure in the reserve table since 250 BC stands higher, the previous low being about four tenths of a year.
- **The southern line understated its own charge by eighteen to twenty-four million a year.** The water and grain carried on schedule to the fourteen posts of the arid northwest line had been booked to naval victualling.
- **Six of the fifty major settlements were founded and not completed,** and three of the five middle-coast places stood under two hundred registered persons against an establishment of eight hundred. The forty-one anchors of the eastern coast and northern belt were found sound, with working yards at eleven.
- **The works and granary lines had been held below their level of 400 AD for fifty years,** works at 228 million against 250 million and ten parts in a hundred of revenue against near eleven, granary at 46 million against 50 million. The shortfall was priced at eighteen to twenty-two million a year and at nine hundred to eleven hundred million across the fifty years, while the military line rose from 800 to 900 million and the standing army from 986,000 to 1,084,000.
- **The muster rolls of three interior provinces were false,** the shortfall assessed at nineteen to twenty-six thousand men drawing pay and ration in the Gangetic interior, the Deccan interior, and one Tamil province.

The southern establishment asked that the reserve figure be kept from the provincial governors. The ruler had it printed instead, in about a hundred sealed circulars struck at the capital's press and carried to provincial establishments only, on the ground that a governor who does not know the reserve is empty will spend as though it were full. The court entered the risk that a circular in a hundred copies reaches a rival within five years, and accepted it.

The treasury gained by the audit, and from about 531 the officer holding the southern establishment reported to it rather than to the throne. The realm's historians entered a formal dissent against the mark of eighty-four given to the previous reign, on the ground that a reign which places a wager of three years' revenue and leaves no reckoning of it has not finished its work. The mark stands with the dissent beside it.

---

### The First Fight: The Ledger

The first fight was fought against the southern establishment, the interior garrison commands, and the household.

**The southern line was cut from about 250 million a year to about 115 million.** New founding was suspended in 526, before the audit closed. The treasury asked for sixty million and was refused, the ground being that coastline lost to a rival who arrives first is permanent. The southern establishment asked that the full 250 million be held and that founding resume, and was refused. Rukshatirtha and Madhyaghata on the middle coast were withdrawn in about 533 and their three hundred and forty people moved to Gopatha and Shankhatirtha.

**The garrison and second-line infantry were reduced from 500,000 to about 445,000 across twelve years,** by not replacing wastage in the three provinces where the false musters were found and in no province holding a frontier. Field infantry held at 430,000, cavalry at 100,000, the elephant corps at 6,000 where it has stood for seven centuries. Two governors were replaced in about 534 for returning false musters. The frontier establishments of the Syr Darya, the Euphrates, the Caucasus, and the Hindu Kush were exempted by name, which is how the fight was won. This is the first fall in the standing military total that the appendices record, that total having risen in every period from 198,000 in 250 BC.

The nineteen million a year saved on the garrison line did not go to the works. It went to the navy, whose crew establishment rose from 48,000 to about 62,000 for the southern route and the engined hulls, at about fifteen million. The reduction paid for the fleet and no more. The works came out of the southern line.

**The household line was held at 45 million through 526 to 531 and set at 55 million after,** against 70 million before. Diplomacy and gifts fell from 28 to 22 million. Temple patronage, trimmed by the previous reign to help pay for ships, was restored to about 70 million, the Brahmin establishment holding the plates of the press under the arrangement of 525 and the printing of law and medicine being worth more than the saving.

**A light reduction in the silver of the paṇa was proposed by the treasury in about 529 and refused.** The reason entered is that a coin of standard weight across the whole territory is what lets a bargain struck in one province be honoured in another, and that the merchant houses' letters and bills rest on it.

The reserve fell to about 120 million in 529, the lowest figure the register carries, and stood at about 800 to 850 million at 550, a third of a year's revenue. The prudent target was not reached.

---

### The Works Restored

The works line rose from 228 to about 255 million a year and the granary line from 46 to about 60 million. The shares are set out here because the audit's own finding was made in shares and not in sums, and a reign that entered a dissent against its predecessor's mark for leaving a reckoning unclosed cannot leave its own loose.

| Line | 400 AD | 500 AD | 550 AD |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 2,285M | 2,280M | 2,460M |
| Works | 250M, 10.9 parts in a hundred | 228M, 10.0 parts | 255M, 10.4 parts |
| Granary and famine relief | 50M, 2.2 parts | 46M, 2.0 parts | 60M, 2.4 parts |

So the granary line was restored above its share in both earlier years and the works line was not. At 550 the works line stands five million above the sum spent in 400 AD and half a part in a hundred below the share spent in 400 AD, because revenue has grown by a hundred and eighty million since. By the measure the audit itself used, the works restoration is partial. To close the remaining half part would have cost a further fourteen to sixteen million a year, and the ruler did not find it, having spent the reign's economies on the navy and on the reserve. The court enters this as a shortfall and not as a completion.

The granary increase was spent on stock and not on building, the granaries having stood at four fifths of establishment.

**The Hindu Kush pass road and the northern forward granaries, 531 to 539, about 40 million a year.** An earthquake in about 320 broke a pass on the road to the Bactrian lands, and the repairs of that year were made by officers holding their posts through court favour. The register carries no entry showing that road brought back to standard in the two centuries since. It supplies the Syr Darya frontier, where China holds the far bank and where the realm's fixed cannon stand, and the court rates China the most dangerous power it faces chiefly because its army stands across a river from the realm's own ground. Two new relay stations were built and the forward granaries brought from a stock of fifty to seventy days of ration to a hundred and twenty.

**The Tigris and Euphrates embankments and canal clearance, 532 to 545, about 30 million a year.** These broke in the last years of the reign of 425 to 450 and the flood cost several hundred million paṇas before the valley came back by about 463. The audit found them kept to a standard adequate for an ordinary flood season and not a heavy one.

**The Deccan and central reservoirs and wells, 533 to 550, about 25 million a year,** with the African drought-hardy sorghum, millet, and cowpea pressed further into the dry northwest and northern Deccan.

---

### The Southern Establishment: From Founding to Holding

Founding stopped and holding began. The forty-one completed anchors were kept and filled, the six incomplete ones completed to a reduced establishment of four hundred persons, thirty-one of the three hundred lesser stations closed, and the fourteen arid northwest posts kept at fifty men each with their resupply moved into the southern line so that the cost appears where the decision is made. Those posts were kept because that shore is where a fleet crossing from Africa would come ashore, and a post of fifty men that sees a sail and reports it is the cheapest watcher the realm can set. The signal-tower relay method was extended along the eastern anchors between 538 and 548, bringing word from the southernmost anchor to the establishment's seat in two to four days in clear weather instead of two to three weeks by ship. Ternate and Banda were resupplied by the southern road, their garrisons brought back to about 1,200 and 700 and their harbour cannon from forty to sixty rounds to about three hundred and fifty each, which no ship could have carried by the direct route.

**The overseas informer establishment was funded from about 532 at about eighteen million a year.** The founders were not removed, including the four or five who had been sent to poor coast and quietly took ship for better ground. Removing them would have cost the realm men who had built harbours on a foreign continent and gained it nothing the audit had not already found. The island holdings were instead placed under a separate establishment reporting to the treasury and the informer service, so that the officers who made the seizures no longer write the only reports on them. At 550 the network covers the forty-one anchors and the two seats of the establishment. It does not cover the fourteen northwest posts, the reduced middle-coast places, or the cold southern island, where one informer among two stations would be known within a season.

**The island terms were offered late and on one island of three.** From about 540 the terms first used in Mesopotamia in about 130 to 150 were offered on the great northern island: local governance under oversight, local faith untouched, and trade terms set so that cooperation pays better than fighting. Funding was about eight million a year, which the court assesses as thin for the purpose. The coastal districts were counted. The interior was not entered and the number of its people is not known within any useful figure. On the cold southern island no terms were offered.

**Mortality registers were required from about 528** at every garrison, yard, and settlement outside the subcontinent, entered by month, by cause where a physician could name one, and by condition of person, free or enslaved. The realm's record of the Spice Islands garrison states that the capital's ledgers there counted the establishment and never the graves. Three measures followed from the first ten years of returns: inoculation against the pox made a condition of boarding for every southern convoy, the washing and antiseptic protocols printed and issued to every station, and convoy crowding cut by a fifth at about four million a year.

---

### The Second Fight: The Delivery to Rome

In about 501 the realm asked Rome for the working method of a marine engine and offered the complete account of the Chinese hand-weapon. Rome set out its own half in full in advance, required one delivery and nothing less, and gave notice that it would find the seams in a padded account. The realm did not have what it had offered, an example reached its engineers only at the close of the previous reign, and nothing was sent. The file of that reign records no decision against sending, only a task prepared and not finished.

The taking apart was completed and the maker's account written by about 529. The fight that followed was not with Rome.

> **The case against sending, as the arsenal and the western command put it.** The realm now makes such a weapon itself. Rome holds ground across the Euphrates and through the Caucasus. To send the complete construction is to arm a neighbour on a line the realm holds with fixed cannon and a frontier establishment just exempted from every economy of the reign. The account should be thinned, or held until the arsenal was further ahead, or traded for the engine rather than given.

> **The decision.** Three grounds were entered. A realm that asks and does not perform is worth less at every future asking. Rome's containment hint of about 385, given for nothing at the time, is what unlocked the realm's first working gunpowder weapon. And the realm had told Rome in about 478 that a one-man firearm lay fifteen to twenty-five years off, had fielded one at about 518, and had said nothing, so that if Rome learned it by any other road it would read a lie where the truth was a bench and a schedule. The account was ordered sent complete with no request attached, the realm's own firearm disclosed in the same letter, and the volume struck at the capital's press rather than copied by hand, the court weighing and accepting that this discloses the printing. The arsenal and the western command were overruled.

**The second request was not sent.** A draft confined to the two points where the realm's own engine actually fails, the seals and the fuel figures, was written in about 533 and sits unsent at 550 under a standing directive that it not go until Rome has answered the delivery. No answer is entered in the register.

---

### The Register of Things Owed

> **The reasoning.** The court went back over how twenty-five years passed with an accepted obligation unperformed and unraised, and found no decision to delay and no officer who had chosen to withhold. The realm records tasks, costs, and prerequisites, so an obligation to a foreign power entered its files as a bench task waiting on an earlier step, and such a task raises itself when it is finished and cannot raise itself when it is not. Nothing in the realm's manner of keeping records had a place to put a thing promised and not given.

From about 534 the treasury keeps a register of things the realm owes to foreign powers, by year, by power, by what was promised, and by what step remains. It is read aloud at each half-year council. It is not kept by the diplomatic office, on the ground that the office which would be embarrassed by an entry should not hold the pen. Three entries stood in it at 550: the Rome delivery, marked performed in 530; the standing courtesy owed Rome for the safety of the Mesopotamian stretch of the road, marked continuing; and the terms offered on the great northern island, marked begun and unfinished.

---

### The Notice to Egypt

A notice was sent to Egypt in about 543. Its purpose was settled at the council before it was drafted, because the first draft had two purposes in it and they wanted different letters.

> **The reasoning.** A claim of first finding is worth what a power can make it worth. There is no court between powers to hear one. The realm's own experience along the East African coast is that Egypt's answer was the same at every harbour whoever had come first, and the realm holds no instrument to compel a hearing of dates. What a letter can affect is Egypt's estimate of what a landing on that coast would cost. The purpose was therefore fixed as deterrence, and the letter was cut to carry only what raises that estimate: that the ground has been held since the survey of 415 to 420 and the depot of 478, that its coasts are administered and taxed and garrisoned along their length, and that they are closed to foreign basing.

**What was struck from the draft, and why.** The first draft, written about 541, gave the fifty major harbours, the three hundred lesser stations, the hundred and fifty to two hundred thousand people carried across, the naming of the eastern coast and northern belt as the settled and taxed ground, and the arid northwest as a line of garrisoned posts. All of it was struck in about 542. Egypt has known that the continent exists since this realm told it in about 462, so the new matter in such a letter is not the claim but the scale, and the scale tells a rival which coast is worth contesting and which is thin. The northwest sentence was the worst of the five, naming in one breath the shore a fleet crossing from Africa would reach and the fact that it is the thinnest ground the realm holds anywhere on that continent. A general statement of strength cannot be checked and must either be believed or be tested at expense. An establishment table can be read once and acted on.

**The cost, entered as the reserve circulars were entered.** The letter tells Egypt that this realm is watchful enough to write, and that the coast is administered rather than merely marked. That invites planning for a contested landing where silence might have invited none. The court accepted the cost on the ground that a rival expecting an empty coast lands where he pleases, while a rival expecting an administered one must bring a force and choose a place, which takes longer and can be seen coming by the fourteen posts of the northwest line.

**Two things the notice did not do.** It did not ask after the shipping seen provisioning on the African coast in about 500 to 505, because a question of that kind tells Egypt that the realm is still watching those harbours and has learned nothing new since, and the tasking of 529 is the proper instrument for that question. And it did not tell Egypt what the realm now thinks of the galvanic guidance of 495, which was told to Rome instead. The reasoning entered is that a rival who believes this realm is still spending effort on the plant path is a rival who does not know the realm has stopped.

---

### What Was Left Standing

- **China.** No message, no probe, no operation. The realm holds in its workshops a hand cannon taken by bribery from a Han arsenal and now fields a firearm of its own, and any exchange invites a question about where the second came from. The Syr Darya cannon, open between the two courts since about 500, was not raised.
- **The Silk Road toll,** left at twelve parts in the hundred while the revenue ebbed from 265 to about 230 million, the court's reading being that the northern steppe route lies beyond reach and that a higher toll drives more traffic onto it.
- **The Brahmin limit on printing.** The arrangement of 525 works and a fight over it would be the largest available at home. The ruler had two fights and spent them elsewhere.
- **Tidore,** not recovered, and no rebuilding attempted into the eastern waters.
- **The coffee plant,** not pursued.
- **The interior of Dakshinadvipa and of the great northern island,** not surveyed.

---

### Errors of the Reign, as the Court Assesses Them

- The audit was ordered in 526 and its southern part closed in 531, two years of that lost to a party that sailed against the season and came home without reaching the anchors.
- The overseas informer establishment was funded in the seventh year of the reign and thirty-two years after the first founding.
- The island terms were offered in the fifteenth year, on one island of three, at eight million a year against a southern establishment of a hundred and fifteen million. The lesson acted on had been written down before the reign began.
- The firearm's standard pattern of 536 gives parts that match within one arsenal's output and not between arsenals. The realm has known since the Alexandria embassy of about 365 that a great power had every hinge in a city matching.

---

### Absences in the Register

These are absences and not findings.

- No confirmed sighting of a foreign sail is entered by the fourteen northwest posts between 525 and 550. The posts sent their scheduled reports throughout, and an absence of report from fifty men on a coast of that length is not an absence of event.
- No answer from Rome to the delivery of 530 is entered.
- No count exists of the people of the great northern island's interior.
- Nothing further is entered on the shipping seen provisioning on the East African coast in about 500 to 505, beyond the original report of over a hundred ocean-faring hulls and past two hundred thousand tons.

---

Yearly revenue about 2,460 million paṇas: 1,045 million land tax, 230 million Silk Road transit, 240 million export duties, 180 million the Rome overland tariff, 140 million maritime and port tolls, 105 million mine royalties, 285 million state monopolies, 210 million internal duties and sundries, 25 million the first revenues of the southern establishment. Provincial retention near 369 million. Surplus to reserve near 29 million a year across the reign and nearer 55 to 60 million in its last decade. Reserve about 800 to 850 million, a third of a year's revenue. Reading and writing stay with about one person in eight to ten.

---

### The Unresolved Matters of the Reign

The southern claim is garrisoned, signalled along its eastern coast, and watched by an informer establishment that reaches its main places and not its edges, and whether it holds against a foreign sail is still a question for the coast and not the ledger. The delivery to Rome was made and no answer is entered, so the realm does not know whether it now stands as a power that performed late or as one Rome had already written off. The engine carries the southern packet in fair season and is not trusted in a heavy sea, and the request that would address its two known faults sits drafted and unsent. The galvanic force carries messages across two lines inside one city. The debt owed to the island peoples is part paid on one island of three. The shipping seen provisioning on the African coast fifty years ago is still unaccounted for.

# MESSAGES OUT

## To Rome, sent in the year reckoned 530, by the Mesopotamian road to the Syrian frontier, with one printed and sealed volume

From the throne at Pataliputra to the throne of Rome.

In the year reckoned 501 this realm asked Rome for the working method of an engine that moves a hull, and offered in exchange the complete account of the hand-weapon carried by China's soldiers. Rome set out its own half in full before receiving anything, and required one delivery and nothing less. The volume accompanying this letter is that delivery, complete. Nothing is asked in return for it.

The delay was twenty-five years and the reason does not flatter this realm. At the time of the offer this court held the men who survived that weapon at the island of Tidore. Those men could describe being shot at. They could not describe how the thing is built so that another maker may copy it, which was the substance of what Rome required. A witness's account had been offered where a maker's account was needed, and the difference was not understood at Pataliputra until Rome's terms arrived. An example of the weapon was then sought. That took the better part of two decades and cost more than is set down here. It reached the engineers of this realm at the close of the last reign, the taking apart was completed under the present one, and the account was written in the year reckoned 529. No decision to withhold was taken at this court at any time. The matter stood in the files as a task not yet finished, and a task not yet finished raises no question until it is finished, so twenty-five years passed with no officer putting it before the throne. The manner of keeping such records here has since been changed.

The volume gives, under separate heads: the weight and the length; the manner of carrying and of holding; the assessed killing range and the range at which aim fails; the time between shots for a trained man with his charges made ready; the effect observed on timber, on stone, and on men; the construction part by part, with barrel, lock, and stock drawn and measured, and the metals and their treatment so far as the metalworkers of this realm could determine them; the means of ignition; the loading sequence; and the full reasoning of the engineers, including the two points on which they remain uncertain and say so. There is no padding in it. Where a thing is not known, the volume states that it is not known.

Two further matters, given for nothing.

First, this realm has since built a weapon of the same class by its own casting work, and has had such weapons in its soldiers' hands since the year reckoned 518. In about 478 this court told Rome that it judged such a weapon fifteen to twenty-five years away. It was reached, and no letter was sent to say so. Rome should have that from this letter rather than from another quarter. The volume describes the weapon China's soldiers carry, as was promised. It does not describe the weapon of this realm.

Second, a warning, the two courts having traded what each sees of Egypt for a hundred and fifty years. In the year reckoned 495 Egypt gave this realm guidance on the force that runs in metals joined in salt water, the force by which Egypt binds its administration through copper lines. Egypt's counsel was that the road to it lies through the study of plants, and that this realm should look for the force in its trees and grasses and grain. The counsel was taken gratefully, every botanist of the medical tradition was set to it, the programme ran twenty-five years, and it produced no understanding of the force whatever. This court now judges the guidance to have been given in order to spend the effort of this realm down a road that leads nowhere. The purpose is this court's reading and may be wrong. The result is not in question. If Egypt has ever offered Rome guidance of that shape, this is what it was worth here.

Nothing is asked of Rome in this letter.

## To Egypt, sent in the year reckoned 543, by sea through Egypt's own waters

From the throne at Pataliputra to the throne of Egypt.

This notice concerns the great dry southern continent that lies beyond the eastern islands, and the islands to its north and to its south. It asks nothing and offers nothing.

That continent was found, and its northwest coast charted, by a survey expedition of this realm in the years reckoned 415 to 420. A depot with shipwrights was placed upon that northwest coast in about 478 and has been kept there since. Its coasts are settled, administered, and taxed by this realm, are garrisoned along their length, and are governed under this realm's law from seats established upon the continent itself. The great island to that continent's north and the cold island to its south are held by this realm also.

Egypt has known of that continent since this realm told it, in the exchange of about the year 462, when the charts of the eastern seas, the harbour notes, and the continent's location were given, and the account of what the copper lines along Alexandria's walls are for was received. That bargain is raised here not as a complaint but to record that Egypt learned of the ground from this realm, that this realm was upon it first, and that the dates above may be tested against Egypt's own memory of the exchange.

One matter of practice. The harbours of this realm upon that continent and its islands are open to Egypt's ships for trade on the same terms as the ships of any other power, and will grant no base, no garrison right, and no ground. That rule is adopted because it was applied to this realm at Aden and along the whole length of the East African coast for four hundred years, and because four hundred years of being turned from those harbours showed this court that the rule works and keeps a coast in one hand. No grievance is raised that requires an answer, and none is expected.

# SPY TASKINGS

One operation was mounted. Two were considered and refused.

**Ordered about 529.**

- **Who.** Two resident trading factors of the realm's merchant houses on the East African coast, one Gujarati and one Omani, with the ship-masters of the Oman gum, hide, and grain trade as carriers. With them from 529, one officer of the realm's own service under a factor's cover, the first man of the service placed on that coast.
- **Cover.** Buying gum, hides, and coastal grain for the Oman market. Both factories have traded those harbours by right since before 500. The officer travels as a junior man of the Gujarati house learning the accounts.
- **Target.** The harbours where bulk provisioning was seen in about 500 to 505. What was bought and in what quantity, what sailed, in how many hulls, in which season, whether those hulls have returned, and whether a second provisioning of the same kind is under way. Timber, cordage, and water orders in particular, the original report having been assembled from provisioning alone.
- **Network age.** The two merchant factories are twenty-five to fifty years established and have never been used for the realm's business before, which the court marks as both their value and their fragility. The realm's own officer is new to the coast as of 529 and has no line of communication independent of the seasonal ships, so a report takes a season to reach Oman and two to three weeks more to reach the capital by the signal towers.

**Refused: against China's arsenals.** The realm's own lesson from the operation that obtained the Han hand cannon is that bribery against a peer's frontier arsenals is murderously costly, that it succeeded once in nearly two decades by the chance of one desperate man in a bad year, that many agents were taken and presumably killed, and that many were turned in by the people they tried to bribe.

**Refused: against Rome.** The delivery of 530 was made for the purpose of standing as a power that keeps its word, and an operation discovered in Rome's yards would cost more than the engine method is worth.

# TERRITORY AT 550

## HELD, SETTLED OR GARRISONED

**The subcontinent and its seas.** The whole subcontinent to its southern tip with Sri Lanka. The capital at Pataliputra. The Kolar gold fields, worked deeper on steam pumping. The Zawar zinc workings. The Punjab salt range.

**The northwest and Central Asia.** The Afghan lands. The Hindu Kush passes, with the road and two new relay stations rebuilt 531 to 539 and forward granaries at a hundred and twenty days of ration. Bactria with its river gold. Sogdia with Samarkand and the Silk Road cities. The Syr Darya line, garrisoned, the fixed cannon kept upon it.

**Iran, the Caucasus, Mesopotamia.** The Iranian plateau with Khorasan and Media. Armenia and the Azerbaijan highlands. Mesopotamia to the Euphrates, governed from the second capital by the Tigris, its embankments and canals rebuilt 532 to 545.

**The Gulf and the Arabian shore.** Oman at the mouth of the Persian Gulf. The tributary trading ports of the gulf's Arabian shore. A tributary hold on eastern Yemen.

**The eastern seas.** The Strait of Malacca with the realm's trading port at the eastern mouth. Ternate, about 1,200 men, and Banda, about 700, resupplied by the southern road, harbour cannon at about three hundred and fifty rounds each.

**Dakshinadvipa, the southern continent.** Forty-seven major settlements standing of the fifty founded.
- *Northern belt:* Uttaradvara, Somatirtha, Vetravati, and nine further anchors.
- *Eastern coast:* Ratnakula, Nauvinda, Gopatha, Shankhatirtha, Tamraghata, Suvarnamukha, Ambupura, Prabhasa, and twenty-one further anchors. Eleven working shipyards across these two districts. Signal-tower relay completed 538 to 548.
- *Middle coast:* three places at reduced establishment. Rukshatirtha and Madhyaghata withdrawn 533.
- *Arid northwest:* the depot at Nauvandha, founded about 478, now a working yard; the northwest line, posts one to fourteen, fifty men each at about 250 kilometre intervals.
- Of the three hundred lesser stations, two hundred and sixty-nine stand and thirty-one were closed.

**The great northern island.** Coastal stations at Dvipamukha, Nagatirtha, Vanatirtha, the coastal districts counted and taxed, terms of local governance offered from 540.

**The cold southern island.** Himatirtha and Dakshinaghata, about two hundred men between them.

## SURVEYED OR CLAIMED ONLY

- The interior of Dakshinadvipa beyond sixty to a hundred and twenty kilometres inland at most points, claimed by the coastal holdings, neither surveyed nor entered.
- The coast of the arid northwest between the fourteen posts, watched at about 250 kilometre intervals, not settled.
- The western and southern coasts of Dakshinadvipa between the northwest post line and the crossing to the cold southern island, run by ship and charted, not garrisoned.
- The interior of the great northern island, not surveyed, its people not counted.
- The waters and coasts east of the eastern anchors, charted by the Malay and Sumatran pilots in the realm's service, not claimed.

## HELD BY NO CLAIM OF THE REALM

- Tidore, lost to China in about 477, not claimed back.
- The East African coast, where the realm trades at Egypt's harbours by permission and holds no ground, as it has held none since first contact at Aden in about 40 AD.
