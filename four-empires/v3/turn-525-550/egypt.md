# Turn 525–550 AD

Working paper of the strategic council. Alexandria.

Confidence classes are written into the sentence: **firm**, **reported**, **rumor**, **assumed**. Any figure concerning a foreign power is a floor. New this turn, on the arsenal's motion: any figure concerning Egypt's own supply of a material is a ceiling, and where two offices disagree the lower figure is the planning figure.

Standing figures: revenue about 132,000 talents at open, reserve about 263,000, two-year target with a floor at one year.

---

## Item 1. The reserve target is recomputed against risen revenue and the rule is kept

Revenue at open is about 132,000 talents (firm, receipts). The projection for 550 is 138,000 to 145,000 (reported, projection office, built on population and toll trend and no new stream). At 132,000 the two-year target is about 264,000 and the reserve is at target. At the 550 projection the target becomes 276,000 to 290,000, so a shortfall of 13,000 to 27,000 opens without any loss occurring.

The treasury proposed restating the target as a fixed sum, which removes the shortfall by arithmetic. Refused. The sum required scales with the state, not with the year the rule was written.

Top-up is set at 600 to 1,200 talents a year, taken from works and colonial construction rather than from research or defence. Harbour and road works in the newer African provinces slow by two to five years each as a result.

Accepted cost: the shortfall closes only if no shock lands. If one lands, the discretionary build-out slows and the reserve is drawn as designed.

---

## Item 2. The Copperbelt is reclassified from a revenue stream to a strategic input

Output holds or rises; cost per unit of metal rises, the richest veins being past (both firm, mine directorate returns). Crown consumption has outgrown every other material demand, because wire is the bottleneck under telegraph, generators, motors and radio, and the drawing mills built in the last turn draw on the same feedstock.

Copperbelt copper is reclassified as a strategic input consumed by the state. Copper's share of reported revenue falls from 9 to 13 percent to a projected 4 to 8 percent by 550 while the metal leaving the ground holds or grows.

The treasury's receipts side opposed, correctly, on the ground that the reserve target is computed on revenue and that removing a stream lowers the target. Resolved by computing the target on receipts including an imputed value for copper consumed internally, so the buffer does not fall by a talent. The mine directorate's receipts office loses its appearance in the revenue tables and is folded under the arsenal's materials account.

Standing instruction: no revenue comparison against any foreign power may use the reported figure without adding back the imputed copper.

---

## Item 3. One note is sent to Rome concerning the Atlantic coast of Africa

Three sailing vessels flying Roman colours passed down the Atlantic coast of Africa in about 524, were shadowed from the Saharan works to the Cape with handoffs by radio, kept well off shore, used a balloon for elevated observation, and turned north (firm, continuous Egyptian observation). Egyptian ships did not fire.

The note is sent for one reason. Egyptian garrisons, works, ships and balloons on that coast were within view of vessels that were not intercepted and were last seen heading home, so the presence is no longer concealable (assumed). An armed presence that has never been stated is one a future encounter can read as an ambush, and the council's standing fear is a captain firing on an unwarned vessel and beginning the war over a coast.

The note states the coast is under Egyptian administration, asks for advance notice of passages, and asks that vessels with no commercial business keep to open water. It does not state when the coast was acquired, does not mention the western ocean, hands over nothing, and threatens nothing. Standing orders are unchanged: shadow, do not fire unless fired upon, issue no threat that will not be enforced.

Accepted cost: refusing to discuss routes concedes that something is not being discussed, and a stated claim can be disputed where an ambiguity cannot.

---

## Item 4. Standing false accounts are restricted to matters a rival cannot visit

For roughly seventy years Egypt maintained an account, given to Rome through the formal channel, that the Atlantic face of Africa was held by capably hostile coastal peoples and that Egypt kept no naval presence there (firm, own file). In about 524 vessels flying Roman colours transited that coast with Egyptian garrisons and works within view (firm as to the transit, assumed as to what was seen).

The account failed because it described ground a rival could go and look at, and the decision on when to look was not Egypt's. Doctrine follows. A standing account may be maintained on a matter a rival cannot visit or verify, and the coffee-origin account and the electrical misdirection are retained on that basis. A standing account may not be maintained on the observable disposition of a coast, a harbour, a fleet or a garrison. On those, the instrument is refusal to discuss. Every retained account now carries a ten-year expiry review and a written successor position, so that its failure is planned rather than discovered.

The intelligence directorate opposed, arguing the Atlantic account bought seventy years that refusal would not have bought. That is true and the practice is restricted anyway, because the same account then cost the state a grievance it cannot remove, at a moment it did not choose. The directorate keeps the two accounts it argued for. The section that drafted and maintained the Atlantic account loses supervision of standing accounts, which passes to a review board sitting with the council.

---

## Item 5. The Enterprise is accelerated and the Caribbean north is held where it stands

Funded through the turn at the scale set in the last. Landings across all Atlantic holdings are firm from transport manifests at 90,000 to 180,000 persons. Surviving settled population at 550, excluding garrisons, is reported by the settlement office at 70,000 to 150,000 and treated as an estimate, the frontier returns lagging.

The temperate south at the Plata takes the Mediterranean and Alexandrian settlers and passes from perimeter to settled ground, with harbour works at two to four sites and wheat, grapes and olives in production (firm, settlement and yard returns). The tropical face from the Guianas through Brazil remains fortified posts and garrison harbours, thin and unbroken, settled from the African provinces because Mediterranean bodies do not hold in tropical lowland. The Caribbean north advances no further than the strongpoints at roughly Caracas and Trinidad. The council declines to advance it, that being the approach on which the two spheres will meet, and the state gaining nothing from creating the meeting early.

No Egyptian observation in this turn places a foreign vessel on the western shore (firm, patrol returns), which is not evidence that none was there: coverage of the western Atlantic is a small fraction of coverage of the African coast. The premise that the crossing may be compromised stands unchanged.

The colonies are reachable from Africa by relay through the depot chain and by vessel, not directly. A long-wave shore station programme to close the ocean gap is funded, window 540 to 565, marked uncertain. Until it returns, a colonial garrison decides for itself for two to four months at a time. Accepted.

---

## Item 6. The Amazon is ascended, with latex as the driver on the requisition

The skill is two hundred and fifty years of latex cultivation in the West African groves and the sulfur curing worked out over roughly 360 to 420. The source is a latex-bearing tree of the western basin whose sap the botanical missions report as superior in elasticity and in resistance to heat and cold (reported, single collection series). The demand is continuous: wire insulation, seals for steam engines and for the shaft where it passes through the hull, mine gear, aircraft.

Method is the Congo playbook, being the only method Egypt has for a great river with cataracts. Steam vessels ascend from the mouth, posts are planted at the major confluences, authority is pushed outward from the posts. Held ground at 550 runs to the posts and the river frontage between them, several hundred miles of ascent. Survey by river parties runs far beyond and is survey only. Cost is 1,200 to 2,600 talents over the turn, plus 150 to 350 a year in garrison.

Standing instruction: no grove is planted at scale until a second assay series confirms the first. The West African groves took a century to become worth their cost.

---

## Item 7. A stud establishment is founded on the temperate southern grassland

Cavalry is the weakest arm and the limit has never been skill. Every part of Africa fit for horses in quantity is narrow highland or lies against the fly belts, where the sleeping sickness kills horses in weeks. The grassland behind the Plata is open, extensive and carries no tsetse (firm, survey and veterinary observation). Stocked from the East African highland studs and by sea from the Arabian coast, at 400 to 900 talents in works and 80 to 200 a year. Breeding stock in quantity 545 to 560; remounts in militarily useful numbers 565 to 590, beyond this turn.

Unresolved: horses bred nine to twelve thousand miles from the Sinai are useless in the Sinai unless they can be shipped, and the transport arithmetic has not been done. Cost not yet assessed.

---

## Item 8. Contact parties are placed under quarantine discipline

Coastal populations in contact with Egyptian landing parties on the western shore have fallen heavily (firm, repeated observation at multiple sites). The physicians attribute this to crowd diseases carried by settlers reaching a population with no prior exposure (reported, medical office, consistent with the empire's epidemic records and demonstrated by no mechanism the establishment can show).

Discipline imposed: a holding period before parties enter an unvisited settlement, exclusion of men showing fever or rash from first-contact duty, separation of survey crews from garrison populations after contact. Reasons entered: a dead informant guides nothing and survey rate on an unfamiliar coast depends on local knowledge more than on hulls; a collapsing population beside a garrison is an epidemic that can turn inward; and the medical office requires surviving populations to continue the series.

Accepted cost: survey slows by a tenth to a fifth. The council does not know whether the discipline will work, not understanding why exposure protects some bodies and not others.

---

## Item 9. The electric-nitrate programme is made directed and the niter beds are expanded

Nitrogen is the ceiling on every Egyptian explosive, and the supply is cave deposits and maintained niter beds (firm, arsenal and assay records).

The electrical line, undirected in the last slate, is made a directed programme. The skill is chemists who have run current through everything and weighed the results for three centuries, in an establishment that measures what enters a vessel and what leaves it. The source is the Livingstone Falls plant, built for aluminum and arc steel, which holds electric current in a concentration greater than has existed anywhere (firm, works returns). The demand is that a nitrogen supply independent of bed acreage lifts the ceiling on explosives, on the propellant in Item 10, and on fertiliser. Allocation 500 to 800 researchers, 700 to 1,500 talents a year, bench process 545 to 585, works scale not estimated. May return nothing.

Independently, the beds are expanded by a factor of two to three over 525 to 545, at 2,000 to 3,500 talents in works and 200 to 400 a year. A research programme with an uncertain window is not a supply plan.

Standing instruction: the assay office's nitrate figure and the arsenal's have not agreed for two generations. The lower is the planning figure. Reconciliation ordered, report by 535.

---

## Item 10. A nitro-propellant programme is opened

Egypt has no firearms and no field artillery, and the 500 review assigned the class to the following century. It is opened now because technical advantage over rivals stands first in the council's order of concerns, and a weapon class a rival may hold and Egypt does not is the plainest available failure of that priority.

Barrel metallurgy is in hand from the arc furnace and from three centuries of boring, gauging and pressure testing. What is missing is a charge that burns at a controlled rate in a closed breech rather than detonating, and Egypt has no powder tradition to draw on, having reached explosives by nitrating organic fibre in the assay houses.

Allocation 400 to 700 researchers, 300 to 700 talents a year, placed under the same directorate as the stabilized explosive programme rather than beside it, so the two do not compete for the same chemists without anyone deciding which wins. The arsenal opposed the opening as a diversion from stabilization, lost the point, and gained the placement. Workable piece 545 to 575. Nothing in the fleet or frontier plan for this turn assumes a gun.

Unresolved: whether a controlled-burn charge is reachable from nitrated fibre at all.

---

## Item 11. The hull rate is reported short of requirement

Construction ran at roughly three hulls a year through the fifth century, giving 250 to 350 major steam hulls that cannot be replaced quickly. Standardisation and interchangeable components bring the rate to 5 to 9 a year at 545 to 550 (reported, yard directorate).

The improvement does not close the vulnerability. Against a rival able to produce iron and cloth at factory scale under permanent coal haze (firm, agent observation of Rome's northern provinces in about 408 to 412, treated as a floor on what that rival can now do), 5 to 9 is not an attrition rate. Standardisation continues with no expectation that the same method yields a further doubling.

Armoured hulls at 550 are 60 to 120 of the 250 to 350. Plate goes on new construction as standard and is retrofitted only where a hull is in yard for other work, retrofit time being time not spent on new hulls.

---

## Item 12. Aircraft pass to series production

The falls plant has poured aluminum since the last turn and structural metal is in airframes as expected. The electrical and precision works is extended to airframes, so craft are built to standard drawings in series rather than singly. Production at 545 to 550 is 20 to 60 airframes a year; in service at 550, 150 to 400, treated as approximate, training and tropical attrition being high and returns lagging. Capability is reconnaissance and incendiary at a useful radius of 300 to 700 miles. There is no air-to-air weapon, Item 10 delivering nothing inside this turn.

Standing instruction: numbers, ranges and basing stay in the highest compartment and are shown to no foreign eye, including the scholars admitted under Item 13.

---

## Item 13. The China arrangement is honoured in full and the compartments are not widened

Twenty of their scholars each decade sit inside Egyptian institutions by agreement, and Egypt opened its full germ and tropical-disease body to them (firm, own file). Nothing shown is slowed, rationed or quietly degraded. The knowledge has already passed and cannot be recalled; the paper stream rests on the arrangement, paper having replaced papyrus as the standard substrate from about 475 to 490; and a partner who finds an exchange narrowed prices every later dealing accordingly.

A register office is established under the Library's branch administration, recording what each scholar is shown, on what date, at whose authority, and what was asked for and refused. Its purpose is that a later estimate of foreign tropical capability starts from a known base rather than a guess. The record of the questions is collection obtained by paperwork, at 20 to 50 talents a year. It is not a tasking and is not entered as one.

Compartments never in the exchange are not widened: electrical work beyond the published optics and mathematics, vacuum, radio, radar, aircraft, the crossing, fleet dispositions, and the reserve's depth. Declining to widen a compartment that was never open is not a change of terms and is not to be represented to anyone as a concession.

Two premises are revised. First, a power previously held out of tropical country by disease is assumed capable of operating there within one to two generations of receiving the practice, window 545 to 585, assumed and treated as a floor; the recipient works from a written body where Egypt worked from nothing. Second, Egypt's file records that China stated at the outset it would not share silk production or weapons manufacture, which is a category a partner named and not a description of anything Egyptian eyes have seen. No estimate of foreign weapons manufacture may be built on the carve-out's existence, and the Quanzhou delegation's not being shown military workshops supports no figure.

The council's disposition toward China is 4 on trust and does not rise this turn. The Cape command is instructed to log the question of whether a power operating in tropical country would come west toward the East African coast, where the patrol line has stood at thirty degrees south since the 450 to 475 period, and not to redeploy against it.

---

## Item 14. Crops

Western stock arrived around 510 to 515 and was distributed for trial across a dozen climates. Empire-wide surplus increase over the 500 baseline is 4 to 9 percent at 550 (reported, treasury and provincial returns), concentrated where expected, in food-marginal provinces flipping toward self-sufficiency or export, the Congo and the West and East African lowlands above all, where cassava grows on soil that carried almost nothing. The Delta is close to unchanged, being already the richest grain land on earth. Population is 27 to 29 million against 25 to 26 at open (reported, returns between censuses).

One absence recorded rather than concealed: the highland interior behind the western coast has never been entered by an Egyptian party, so what grows there is unknown. Every crop obtained so far comes from the two zones Egypt has walked on, tropical lowland and temperate coast.

---

## Item 15. Freed term men are directed to colonial grants, and no reform of estate labour is proposed

Chattel slavery for mine workers was replaced over 352 to 368 by defined-term contracts ending in manumission and a settlement grant, with mechanical substitution required in the deadliest work. The reason recorded then and never revised is that large concentrations of indefinitely bonded men are the gravest danger inside the wall and that cutting the headcount is a war measure. The Copperbelt revolt of about 345, coordinated across three sites and retaken in eleven days by the Zambezi steam fleet, is the event the reform answers.

Freed term men owed grants are routed to the Atlantic colonies as a standing preference. This lowers the bonded headcount at the concentrated African sites and supplies the Enterprise with settlers already accustomed to the tropics.

The settlement office is instructed that term-contract labour, not purchased chattel labour, is the instrument for colonial works. Reason entered: the 345 precedent. A concentrated bonded population nine to twelve thousand miles from the fleet, where relief takes months, is the worst arrangement available.

No reform of labour outside the mines is proposed. About sixty percent of revenue rests on the land and grain tax of the Nile, the Levant and the islands, and the estates under that base run on bound people. Nothing has been referred to the council on the question and it is not entered as unresolved.

---

## Item 16. The interval to a Roman war is not re-estimated

The estimate last revised in about 408 to 412 put the confrontation at ten to twenty-five years and is now more than a century past, which means the estimate was wrong and not that a war happened unobserved. No observation in this turn bears on Roman intent. External intelligence is commercial and sees what merchants see in ports and corridor cities. A number produced from nothing would be worked to as though it meant something.

Standing instruction: the planners work to the case, not to the date. The case is a sequential single front, survivable through terrain and depth, and a coordinated two front with the Maurya, which is not. Preventing the coordination remains the central diplomatic objective, and no letter this turn addresses it.

---

## Item 17. Taskings

One authorised, at SPY TASKINGS. Three declined. Against China, the register office in Item 13 collects what an operation would seek at no risk, and an operation discovered would cost the paper and silk streams and the exchange. Against the Maurya, the disposition is 2 on trust and anything they learn is already assumed to reach Rome; no question about them would change a decision this turn. A second Roman tasking on industrial output was proposed by the confrontation planners and declined, because the northern network has stood since about 408 to 412 and its value is that it has not been rolled up.

---

## Item 18. Treasury note

Committed: the Enterprise at continued scale, the Amazon ascent, the southern stud, the bed expansion, the directed nitrate programme, the propellant programme, continued standardisation and plating, the airframe works, the long-wave stations, the register office, and the standing slate.

Affordable against revenue of about 132,000 rising to a projected 138,000 to 145,000, and a reserve of about 263,000 rising to a projected 276,000 to 290,000. The top-up is funded out of works and colonial construction, which is why provincial harbour and road works slow by two to five years each. Slack for a shock is thinner at the close than at the open. The reserve absorbs a shock. The build-out is what slows.

Research runs at roughly five percent of revenue, 6,600 to 7,300 talents a year. The funded establishment is 24,000 to 40,000 at 550 against 20,000 to 35,000 at open (reported, Library and branch returns across every budget). Named programmes account for 4,000 to 7,700 of that headcount; the remainder is the standing establishment and is not itemised.

# MESSAGES OUT

One letter. None to China. None to the Maurya.

## To Rome

> From the council at Alexandria to the government at Rome.
>
> The Atlantic coast of Africa, from the Moroccan approaches south to the Cape, is under Egyptian administration. Egypt keeps harbours, works, signal stations and naval patrols along its length.
>
> Vessels flying Roman colours have passed along that coast in recent years. Egyptian ships escorted them and did not interfere with them. Egypt intends to continue that practice and asks two things.
>
> First, that Rome give advance notice of the passage of its vessels along this coast, in the manner Egypt has previously asked in respect of significant movements in the Mediterranean. Notice allows Egypt to instruct its captains before a passage rather than during it, and removes the risk that a passage is taken for something other than what it is.
>
> Second, that vessels having no commercial business on this coast keep to open water and not approach Egyptian harbours or works. Egypt does not propose to fix a distance and will not treat an honest error of navigation as an act.
>
> Egypt makes no request concerning Roman shipping in any other water and gives no undertaking concerning water that is not its own. Egypt will not discuss its routes, the disposition of its fleet, or its arrangements with other parties, and asks that the refusal be taken as the ordinary reserve of a state.
>
> Egypt has no complaint to make and makes none.
>
> Alexandria.

# SPY TASKINGS

**Target.** Rome.

**Wanted, in order.** First, whether the Roman government now holds that Egypt has crossed the western ocean and established itself on the far shore, or holds Egypt's presence to be confined to the African coast. Second, whether the Roman account of its vessels lost in Atlantic waters south of Morocco has hardened into a settled belief that Egypt destroyed them, and whether that belief is now attached to the garrisons and works observed on that coast.

**Cover.** Commercial, through the trade-corridor stations, reporting as a sideline to genuine trade. No officer carries electrical apparatus, radio being held to fixed shore stations and warships and put in the hands of no agent abroad.

**Who runs it.** The intelligence directorate, through the recruited-insider and Greek-Egyptian embedded channels in combination, no single source trusted alone, reporting checked across two of the three channels before it reaches the council.

**Age of the network.** The commercial-cover network in the Roman ports is generations old. The dedicated military-technical placement in the northern provinces has stood since about 408 to 412, and the tasking is written to ask it nothing, these two questions being answerable in the ports.

No results are written here. A report is expected at the next turn.

# TERRITORY

## Held

**Mediterranean and Near East.** The Nile Valley and Delta. Cyrenaica and Tripolitania. Cyprus. The Levantine coast. The Asia Minor coastal holdings, confirmed still held at this turn's close. The Sinai. The Yemen coast, Aden and the former Himyarite highlands, holding the Bab-el-Mandeb. The Western Desert oasis chain and the forward Saharan stations.

**Africa.** Nubia and the Nile to Khartoum. The former Aksumite highlands. Djibouti and the Horn. The East African coast entire, from Eritrea to the eastern coast of the south. The East African highlands to the Rift, the Lake Victoria basin and the Great Lakes zones. The Zimbabwe plateau. The Copperbelt. The Zambezi corridor and the Hwange coalfields. The Cape region. Botswana, the Namibia interior, Lesotho, Eswatini. The Congo basin, administered through river control from the Livingstone Falls portage, with the industrial city and the aluminum plant roughly a thousand miles up the river behind the cataracts. The Gold Coast and the Akan goldfields. The West African coast from the Congo mouth to Senegal. The trans-Saharan corridor and the integrated Sahel. Morocco's Atlantic coast and the Western Saharan coast, garrisoned last turn, with bunkers and heliograph stations tying the West African relay into the Mediterranean network.

**Indian Ocean.** Madagascar, a full province.

**Western Atlantic.** The coastal perimeter of the tropical face from the Guianas through Brazil, thin and unbroken, as fortified posts and garrison harbours. The temperate south at the Plata, now settled ground rather than perimeter, harbour works at two to four sites. Strongpoints at roughly Caracas and Trinidad, not advanced. Posts on the lower and middle Amazon at the major confluences with the river frontage between them.

**Claimed, not settled.** The deep rainforest and deep desert interiors of Africa, as since 300 AD.

## Surveyed, not held

The Amazon basin beyond the posts, by river survey parties.

The highland interior behind the western Atlantic coast. Never entered by any Egyptian party. Recorded as unvisited, not as unpromising.

The Caribbean and the coast north of the Caracas strongpoint, coastal survey only, no advance authorised.

The western Atlantic shore south of the Plata toward the cold ocean, coastal survey only.

Australia, from Mauryan disclosure in the 425 to 450 period, in the highest geographic classification, never visited by an Egyptian vessel. No action proposed. The reason recorded then stands: a party that has known of the coast longer has very likely set itself there, so a single expedition around the bottom of the world buys nothing usable.

The crossing route itself. Held, used, and neither asserted nor denied to any power. The premise that it may be compromised stands and is not tested this turn.
