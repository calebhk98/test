# Second packet for the throne at Pataliputra

This is the second distribution for the period: correspondence addressed to this court and arriving in or after 550.

---

## Documents received

### 1. From the Senate and the Emperor of the Romans. Drafted 550. To go by the Euphrates road with the next convoy.

> From the Senate and the Emperor of the Romans to the throne at Pataliputra.
>
> 1. The volume came to Rome in the year Rome reckons 531 and Rome has sent nothing back for nineteen years. There is no explanation of that worth setting down and this letter offers none. It is a fault of this court and Rome names it as one.
>
> 2. The volume is what was asked for and more than Rome expected. It was translated and set out within two years, its drawings were built to directly, and weapons made to them serve in Roman hands. The three warnings set beside its figures were the most useful part of it after the drawings. Each proved material. Rome would rather have a document that marks what it does not know than one that reads smoothly.
>
> 3. One part of Rome's offer of 525 was never performed. Rome undertook to send its engineers to build the first hull engine beside your own, and no Roman engineer went. Twelve engineers and two master fitters sail with the convoy that carries this letter. They are to work in whatever yard your court names, to build beside your men and not apart from them, to answer what they are asked, and to stay as long as they are wanted. They carry the working drawings again in case the first set went astray. Nothing is asked for them and they are not to be paid by your court.
>
> 4. Rome gives one thing in return for the warning about the counsel of 495, which was given for nothing and which Rome has acted on.
>
> In the last generation Rome sent three ships south down the Atlantic coast of Africa with orders to stand well off, enter no surf, attempt no contact, and look through the glass. All three came home. What they saw is this. The coast below the edge of Roman reach is not empty. It is built and administered continuously for some three thousand miles, in cities of cut stone laid to a plan, with harbour works, ranked warehouses, foundries at work, and a chain of stone signal towers throwing mirrored light between them. Native towns and villages lie behind and between them beyond counting. A single ship shadowed the survey its whole length in relay and let it go home. And at the southern end the continent ends. There is a corner, and open sea runs east around it, and at that corner stands the greatest city of the whole coast with the largest gathering of shipping the survey saw anywhere.
>
> Rome sets down the last of those plainly because it concerns your ocean more than it concerns Rome's. The eastern sea is not enclosed. It opens to the west around the foot of Africa. Rome held the contrary for four hundred years on the authority of Ptolemy and was wrong. Rome adds that the corner is held, and by whom you may judge from the rest.
>
> Rome does not send this as intelligence traded for intelligence. It is sent because your court sent Rome something for nothing and Rome will not be the party that only receives.
>
> 5. Rome asks one thing, and asks it knowing which of the two courts is in debt. The volume states that its figures of range and of rate were got with your own powder from your own plains at your own proportions, and that a different powder moves both. That is exact. Rome's powder is not yours and the tables had to be got again. Rome asks for a measured quantity of your powder, enough to fire and weigh against Roman powder, so that Rome may know the size of a difference it can presently only name.
>
> Rome sends none of its own in return, and says so rather than letting it be noticed. If your court holds the exchange unequal, it is unequal, and Rome will not pretend otherwise.
>
> 6. One correction of the Roman record. For a century Rome complained that the Mesopotamian road lost cargo and that your court reported otherwise. Rome's factors on that road now report that it carries roughly twice the waystations it did a generation ago and that mounted patrols work the long runs between them, and that the building was done in their own time. Whatever was said at the time, the road was mended. Rome had not acknowledged it and does so here.
>
> 7. Nothing further is asked of your court in this letter.

---

## Enclosures physically received

*With the letter from Rome, as it states:* twelve Roman engineers and two master fitters, travelling with the convoy that carries the letter, to work in whatever yard this court names; and the working drawings for the hull engine, carried again in case the first set went astray.
