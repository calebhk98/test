# Encounter adjudication: Egypt and the Maurya on Dakshinadvipa, 525–550

Adjudicated from the two powers' **standing orders only**. Neither court wrote what it saw; both wrote what they do. This supersedes the observation sections that were struck from both turns.

## Coordinator note

**The geography does the work.** Egypt sails from the Cape and Madagascar on the westerly run, so landfall is the **southwest corner** — about 34°S — and the squadron works north up the west coast and east along the south coast. The Maurya hold exactly one thing on that side: the arid northwest post line, twenty-two posts at ~160 km with eight walled stations of two hundred, sited by the realm's own survey at *"the only landings on that coast where a large fleet could water."* Everything south of the line is in the realm's own **surveyed-or-claimed-only** column: roughly three thousand kilometres of claimed coast with no post commander on it.

**The two instruments collide at water and resolve in silence.** Egypt's order forbids landing at any harbour showing works, vessels or people. The only places a squadron can water on the arid northwest are the eight walled stations, every one of which shows all three. **Egypt is forbidden by its own orders from landing at precisely the places the Maurya garrisoned to stop it landing.** Both dispositions are aimed at the same eight bays and neither is ever tested.

Consequences:
- **Degree 4 never triggers in twenty-five years.** The realm's most generous rung, water offered from the post's own cistern so no foreign well is dug, is never used, because Egypt will not ask.
- **Degree 5 never triggers against a party.** Egypt raises no work, stays no night and enters no held ground on any coast the realm garrisons. It does all of that freely on the coast the realm claims and does not garrison, where there is no commander to warn, remove or seize.
- **Degrees 1 and 2 trigger repeatedly and by design.** Survey work is slow and stationary, so a hull sounding a bay stays in sight past a day, which is exactly the Degree 2 trigger. The Maurya wrote that rung so *"a rival counting this coast counts a garrison and not an empty beach."* Egypt counts the garrison. **This is the largest single transfer of information in the encounter, and the Maurya chose to make it.**
- **Degree 3 is possible only from 546**, when the Nauvandha squadron stands up. The first eighteen years are a coast that watches, shows itself, and never speaks.

**Nobody fires, either side, in twenty-five years.** There is no rung either side climbs unilaterally. The one place a shot could come from is a Degree 2 display misread as massing, and Egypt's response to massing is to hold and not close, which defuses it.

### The open gap

A beach repair of eleven days at about 31°S in 543, after reef damage, left a cairn with a cut timber marking the position. The Nauvandha squadron found it in **549, six years cold, with nobody to warn** — and Degree 5's removal step is conditioned on a written warning first. The commander recorded it, drew the marks, left it standing, and referred the question to Ratnakula. **Unanswered at 550.** A real want in the instrument, left open rather than resolved.

### What each side ends with

**Egypt has:** charts of the west coast from ~34°S to the northwest turn and a long run of the south coast; **thirteen** works counted of twenty-three there; four read as walled with cisterns, of eight there; one yard with hulls building; beach counts all short of true establishment; from 546, cannon-armed hulls carrying sail and engine together; and a sealed instrument in three languages naming the holder.

**Egypt does not learn:** anything of the eastern coast where Ratnakula and thirty-three anchors and eleven shipyards are; the northern belt; that there are sixty-two harbours, 250,000 registered persons, forty inland stations, a southern road, or a hundred and ninety cannon-armed warships; whether occupation continues past the turn; whether the interior is held. Its doctrine makes every number a floor, which is correct — every one of them is.

**The Maurya have:** hulls of an unknown build, iron-sided, gun-ported, **moving without sail**, one carrying a tethered ball on a line with a man beneath; counts never above four in a season; a close look at three hulls at hail in 547; two records of a statement handed over and taken without answer; and reports from inland people of boats and strangers at the southern water, which is how the realm hears about the coast it does not garrison.

**The Maurya do not learn:** the visitors' identity as fact; that there were nine hulls; that the visitors were forbidden by their own orders from landing on occupied ground, so the realm cannot tell restraint from indifference; that every sighting reached Alexandria within weeks by an apparatus it cannot see, while its own word takes a fortnight to Ratnakula by rider and a season to Pataliputra.

### Identity, and the two channels

**Egypt gets it firm, from the paper.** The Degree 3 statement is struck and sealed at a capital in three languages, one of them Greek. Its Sanskrit text and seal identify the issuer, and its operative formula — *no base, no garrison right, and no ground* — is Egypt's own Aden rule of 25–50 AD handed back word for word, which the Maurya file says was deliberate.

**The Maurya get it as interpretation only.** Build, bearing, and their masters' sight of Egyptian hulls at the East African harbours make a strong reading, and the realm's own previous reign predicted Egypt would reach that coast within fifty to eighty years. It goes in the interpretation column with its basis stated; the fact column never names a power.

**Two notes for the coordinator.** The Mauryan notice of holdings sent to Egypt in 543 reaches Alexandria *before* the expedition's document. The council therefore gets the same claim twice — once as a letter that could be a bluff, once as a sealed paper handed over at sea by an armed squadron, backed by works its own officers counted. Those corroborate, and the corroboration is worth more than either alone. And **Egypt sent no reply**, so the Mauryan court does not know the notice was received, nor that its rule was applied at sea in 547 and 549 to the very power it wrote to.

No aircraft appear: Egypt's own turn states aeroplanes range from a coast Egypt holds and cannot follow the fleet into open water. Radar is kept out of both reports as a compartment question; nothing in the outcome turns on it.

# B. To the strategic council at Alexandria

**Report of the southern-landmass survey, hulls out of Madagascar and the Cape, 527 to 550. Confidence classes attached in the sentence. Every figure concerning a foreign power is a floor.**

## 1. Passage and disposition

Nine hulls were committed across the turn in four reliefs, three or four on the coast at a time, the remainder refitting at the Cape and at Madagascar. The westerly run was made by the standing method. Landfall was made in 528 at about thirty-four degrees south on the western face of the landmass.

Losses were to sickness and to the sea in ordinary proportion. One hull took reef damage on the southern coast in 543 and was repaired on a beach at about thirty-one degrees south over eleven days. Survey ran roughly a sixth slower than planned throughout, being under the contact discipline.

## 2. What was charted

The western coast is charted from about thirty-four degrees south northward to the point where the coast turns eastward in the northwest. The southern coast is charted eastward from the landfall for a long run. The squadron turned back short of its end and short of the northwest turn, and **the extent of the landmass beyond those two points is not known** (firm as to the limit of the survey).

The southwestern corner and the western coast below about thirty degrees south carry good anchorages and running water. The southern coast carries fewer. Both were entered freely. Water was taken at eleven places. **Nothing on either stretch showed works, vessels or people not indigenous** (firm, own observation, boats away and parties ashore at nine of them).

Indigenous coastal peoples were met at fourteen places, in settlements of a few score to a few hundred, under the quarantine discipline.

## 3. Where the coast changes

From about thirty degrees south on the western face, and continuing north and then east to the limit of the survey, the coast carries **works that are not indigenous** (firm, own observation, at close range and from the balloon).

**Counted, and entered as a floor: thirteen separate works.**

- **Four** are walled, of masonry and rammed earth, with a cistern-head, a cleared approach and a beaten track inland (firm, seen at under two miles and from the balloon at height).
- **Eight** are smaller and unwalled, a cistern, a store and a few buildings (firm).
- **One**, at about twenty-one degrees south, is a harbour with a yard: wharves, sheds, timber stacked and seasoning, and hulls on the stocks and in the water. Hulls counted alongside and building at the one passage in 545: **not fewer than nine** (firm, counted from the deck and from the balloon at two thousand feet).

The works stand at even spacing where they were seen at all, about thirty leagues apart. Where the count shows a wider gap, **the council should read a work the squadron did not see rather than an absence** (assumed, on the regularity of the intervals actually measured).

## 4. What the works did when the squadron worked in front of them

On five occasions, when a hull lay in sight of a work for more than a day at its charting, the same sequence followed at each (firm, own observation, five separate occasions at four works and at the yard, 537 to 548):

A standard was raised. Armed men were brought out of the work and drawn up on the open beach, in view, and held there. At nightfall fires were lit along the shore at intervals for some distance either side.

Men counted on the beach, taken as floors, the count made from the deck and from the balloon by three observers:

- at the largest, **not fewer than a hundred and forty** (537, a walled work)
- at a second walled work, **not fewer than a hundred and twenty** (544)
- at the smaller works, **not fewer than thirty-five** and **not fewer than forty** (538, 548)

The men were in cloth dress and carried arms at the shoulder. Horses were seen at three of the five, in sections of about twenty. **No boat came off. No hail was made. Nothing was fired, at any of the five** (firm).

The council should note that this display was made each time only after a hull had lain still nearby, and that its plain effect is to have a passing ship count a garrison. **Whether the count so offered is the true establishment of the work or a part of it cannot be told from a beach** (assumed). It is entered as a floor.

In 538 a boat party pulled toward a bay that had appeared empty from seaward, made out a cistern-head and a beaten track at about half a mile, and put about without landing, under orders. **No landing was made at any place on that coast showing works, vessels or people** (firm, own record, whole period).

## 5. The vessels, from 547

No vessel not indigenous was seen anywhere on that coast before 546 (firm).

**In 547**, three hulls of the squadron working in front of a walled work at about twenty-eight degrees south were approached by **four vessels** coming from the north. Build: wooden, ship-rigged, guns in the sides, and **two of the four carried a funnel and made way with sail set and smoke together, holding station and speed in a wind that would have stopped the other two** (firm, observed over some hours at under half a mile). They placed themselves between the squadron and the shore and stayed there.

The squadron held position and did not close, under orders. A boat came from the largest of the four to within hail. **Nothing was fired.** A paper was passed across. It is carried home with this report.

**In 549** the same or a like force, counted at **four vessels, one with a funnel**, did the same at the approaches to the yard harbour, and the paper was passed a second time. **Nothing was fired.** No word beyond the passing of the paper was said on either occasion, and none was asked.

**Counted at once, whole period: four vessels. That is a count from one deck on two days and is a floor** (firm as to the count, assumed as to anything beyond it).

## 6. The paper

The document is written three times over in three tongues. The first is **Greek** and was read at sea. The second is **Latin**. The third is in the **script of India**.

The Greek text states that this coast was found, charted, garrisoned, settled and taxed by the realm issuing it; that its harbours upon the coast are open to the ships of any power for trade, water and repair on the same terms; and that **no base, no garrison right and no ground will be granted upon it**.

The document is struck at a press and sealed. The master of the Madagascar hull, who has traded to the Indian ports for twenty years, reads the seal and the style as those of the throne at Pataliputra (**reported**, on his reading; **firm** as to the document itself, which is in the council's hands with this report and may be read).

The council will observe that the operative words of the last clause are Egypt's own rule at Aden and along the East African coast, returned in the same order (firm, on the text).

## 7. What this report does not answer

The squadron did not round the northwest turn and did not reach the eastern end of the southern coast. **What lies on the far side of that landmass is not known and no estimate is offered.** Nothing was learned of the depth of the occupation inland beyond a beaten track and a cleared approach; boats did not land on occupied ground and no party went inland there. **Whether the thirteen works counted are the whole of the occupation, a face of it, or its thin edge, cannot be answered from this survey, and the standing rule applies: they are a floor** (assumed).

Sightings were signalled to Madagascar and the Cape on each occasion under standing order. No ground on that landmass has been entered as held. No settlers were carried.

# C. Returns of the coast establishment, Dakshinadvipa

**To the establishment at Ratnakula, and thence to the throne at Pataliputra.** Fact is entered above; interpretation is entered below and separately, and is the establishment's reading and not the record. Posts are numbered from the southern end. The line carries no signal tower; word from a post travels by rider.

### 530 to 533. Before the Order

**FACT.** Four reports, from Posts 3, 5 and 6, of a hull or hulls standing off the coast, in 530, 531 and twice in 533. **These were made before the Order and are in no common form. Hour, bearing, distance and build were not required and were mostly not given. The date of the first hull upon this coast cannot be fixed from the realm's own papers.**

**INTERPRETATION.** The establishment cannot say whether these were the first, or the first that a post happened to see.

### 535 to 544. The Order in force

**FACT.** **Twenty-one entries**, at nine posts, all under **First Degree, sight and record**. Across the entries: one hull at fourteen, two at five, three at two, never more than three at one post at one time. Sides of iron or iron-plated. No masts carrying sail. One upright pipe amidships, little smoke. Guns in the sides, ports counted between six and ten a side. **Sail set: none, at every entry. Moved without sail: yes, at every entry**, against the wind at eleven. Distance two to nine kilometres, working slowly, stopping, lowering boats, taking soundings.

**Second Degree, three occasions.**

- **537, Walled Station 2.** A hull lay within sight two days and a night. Standard raised, garrison drawn up morning and afternoon of both days, fires lit at intervals both nights. The hull remained, lowered a boat which sounded off the point, stood away north on the third morning. **Nothing fired. No hail made.** *Recorded here and at no other station: a grey ball, larger than a boat, held above the after part of the hull on a line, with a man in a basket beneath it, put up in the forenoon and taken down at dusk both days.*
- **538, Post 6.** A hull lay off two days. Line shown manned. **Two boats pulled in toward the bay to about half a kilometre, lay on their oars, and put about without landing.** Nothing taken, nothing left, no man ashore. Nothing fired.
- **544, Walled Station 3.** A hull within sight three days. Line shown manned three days and nights. The grey ball seen again. Stood away north.

**545, Nauvandha.** A hull lay off the approaches four days, out of gun range, sounding. **Second Degree** shown. Boats worked the approaches and did not enter. Stood away. **Nothing fired.**

**INTERPRETATION.** The hulls move without sail. No hull of this realm does. That is fact and is the establishment's chief report. The build answers hulls lying at the East African harbours, given by masters of this realm trading there by permission. Every approach and departure bears from and to the west and south. On those grounds the establishment reads these as Egypt's, and records that this is a reading and not a thing seen or told. The soundings, boats and slowness are read as charting.

### 545. The Amendment applied

**FACT.** Registers of all twenty-two posts and Nauvandha sent entire to Ratnakula at season's end, and each season after. On consolidation the establishment can state as fact that in 543 hulls of the same build were entered at Post 4 and Post 11 within six days, which the distance will not allow of one hull. **There is more than one.**

### 546

**FACT.** Six cannon-armed hulls based at Nauvandha, two engined. No foreign hull this season.

### 547. Third Degree, first application

**FACT.** Four hulls of the squadron, two engined, went out to three foreign hulls working before Walled Station 5, placed themselves between them and the shore, and held. **The foreign hulls did not close, did not withdraw, and did not alter station.** They held.

The squadron closed to hail. A boat went across from the flag. **The written statement, in Greek, Latin and Sanskrit, was handed over and taken.** Nothing further said. Nothing asked. **Nothing fired by either party.**

Recorded at close range: sides plated in iron, riveted, no timber wale showing. No mast to carry sail. One pipe, warm, smoke thin and near colourless. Guns behind hinged ports, one gun on the fore deck under a cover. The grey ball on its line above one of the three, down and secured. Men fair and dark both, in white and blue cloth, clean, forty to sixty on the nearest deck. Speech heard, not understood by the boat's officer nor by the interpreter carried. **No answer of any kind was made to the statement.** The paper was taken and carried below. The three held station until the squadron drew off, then stood away seaward and were not seen eleven days.

**INTERPRETATION.** The statement was taken and not answered. The establishment cannot tell whether it was read, understood, or whether the officer had authority to answer. **Nothing follows from silence and nothing is entered as following from it.**

### 548

**FACT.** A hull within sight of Walled Station 2 two days. Line shown manned. Stood away south. **Nothing fired.**

### 549. Third Degree second application, and a work found

**FACT, at sea.** Four squadron hulls, one engined, went out to two foreign hulls at the Nauvandha approaches, placed themselves between them and the harbour, and held. Closed to hail. **The statement handed over a second time and taken.** Nothing further said. **Nothing fired.** The foreign hulls stood away seaward the same day.

**FACT, ashore, beyond the line.** Three squadron hulls worked south past Post 1 to see how far the foreign hulls had gone. At a bay about a hundred and thirty kilometres south of Post 1, a landing party found the marks of a large boat hauled up and a second beside it; a hearth lined with stone, long cold, and the ash of a fire kept some days; cut and shaped timber, shavings and iron scale, being the leavings of repair work; and **a cairn of stones with a squared timber set upright in it, cut with marks the party could not read.**

**Nothing and nobody was there. The party judged the marks not less than several years old.**

**What was done.** The Fifth Degree provides for warning in writing, and for removal if the work continues after warning. **There was no party present to warn.** The commander recorded the place, described the work, drew the marks on the timber, and **left it standing**, referring the question to Ratnakula, this bay lying beyond the last post and under no post commander's charge. **No instruction has come back at the close of 550.**

**INTERPRETATION.** A work has been raised on ground the realm claims, on a stretch the realm charts and does not garrison, and the ladder as written reaches it only through a commander who is not there. The establishment enters this as **a want in the instrument and not as an event.**

### 550

**FACT.** Two First Degree entries, Posts 2 and 4, early season. **Nothing after.**

**INTERPRETATION.** The hulls move without sail. That is the return the establishment puts before the throne first, and it is fact and not reading. The build, the bearing, and the account of hulls at the East African harbours given by this realm's own masters read as Egypt's. This is a reading. Nothing was told to this establishment by any foreign party, and no foreign vessel or man gave any name.

Every hull entered in twenty years was working, sounding and boat-lowering in the manner of a survey. No hull came into a harbour of this realm. No hull fired, closed on a post, or set a man on ground this realm holds. Whether that is their order or their choosing is not a thing this establishment can see, and none is entered.

The realm's own ground below Post 1 was visited, camped on, worked on and marked upon, in a year in which no post saw a hull for eleven months. **Three thousand kilometres of the coast this realm claims returned nothing to this register in twenty years, having nobody upon it to enter anything.**
