# Encounter adjudication: Egypt and the Maurya on Dakshinadvipa, 525–550

Adjudicated from the two powers' **standing orders only**. Neither court was told what it saw; both wrote what they do, and one process produced both records. This supersedes all earlier drafts, including one written on a false geography.

## Corrections applied to the first adjudication

Four, all of which changed the outcome:

1. **The realm holds the whole coastline.** The first draft placed the Maurya only on an arid northwestern post line and treated everything below thirty degrees south as empty claimed coast. That was wrong. The realm's harbours, stations, posts and settled ground run the length of that continent's coasts. Egyptian hulls find worked, occupied, administered shore essentially wherever they close with it.
2. **Egypt has no guns.** Its armament is the moored mine, the spar and drifting charge, the petroleum incendiary and the aircraft. No gun port, no hinged lid, no gun on a fore deck can appear in any description of an Egyptian hull.
3. **Egypt speaks Coptic.** Since the reform of about 208 AD. Its ships carry men who read Greek and Latin because those are the tongues of trade and diplomacy, so a Greek text is read at sea by a translator, not by the ship's company.
4. **Identification is mutual and immediate.** Egyptian hulls, rig, dress and speech are known on sight to any Indian sailor who has worked the East African harbours, which is most of them. Both sides know who they are dealing with within minutes of closing. It is not one man's recognition on either side, and the sealed paper confirms what is already understood rather than disclosing it.

## The adjudication

**The two instruments slide past each other at water, and neither is tested.** Egypt's orders forbid landing at any harbour showing works, vessels or people. On an occupied coast that is nearly everywhere, so the squadron waters at the few places it can and goes short between them. The Maurya's Coast Order was written to graduate a response to landings that, in the event, almost never come.

- **Degree 4 never triggers.** The realm's carefully designed rung — water offered from the post's own cistern so no foreign well is dug, a dug well being a work raised — is never used in twenty-five years, because Egypt will not ask.
- **Degree 5 never triggers against a party.** Egypt raises no work and stays no night on ground the realm garrisons.
- **Degrees 1 and 2 trigger repeatedly and by design.** Survey work is slow and stationary, so a hull sounding a bay stays in sight past a day, which is exactly the Degree 2 trigger. The realm wrote that rung so *"a rival counting this coast counts a garrison and not an empty beach."* Egypt counts the garrison. **This is the largest single transfer of information in the encounter, and the realm chose to make it.**
- **Degree 3 waits on the Nauvandha squadron of 546.** What was absent before then is a *patrol vessel*, which the rung names. What was never absent is shipping: eleven yards and a supplied southern road put coasting craft, despatch boats and transports on that coast throughout. They see the foreign hulls, get closer looks than any post does, and keep company at distance without hailing, because a transport master holds no authority on the ladder.

**Nobody fires, either side, in twenty-five years.** There is no rung either side climbs unilaterally.

### What each side ends with

**Egypt has:** charts of the western face and a long run of the southern coast, both stopping short of their ends; not fewer than thirty-one works counted as a floor, including fenced holdings under crop behind several, which the council reads as settled country rather than a post line; beach counts of drawn-up garrisons, every one a floor and every one offered rather than found; water taken at four places only; and a sealed instrument in three tongues, none of them Coptic, read at sea by a translator.

**Egypt does not learn:** the eastern side, the depth inland, the extent beyond its two turn-back points, or any number that was shown rather than discovered. Its doctrine makes every figure it carries home a floor, which is correct — every one of them is.

**The realm has:** twenty-nine First Degree entries, six of them from its own coasting masters; a build description sharp from about 539; and the fact, entered from 547 and from the first close approach, that these are Egypt's ships. Also: **no port, no opening, no lid or shutter anywhere in those sides, at three hundred metres and again at four metres alongside, and nothing on deck any officer can name as a weapon.**

**The realm does not conclude they are unarmed**, and the register says why: no officer on that coast has seen a hull of that construction, and a flush side may be a side whose ports have not been found. So the Nauvandha squadron interposed twice between a survey ship and the shore without any officer aboard being able to say what stood on the other side of the water — pointing cannon at a vessel with nothing aboard that could answer, and nobody on either side knowing it. The register enters that it will be true again.

### The instrument gap, and what it produced

A beach repair of eleven days below the last post left a cairn with a squared timber cut with characters nobody could read. Word came inland **within the season**, from people of the country trading at a post's well — the realm hearing of its own claimed coast from its inhabitants, because it has no post there. A party of ten went down overland and found the place months cold.

Degree 5's removal step is conditioned on a written warning and on the work continuing after it. **There was nobody to warn.** The commander recorded, measured, copied the characters, left the work standing, and referred the question to Ratnakula, which sent it on. No instruction had come back at 550, and the cairn still stood when the squadron passed in 549.

The realm's answer, the **Second Amendment of 550**, is the best institutional product of the encounter. An unattended foreign work is *not* thrown down. It is recorded, measured, drawn, its characters copied — and a dated stone marker of the realm's own is set beside it in the same three tongues, and the place garrisoned. The reasoning: throwing a marker down destroys the evidence of what was claimed and when, and buys a grievance for nothing, while a dated marker beside it contests the claim *by the same custom of first presence that made the realm's own*.

The Amendment also closes three faults found by use: the Order's geography stopped at the last post, so the referral fell to nobody; masters made entries and approaches with no authority; and the pre-Order reports were too loose to date the first hull. **At 550 neither the marker nor the post yet exists** — the instrument went south in the last season and the passage is a season.

### Two notes for the coordinator

**The claim arrives twice.** The realm's notice of holdings reached Alexandria in 543, before the expedition's document. The council therefore holds the same claim as a letter that could have been a bluff and as a sealed paper handed over at sea by an armed squadron, backed by works its own officers counted. The corroboration is worth more than either alone, and Egypt's reply — sent this turn, ending seven years of silence — follows from it.

**The realm's notice overstates.** It says the coasts are "garrisoned along their length." The realm's own territory list puts stretches of the western and southern coasts as charted and not garrisoned, which is precisely why the 544 landing fell in no commander's charge. The realm records this internally as a knowing overstatement and records its cost: the notice ran ahead of the fact until the Second Amendment's post is raised.
