# Letters audit, v3 turn 525–550

Eight letters across four courts, tested against seven questions: does the sender know what it asserts; is anyone offering a power its own knowledge back; is anyone bargaining for something already given; are terms quoted that were never sent; are deliberate falsehoods marked internally; does the letter breach the sender's own doctrine; is it coherent with its own body text.

## The three that fail

**Egypt to China breaks two of Egypt's own compartments in one sentence.** The bark article opens "A bark of the **western highlands**… tested across eleven hundred cases in the Congo, the Great Lakes and **the western posts**." Egypt's Item 8 holds the *source* in the highest compartment on the coffee practice; Item 19 lists the Atlantic crossing among the standing compartments and TERRITORY says the route is "discussed with no power." Naming western highlands and western posts beside the African trial sites tells China that Egypt holds highlands and posts in a west that is not Africa. It is also self-refuting: the preceding article tells China that what Egypt has across the western ocean "remains what it was, a crossing made and cargoes brought back." A court told the second paragraph and then the third reads the second as a lie. The medicine can pass without the source.

**China's 527 letter to Rome reports facts from 543–550.** It states that posts are established along the far coast, the shore marked between them, and the country behind entered under a commandery. China's own turn plants those stations 543 to 550 and sends the first charting voyage in 532. In 527 there was no station, no marker and no commandery to bluff about. The body has partly absorbed the error too. Either it is a ~549 letter mis-headed, or it claims sixteen years before the fact.

**China's 529 letter to Egypt re-gifts the pendulum clock.** It offers "two clocks regulated by a swinging pendulum, with drawings of the escapement and the table of the beat, this court's own work and its own to give" — as consideration for three new asks. The previous generation already gave Egypt exactly that: China's own canon turn lists "the pendulum clock" among what it opened, Egypt's canon turn calls it one of the two items "genuinely new to Egypt," and Egypt's research table has "pendulum in the clock shops 538." Two physical clocks are a courtesy; the escapement drawings and the beat table are the same knowledge a second time. The letter's closing line — "this court is aware that what it sends is smaller than what it asks for" — makes it worse: the court audited the imbalance and still counted an item twice. Egypt's register office records what is asked and given, and will catch it.

## What the clear ones did right

**The Maurya's notice to Egypt is the model for a marked lie.** It says the coasts are "garrisoned along their length"; the body names that as a knowing overstatement, states which coasts actually are, gives the purpose, and prices the cost — "a power that tests the claim finds the gap, and the landing found overland in 544 lay in precisely the stretch the notice covered with a word." The territory list keeps the truth. No contamination.

**Egypt's note to Rome carries two deliberate falsehoods and enters both**, states what each buys, and bars contamination expressly: "no Egyptian paper is to adopt the letter's account."

**China's far-coast bluff is the best-handled deception in the generation** — filed under its own heading, with the true position (seven stations of thirty-six men, relieved once a year, eight months from the nearest Korean port, no ship of force and no means of sending one) written down beside the claim, and the trade priced: "a claim made before a foreign settlement is grown costs a letter, and the same claim made after costs a war at eight months' distance."

## Cross-cutting

**1. The delivery collision, and the coordinator's ruling.** The Maurya sent the sealed volume in 530 by the Mesopotamian road. Rome's entire turn rests on nothing having arrived: it put the powder programme on a receiving-nothing footing in 528, weighed four readings of the silence, hardened its disposition, and opened an operation in 549 to learn whether its own 525 message even arrived.

**Ruling: the volume was sent and did not arrive.** Rome's own file records persistent banditry on the Mesopotamian leg, reported by Roman merchants and denied by the Maurya at the time. A single sealed packet on a route that loses cargo is a packet that can be lost. This preserves both turns without either court being wrong, and it makes Rome's *first* reading — the one it weighted lowest, that the message never arrived — the true one. Neither court learns this. The Maurya believe they performed; Rome believes it was ignored. Both act on it.

**2. Rome's western article collides with what the Han already hold.** Rome's article 9 refuses latitude, departure point, passage time, wind, soundings and chart. But China's canon turn records Rome's earlier disclosure as having already given "islands six weeks west of the Canaries on a one way wind and current" — departure point, passage time and wind, all three. Neither letter is at fault; each is faithful to its own file. But to the Han, Rome's refusal will read as a court retracting in 550 what it volunteered in 515.

**3. Two transoceanic disclosures crossing, possibly about one continent.** Rome tells the Han it holds a mainland in the western ocean; the Han tell Rome they hold a far coast across the eastern ocean. Both offer the same reciprocity toward ground neither knows may be the same landmass.

**4. Egypt and Rome are already touching in the Caribbean.** Egypt holds an island arc north from Trinidad; Rome holds Hispaniola, Cuba and the lesser islands of the chain. No letter mentions it, and Egypt's survey squadrons are ordered north and west of the arc with instructions to enter no harbour showing occupation.

**5. Rome's gift lands on China's named open problem.** Rome sends coal-gas lighting with retorts and mains. China's first open question is the electrical force, and its arc light stands at three installations, each needing a turning shaft, an attendant and dressed charcoal, none able to go aboard a ship or to a frontier post. Rome's method solves what China has been funding open-endedly.

**6. Egypt and the Maurya do not agree about the southern coast.** Egypt logs thirty-one works with fenced holdings under crop along the south; the Maurya's own list says the western and southern coasts are charted and held by nobody. The two records agree with precision elsewhere — Egypt's "walled work at about twenty-eight degrees south" in 547 against the Maurya's Walled Station Five in 547, Egypt's "about thirty leagues apart" against the Maurya's hundred-and-sixty-kilometre intervals. The fenced holdings cannot be reconciled, and Egypt's next-turn tasking runs straight into it.
