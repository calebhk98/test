# HAN CHINA: COURT PROFILE, 525 AD

Prepared for the use of the office that keeps these records. Not a memorial. No copy leaves the office.

---

## 1. THE THREE OTHER POWERS

Each power is rated twice. Trust means how far the court acts on what the power tells it without independent verification. Danger means how much harm the court judges the power capable of doing to the empire.

A trust rating is only worth what the court has verified independently. Each entry below therefore states what the empire checked with its own people and what it was merely shown.

### Rome

**Trust: 7, extended credit rather than a finding. Danger: 6.**

The court holds no independently gathered fact about Rome. It runs no espionage there by policy, has no resident presence in Roman territory, and every item in the file was chosen by Rome and handed over: the kept Dnieper agreement, the unprompted disclosure of the ocean crossing, the rail method with the withheld portion named, the admission of two contested successions. All of that is consistent with an honest partner. It is equally consistent with a power that has judged candour cheaper than concealment on the matters it does not mind China knowing, and the court cannot tell those apart from where it stands.

Two things are checkable, and they are what the 7 actually rests on. First, the frontier has been quiet for two hundred years, which is observed by China's own garrisons rather than reported by Rome. Second, Rome's disclosures have twice been cross-checked against a separate source: the Maurya account of Roman extent in the fourth century, and Egypt's account of the western crossing in this period, and on both occasions Rome's version survived the comparison better than the third party's. That is thin, and the court records it as thin.

The rest is credit the court extends because the alternative is paying for an intelligence network on a frontier it does not fear, out of a treasury with no slack, against a power that has given it the single most useful technique it has ever received. That is a decision, not an assessment, and it is entered here as a decision.

**What would have to happen for the 7 to be wrong.** Roman force on the Dnieper growing beyond what a settled frontier needs. Roman interest in the eastern grassland it has twice said it does not want. Roman knowledge of Chinese interior distances or supply intervals appearing in Roman correspondence, which would mean the sketch in the dirt was defeated by other means. Or a Roman account contradicted by something China observed itself rather than by something Egypt says.

### Egypt

**Trust: 6, and lower than Rome's on knowledge though higher on verification. Danger: 5.**

The twenty scholars at Aden have independently confirmed three things: that the white light burns without flame and is installed across a whole city rather than at one showpiece, that vessels move without sail or oar, and that copper wire carries messages faster than a rider. Those are observations, not claims, and they are why the court believes Egypt about its own capability.

What the scholars have never confirmed is anything Egypt says about a third party, about its own intentions, or about what it is withholding. Egypt withheld the electrical mechanism and the steam hull. It told the Maurya court a different account of the electrical force than it told China, and China detected this only because the Maurya boasted of it. It opened this period's correspondence by attempting to interest China in fearing Rome, then conceded under a direct question that the veteran settlement plan was port rumour. The concession was creditable and unprompted, and it is also the only reason the court knows the overstatement existed at all.

The 6 therefore means this: verified on what Egypt shows, unverified and discounted on everything Egypt reports. Egypt is rated low for danger because it cannot reach the empire, has no land route to any Chinese frontier, profits from the trade, and has been defensive in every contact for a hundred and fifty years. The danger it carries is indirect. It is the only power that can hand a capability to someone who can reach us, and it has already been teaching the Maurya something.

### Maurya

**Trust: 2. Danger: 9.**

Every Maurya claim the empire has been able to check independently has been inflated. The Strait authority was thinner than stated. The Egyptian tolls were reported at the top of a false range to make the sea road look dear. The mastery of the electrical force was announced before any working device existed. The court's standing rule is that Maurya framing serves Maurya interests, and the rule has never once failed.

The danger rating is the highest of the three for reasons that have nothing to do with honesty. The Maurya are the only peer that shares a land frontier with the empire, they hold cannon on the far bank of the Syr Darya and have refused four times to withdraw them, they now have the powder formula and are reinforcing hulls for guns, they sit astride both trade roads, and they are of a size and age comparable to the empire's own. They also coordinated a land demonstration with a sea claim once already, in the 450s and 460s, which means they plan across theatres.

## 2. THE SINGLE MOST IMPORTANT THING

That the throne and no one but the throne commands the arsenals and the powder, because every other thing the state holds, the frontier, the revenue, the six centuries without a break, rests on no rival inside the empire being able to arm.

---

## 3. THE EIGHT CONCERNS AS THIS COURT ACTUALLY RANKS THEM

1. **Survival of the dynasty.** Everything below is a means to this. The court has never treated it as one item among others.
2. **Military or technical advantage over rivals.** Sixty-two percent of all revenue for six centuries. When the monopoly was declared lost in this period, the court did not reduce the program; it redefined the advantage as precision, range, and metallurgy and kept spending. The advantage is not held for conquest. It is held because it is the mechanism by which the first item is achieved.
3. **Internal order and the suppression of revolt.** Held so cheaply for so long that the court treats it as maintenance rather than crisis, which is precisely why it ranks this high: the maintenance is never allowed to lapse. The only reason land reform exists at all is that a revolt in the 350s took six months instead of six weeks.
4. **Defence of the core territory.** Ranked below the three above because the court does not experience it as a live problem. The heartland has not been threatened in six hundred years. The frontier system exists so that the question never arises, and the court thinks about the frontier rather than about the core.
5. **Revenue and the treasury.** Genuinely binding on decisions. In this period the navy was not expanded because it was unaffordable, the mills were built with merchant capital because the treasury had none, and the trunk line was part funded by silk houses for the same reason. Ranked fifth rather than higher because the constraint is respected only at the moment of decision and never converted into a reserve.
6. **Trade routes and access.** The empire fought its only naval war over this and would fight again. The 500 audit nonetheless established that tolls and silk export together are nine percent of revenue, and the court has since acted accordingly, keeping the Egypt road for the technology access rather than the money.
7. **Standing and prestige among the other powers.** Used as an instrument, not held as a good. The court maintains the appearance of a strong Syr Darya line because the appearance does defensive work. It refused the war the military faction wanted in this period even though refusal cost it standing at home.
8. **Religious and ideological legitimacy.** Almost nothing in the record turns on it. The tributary order is a form of words the court is content to leave as a mutual fiction with the Maurya. Christianity is a minor presence in the western trade towns and has never appeared in a policy decision.

**The two it would claim to rank highly and does not:**

- **Religious and ideological legitimacy.** Every memorial opens with the emperor at the centre of all civilisation. In practice the court settled for a fiction it knew to be a fiction at the Syr Darya three hundred years ago, has never let the tributary form dictate a decision against interest, and has no religious institution whose objection would stop anything.
- **Revenue and the treasury.** The court audits constantly, argues about cost in every memorial, and has produced no saved cash in six centuries. Whatever margin appears is committed to the next garrison or the next segment of rail before it can accumulate. The empire is grain rich and cash poor by habit rather than by accident.

## 4. HOW THIS COURT DIFFERS FROM THE REAL HAN IN CHARACTER

The real Han had to bargain. It bargained with the steppe by tribute, with the great families over land, and with its own generals over emergency commands, and in the third century the last of those bargains ended the dynasty. Bargaining leaves a court with a political vocabulary: arguments about right order, factions that must be persuaded, moral claims that carry force because a court that ignores them can be destroyed.

This court never had to bargain with anyone inside its own borders, and so it never built that vocabulary. Every revolt for five hundred years was suppressed by the garrison already standing there, which meant no emergency command was ever delegated, no provincial commander ever accumulated leverage, and no grievance from below ever arrived with force behind it. What replaced political argument was procurement. Problems reach this court as questions of supply distance, powder stocks, kilometres per season, share of revenue, and cost per garrison. The land concentration problem was not ignored out of callousness; it simply never presented itself in a form the institution recognised as urgent, because the only mechanism that could have made it urgent had been disarmed. When it finally did register, in the 350s, it registered as a suppression schedule slipping from weeks to months, which is the language the court can hear.

The second consequence is an unusual internal honesty. A court that has never come close to destruction can afford to write down bad news accurately, and this one does. Its own files record that the Syr Darya line was a bluff, that the gunpowder monopoly was lost a hundred and fifty years before it was admitted, that the westward expansion lost money, and that the naval threat against Ternate had nothing behind it. Six centuries of security produced an institution that keeps candid books, because nothing it writes down can be used to bring it down.

The third consequence is that the scholar class was transformed rather than victorious. In the real record the Confucian officials won the argument for restraint and turned the state inward. Here they never won it, and the examination race instead filled their ranks with men trained in mathematics, survey, and accounts. The empire's officials quote the classics and think in quantities.

## 5. THE VOICE OF THE DOCUMENTS

**Tense and person.** Present tense throughout, including for events six hundred years past. Third person, impersonal. The subject of almost every sentence is an institution: the court, the arsenals, the garrison, the throne, the empire, or simply China. There is no "we". No official is named after Emperor Wu, and no minute is quoted.

**Genre.** Neither a memorial nor a chronicle of reigns. It is a staff assessment with periodic balance sheets. The long file is organised by mechanism, not by emperor, and interrupts itself every fifty years with a State of the Realm giving population, output, revenue, extraction rate, literate population, technical workforce, and current weapons. The turn file is a court memorandum: headed sections, bulleted decisions, terms of approval listed as terms, and a set of open threads carried forward.

**What it records obsessively.** Numbers with ranges attached, and the confidence stated. Dates as spans rather than points. Shares of revenue and shares of spending. Distances, and days or months of supply. The provenance of every fact, separated into what the empire saw directly, what it inferred, and what an interested party told it, followed by a cold reading that discounts the interested party. Its own weaknesses, written down in plain terms while they are still cheap to shape. Mechanisms and the constituencies that keep them running, usually stated before the event they explain.

**What it barely mentions.** Religion, ritual, court ceremony, art, and literature. Emperors as persons. Battles as drama. The interior lives of subjects. Any moral claim about the right order of things. Individual heroism, including the one soldier on the Pontic steppe who prevented a war, who appears in the record as a debriefing document rather than as a man.

**Commitment.** The turn will be written in this voice: present tense, institutional subjects, headed sections, quantities with ranges, provenance marked, weaknesses recorded, and open threads listed at the end.

## 6. THE OWN FILES AND THE LETTER: TWO SEPARATE HABITS

The candour described above governs this office and no other. Internal candour and external disclosure are unrelated habits, and the court keeps them apart deliberately. Bad news entered in the empire's own files cannot be used against the dynasty, because the dynasty has been secure for six centuries and the files are read only by the men who wrote them. Bad news put in a letter to a rival is a capability handed over for nothing.

**What goes in this court's own files, plainly and without softening.** That the Syr Darya line was held on appearance and could not have survived a long war. That the powder formula left state control around the 350s and the monopoly was admitted lost only in this period. That the westward expansion returned nine percent of revenue against the largest sustained military cost in the empire's history and lost money. That the Ternate threat had nearly no powder, no marines, and no second fleet behind it. That the empire has one steam hull in home water trials where Egypt has had a fleet for generations, a hand cranked generator where Egypt lights a headland all night, and no continuous source of the electrical force at all. That the naval range advantage is on a clock because the Maurya are arming hulls. That the frontier garrison colonies farm a margin the climate is turning against. That estate ceiling enforcement is imperfect and the great families keep finding workarounds. That the mills' tax framing will not survive the scale of their profits and the court has not yet decided what to do about it.

**What never goes in a letter, to any power, in any circumstance.** Powder, formula, production volume, magazine stocks, or the number of days a frontier position can fire. Interior distances, supply intervals, depot spacing, railhead positions, or any map or figure from which those can be worked out. Which frontier the court believes it could not hold. Any audit finding about revenue, treasury slack, or the profitability of a route or a conquest. Silk production method, without exception and outside any trade discussion. The steam hull, its trials, or its faults. How far behind the empire stands on the electrical force. Any capability under development and not yet fielded, including the breech loader and the rifled naval gun's true reach. What the empire has learned from a third party, and by what means it learned it.

**The rule the court applies.** A letter states what the empire wants, what it will give, what it has decided, and what it observed directly and chooses to share. It does not state what the empire lacks, what it fears, what it cannot afford, or how it knows. Where the truth would disclose a weakness, the court says nothing rather than say something false, because a lie that is later checked costs more than a silence, and the empire's dealings with Rome have been worth more than any single concealment would have been. The two records are kept in different rooms and the wording is never carried across from one to the other.

## 7. THREE THINGS THAT WOULD IDENTIFY THIS DOCUMENT AS CHINA'S

1. **Every fact carries a provenance tag and a cold reading.** The document separates what the empire watched with its own eyes from what it worked out from evidence from what somebody with an interest told it, and then states what the fact is worth after that discount. Reports arrive as ranges (seven to fifteen hulls, twenty to forty cannon) and the range is left as a range rather than resolved into a single figure the court does not have.

2. **The empire's own weaknesses are written down in the plainest available words, in the same paragraph as its successes.** The line is a bluff. The monopoly is gone. The frontier lost money. The threat had nothing behind it. The file records the fragility now, while the program is still cheap to shape, because the office keeping these records is the only office that will ever read them.

3. **Causes are given as constituencies, distances, and costs, and the mechanism is stated before the outcome.** Nothing happens because a man decided it. Things happen because a foundry's staff depend on the program continuing, because the powder stock runs out in a limited engagement, because a garrison of fifty can hold against a larger force and therefore the supply burden stays manageable, because the survey advances fifty to a hundred and fifty kilometres in a season. The same template is applied to every new problem in turn: the outpost chain put on water, the outpost chain put on rail, the undirected empirical search funded as an open ended chain so the money is not pulled when it fails on schedule.
