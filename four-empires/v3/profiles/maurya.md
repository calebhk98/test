# The Maurya Realm: Court Position Paper, 525 AD

*Prepared at Pataliputra for the advisory council, from the court registers, the treasury's reckonings, the military archives, and the reports of the realm's agents. Where a figure is an estimate, it is marked. Where a judgment is the court's reading rather than a thing the realm has seen, it is marked. Sums are in paṇas of silver.*

*This paper replaces an earlier draft of the same title. The earlier draft recorded the steam exchange with Rome as executed on both sides. It was not executed on either side. Nothing went west and nothing came east. The error was in the draft and not in the file, and the ratings and assessments that rested on it are re-derived below.*

---

## 1. The Three Powers: Trust and Danger

The court rates trust and danger on separate scales of one to ten. Trust means how far the realm can act on what a power says without checking it. Danger means how much that power can take from the realm if it chooses to. A power can be honest and dangerous at once, and all three of these are honest in some measure.

### China

**Trust: 3 of 10.** China keeps the agreements it signs, does not lie about what it has done, and has held the Syr Darya line for three hundred years without crossing it, and it gave the realm the northern weather reports of about 495 with no evident guard and no price attached. Against that, it took the interpreter the realm lent in about 310 and used it to speak to Rome around the realm, it did not answer the realm's demand for the Strait toll at all, and it fired on the realm's ships and took Tidore while both powers were formally at peace.

**Danger: 10 of 10.** China is the only power that has killed the realm's soldiers and sunk the realm's ships, ninety hulls of a hundred and ten lost in about 476 to 478 with not one Chinese ship sunk in return, and it is the only power whose army stands across a river from the realm's own ground. Two facts of this reign raise rather than lower the rating: the realm now holds a hand cannon stolen from a Han frontier arsenal, which is Chinese state property taken by bribery and never disclosed, and the realm's answer at sea is thirty to forty cannon-armed vessels in home and Gulf waters and a handful of first-generation engines, which is a beginning and not a match.

### Egypt

**Trust: 1 of 10.** Egypt has never been caught in a checkable lie and has never told the realm one thing it could build from, giving function and withholding construction across a hundred and sixty years of dealing. The court now assesses the galvanic guidance of about 495, which sent the realm's botanists to look for the force in trees and grasses, as a deliberate sabotage that consumed a share of one research programme for twenty-five years, and a power that gives false theory as a gift is rated at the floor.

**Danger: 9 of 10.** Egypt threatens the realm's territory little by force of arms and has never attacked it, and everything the realm has lost to Egypt it has lost by arriving second: every western harbour, the East African coast whole, the coffee plant, and the price of its own goods into the Mediterranean. The rating rises by one from the earlier draft for a plain reason. The southern race was argued on the strength of a hull that ignores the monsoon beating a hull that waits on it, and the realm's engine is heavy for its power, thirsty, few, and trusted in fair season only, so the advantage the whole 6.25 billion paṇas was staked on is thinner than the reign's own summary claims.

### Rome

**Trust: 7 of 10.** The number is unchanged from the earlier draft and its whole basis has changed. It rested before on Rome having given the realm a capability it could build from, which is now known to be false: Rome sent no method, no fuel figures, no seals, and no engineers, and the realm's engine is the realm's own. The seven now rests on conduct alone. Rome stated its terms in full and in advance, laid out its own half complete before receiving anything, held to one delivery and nothing less exactly as it had said it would, did not renegotiate, did not press, and has not once in twenty-five years complained of a thing it is owed. That is the behaviour that earns a high trust mark. It is held to seven and not raised because Rome withholds as the realm withholds: it concealed how far ahead its own steam work was until the realm reasoned it out from the containment hint of about 385, it sent Egypt a subsidised relief fleet after the earthquake of 365 and said nothing of it, and it holds an overland tie to China the realm cannot see into.

**Danger: 5 of 10.** Rome sits across the Euphrates and the Greater Caucasus, has never tested that line, and holds no grievance of ground, since the realm halted at the river in about 150 rather than lunge past its supply. The rating rises by one from the earlier draft because the realm has made itself the party in default on the one matter open between them, and a counterparty who is owed something and says nothing about it holds a price-setting advantage over a counterparty who wants something and has already shown that he wants it badly. Rome can also cost the realm more by commerce than by arms, being the counterparty on a road worth about a hundred and fifty-five million paṇas a year in tariff and the buyer of the realm's steel, cloth, spice, gems, and sugar.

## 2. The Single Most Important Thing

The unbroken working of the tax-gathering administration over the integrated heartland, because that is the thing the realm counts as itself and the thing that pays for everything else it does.

---

## 3. The Eight Concerns, Ranked as the Court Actually Ranks Them

**1. Military or technical advantage over rivals.** The realm has funded weapons research at or near a tenth of revenue for two hundred and seventy-five years, and in this reign it wrote that level down as doctrine with the advisory council behind it so that a later ruler would have to fight an institution to cut it. In about 478 to 500 it gave up the Strait tolls, the free passage, and its doubled Silk Road rate, and refused only the one demand that touched the cannon on the Syr Darya. That trade shows the ranking plainly: commercial income was spent to keep a weapon.

**2. Revenue and the treasury.** Every decision in the record is argued in paṇas, and the realm's assessment of its own century is an assessment of its revenue lines. The treasury is also the instrument by which the realm reaches everything else, since its method against hard problems is to put thousands of paid hands on the same step at once, which only a full treasury can do.

**3. Trade routes and access.** This is close enough to revenue that the court often argues them as one thing, and it is ranked separately because the realm's temper lives here and not in the ledger. Being walled out of the western sea harbour by harbour produced a hundred and fifty years of grievance and then a venture costing 6.25 billion paṇas, which is near three years of the whole realm's revenue, spent on coast the realm had not yet been refused.

**4. Defence of the core territory.** The core has never been reached by a foreign army in seven and a half centuries, and the Rolling Frontier Doctrine exists so that it never will be. A concern this well secured commands less argument at court than the ones above it, and the realm still would not strip Oman and the Gulf to feed the southern programme.

**5. Internal order and the suppression of revolt.** Revolts happen and fail easily, as the Mesopotamian rising of about 240 failed, because nothing a province can field stands against the field army. The court treats order as routine work rather than as a danger, and the part of it the court is bad at is not suppression but integration.

**6. Standing and prestige among the other powers.** The court would place this near the bottom and its conduct places it here. Losing eleven ships while collecting the realm's own toll was read at court as an act of war rather than as a commercial loss, and the southern venture was launched by a court the record itself describes as moving at the speed of injured pride.

**7. Survival of the dynasty.** The realm's own record states that a change of dynasty is not a change of realm, and it has passed through several ruling houses, twice through open succession quarrels of two to three years, while taxes came in and the frontiers held. The officials draw their pay from the treasury and the army's loyalty runs to the throne as an office, so the men who write these records do not expect their own positions to depend on which house wears the crown.

**8. Religious and ideological legitimacy.** The realm's founding decision was the refusal of a religious turn, and its standing rule since has been to tax conduct and leave belief alone, which is a rule of administration rather than of faith. Religious spending stands at about sixty-eight million paṇas a year against nine hundred million for the army, and in this reign the temple line was trimmed to help pay for ships.

### The two it would claim to rank highly and does not

**Religious and ideological legitimacy.** The realm keeps the Brahmins at the summit of the order, patronises temples in every province, and formally embraced the Zoroastrian faith of Iran in about 260, and each of these was done for a governing reason. The embrace of Zoroastrianism was a measure for binding a conquered province. Giving the Brahmins the plates and seals of the press in this reign was a measure for getting standardised law and medicine printed. When the southern programme needed money, the temple line was among the first cut.

**Survival of the dynasty.** The court speaks of the throne as permanent and of the ruling house as its holder, and its own historians grade rulers on a numeric scale of competence, mark two houses as discredited, and treat a broken succession as a quarrel among great men. An institution that scores its kings out of a hundred and records that the state ran on without one has already decided which of the two it serves.

A note on the reverse case. Prestige is the concern the court claims to rank lowest and acts on most quickly, and the record has begun to admit this about itself.

## 4. The Difference From the Maurya Empire That Ended Early

The comparison concerns the institution's character and not its map. Four differences follow.

**The realm never acquired a language for judging its own conduct.** An empire whose founder had turned to a faith after Kalinga would have left behind a vocabulary of righteous rule, and its records would measure the state against that standard. This realm kept only the Arthashastra, which is a science of costs and pillars. So its records grade errors as miscalculations. The sale of the Latin interpreter in about 310 is written up as a clean and total loss and a piece of failed cleverness. The seizure of the northern and southern islands in this reign is recorded as a fact the court marked and did not disguise, with no argument offered that it was right or wrong. The realm keeps an honest ledger of its mistakes and has no words at all for a sin. Section 5 sets out what this costs the realm in practice.

**Seven and a half centuries taught the institution that it outlives its rulers, which the short-lived empire never had time to learn.** The administration is paid from the treasury, the army is trained to see a march for one claimant against another as the ruin of the thing it serves, and the historians assign each reign a mark and a quarter-century block. The practical result is a state built to absorb bad rulers rather than to prevent them. The reforms of about 390, an advisory council with standing to bring hard truths to the throne and senior officials paid outside patronage, were friction against future weak kings and not a cure for them. The realm expects roughly one reign in four to be wasted and has arranged its reserve so that a wasted reign spends the inheritance instead of the state.

**The Brahmanical settlement was never disturbed, and that settlement is the ceiling on the realm's technique.** Without an imperial embrace of Buddhism there was no monastic and scribal class outside the caste order, and so after seven and a half centuries reading and writing remain with about one person in eight to ten, and those the men of the cities, the merchants, the officials, and the upper castes. Printing existed from about 395 and was confined to tax rolls, law codes, military orders, and medical texts for a hundred and thirty years because the Brahmins' place rested on their sole keeping of the sacred word. This reign moved printing forward by handing the Brahmins the plates rather than by breaking them, and it set as its aim a merchant and official class that reads reliably and not general literacy, because the caste order will not permit general literacy. The realm's mathematics leads the world and its literacy does not, and the second figure limits what the first can be turned into.

**The realm learned to treat discovery as a search that money and hands can quicken.** This is a habit of mind the earlier empire had no occasion to form, and it came from two centuries of chasing the Chinese cannon. The court now reasons in prerequisite chains and in numbers of parallel attempts, holds that no coin buys a step before the step it rests on, and holds that any step already known to be possible will fall to enough funded hands. Its own record notes that the same improvement is sometimes reached in two of the realm's workshops at once, neither knowing of the other, and reads that as proof of the method. The court also holds, from its own history, that every research catastrophe it has suffered was a purge and never a shortage of ideas.

## 5. The Rome Bargain: What Was Done, What Was Not, and the Position It Leaves

The record gives much of this in durations rather than in dates. Where a year below is arithmetic from a stated duration, it is marked as reckoned.

### The sequence

**The realm moved first, about 500 to 501 (reckoned).** The realm asked Rome for steam that could move a hull, a capability it had worked out Rome held and had concealed behind the containment hint of about 385. It offered in exchange the account of the Chinese hand-weapon drawn from the men who had survived that weapon at Tidore, on the ground that no other power could supply it, the realm alone having been on the receiving end of the weapon and lived.

**Rome accepted and sent its demands in full, about 501 to 502 (reckoned), before any Chinese weapon was in the realm's hands.** Rome laid out its own half complete: the whole working method of the marine engine from boiler through transmission, the seals and where they fail, the fuel figures, and Roman engineers sent into the realm's yards to build the first engine beside the realm's own, on the reasoning that paper builds nothing without hands that have already ruined a few. Its price was the complete account of the hand-weapon: weight, length, how carried and held, killing range, time between shots, effect on timber and on a man, how it was constructed so as to be replicated at the best detail the realm could manage, how it was powered, and every judgment the realm's engineers had formed. One delivery, no installments, on the reasoning that pieces serve no one at the distance the two powers speak across. Rome gave notice that it would test what it received against what it already knew, that it would see the seams if the account were padded, and that it would answer padding in a manner that fit.

**The bind, from about 502.** The Tidore survivors could describe being shot at. They could not describe how the weapon was built so that another maker could copy it, and that was the heart of Rome's demand. Rome had asked for a maker's account where the realm had offered a witness's account, and a maker's account can only be written after the object has been had in hand and taken apart.

**The espionage line ran nearly two decades and delivered late, about 521 to 524 (reckoned).** The realm set the effort going against the Han arsenals in the reign's early years, after Rome's terms showed what was needed. The break came from an armorer at a frontier arsenal, deep in debt from a bad harvest, who over several years grew comfortable diverting a single scrapped hand cannon during inventory culls and making up its weight and metal elsewhere in the books. Barrel, stock, and lock left Han territory separately in three shipments across the better part of a year, and the weapon was reassembled only in the realm's own hands. The armorer was paid nine hundred silver weight-units, about fifteen kilograms of silver. The rest of the effort was a heavy loss. Many agents were taken and presumably killed, and many were turned in by the very people they tried to bribe. The court's conclusion was that bribery against a peer's frontier arsenals is murderously costly, that it succeeded once by the chance of one desperate man in a bad year, and that further work belonged in the realm's own casting shops.

**The realm sent nothing, and Rome sent nothing.** The object arrived near the close of the reign and the taking apart was still in hand when the reign ended, so there was no maker's account to send. No account, thin or complete, went west in these twenty-five years. The file records no decision against sending one. It records a thing prepared and not finished and a court that did not raise it again. Rome, having given notice that it would test what it received before sending its assistance, received nothing and sent nothing, which is exactly what it had said it would do. No Roman method came, no fuel figures, no seals, and no engineers entered the realm's yards.

**What the realm withheld, it withheld by default and not by policy.** Rome does not know that the realm's knowledge rests on a physical weapon in its own workshops rather than on frightened survivors, does not know the channel that brought it, and does not know that the realm has become a maker of this class of arm.

### The engine the realm actually has

Steam crossed its threshold late in the reign, about 518 to 523 (reckoned), on the realm's own work. It came out of the pressure-valve line settled in about 460 and out of the stationary pumps sent to clear the flooded deep shafts at Kolar in about 465 to 475, by the hands of the realm's own engineers, with no Roman method and no Roman hands. It runs. It is a first engine and shows it: heavy for its power, thirsty, held in small numbers, and trusted on the southern road in fair season rather than in any weather. The realm knows the difference between a thing that runs and a thing that runs reliably at sea, and it still wants the Roman method for that reason and no other.

Two entries in the reign's own record must be read against this. The closing summary states that the reign left the realm with working steam at sea that closed much of the naval gap with China. That claim is not supported by the engine as the programme section describes it, and the court should treat it as the summary overstating, in the same way the island ledgers counted the garrison establishment and never the graves. Separately, the standing directive that the first working marine engines go to the southern route ahead of the navy and every other use was written when no engine existed, and it now governs the realm's own engines, few as they are.

The other programmes of the reign are unaffected by the correction and stand as recorded: the crew-served two-man piece at about 506, useful paper at about 513, the one-man firearm fielded at about 518, and the wire-path galvanic understanding reached about 521 and four years matured at the close.

### The position, stated without softening

The realm asked. Rome named its terms in advance, in full, and generously. The realm accepted terms it could not perform, failed to perform for twenty-five years, said nothing about the failure in all that time, built a worse engine alone, and now wants the same counterparty to hand over the better one. That is a debtor asking for a second loan before the first is repaid, and the debtor is the party that opened the account.

Three details make the position worse than the bare summary.

**The realm fielded a one-man firearm of its own at about 518.** It had told Rome in about 478 that such a weapon lay fifteen to twenty-five years off. Rome asks sharp and particular questions about firearms and has met gunpowder on its own northern river frontier. If Rome learns that the realm armed its soldiers with the very class of weapon it had promised to describe, and described nothing, Rome will read bad faith where the truth is a bench and a schedule.

**Twenty-five years of silence is not neutral.** The realm cannot know whether Rome's file says awaiting delivery or says counterparty unreliable, closed. The officials on both sides who set the terms may be dead. A cold file cuts both ways: it may have to be reopened by the realm at the cost of reminding Rome of a default Rome had let go, and it may equally have been closed already with a judgment in it that the realm has never seen.

**The realm's opening offer may now read as a bluff.** The realm offered a maker's account when it held only survivors, and it did not know at the time that this was the distinction Rome would draw. Rome, drawing it, may have concluded that the realm claims to hold more than it holds, which is the single most damaging thing Rome could believe about the realm's reporting, since the trading of intelligence is the whole substance of the tie.

### Whether this court can see that about itself

Partly, late, and by a known mechanism.

It can see it, because self-indictment is a genre feature of the realm's records. The interpreter sold to China, the charts handed to Egypt for one sentence about copper, the mathematician appointed to a programme that needed a metalworker, telling Rome too much about the realm's weakness for nothing in return: all are written up plainly, with the reasoning that produced them and no defence offered.

It will see it late, because every one of those entries was written a reign or more after the act, by officials going back over old reports. The organ that exists for this work is the advisory council raised in about 390 with standing to bring hard truths to the throne directly. This paper is that organ doing that work.

The part it will resist is the framing. The realm has no vocabulary for obligation, only for cost and prerequisite, which is the first divergence set out in section 4. An institution with no word for a broken promise files a default as a delay. That is why twenty-five years passed with the matter unraised. Nobody at Pataliputra was looking at an unpaid debt. They were looking at an unfinished bench task, and an unfinished bench task raises itself when it is finished, so there was nothing to put on an agenda. Rome does have that vocabulary, and a Christian Rome holds it in a sharper form than the Rome the partnership began with. The two courts therefore hold the same fact in two different files. The realm's file records a schedule and Rome's file records a debt. The realm must learn to read its own conduct out of Rome's file and not only out of its own, and it has never before had to do that with any power.

### Whether it can raise the matter at all, and how

It can, and the order is the whole of it, as the order was the whole of it the first time.

**It must deliver before it asks, and it must ask separately.** The taking apart is in hand. When the maker's account is written, it should go to Rome complete, unprompted, with no request attached to it, no explanation beyond the plain one, and no padding, since Rome promised to find seams and the account will be true enough to have none. That converts the realm from a power that defaulted into a power that performed late, which is the only standing from which a second request can be made at all. A request sent alongside the delivery, or in place of it, tells Rome that the realm's word arrives only when the realm wants something.

**The explanation should be the unflattering one.** The account was not ready, the object came late, and nobody raised it. The realm's habit of recording its errors plainly is an asset here, and a padded excuse would be tested by a counterparty that has already said it tests things.

**The interval matters more than the words.** A decent gap between the delivery and the second request costs the realm nothing but time, and the realm has just spent twenty-five years demonstrating that it can wait.

**The second request should be smaller than the first was.** Not the whole method with engineers in the yards, which is what a party in good standing asks for, but a defined and testable piece: the seals and where they fail, and the fuel figures. The realm's engine is thirsty and heavy, which is to say it fails at exactly those two points, and a narrow request is both cheaper for Rome to grant and harder for Rome to refuse.

### What the realm has to pay with, priced

The gun account is no longer the only item, and it is no longer currency in any case. It is the debt, and a debt is paid rather than traded.

**Saltpetre. The best currency the realm holds.** The Gangetic plain yields more of it than any rival possesses, Rome has met gunpowder on its own northern frontier and will want powder, and a supply is the one form of payment that can be metered, priced, renewed, and stopped. Every other item on this list is given once and is then gone. This one creates a standing dependency that runs the right way and can be revised at each renewal, in the manner of the transit tariff.

**The knowledge that Egypt gave the realm a false theory. Free, and should be given free.** The two courts have traded word of Egypt's doings for a hundred and fifty years. Telling Rome that Egypt supplied the realm with a sabotage dressed as guidance costs the realm no capability at all, warns a partner against a method that may have been used on it too, and buys credibility with the one power whose good opinion the realm currently needs. It is the cheapest useful thing in the whole account.

**The realm's own marine engine, as object, method, and failure record.** It is worse than Rome's and it is still a second independent solution to the same problem, and an engineer values another man's failures. The cost is that it shows Rome exactly how far along the realm is, which is less far than Rome may currently assume. That disclosure is survivable and is probably unavoidable, since a request for the seals and the fuel figures implies the engine in any case.

**Mathematics, split.** The realm's decimal notation, the zero handled as a working number, its systematic algebra, its number theory, and its geometry are ahead of anything any rival holds, and the realm has never offered any of it to anyone. It is the largest unspent asset in the account, it costs nothing to copy now that the realm has paper and a press, and it converts into weapons slowly, which makes it the safest large gift the realm can make. The realm should hold back the applied part: the mathematics of projectile flight developed in about 350 to 375 and the trigonometric tables already fitted to the realm's guns. The realm made that same division inside its own house between the schools and the arsenal, so it can hold it in a negotiation.

**Lesser items already proven to have value in exchange.** Charts, harbour notes, and route accounts, which Egypt wanted in about 462 far more eagerly than the realm expected, and which the realm should therefore price higher this time. The inoculation against the pox, the washing protocols, and the antiseptic and analgesic finds of the plant programme. Printing under the Brahmin arrangement. The mechanical clock with its escapement, the spyglass, and the signal-tower relay method. The drought-hardy African grains. Sugar and wootz steel, which are already trade rather than currency.

**What must not be offered.** The galvanic theory, four years matured, which is the realm's only thread toward the copper network and its most valuable secret. The interior survey of the southern continent. The Han channel and the fact of the stolen weapon, both of which are worth more to China than to Rome and would cost the realm the frontier peace.

**The standing test on all of it.** The discipline the realm took from Rome's own hint in about 385 is to give true knowledge and to measure first what the gift will grow into. Rome borders the realm at the Euphrates and the Caucasus. Rome holding the realm's firearm account, the realm's ballistics, and a supply of the realm's saltpetre is a materially stronger Rome on that border. The realm's judgment for four hundred years has been that the trade is worth making because Rome has never moved against it, and that judgment should now be written down as a judgment rather than left standing as an assumption.

## 6. The Voice of the Realm's Documents

**What the two files are.** They read as a chronicle assembled out of working papers. The frame is past tense and third person, and the grammatical subject is almost always "the realm" or "the court" rather than any person. Inside that frame sit set-apart blocks headed with a plain label, most often "The reasoning," which are working analysis rather than narrative, and which state what the court believed and why it decided as it did.

**Nobody in the realm has a name.** Ashoka is named, and Kautilya, who is a body of doctrine rather than a man. After that the record runs seven and a half centuries without naming a single minister, general, governor, engineer, or later ruler. Rulers appear as a quarter-century block of dates, an assessed quality, and sometimes a numeric mark out of a hundred. Foreigners are named freely, Menander and Kujula Kadphises among them. The realm's own men are described by their function and their competence.

**What the record counts obsessively.** Money in paṇas, to three significant figures, by revenue line and by spending line, across seven benchmark years. Headcounts by arm of service. Yearly all-in cost per soldier, per horseman, per elephant, per sailor. The reserve stated in years of revenue against a stated prudent target of one year. Research budgets as parts in a hundred of revenue. Years from a programme's founding to its result. Cost per founded settlement and per garrison post, and the higher running cost of a post on ground that feeds nobody. Ships lost against ships sunk. Fifteen kilograms of silver paid to one armorer. Ranges wherever a single number would be false, with the range stated rather than an average.

**What it also records obsessively, which is unusual.** Its own errors, in detail, with the reasoning that produced them. The interpreter sold to China. Telling Rome too much about the realm's weakness in exchange for nothing. Handing Egypt the charts and the southern continent for one sentence about copper. Appointing a mathematician to run a programme that needed a metalworker. It marks interpretation apart from fact inside the same paragraph, with sentences that say plainly that a conclusion is the court's inference and not a thing the realm confirmed. It states what it does not know, including what it does not know about its own garrisons, and it notes that the capital's ledgers counted the establishment on the islands and never the graves. It has lately begun to book its own temper as a cause, writing down that a decision was reached by a court moving at the speed of injured pride and then holding that the decision was correct anyway.

**What it barely mentions.** Faith appears as a spending line, a tolerance rule, and an obstacle to printing. The enslaved, perhaps one person in ten to twenty of the whole realm, get one paragraph in seven and a half centuries. Battles are recorded for the lessons drawn, with the lessons listed. No ruler has a personality, a family, or a death worth describing. Art, literature, and monument appear only as evidence of craft. Ordinary subjects appear as registered population, as literacy fractions, and as taxpayers. Obligations owed to other powers appear as scheduled tasks, which is how a twenty-five-year default came to be recorded as a thing prepared and not finished.

**Committed voice for the turn.** The turn will be written in that voice: past tense, third person, "the realm" and "the court" as subjects, no named officials of the realm, labelled reasoning blocks, epistemic marking on every judgment about a foreign power, and a number or a stated range attached to every claim that can carry one.

### Three things that would identify the document as this realm's with the name removed

**First, every assertion carries a figure, and where no figure can be had the document says whose estimate it is and how it was got.** Sums in paṇas, headcounts, years from founding to result, parts in a hundred of revenue, kilograms of silver to an informant, hulls lost against hulls sunk. Ranges are given as ranges. No other court would price a bribe, a garrison post on dead ground, and a continent in the same paper.

**Second, the realm's own people are anonymous while foreigners are named, and rulers are graded.** A reign is a date block, a competence assessment, and a list of what it settled or wasted. Policy belongs to "the realm," never to a man. A document that scores its own kings out of a hundred and does not record their names is a document written by an administration that expects to outlast them.

**Third, the paper keeps a running ledger of its own errors and of the limits of its own knowledge, and it reasons in prerequisite chains.** Mistakes are written up with the reasoning that produced them and no defence offered. Foreign intentions are labelled as the court's reading. Capabilities are discussed as ordered steps, with a statement of what must exist before the next step can be attempted and an estimate of how many years and hands the step will take. When temper drove a decision, the temper is entered as a cause and the decision is defended on its arithmetic separately.
