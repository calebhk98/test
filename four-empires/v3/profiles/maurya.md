# The Maurya Realm: Court Position Paper, 525 AD

*Prepared at Pataliputra for the advisory council, from the court registers, the treasury's reckonings, the military archives, and the reports of the realm's agents. Where a figure is an estimate, it is marked. Where a judgment is the court's reading rather than a thing the realm has seen, it is marked. Sums are in paṇas of silver.*

---

## 1. The Three Powers: Trust and Danger

The court rates trust and danger on separate scales of one to ten. Trust means how far the realm can act on what a power says without checking it. Danger means how much that power can take from the realm if it chooses to. A power can be honest and dangerous at once, and two of the three are.

### China

**Trust: 3 of 10.** China keeps the agreements it signs and does not lie about what it has done, and it has held the Syr Darya line for three hundred years without once crossing it. Against that, it took the interpreter the realm lent in about 310 and used it to speak to Rome around the realm, it did not answer the realm's demand for the Strait toll at all, and it fired on the realm's ships and took Tidore while both powers were formally at peace.

**Danger: 10 of 10.** China is the only power that has killed the realm's soldiers and sunk the realm's ships, ninety hulls of a hundred and ten lost in about 476 to 478 with not one Chinese ship sunk in return, and it is the only power whose army stands across a river from the realm's own ground. It holds heavy cannon, deck guns, bursting shells, the firearm one man carries, and warships that set their own speed against the wind, and the realm's northern frontier is defended by fifty to eighty fixed pieces on ground supplied through one repaired mountain pass.

### Egypt

**Trust: 1 of 10.** Egypt has never once lied to the realm in a checkable statement and has never once told the realm anything that could be built from, giving function and withholding construction across a hundred and sixty years of dealing. The court now assesses the galvanic guidance of about 495, which sent the realm's botanists to look for the force in trees and grasses, as a deliberate sabotage that consumed a share of one research program for twenty-five years, and a power that gives false theory as a gift is rated at the floor.

**Danger: 8 of 10.** Egypt threatens the realm's territory little by force of arms and has never attacked it, and everything the realm has lost to Egypt it has lost by arriving second: every western harbor, the East African coast whole, the coffee plant, and the price of its own goods into the Mediterranean. Its technical lead compounds year on year in printing, lighting, fuel, canal engineering, standardized fittings, steam at sea, and the copper message network, and it now holds the realm's own charts of the southern continent, handed over in about 462, with over two hundred thousand tons of shipping provisioned on the African coast and its destination still unconfirmed.

### Rome

**Trust: 7 of 10.** Rome is the only power that has given the realm a capability it could build from, the marine engine method with engineers sent to the yards, and the containment hint of about 385 that unlocked the first working gunpowder weapon, and it asks plainly for what it wants rather than fishing for it. It is rated at seven and not higher because it concealed how far ahead its own steam work was until the realm worked that out for itself, because it sent Egypt a subsidized relief fleet after the earthquake of 365 and said nothing of it to the realm, and because it holds an overland tie to China that the realm does not see into.

**Danger: 4 of 10.** Rome sits across the Euphrates and the Greater Caucasus and has never tested that line, and the realm halted at that line in about 150 rather than lunge past its supply, so neither power has a grievance of ground against the other. The danger is that Rome is the realm's largest market and the counterparty on a road worth a hundred and fifty-five million paṇas a year in tariff, so a Rome that changed its mind could cost the realm more than an army could, and Rome now holds the complete maker's account of the Chinese hand-weapon because the realm gave it.

---

## 2. The Single Most Important Thing

The unbroken working of the tax-gathering administration over the integrated heartland, because that is the thing the realm counts as itself and the thing that pays for everything else it does.

---

## 3. The Eight Concerns, Ranked as the Court Actually Ranks Them

**1. Military or technical advantage over rivals.** The realm has funded weapons research at or near a tenth of revenue for two hundred and seventy-five years, and in this reign it wrote that level down as doctrine with the advisory council behind it so that a later ruler would have to fight an institution to cut it. In about 478 to 500 it gave up the Strait tolls, the free passage, and its doubled Silk Road rate, and refused only the one demand that touched the cannon on the Syr Darya. That trade shows the ranking plainly: commercial income was spent to keep a weapon.

**2. Revenue and the treasury.** Every decision in the record is argued in paṇas, and the realm's assessment of its own century is an assessment of its revenue lines. The treasury is also the instrument by which the realm reaches everything else, since its method against hard problems is to put thousands of paid hands on the same step at once, which only a full treasury can do.

**3. Trade routes and access.** This is close enough to revenue that the court often argues them as one thing, and it is ranked separately because the realm's temper lives here and not in the ledger. Being walled out of the western sea harbor by harbor produced a hundred and fifty years of grievance and then a venture costing 6.25 billion paṇas, which is near three years of the whole realm's revenue, spent on coast the realm had not yet been refused.

**4. Defence of the core territory.** The core has never been reached by a foreign army in seven and a half centuries, and the Rolling Frontier Doctrine exists so that it never will be. A concern this well secured commands less argument at court than the ones above it, and the realm still would not strip Oman and the Gulf to feed the southern program.

**5. Internal order and the suppression of revolt.** Revolts happen and fail easily, as the Mesopotamian rising of about 240 failed, because nothing a province can field stands against the field army. The court treats order as routine work rather than as a danger, and the part of it the court is bad at is not suppression but integration.

**6. Standing and prestige among the other powers.** The court would place this near the bottom and its conduct places it here. Losing eleven ships while collecting the realm's own toll was read at court as an act of war rather than as a commercial loss, and the southern venture was launched by a court the record itself describes as moving at the speed of injured pride.

**7. Survival of the dynasty.** The realm's own record states that a change of dynasty is not a change of realm, and it has passed through several ruling houses, twice through open succession quarrels of two to three years, while taxes came in and the frontiers held. The officials draw their pay from the treasury and the army's loyalty runs to the throne as an office, so the men who write these records do not expect their own positions to depend on which house wears the crown.

**8. Religious and ideological legitimacy.** The realm's founding decision was the refusal of a religious turn, and its standing rule since has been to tax conduct and leave belief alone, which is a rule of administration rather than of faith. Religious spending stands at about sixty-eight million paṇas a year against nine hundred million for the army, and in this reign the temple line was trimmed to help pay for ships.

### The two it would claim to rank highly and does not

**Religious and ideological legitimacy.** The realm keeps the Brahmins at the summit of the order, patronizes temples in every province, and formally embraced the Zoroastrian faith of Iran in about 260, and each of these was done for a governing reason. The embrace of Zoroastrianism was a measure for binding a conquered province. Giving the Brahmins the plates and seals of the press in this reign was a measure for getting standardized law and medicine printed. When the southern program needed money, the temple line was among the first cut.

**Survival of the dynasty.** The court speaks of the throne as permanent and of the ruling house as its holder, and its own historians grade rulers on a numeric scale of competence, mark two houses as discredited, and treat a broken succession as a quarrel among great men. An institution that scores its kings out of a hundred and records that the state ran on without one has already decided which of the two it serves.

A note on the reverse case. Prestige is the concern the court claims to rank lowest and acts on most quickly, and the record has begun to admit this about itself.

## 4. The Difference From the Maurya Empire That Ended Early

The comparison the coordinator asks for concerns the institution's character and not its map. Four differences follow.

**The realm never acquired a language for judging its own conduct.** An empire whose founder had turned to a faith after Kalinga would have left behind a vocabulary of righteous rule, and its records would measure the state against that standard. This realm kept only the Arthashastra, which is a science of costs and pillars. So its records grade errors as miscalculations. The sale of the Latin interpreter in about 310 is written up as a clean and total loss and a piece of failed cleverness. The seizure of the northern and southern islands in this reign is recorded as a fact the court marked and did not disguise, with no argument offered that it was right or wrong. The realm keeps an honest ledger of its mistakes and has no words at all for a sin.

**Seven and a half centuries taught the institution that it outlives its rulers, which the short-lived empire never had time to learn.** The administration is paid from the treasury, the army is trained to see a march for one claimant against another as the ruin of the thing it serves, and the historians assign each reign a mark and a quarter-century block. The practical result is a state built to absorb bad rulers rather than to prevent them. The reforms of about 390, an advisory council with standing to bring hard truths to the throne and senior officials paid outside patronage, were friction against future weak kings and not a cure for them. The realm expects roughly one reign in four to be wasted and has arranged its reserve so that a wasted reign spends the inheritance instead of the state.

**The Brahmanical settlement was never disturbed, and that settlement is the ceiling on the realm's technique.** Without an imperial embrace of Buddhism there was no monastic and scribal class outside the caste order, and so after seven and a half centuries reading and writing remain with about one person in eight to ten, and those the men of the cities, the merchants, the officials, and the upper castes. Printing existed from about 395 and was confined to tax rolls, law codes, military orders, and medical texts for a hundred and thirty years because the Brahmins' place rested on their sole keeping of the sacred word. This reign moved printing forward by handing the Brahmins the plates rather than by breaking them, and it set as its aim a merchant and official class that reads reliably and not general literacy, because the caste order will not permit general literacy. The realm's mathematics leads the world and its literacy does not, and the second figure limits what the first can be turned into.

**The realm learned to treat discovery as a search that money and hands can quicken.** This is a habit of mind the earlier empire had no occasion to form, and it came from two centuries of chasing the Chinese cannon. The court now reasons in prerequisite chains and in numbers of parallel attempts, holds that no coin buys a step before the step it rests on, and holds that any step already known to be possible will fall to enough funded hands. Its own record notes that the same improvement is sometimes reached in two of the realm's workshops at once, neither knowing of the other, and reads that as proof of the method. The court also holds, from its own history, that every research catastrophe it has suffered was a purge and never a shortage of ideas.

## 5. The State of the Steam Bargain at 525

The record gives some of this in durations rather than in dates. Where a year below is arithmetic from a stated duration, it is marked as reckoned.

**Before the bargain.** The realm's ships were shattered by Chinese gunfire in about 476 to 478, and Tidore fell to a landing of a few hundred men each carrying a gun he fired in his own hands. The realm's engineers then judged a two-man crew-served piece five to ten years off and a true single-soldier firearm fifteen to twenty-five years off at the least. The intelligence effort against the Han hand-firearm was set going in the years after that defeat and ran for nearly two decades. From about 385 the realm knew that Rome understood pressure vessels better than it admitted, and it had worked out that Rome held steam able to move a hull.

**The realm moved first, about 500 to 501 (reckoned, the reign's early years).** The realm asked Rome for marine steam and offered in exchange the account of the Chinese hand-weapon, drawn from the men who had survived that weapon at Tidore. No other power could supply that account, because only the realm had been on the receiving end of the weapon and lived.

**Rome accepted and sent its demands in full, about 501 to 502 (reckoned), and sent them before the realm's agents had brought home any Chinese weapon at all.** Rome would deal in one delivery only, with no installments, on the reasoning that pieces serve no one at the distance the two powers speak across. Rome's own half was laid out complete: the whole working method of the marine engine from boiler through transmission, the seals and where they fail, the fuel figures, and Roman engineers sent into the realm's yards to build the first engine beside the realm's own. Rome's price was the complete account of the hand-weapon: weight, length, how carried and held, killing range, time between shots, effect on timber and on a man, how it was constructed so as to be replicated at the best detail the realm could manage, how it was powered, and every judgment the realm's engineers had formed. Rome gave notice that it would test what it received against what it already knew, would see the seams if the account were padded, and would answer padding in a manner that fit.

**The bind.** The Tidore survivors could describe being shot at. They could not describe how the weapon was built so that another maker could copy it, and that was the heart of Rome's demand. Rome had asked, without meaning to, for a maker's account where the realm had offered a witness's account, and a maker's account can only come from having the object in hand and taking it apart. The realm stood committed to delivering a thing it did not have.

**The weapon arrived inside the same window, about 502 to 503 (reckoned).** The break came from an armorer at a Han frontier arsenal, deep in debt from a bad harvest, who had grown comfortable diverting a single scrapped hand cannon during inventory culls and making up its weight and metal elsewhere in the books. Barrel, stock, and lock left Han territory separately in three shipments across the better part of a year, and the weapon was reassembled only in the realm's own hands. The armorer was paid nine hundred silver weight-units, about fifteen kilograms of silver. The rest of the effort was a heavy loss. Many agents were taken and presumably killed, and many were turned in by the very people they tried to bribe. The court's conclusion was that bribery against a peer's frontier arsenals is murderously costly and had succeeded once by the chance of one desperate man in a bad year, and that further work on the weapon belonged in the realm's own casting shops.

**The realm delivered its half, about 503 to 504 (reckoned).** It sent the account whole and honest: the weight and length, the killing range, the rate of fire, the effect on timber and on men, the construction, the means of ignition, and its engineers' full reasoning. It gave no seam for Rome to find, because it now genuinely held the weapon and a true account was the only safe one to send. Three things were withheld. Rome was not told that the account rested on a physical weapon in the realm's workshops, was not told the channel that brought it, and was not told that the realm had become a maker of this class of arm. What Rome inferred from the sheer quality of the account was left to Rome to reason out.

**Rome delivered its half, about 504 to 505 (reckoned), and its engineers came into the yards.** The record states that the marine engine work was quickened by the Roman method and by the Roman engineers in the yards, which establishes both that the paper arrived and that the men arrived. The record does not say when or whether those engineers went home, and the realm should not assert either way.

**The engine ran at sea in about 506 (reckoned from "about six years" into the reign).** The realm moved from the stationary pumps clearing the flooded deep shafts at Kolar to a working marine engine. A standing program directive, set down as doctrine rather than as a preference, sends the first working marine engines to the southern route ahead of the navy and ahead of every other use.

**Position at the close of 525.** The exchange is executed on both sides. Nothing in the record is outstanding from Rome and nothing is owed to Rome. The realm holds the engine, kept the physical weapon, and kept the channel. Rome holds a true and complete maker's account of the Chinese hand-weapon and has lodged no complaint about it, which is consistent with an account that passed the test Rome promised to run.

**What is not established, and must not be assumed.** The record contains no Roman statement of satisfaction, no report of Rome's testing, and no measure of what Rome now believes about the realm. Three risks follow, and all three are the court's reading rather than anything the realm has seen. First, a maker's account of that quality is hard to reconcile with frightened survivors, and Rome is careful enough to have noticed the gap. Second, the realm fielded a reliable one-man firearm of its own about eighteen years into the reign, near 518, having told Rome in about 478 that such a weapon lay fifteen to twenty-five years off, and Rome asks sharp and particular questions about firearms. Third, the realm has now taken from Rome the two capabilities that mattered most to it, the containment hint and the marine engine, and it does not know what Rome will ask for next or what it will have left to pay with.

## 6. The Voice of the Realm's Documents

**What the two files are.** They read as a chronicle assembled out of working papers. The frame is past tense and third person, and the grammatical subject is almost always "the realm" or "the court" rather than any person. Inside that frame sit set-apart blocks headed with a plain label, most often "The reasoning," which are working analysis rather than narrative, and which state what the court believed and why it decided as it did.

**Nobody in the realm has a name.** Ashoka is named, and Kautilya, who is a body of doctrine rather than a man. After that the record runs seven and a half centuries without naming a single minister, general, governor, engineer, or later ruler. Rulers appear as a quarter-century block of dates, an assessed quality, and sometimes a numeric mark out of a hundred. Foreigners are named freely, Menander and Kujula Kadphises among them. The realm's own men are described by their function and their competence.

**What the record counts obsessively.** Money in paṇas, to three significant figures, by revenue line and by spending line, across seven benchmark years. Headcounts by arm of service. Yearly all-in cost per soldier, per horseman, per elephant, per sailor. The reserve stated in years of revenue against a stated prudent target of one year. Research budgets as parts in a hundred of revenue. Years from a program's founding to its result. Cost per founded settlement and per garrison post, and the higher running cost of a post on ground that feeds nobody. Ships lost against ships sunk. Fifteen kilograms of silver paid to one armorer. Ranges wherever a single number would be false, and the range is stated rather than an average.

**What it also records obsessively, which is unusual.** Its own errors, in detail, with the reasoning that produced them. The interpreter sold to China. Telling Rome too much about the realm's weakness in exchange for nothing. Handing Egypt the charts and the southern continent for one sentence about copper. Appointing a mathematician to run a program that needed a metalworker. It marks interpretation apart from fact inside the same paragraph, with sentences that say plainly that a conclusion is the court's inference and not a thing the realm confirmed. It states what it does not know, including what it does not know about its own garrisons, and it notes that the capital's ledgers counted the establishment on the islands and never the graves. It has lately begun to book its own temper as a cause, writing down that a decision was reached by a court moving at the speed of injured pride and then holding that the decision was correct anyway.

**What it barely mentions.** Faith appears as a spending line, a tolerance rule, and an obstacle to printing. The enslaved, perhaps one person in ten to twenty of the whole realm, get one paragraph in seven and a half centuries. Battles are recorded for the lessons drawn, with the lessons listed. No ruler has a personality, a family, or a death worth describing. Art, literature, and monument appear only as evidence of craft. Ordinary subjects appear as registered population, as literacy fractions, and as taxpayers.

**Committed voice for the turn.** The turn will be written in that voice: past tense, third person, "the realm" and "the court" as subjects, no named officials of the realm, labelled reasoning blocks, epistemic marking on every judgment about a foreign power, and a number or a stated range attached to every claim that can carry one.

### Three things that would identify the document as this realm's with the name removed

**First, every assertion carries a figure, and where no figure can be had the document says whose estimate it is and how it was got.** Sums in paṇas, headcounts, years from founding to result, parts in a hundred of revenue, kilograms of silver to an informant, hulls lost against hulls sunk. Ranges are given as ranges. No other court would price a bribe, a garrison post on dead ground, and a continent in the same paper.

**Second, the realm's own people are anonymous while foreigners are named, and rulers are graded.** A reign is a date block, a competence assessment, and a list of what it settled or wasted. Policy belongs to "the realm," never to a man. A document that scores its own kings out of a hundred and does not record their names is a document written by an administration that expects to outlast them.

**Third, the paper keeps a running ledger of its own errors and of the limits of its own knowledge, and it reasons in prerequisite chains.** Mistakes are written up with the reasoning that produced them and no defence offered. Foreign intentions are labelled as the court's reading. Capabilities are discussed as ordered steps, with a statement of what must exist before the next step can be attempted and an estimate of how many years and hands the step will take. When temper drove a decision, the temper is entered as a cause and the decision is defended on its arithmetic separately.
