# Turn: 525–550 AD

## Standing Situation at Turn Open

- Revenue near 132,000 talents, reserve near 263,000. The previous turn committed the deployment stream heavily and the slack is thin before this turn spends a coin.
- The Western Enterprise is a thin unbroken coastal perimeter with almost nothing behind it. The Plata is the only ground filling with people at a rate worth reporting.
- Rome has not attacked anywhere, has not answered the Atlantic posture, and has not repeated the 524 reconnaissance. The council does not read silence as absence. Silence is a policy, and Rome is disciplined enough to keep one.

## The Settled Charge, 532

The oldest open problem in Egyptian chemistry closes this turn. Worked since the 320s, through two centuries in which the assay houses could make a compound that would tear a rock face apart and could not make one a man could carry to the rock face without dying on the way.

The answer is not a new compound but a way of holding an old one. The unstable oil is soaked into an inert porous earth, a fine siliceous silt dredged from Rift lake beds, which carries it like a sponge and will not pass a shock through it. Soaked cartridges can be dropped, struck, carried on a mule, stacked in a magazine through a Nubian summer, and set alight, and they burn sullenly rather than detonating. What detonates them is a separate small initiating charge, a mercury compound the chemists have known and avoided for a generation, packed in a copper cap and fired electrically or by fuse. The whole art is the separation: the mass is stupid, the cap is clever, and the two meet only at the moment of use.

Adopted in 532 as the settled charge, third formulation. It is the most consequential result of this turn and it is not, mostly, a weapon.

**Mining.** Rock that was cut is now broken. Bulk blasting reaches the Copperbelt, Hwange, the Zimbabwe plateau and the Nubian fields inside four years, with overburden removal at a scale that makes working a shallow ore body from the surface a real alternative to a shaft for the first time. Copper output rises by a third against 525 without one additional bonded worker: the mechanization argument of the slave reform arriving by a door nobody was watching. Gold is not ramped, for the standing reason that a known output invites attention, though the cap is quietly lifted (see the treasury note).

**Engineering.** Harbor deepening at the Plata and Trinidad, cuttings for mine railways that took a decade of picks, a straightened alignment on two of the canal's worst bends. The Sinai engineers rewrite their demolition doctrine: every bridge, sluice, cistern and pass in the fortress system now has prepared chambers, and the corridor can be made impassable on a signal rather than over a fortnight.

**Weapons.** Moored mines gain a charge that keeps for years instead of months, and aerial munitions become bombs rather than incendiary cans. A self-propelled underwater charge on compressed air, begun 536, is undelivered at 550: it runs but will not hold a depth, and four hulls' worth of funding has bought weapons that swim under their targets or broach in front of them. It stays funded because the council can see what it would be worth.

What the settled charge does not give Egypt is any means of throwing a charge at a distance. Egypt can break rock, mine a channel, and drop a bomb from an aircraft. It cannot push a projectile down a tube, and nothing in its chemistry suggests to it that this is a thing that might be done. The gap is not identified as a gap.

## The Nitrogen Ceiling, and the Failure at the Falls

The success creates the crisis. Demand across the mines, the engineers, the Sinai works, the fleet and the colonies rises roughly fivefold in eight years. Every grain of it is nitrogen, and every grain of Egyptian nitrogen comes out of the niter beds: turned, watered, leached, four to five years from building a bed to drawing from it. The beds cannot be hurried.

Twenty-five further years at the Livingstone Falls have not broken that ceiling and will not. The line was kept undirected on the reasoning that Egypt had more concentrated current in one place than had ever existed, and chemists who run current through everything, so something might fall out. Nothing did. Currents through nitrate solutions gave back the nitrate that went in; molten salts gave metals already known. The one result that looked like a result was an arc drawn in open air, which does yield a trace of nitrous fume that can be washed down into acid. The assayers measured it and reported that the falls, running every generator into arcs and making nothing else at all, would produce a fraction of one year's niter-bed output at a current cost that beggars the product. Closed as a directed program in 543, kept as a bench curiosity.

Reported as what it is: a sink. Roughly 3,000 talents and four hundred chemists across twenty-five years, and the byproducts, better chlorine and caustic circuits and cleaner gold reagents, do not come close to covering it. The finding is that the money was not badly spent, since the prize would have been decisive, but that the method was, because nobody was ever told what to look for.

An allocation board sits in Alexandria from 544 dividing the empire's explosive between mining, engineering and the arsenals: the first standing body in three centuries that exists to ration a thing Egypt cannot buy, make faster, or conquer.

## The Lower Congo Navigation Works, 534–545: A Failure

With rock now breakable, the engineers proposed cutting a navigable channel through the Livingstone cataract chain to join the Atlantic directly to the industrial city and the upper river. The council approved it in 534 largely on the strength of the new tool and the pleasure of having one.

It failed. The river's discharge is beyond anything Egyptian hydraulics had modeled; blasted rock does not vanish but resettles, and each cleared drop reformed as a new rapid below itself. In 541 a bank charge undercut a slope carrying the portage railway, which came down with two hundred and ten men on and under it. Work continued four more years on sunk-cost reasoning before abandonment in 545. Roughly 19,000 talents and eleven years, against a doubled portage railway, which was the cheap answer in 534 and is the answer anyway.

The council's review found the deeper error. The falls city is Egypt's most advanced industrial base and its value is that it sits a thousand miles up a river no fleet can ascend. The project's success would have destroyed the reason for the project. Nobody said this aloud for seven years.

A second, smaller disaster: the initiating-cap works outside Berenice detonated in 539, killing three hundred and eighty-one, most of them in the town rather than the works. Cap manufacture now sits on cleared desert ground and two Red Sea islands, none within four miles of a dwelling, written into the code rather than left to the arsenals.

## Aircraft

Operational service, in small numbers. Metal-framed on falls aluminum, with arc-furnace fittings and vulcanized seals. Two types: armed reconnaissance, carrying a pilot, an observer, a radio set, a telescope and a light load; and a larger incendiary type carrying settled-charge bombs.

By 550, something over two hundred airframes built, perhaps ninety serviceable on a given day, in flights of six to twelve at the canal, the Sinai approaches, Cyprus, Aden, the Cape, the Plata and Trinidad. Attrition is the story nobody expected: eighty-one machines and fifty-three fliers lost, four of them in anything resembling an operation and the rest to engines, weather and ground. Training fliers is now harder than building aircraft, and the school at Memphis is the bottleneck under the whole arm.

What growth exists comes from the industrial turn rather than the aircraft program. Series production of engines, instruments and radio sets is what makes ninety machines possible at all; a workshop tradition would have delivered fifteen. The council accepts that the capability cannot stay classified much longer, since an aircraft over the canal is visible to every hull in the roads. Standing instruction: not flown over foreign ground or foreign hulls, so that Rome may hear of them and not measure them.

## American Contact with Rome, 534

Survey parties working north from Trinidad through the island chain found Roman-held ground in the spring of 534: cleared land, a stockade, a jetty, boats drawn up, a standard. Neither power held fortified home ground within a thousand miles. Squadron commander Senuthios of Berenice had four hulls, two aircraft, no orders that covered this, and radio that reached his own ships and nobody else.

He did not fire and did not withdraw. He anchored in sight, sent a boat under a plain flag with a Latin-speaking officer, and asked for the officer commanding.

From his log: each named himself and his crown, and each stated that the ground his flag stood on was his crown's. Neither asked where the other's claim ended and neither volunteered it. They agreed, on no authority whatever, to three things: neither lands where the other's flag already stands; vessels meeting at sea here pass and do not close; either may send a boat to the other's nearest post to say something, and it will be received.

Senuthios then did the thing the council commended most. He let the Roman officer believe the southern coast was long held and deeply settled inland, without saying so, by speaking of harbors and provinces as a man speaks of home. It is a bluff Egypt can support at the shoreline and cannot support fifty miles behind it.

Report reached Alexandria in thirty-four days by packet to Senegal and heliograph up the coast. The council ratified everything in 536 with its reasoning: a war begun at the end of the longest supply line in the world, over an island neither crown can reinforce, against a peer, is the worst war Egypt could choose, and two captains' understanding commits neither crown while a treaty would commit both. Egypt will not propose a treaty, because a treaty must name latitudes, and naming latitudes tells Rome the size of the Egyptian claim and hands Rome a document to hold up later.

Egypt took no ground and gave none. What it extracted was measurement: hull counts, the state of the Roman post, the health of the men in it, four place-names. The northward advance stops at the southern arc of the island chain, and the effort goes into fortifying Trinidad properly, which is worth more than three more islands.

The note to Rome, sent 537 through the formal channel, asserts no latitude. It proposes mutual advance notice of squadron movements in Atlantic waters and names Alexandria and one Atlantic port where each may address the other. Rome's reply in 541 acknowledges receipt and says nothing else. That is Rome's whole answer to the Atlantic posture in twenty-five years, and the council reads it as a decision neither to be provoked nor to concede, which is what Egypt would do.

Spy tasking from the last turn returns partial. Roman naval circles do now hold that Egypt is across the ocean, which the island contact settled in any case; the lost-ship question has hardened into grievance in some quarters and not others, with no sign of a crown position.

## The Dying

The collapse of contacted coastal populations is now undeniable in scale. The physicians' reports from the Guianas, the Brazil coast, the Orinoco and the islands are consistent and careful: a spotted fever, then a coughing sickness, then a flux, in that order, within two to four years of first contact, killing across all ages rather than the young and old, running inland along native trade paths faster than any Egyptian has walked them, so that villages sixty miles from any Egyptian have died without seeing one. Where Egypt returns after ten years it finds a third of the houses standing and a tenth of the people. Egyptian and African-provincial settlers are barely touched.

The observation is excellent. The explanation is wrong in the way that matters. Egypt's physicians hold that specific organisms cause specific diseases, which is right, and conclude that these peoples' constitutions and lineages were never seasoned to the organisms of the wider world, which is close enough to be dangerous. From there, two false steps: that the cause is a weakness in the peoples rather than a carriage by Egyptians, since Egyptians are not sick; and that the dying is largely spent once it has passed through a district.

The policy is worse than the error. Contact parties are pushed ahead of settlement deliberately, on the reasoning that the dying will happen anyway and had better happen before Egyptian towns need the ground. Quarantine is not imposed, the physicians who argued that Egyptian bodies carry the sickness having been overruled on the grounds that Egyptian bodies are visibly well. Nine headmen brought to Alexandria in 538 to be shown the empire were dead within the year, recorded as evidence for the constitutional theory rather than against it.

The council's entry is a labor entry. Egypt went to that coast expecting to trade with its inhabitants, recruit from them and feed garrisons from their fields, as it did in Africa for five centuries. There is nobody to trade with. Every colony must import every person and every sack of grain across forty days of ocean, and the Western Enterprise costs on the order of twice what was budgeted in 525.

## The Rest of the Turn, Briefly

- Shipyards on standardized components reach seven to nine steam hulls a year against three in 500. The most valuable number in the industrial program, and still not an industry.
- Radio fitted to a further two hundred and forty hulls and to every colonial post. The Atlantic colonies stay a packet-and-heliograph problem, since no set Egypt can build reaches across the ocean.
- The American crops mature into the generational wave. Cassava and maize now carry whole districts of the Congo, the West African lowlands and the Great Lakes. Population reaches roughly twenty-eight million.
- The China exchange runs at twenty scholars each way and Egypt's germ and tropical-disease body has been opened in full. Delayed report suggests China is moving on its southern frontier. Egypt priced that knowledge at optics, mathematics and a consultation clause, and the council records, without regret and without pleasure, that it may have sold a wall.
- India: nothing given, nothing taken.

## Open Questions Carried Forward (550–575)

1. The niter ceiling is now the binding constraint on Egyptian power, with no known route through it: beds, guano, or a chemistry nobody has found. Directed program this time, or none.
2. Whether the captains' understanding in the islands survives its captains. It is written in two logs and no treaty. What happens the first time a Roman officer arrives who has not read his.
3. Whether to tell Rome plainly that Egypt is in the New World, in exchange for something. The claim is already inferred, and the concession may be worth selling before it is worth nothing.
4. Whether aircraft should be shown deliberately, as the Cape fleet never was.
5. Whether the dying can be slowed, and whether Egypt wishes it slowed, now that empty ground has proved to cost more than peopled ground.

## Treasury Note

Revenue reaches roughly 141,000 talents by 550, the growth coming from copper, colonial customs and the crop-fed provinces rather than from anything new.

Against that: the Western Enterprise at double its budgeted cost, the abandoned Congo works at 19,000 talents, the aircraft arm, the explosive plants and niter-bed expansion, the Trinidad fortifications, and the falls city still absorbing capital. The reserve stands at roughly 205,000 talents at turn close, down from 263,000, below the two-year target for the first time since the tsunami and comfortably above the one-year floor.

Two measures were taken that no easier decade would have produced. Gold output was lifted about a quarter, quietly, breaking a century-old restraint on visibility. And the crown accepted the first true state loan in its history against its own name, 12,000 talents at low interest from the Alexandrian banking houses, who were delighted to make it, and which the council regards as a door it would rather not have opened.

Egypt is not poor. Egypt is, for the first time in four hundred years, spending faster than it earns, in a quarter-century when Rome did nothing at all.
