# 525 to 550 AD

## Court Posture at the Opening of the Period

The period opens with the court correct about its own weaknesses and unable to pay for the remedies. The three audits of the previous turn stand unrepealed: powder is no longer the empire's alone, the frontier does not pay for itself, and the fleet's victory was won on wind and range with the range on a clock. Nothing in the twenty-five years that follow overturns any of the three, and two get worse.

The military faction presses to double the fleet before the Maurya finish arming. The throne refuses on arithmetic rather than judgment: the trunk line is building, the Northern Road is commissioned, the Pontic grants are running, and there is no fourth call the treasury can answer. It concedes priority in the arsenals instead, the naval program taking the rifled barrels and the breech-loading work ahead of the frontier garrisons, on the reasoning that a frontier held by rail can wait for new weapons and a fleet caught at sea cannot.

## Research and Development Outcomes

**The color-corrected lens, about 538.** The standing order was to vary the batch itself, melt every mineral on the alchemists' shelves into the glass, and grind whatever came out. It runs eleven years and some four hundred recorded failures before it yields the answer, which is not a grinding method at all but a second kind of glass. A heavy mineral the glasshouses had always skimmed off as contamination gives a dense glass that bends light strongly and spreads the colors differently from the ordinary batch. A lens of each, ground to opposing curves and cemented together, bend the light once and undo each other's color, and the fringe that has haunted every Chinese telescope since the first two-lens tube is gone.

The result is valuable and expensive. The heavy glass will not melt evenly and cools with streaks no polishing removes, so usable blanks run perhaps one in six. The arsenals produce some hundred and fifty good compound objectives in twelve years and assign every one: fleet flag captains, frontier observation posts, the survey parties on the trunk line and the Northern Road, the observatories. Nothing is sold and nothing is given. The second-order gain exceeds the instrument, since a true glass in a gun captain's hands is the front half of ranging a rifled cannon at nine hundred meters, which was previously experience and luck. The court records the lesson it thinks it has learned, that the undirected search worked as designed and should be funded again.

**The continuous source, about 545.** It arrives exactly as the court predicted it would, which is the part the throne finds most worth recording: by accident, during unrelated work, out of the same mineral-and-melt search. Plates of two unlike metals were left standing in a brine bath in a trial of which minerals a salt liquor eats and which it leaves, a question about crucible lining and nothing else. A man handling them felt the same sting the cranked machine gives, and, being of the examination-trained second tier and drilled to record what he did not expect, wrote it down rather than drying his hands.

Confirmed against the electrical scholars' catalogue within months: a stack of such plates, unlike metal against unlike metal with brine-soaked cloth between, produces the force steadily and without a handle, for hours, until the plates foul. It is weaker than a discharged jar and it does not stop.

The court does not know what it is for, and the memorial says so rather than dressing it up. What the empire holds at 550 is a device that makes the force continuously, a taxonomy of what carries it and what arrests it, a jar that stores a burst, and no application whatever. Two cheap lines of work are funded from it: vary the metals and the liquor to see what strengthens the effect, and run the force down a long copper wire to learn whether it survives the distance.

**Boiler safety at sea is not solved.** The engine turns a shaft reliably, the paddle wheel is understood, and the hull moves in home water at a speed no sail matches in a calm. None of that is the problem. A cannon is fired and then inspected; a boiler is held at pressure for weeks with no man able to see inside it, and the failure the engineers cannot predict is the slow weakening of riveted plate that no gauge catches until the seam opens. Two trial hulls are lost in the window, one in the Vietnamese yards and one in protected water off Korea, something over sixty men killed between them. The second loss is decisive, because it followed a full inspection and a certificate of soundness.

The engineers report honestly that they can make a boiler safe for a week under a watchful crew near a shore and cannot yet certify one for a voyage of months out of reach of any yard. The court accepts it and holds its rule: no steam in the Northern Road hulls, none on the Egypt run. This one unsolved problem is the limiter on everything the empire wants to do on water, and the court names it as such.

## The Mills and the Tax Question

The framing on which the mills were licensed, that a great works is a weaver with an unusual tool and should be taxed as one, does not survive the fourth accounting. By mid-period the largest Yangtze house returns in a year what a middling commandery returns to the treasury, and pays the tax of a household loom. The censors report it because it is impossible not to; the Confucian faction has been waiting for the figures since the license was granted.

The fight runs six years on three positions.

- **Confucian:** the mills are a new thing wearing an old name, the profit unearned in the classical sense, the household weaving of whole districts destroyed. Tax the works heavily or take them.
- **Merchant:** the works were built with merchant silver on terms the state set, and a state that changes those terms after the building will never see a second thing built by anyone.
- **Military, which decides it:** the mills buy iron and machine parts through the monopoly and can be made to clothe the army at a fixed price, and a treasury that cannot fund a fleet cannot fund a works either. Kill them and the state gains nothing it does not already have.

The settlement is a compromise none of the three calls a victory. The mills are taxed by count of driven spindles and by the water or coal power a works draws, not by household or loom, on the reasoning that the machine is what earns and the machine can be counted by an inspector who need not be trusted with the books. The rate sits well above a weaver's and well below the confiscatory. A fixed annual quota of army cloth at a set price is the military faction's payment. The houses are barred from acquiring land above the estate ceiling in their operating districts.

The cost falls where the Confucians said it would. Household silk income in three prefectures collapses inside a decade, and it was largely women's income in families with no other cash. There is loom-breaking and two serious disturbances at mill gates, one put down by the garrison with casualties, and the frontier grants are opened wider to drain the displaced. The relief valve is used again. The court notes without pleasure that it has a class of subject ruined by a licensed and taxed enterprise rather than by a flood or a landlord, and no precedent for what is owed him.

## Infrastructure

- **The southern trunk line is completed** in this window, from the northern and Mongolian coal country across the Yellow River and on to the Yangtze centers. It runs well over estimate: the river crossings cost what three ordinary stretches cost, and the silk houses' contribution, calculated as a share of profits that arrived later than promised, covered materially less than the memorial assumed. The state ate the difference from the only place available, the frontier garrison replacement schedule, deferred five years.
- **What the line delivers is not only what it was justified as.** Arsenal coal and a road to the Vietnamese yards, both as argued; and, in the last three years, coal at the Yangtze at a price that makes a mill independent of a river site. The first two coal-fed works are building at the line's southern end at 550, approved as ordinary licenses.
- **Pontic black earth** settlement continues and is the one program cheaper than estimated, the towns along the completed Dnieper rail absorbing grants faster than they can be issued.

## The Northern Road

The program's second period goes badly, for the reasons the previous turn identified and priced too generously.

Without steam the ice-built hulls work the coast under sail on a season that is short and getting shorter. The eastern squadron out of the Siberian river mouths loses four of its six hulls: two crushed wintering over when a season closed early, one on unmapped rock, one abandoned. Something over two hundred men are lost, most in the wintering. The squadron working north from Japanese waters does better and advances further, but is halted less by the sea than by supply, since a post can only be victualled from the post behind it and each new one costs more than the last.

Surveyed coast falls near the floor of the estimate; the posted and held line falls below it. The revised judgment is that the far shore is three periods off, not two, and will not be reached at all until the boiler can be trusted, which returns the program to the one problem the empire has not solved. Funding is not cut but flattened, which in a treasury with no slack is a cut with the political cost deferred.

## Military Posture and the Maurya Clock

No offensive operations. Garrisons held. The fleet is not expanded in hulls; it is rearmed in place.

The reports arrive in pieces across the whole window, which is how the court comes to believe them. Early: a handful of hulls with reinforced decks and something heavy aboard. Middle: gun ports cut into the hull sides in the Chinese manner rather than guns worked on open deck, meaning the Maurya have not merely mounted cannon but understood why China mounts them where it does. Late: squadrons drilling in formation, crews the dockyard talk no longer calls green, powder no longer short-rationed in exercises.

**Read cold:** the range advantage is gone. Not narrowed, gone. The conditional victory of 477 rested on holding four hundred meters against a fleet that could not answer, and there is no longer a fleet that cannot answer. What remains is rifled range, the corrected lens that lets a gun captain use it, and the breech-loader, issuable in the last years of the window and issued to the fleet before the frontier. A second naval war would be a fight and not a demonstration. Tidore, taken in an afternoon and garrisoned expensively ever since, is the most exposed position the empire holds; the garrison is reinforced rather than withdrawn, because withdrawing it would say aloud what the court believes and has no intention of confirming.

## Diplomacy: Rome

Rome's reply on the joining of the rail lines arrives early in the period and refuses the thing asked for while granting the thing wanted.

Rome will not join the lines. It offers a transshipment yard at the frontier instead: Han cars run to the east bank and stop, the load is taken across, Roman cars carry it west. Rome states the reason plainly and without apology, that a rail running unbroken from one empire into another is a road for an army, that it will not have such a road, and that it assumes China would not either. It reaffirms the river agreement and the standing tariff and asks nothing new.

The court's assessment is that Rome is correct, that China would have said the same in the same words, and that the honesty is worth more than the concession would have been. The yard delivers most of the commercial gain, the break of bulk costing a day against a doubled volume worth many. Recorded as a cost of the arrangement: Chinese men are not permitted across to the western bank, the loading there is done by Roman crews out of sight, and China therefore learns from the yard exactly what Rome intends it to learn, which is the weight of goods and nothing else. The court has run that same arrangement itself for six hundred years and does not complain of it. The gifts of the previous turn are not repeated, and nothing of the corrected lens goes west.

## Diplomacy: Egypt

The disease knowledge is applied and works partially. Egyptian fever practice and the barks that answer to it go to the Shan Plateau garrisons and to a survey party sent into the Irrawaddy lowland; the party's mortality falls to something a commander can look at without flinching, but not far enough to make the lowland administrable, and the court, holding to the halt on new conquest, files the result rather than acting on it. The same practice goes to the southern ports and the quarantine protocol, and no general epidemic occurs in the window, which the court is careful not to credit entirely to itself.

Nothing is said to Egypt of the continuous source. The scholars at Aden are sent one new instruction instead: stop describing the copper wire, establish where it terminates and what stands at the terminus, now that the court has reason to believe the ornament on Egypt's buildings was never ornament.

## Spies

- **Maurya:** continued through the Sogdian networks, dockyards and commercial districts only. The naval-clock reporting above comes from this channel and it has earned its cost twice over.
- **Egypt:** eyes only, through the twenty-scholar exchange. No infiltration.
- **Rome:** none, the previous turn's judgment reinforced by Rome's own candor about the rail.

## Fiscal Position

Revenue rises across the window on the monopolies, the mills' iron purchases and new levy, and the doubled Dnieper flow, and is entirely consumed. The trunk line's overrun, the Northern Road's losses, the Tidore garrison, and the fleet's rearmament leave the treasury at 550 where it stood at 500: solvent, grain-rich, cash-poor, one shock from difficulty.

## Open Threads Carried Forward

- Boiler safety at sea is the limiting problem: no steam on the Northern Road or the Egypt run, the far shore now judged three periods off.
- The continuous source exists and has no application. Two cheap lines of work run on it.
- The naval range advantage is gone; Tidore is held on appearance.
- The mills are taxed, quotaed, and fenced from land; the coal-fed works are building.
- The displaced household weavers are a kind of subject the court has no precedent for.
- Rome refused a joined rail in terms China judges correct, and shows China nothing of its western bank.
