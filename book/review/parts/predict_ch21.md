## Prediction (written before reading ch21)

The novel has been building toward two converging pressures: Aulus Vibenius (the haruspex who has silently watched Daniel since ch7-8, reappearing at the salon in ch16) and Crispus's sustained campaign to destroy him legally on a blasphemy/impiety charge. Now that Tyche knows English and the encyclopedia project is explicitly alive, the most logical single largest turn in ch21 is that Vibenius makes his move — either formally accusing Daniel of impiety/magic before a magistrate (the "graver word the law knows"), or summoning him to a confrontation that forces Daniel to flee, recant his work, or find an unexpected protector. Given that Daniel is now an imperial freedman, a direct accusation would implicate the emperor's household, so I predicted Vibenius acts through Crispus as proxy, filing or threatening a formal charge — forcing Daniel to choose between his work, his freedom, and his survival.

## Actual ch21 turn

The chapter delivers something different in shape but related in theme: Daniel deliberately builds and detonates the gunpowder cannon. The biggest single turn is that Daniel breaks his long-held vow not to develop fire-powder weapons, stages a controlled demonstration for army officers in which a bronze cannon explosively fails — and uses the spectacle to argue that a better metal (the steel he and Hermes cannot yet make) must be funded. The chapter reveals he has already burned a man's hands (Geta's) learning the powder ratio, and it ends with him sitting by Geta's bedside in the dark, holding the wax tablet with the ratio written in English, not erasing it. The political threat from Vibenius/Crispus is entirely absent; the chapter is inward and technical, about Daniel selling off his own vow "in slivers."

## Score: WRONG

The prediction was wrong about the agent and mechanism of the chapter's central event. Vibenius and Crispus do not appear. There is no legal charge, no summons, no confrontation with religious authority. The chapter's turn is entirely internal and self-generated: Daniel is the antagonist of his own vow. The "graver word the law knows" threat that dominated chs 15-16 is not paid off here.

That said, the prediction was PARTIALLY RIGHT in thematic register: ch21 is indeed about Daniel crossing a threshold he had sworn not to cross, with dangerous consequences for someone near him (Geta, not Daniel himself), and the chapter does end with a new and heightened danger — the powder and the steel question are now loose in the hands of powerful men who "do not unsee a weapon," making Daniel more watched and closer to the edge. The sense of irrevocable exposure is exactly what the Vibenius/Crispus thread had been building toward; the chapter delivers it through a different door.

## What telegraphed it

The ch21 turn was well-telegraphed but I misread which thread would pay off. The specific setups that pointed directly here:

- **Ch2, the cell notes**: Daniel writes "GUNPOWDER" and then "sulfur, charcoal, and the third one" followed by "???" — the white salt he cannot remember. The novel planted the incomplete trio on the second night of the story, ten chapters before the Vibenius threat ever appeared.

- **Ch14, the road south**: Daniel explicitly decides to "choose which of the things they wanted from me I gave eagerly and which I gave slow, and which I pretended I could not do at all." He names the powder directly in the same breath as the manned balloon: "the powder I knew the army would want from me the moment somebody clever asked the right question, the white salt and the charcoal and the third thing... I was not going to be the one who taught fire to do it on purpose, not yet, maybe not ever."

- **Ch15, the steel chokepoint**: The whole chapter argues that better steel is the wall under everything. Daniel frames it as necessary for saving instruments (medicine, clocks, tools) but the chapter ends with him seeing the wall clearly — and the cannon is the solution he uses to force funding for the forge.

- **Ch18, the Dacian siege**: Daniel uses his water-knowledge to cut a city's supply — "the same knowledge" as the boiled water, wielded as a weapon. Apollodorus says flat-out: "You didn't think it would stay on the one side." This directly prefigures ch21's thesis that Daniel's vow was already being chipped away.

- **Ch19, the contest**: Felix's winning tale describes a steam engine and names the obstacle: "a metal that didn't yet exist." Daniel knows this is the steel wall. The prize entry plants the idea that making powerful men dream about a new metal is the path to funding Hermes's forge — the exact logic Daniel executes in ch21 with the cannon demonstration.

The Vibenius/Crispus thread was a genuine danger that had been accumulating since ch7, but chs 14-19 had steadily shifted narrative weight toward the internal vow-and-powder problem. The cannon was the more thoroughly prepared turn; I over-weighted the external threat.
