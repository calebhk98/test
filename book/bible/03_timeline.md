# Master Timeline — story years vs real history vs Daniel's age

Daniel is 17 in 98 AD (born, in story-relative terms, ~81 AD). Real historical
events are marked [HIST]. Story/invented events are marked [STORY]. Tech state in
(parentheses). This is plot-time (the order things happen), which is mostly the
order the book tells them, with flagged exceptions.

## 98 AD — Daniel 17 — ARRIVAL (Part I)
- [HIST] Late Jan: Nerva dies; Trajan succeeds but stays on the Rhine/Danube
  frontier. The emperor is physically absent from Rome all year.
- [STORY] Spring: Daniel wakes in the countryside near Rome. Panic, the Colosseum
  sighting, arrest by local authorities/urban cohort, the rope, the cell.
- [STORY] The phone: ~2 weeks of dwindling use; he wastes the start panicking,
  writes rushed partial notes, then it dies for good.
- [STORY] The map (his first wordless move). Heras called to examine him. The
  numerals demonstration earns him "useful curiosity" status. Latin begins
  (gestures -> words). He is placed in a minor household/workshop under loose
  guard. (numerals seeded; germ framing forming)

## 99 AD — Daniel 18 — FOOTING (Part I->II)
- [HIST] Trajan enters Rome on foot, a modest adventus, greeting senators as
  equals.
- [STORY] Functional broken Latin. The germ framing tried on Heras (he punctures
  it, then half-buys the practices). Boiling/washing/filtration seeded. Daniel
  commits to the balloon as the project that will make him un-ignorable. First
  failed balloon experiments (paper, then linen; fire, leaks).

## 100-101 AD — Daniel 19-20 — THE MAN WHO FLEW (Part II)
- [STORY] The unmanned demonstration balloon rises in public = sensation. Titus
  Flavius Macer invests; the workshop is founded. Tyche assigned to Daniel.
- [STORY] First spread: crop rotation/plow/horse collar on Macer's estates; block
  printing begun; water power repurposed; spinning wheel prototype. Quintus
  Marcius Celer sees the balloon's military value. (balloon unmanned works; manned
  attempts begin to fail)

## 101-102 AD — Daniel 20-21 — THE FRONTIER (Part II)
- [HIST] First Dacian War. Trajan crosses the Danube; Apollodorus of Damascus
  builds the bridges. Decebalus sues for terms (client-king peace).
- [STORY] Daniel sent toward the Danube frontier with Celer to deploy tethered
  observation balloons and signaling. He meets Apollodorus at the bridge (peer
  recognition/rivalry). The manned-balloon DISASTER happens here: a crash/burn
  that injures or kills someone. Daniel sees war's reality up close.

## 103-105 AD — Daniel 22-24 — HOW FAR TO GO (Part III)
- [STORY] Back in Rome, rising. Steel work begins with Gaius Julius Hermes (the
  chokepoint bites). Telescope frustration. Printing press progress; block-printed
  books appear. The story/writing contest launched (~104). Germ framing spreading
  in distorted form. Lucius Vettius Crispus's antagonism grows (fraud/Thule-isn't-
  real). (steel unreliable; optics blurry; press = blocks)
- [STORY] ~104-105: Trajan grants Daniel citizenship as a reward (gift of the
  emperor). Daniel can now make a will and marry. First audience with Trajan
  (set-piece).

## 105-106 AD — Daniel 24-25 — THE COST (Part III)
- [HIST] Second Dacian War. Trajan crosses Apollodorus's stone bridge at Drobeta,
  storms Sarmizegetusa (106); Decebalus commits suicide. Dacia annexed; Dacian
  gold floods Rome; 107 = 123 days of games.
- [STORY] Daniel's sanitation/hydraulics knowledge ironically helps a siege (the
  water-supply cut). His powder work is coveted for war. Possible death of Celer
  here (decide in drafting; if not here, hold for Parthia). Daniel confronts that
  his gifts kill.

## 106-110 AD — Daniel 25-29 — PEAK BUILDING (Part III->IV)
- [STORY] Niter beds (multi-year slog) toward gunpowder. The cannon-that-bursts
  demonstration (~108) argues for steel; an assistant may be hurt in a powder
  accident. Distillation/antiseptics mature. Printing producing books (~108).
  Marcia enters (~107-108); contubernium -> marriage after citizenship. Crude
  optics. The clock promised and never ticking. Rail carts demoed in a mine and
  barely adopted (slaves cheaper). Tyche freed and apprenticed; English teaching
  begins as "homeland script."

## 112-113 AD — Daniel 31-32 — TOO VALUABLE (Part IV)
- [HIST] Trajan's Forum and Column dedicated (112-113). Parthian War begins (113).
- [STORY] Daniel at peak influence and peak danger. Scaeva (senator) tries to use
  him as a factional tool; Crispus escalates; Vibenius (haruspex) holds the
  religion card. A near-fatal crisis: a magic/impiety/fraud accusation that
  usefulness or a well-bought omen barely survives.

## 113-117 AD — Daniel 32-36 — WAR AND THE BLINDSIDE (Part IV)
- [HIST] Parthian War: Armenia/Mesopotamia annexed, Ctesiphon taken (116), the
  Persian Gulf reached; revolts erupt (Kitos War, Jewish diaspora revolt 115-117).
- [STORY] Daniel works army logistics/medicine (dysentery). The world-map ocean
  gambit begins forming. He does NOT know when Trajan will die.
- [HIST] 117, 8 Aug: Trajan dies of a stroke at Selinus in Cilicia while
  withdrawing. [STORY] Daniel is blindsided.

## 117-118 AD — Daniel 36 — STARTING OVER (Part V)
- [HIST] Hadrian proclaimed at Antioch (deathbed adoption widely doubted; engineered
  by Plotina + Attianus). 118: the Four Consulars affair (Palma, Celsus, Nigrinus,
  Lusius Quietus killed without trial) poisons Hadrian/Senate relations.
- [STORY] Daniel's patron-web is shredded; Macer's position shaken; protectors gone
  or compromised. Daniel must rebuild every relationship from scratch with a new
  emperor who has different priorities. The major midpoint reset.

## 118-125 AD — Daniel 37-44 — THE OCEAN GAMBIT (Part V)
- [HIST] Hadrian abandons Trajan's eastern conquests (consolidation), travels the
  empire, begins Hadrian's Wall (~122), rebuilds the Pantheon (~118-128).
- [STORY] Daniel deploys the true-and-false world map to Hadrian: uninhabited
  western lands a year's voyage away, reachable only with ships Rome doesn't yet
  have. Goal: redirect ambition from conquest toward ocean exploration and the
  shipbuilding/navigation it requires (and quietly imply Thule lies out there).
  Apollodorus falls to Hadrian's enmity (historically exiled/killed); Daniel loses
  a peer and maybe tries and fails to save him.

## 125-138 AD — Daniel 44-57 — THE LADDER (Part VI)
- [HIST] Antinous drowns in the Nile (130); deified. Bar Kokhba revolt (132-136)
  crushed; Jerusalem refounded as Aelia Capitolina. Backdrop only.
- [STORY] Institution-building: the press and the contest made to outlive him; a
  craftsmen's patronage system; an academy/workshop as a lasting body. The
  encyclopedia in earnest (English first, partial Latin). Teaching Tyche + a
  couple of apprentices English. Family: children grow; the politics of educating
  a daughter fully. Deaths of old friends across these years (Heras earlier;
  others). Daniel ages.
- [HIST] 138, 10 July: Hadrian dies at Baiae; Antoninus Pius succeeds.

## 138-~155 AD — Daniel 57-~74 — THE LONG WAY HOME (Part VII / Coda)
- [STORY] Daniel's final decades and decline. Finishing and securing the
  encyclopedia; the last deaths (Macer, others); Tyche and the children carry it.
  Daniel dies far from home, the work institutionalized, most of the dreams still
  unbuilt but the bottom rungs moved.

## EPILOGUE — someone climbs
- [STORY] A jump forward (a generation, then a distant whisper). The press still
  runs, the contest is still held; a young reader begins decoding the English
  ladder and solves something with it. A final, quiet sign that the empire's
  trajectory has bent. No tidy bow.

## DIVERGENCE LEDGER (log every deliberate break from real history here)
- Daniel's accumulating influence begins deflecting events from ~Part V onward. Any
  change to real outcomes must be added here with a reason.
- [ch36, ~121 AD] FIRST DIVERGENCE - THE OCEAN PROGRAM. Real Hadrian consolidated,
  toured, and built fortified frontiers (e.g. the British wall ~122); he did NOT pursue
  Atlantic/oceanic exploration. In-story, Daniel's world-map gambit turns Hadrian a few
  degrees seaward: Hadrian sanctions a BOUNDED program (NOT an armada) - (1) a single-
  scale world survey in the new figures, pushing the African coast south a season at a
  time; (2) a western-coast (Gaul/Spain) school/yard of pilots and shipwrights testing
  open-ocean hulls at a capped cost; (3) a standing imperial PRIZE for a clock that keeps
  true time a month at sea (the longitude problem). REASON it's plausible (not a wild
  break): it is framed as prestige/knowledge and consolidation, fits Hadrian's
  philhellene legacy-hunger and cost-counting, and is gated on real tech walls (hulls,
  navigation, the unsolved sea-clock) so it cannot leap ahead. WATCH: this seeds a
  shipbuilding/navigation thread for Part VI and a long-fuse lie (uninhabited west /
  year's sail). Track whether/how far the program actually gets in Daniel's lifetime
  (likely slow, possibly lapsing) and into the epilogue.
- [ch44, ~135 AD] ACCUMULATING DIVERGENCES (concrete signs the arc has bent; all
  MEASURED/partial, deep tech still unsolved):
  (a) NUMERALS GONE MONUMENTAL - Hindu-Arabic numerals WITH ZERO cut into the Aurelian
  milestone at the 2nd mile, an official magistrate's distance reckoning. The marks have
  moved from commercial slates to PERMANENT OFFICIAL STONE (real Rome never adopted
  positional numerals officially).
  (b) A PRINTED LIBRARY - a printed-codex collection in a portico off the Argiletum
  (endowed by an imperial freedman); press output now OUTNUMBERS the hand-copied scrolls
  there, in codex form, read by poor apprentices and a slave girl (literacy escaping the
  lock-and-key of copying cost).
  (c) AN OCEAN HULL - a THIRD open-ocean experimental hull on the ways at the western
  yard (south of the Garonne mouth), deep and double-planked, unlike any Roman ship.
  HONEST LIMIT: she floats but CANNOT cross - navigation/longitude unsolved, the
  sea-clock prize still unwon, two prior hulls lost. The bend is real; the dream is not
  delivered.
  (d) CULTURAL DREAMING - a carter's boy (~9) plays/recites Felix's printed steam-ship
  contest tale (an oarless, sailless, fire-eating ship to the undiscovered western
  coast): a civilization dreaming the technology a generation before it exists.
  NOTE: each sign cuts both ways (a figure serves the tax-farmer, a book serves true or
  false text, the ship goes where its owner points, the powder is loose) - the
  hope/unease braid that detonates in ch45.
- [ch46, 138 AD] OCEAN PROGRAM LOSES ITS CHAMPION. Hadrian (its sole imperial patron)
  dies; ANTONINUS PIUS (stay-at-home, no appetite for adventure) succeeds. The program is
  now PRECARIOUS/LAPSING (NOT formally killed): Daniel foresees a procurator letting it
  die by neglect (smaller grants, the Garonne yard rotting, the sea-clock prize quietly
  dropped, the African survey halted at its current headland). Daniel tells Ulpia to keep
  the survey CURRENT-BUT-UNWORKED ("true to the last headland... laid down, not closed")
  so a future hand need not start the chain from the river-mouth. He relinquishes the
  private "road west / home" hope. The ENDOWED institutions (press, contest, patronage,
  the encyclopedia) HOLD - they were built to need no living emperor (the ch38-39 point
  vindicated). WATCH: whether the ocean program revives later or stays a graded-but-empty
  road into the epilogue.
