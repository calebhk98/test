## Non-blocking notes

- [retrospective vantage — borderline rule 4] Two forward-looking phrases use the
  later-narrator position: "it would turn out I didn't know the half of how much I
  didn't know" (para 11) and "not the engineering, that came later and cost me"
  (final para). The style guide permits close retrospective as the DEFAULT POV and
  only bans using it to telegraph/spoil ("little did I know how important..."). These
  forecast cost/difficulty, not an outcome the reader is waiting on, and they serve
  the "earn the tech, it will cost him" rule. Defensible as-is. If the coordinator
  wants zero future-leak, the second is the more cuttable ("that came later and cost
  me" -> "the engineering" alone), but I would not block on either.

- [voice consistency] Daniel's narration is leaning toward lyrical/aphoristic in
  places ("I had the truth and no ladder to it," "a little flag that said foreign
  before I'd finished the word"). It mostly stays anchored by the dry teen undercut
  ("which, fair"; "playing doctor at him"; "a man let out of a cell"). The balance
  holds, but the prose is at the ornate edge of his register; watch that it does not
  drift further into Victorian-narrator territory in later chapters. Not a violation
  here.

- [time-skip rendering] Confirmed NOT cheated. The year is delivered as four discrete
  scenes (the aqua/Stichus opening, the name-getting, the egg-market joke, the
  germ-pitch + boiled-water sequence) plus a spring-anniversary closing beat. The
  montage-y framing sentences are kept short and always hand off to a concrete scene.
  This satisfies "3-5 concrete scenes, not montage."

- [language acquisition honesty] Strong. Broken Latin shown with specific mechanics:
  v-as-w accent, hard-c relearning, wrong grammatical case (announcing himself as the
  object of sentences), counting/haggling fluency but losing educated Greek at speed,
  "barely read, only the figures and a few words." He is explicitly NOT fluent. The
  French-pattern crutch is honest (one idea: words bend) and matches his stated
  background. Meets world rule 5.

- [germ framing + Heras] Both halves land sharply. The pitch is rendered (dots,
  finger-legs, "small living ones the gods send... eat you from the middle out"). The
  demolition is specific and in-character: marsh/bad air, the four humors named on his
  own arm as real Hippocratic doctrine, divine wrath, and the witch-talk warning as a
  genuine danger (curses/evil eye/the law's teeth). The quiet adoption is shown
  through behavior weeks later (sends the girl to boil water, washes first, gives
  Daniel "no credit, no concession") rather than stated. Posca included. Meets world
  rule 6 and the [ch05] canon entry exactly.

- [proof-honesty] The "boy lived, proves nothing" passage is exactly the world-rule-9
  / rule-6 honesty the brief wants: no count, no control, no second untreated boy, "a
  coin flip." Good.

- [loneliness shown not stated] Mostly shown via concrete failures: the swallowed
  movie line that "dies in the back of his throat," the dead phone against his hip,
  counting eggs in Latin to avoid counting other things, "the only applause in my
  life." A couple of lines edge toward stating it ("it got worse as the words came,
  not better"; "It was the loneliest I'd been since the phone died"). These are the
  weakest moments against rule 5/7, but each is immediately grounded in a physical
  detail, so they read as earned rather than announced. Acceptable.

- [predictability / intelligent-reader] Passes. The egg-joke beat and the
  boiled-water reframe are not over-explained; the reader is trusted to see why "clean"
  works where "creatures" does not. The balloon arrives as a felt discovery (warm
  draft under the tiles) rather than an announced plot point, and stays unnamed and
  unbuilt, correctly deferring the engineering.

- [length] 4,057 words, matching the brief's figure. Does not feel padded; every
  scene carries plot or character. The closing reflection is the longest non-scene
  stretch but earns its length by converting the germ-pitch defeat into the balloon
  motive (cheap / mine / undeniable), which is the chapter's structural hinge.
