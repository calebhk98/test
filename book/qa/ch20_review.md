# QA — Chapter 20: Tyche

## Zero-tolerance sweep
- **Em dashes:** none. Zero occurrences of "—". The two parenthetical asides use normal
  dashes set with spaces (L97 `do this" - the hand on the columns - "`; L25 grep artifact),
  which is the permitted form. Clean.
- **Correctio / epanorthosis:** no banned pivoting "not X but Y," "it isn't X it's Y,"
  "not just X but Y," or "less X than Y." The contrast structures present are the permitted
  sentence-split direct form (same standard applied in ch19):
  - L103 "It was not the look from the versions in my head. It was the look she gave a column
    that didn't sum." — closest call in the chapter. It is split into two full sentences and
    the positive half is a concrete, specific image (her error-hunting look), not an abstract
    antithesis. It also brushes negative narration, but it renders a real observed expression
    rather than narrating an absence to imply meaning. Within bounds; flagged as the single
    line to watch.
  - L119 (Tyche) "Not the figures, everyone has the figures now... The other thing." — spoken
    emphasis, broken across sentences, fully in her clipped voice. Characterized speech, not
    authorial antithesis. Clean.
  - L75 "a citizen of limited kind and not a Junian half-thing the way Pamphilus had come
    out" — a true legal contrast between two real manumission outcomes, not a rhetorical
    pivot. Clean and historically correct.

## VOICE WATCH (the "I never / I have never / as long as I live / I still" tic)
- L139 "because I never said *at home*, I had spent nine years not saying *at home*" — this is
  "I never [verb]," NOT the flagged retrospective-punctuation tic ("I never forgave," "as long
  as I live"). It is a present-scene factual statement of a habit, load-bearing to the plot
  beat (his slip). Acceptable; does not recur as a closing flourish.
- L11 "I still couldn't drape" / "I had been telling myself that for two years" — ordinary
  close-retrospect, no future-spoiling. No "as long as I live" / "I have never" instances.
  The ch13 tic does not resurface. Clean.

## Brief objectives — met
- Anti-slavery weight is in EVENTS and her personhood, NOT a Daniel speech. The world-rule is
  honored almost pointedly: at L43 Daniel HAS the abolitionist speech ready ("I had the words,
  I'd had the words for years, the whole speech") and deliberately swallows it because it would
  cost the deal and change nothing about the law. The horror is carried by the bill of sale,
  the five years of forced invisibility, and Tyche's own analysis of what freedwoman + woman +
  alone means. Exemplary execution of rule 7.
- Romance subverted: explicitly. Daniel names the sibling/found-family frame (L63 "the closest
  thing I had to a sister"), and the chapter's emotional engine is his shame at having wanted
  her gratitude / a particular look (L77, L93). Tyche reads and refuses exactly that wish. She
  is angry, difficult, arithmetical, and fully a person; the "buy a young woman" expectation is
  weaponized into the gossip/danger beat rather than indulged. NOT a love interest. Met.
- Ends on a concrete image (the letter E, larger, in bad light). Met.

## Predictability / respecting the reader
- The premise (buy and free Tyche) is known from the brief, but the chapter's real moves are
  not predictable from page one: Macer having known all along and the price being a true bill
  for her mind (L41); Daniel swallowing the speech; Tyche refusing thanks and re-framing the
  five years as control rather than rescue; the danger landing on her; her demanding the
  English cipher as the currency she'll accept. The reader is not spoon-fed; her dialogue does
  the reframing, Daniel does not annotate it. Good.
