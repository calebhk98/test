# QA — Chapter 24

VERDICT: REVISE

## Blocking issues (must fix)

- [ZERO-TOLERANCE correctio] (para after "It does nothing") "because the thing
  it needs is **not** heat or force or skill, **it is** a current I cannot make
  and could not explain..." — This is the banned epanorthosis pivot ("it is not
  X, it is Y"), defining the thing by what it isn't and then substituting. Per
  the QA brief this is always blocking and forces REVISE regardless of how well
  it reads. Fix by stating it directly: "...because what it needs is a current I
  cannot make and could not explain and have given up grieving for the way you
  give up grieving for a country." (Drop the "not heat or force or skill" negation,
  or recast as a positive list: "It needs a current, and there is no current here.")

## Non-blocking notes

- [define-by-negation, borderline] "Not a metal ladder. A ladder of knowing."
  This is the correctio shape rendered as two fragments. It is softer than a
  one-clause pivot and reads as natural emphasis, so I am NOT calling it blocking,
  but if you are tightening line 57 anyway, consider whether this one earns its
  keep. The contest reveal would land just as hard with "A ladder of knowing.
  She'd written..." opening the beat. Writer's call.
- [negation tic frequency] The chapter leans hard on stacked negatives in a few
  spots: "no fire I can build, no wheel I can turn, no clever Roman trick and no
  clever Thulean one either"; "there is no one, there is no glass, there is
  nothing in this entire world." These are NOT banned negative-narration (they
  render present absence with rhythm, they don't imply meaning by listing what
  fails to happen) and the cadence is good, but they cluster in the phone section.
  No fix required; flagging so it doesn't grow across Part IV.
- [memoir "I have/I will" lines — judged EARNED] "I have it still" (the clay
  train), "I have never in my life been so completely alone," and "I have a
  feeling now, writing this, that I will be standing in front of it the day I
  die." Each is retrospect-as-it-felt, not a spoiler or future-narrator weight-
  assignment: the "writing this" frame is canon, the train line is a present
  possession, the loneliness line is the chapter's emotional core (and the
  narrator immediately refuses to elaborate, which is correct restraint), and the
  "day I die" line points at an unanswered question, not a plot event. The
  "as long as I draw breath" / "before I die" pair both sit in HERAS's mouth, not
  the narrator's, so the per-chapter "I never / as long as I live" narrator tic
  flagged in the canon VOICE WATCH does NOT over-cluster here. All pass.
- [subtext held — PASS] Loneliness is shown (the wrong song, the unsendable
  current, the mother's voice he can't verify, "no one I can turn to and say: she
  got it") and explicitly walled off from being lectured ("I am not going to write
  any more about it than that"). The hubris/duty question is opened by Heras and
  left open ("He hadn't said either word... I will be standing in front of it the
  day I die"); Daniel deflects and the silent almost-confession is rendered as
  what he COULD have said, never resolved. Correctly subtextual.
- [dramatic irony — intended, PASS] Daniel withholding the truth from Heras while
  the reader sees the whole staircase confession is the briefed intended irony,
  built architecturally (the unspoken paragraph), not declared. Compliant.
- [ending — PASS] Closes on a concrete image (lying beside Marcia on the high
  ground, listening to the city's small fires, "waited... for whatever was coming
  up the slope"), not a takeaway thesis. The high-ground/"they come for you"
  material is dramatized through the soldier's-lore framing and Daniel's physical
  unease ("a coolness on the back of the neck"), not stated as a moral.
- [beat-tags — clean] No nodded/sighed/chuckled/shrugged punctuation. Beats are
  specific to character (Heras "patted the bench twice... the wood not me";
  "turned the flask in his hands"; Daniel "had to go and stand by the wine").
- [voice] Roman-cadence drift is appropriate for 110 AD / 12 years in, and the
  modern teen still surfaces ("she got the whole thing. Look."; "a guy who got
  lucky with a map"). No anachronism. Consistent with the gradual-drift rule.
