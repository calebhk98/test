# QA - Chapter 39

VERDICT: REVISE

(Two clean, hard-to-miss continuity defects sit on otherwise strong, fully-rendered
material. Both are surgical fixes. Once corrected this chapter passes easily; the
prose, scene-rendering, and theme-handling are among the best in the run.)

## Blocking issues (must fix)

- [CANON / name clash] line 32-33: "His name was Onesimus, which I disliked, because
  the old accountant who had called my figures shopkeeper's marks had been an
  Onesimus, but the boy was twelve and not the accountant and the name is as common
  as dirt." This is the BLOCKING clash the brief flagged, and it is real. ONESIMUS is
  an established on-page character: the senior house accountant who dismissed the new
  figures as "shopkeeper's marks" (ch10, ch16) and who told Macer about Tyche's math
  aptitude (ch20). Two distinct named characters inside the same enterprise cannot
  share a name. The text's self-aware hand-wave ("the name is as common as dirt")
  does NOT fix it - it actually makes the collision louder by reminding the reader of
  the accountant at the exact moment it reuses his name, and it spends three clauses
  of narration apologizing for a problem the writer could simply have avoided.
  FIX: rename the apprentice to a fresh, unused name. He is a freeborn Subura
  wood-carver's son, ~12. Clean options that are NOT in the principal cast or already
  used as walk-ons (Pamphilus/Eros/Stichus/Davus/Geta/Syrus/Felix/Fortunatus are
  taken): suggest **VITALIS** (freeborn-sounding, plain, never used) or **PRISCUS**.
  Avoid anything ending the way existing names do. Cut the entire "which I disliked
  ... common as dirt" aside; replace with a plain introduction of the new name so the
  Onesimus accountant is never invoked. (Bonus: removing the aside also removes a
  small repetition-of-established-dynamic beat re-explaining the shopkeeper's-marks
  history, AGENT_RULES #13.)

- [CANON / age] line 87: "when he was fifteen and came to the press once because I
  asked him to". Lucanus is b.~112 (named ch26; the AGE ANCHOR and ch38 fix Lucanus
  pre-war birth). At this chapter's setting (~128-129) he is ~16-17, not 15. FIX:
  change "fifteen" to "sixteen" (or "near seventeen"). Confirm the year you are
  pinning ch39 to and compute: 129 - 112 = 17; 128 - 112 = 16. Either lands; "fifteen"
  does not.

- [NEGATIVE NARRATION - the closer, judged] line 95: "I set it on the bench where his
  hand would find it in the morning, and left, and did not look at the block, because
  I could not have read it close enough to spoil my own pleasure in not needing to."
  This is ACCEPTABLE, not banned negative narration. The rule (AGENT_RULES #2) bans
  narrating absence to imply meaning ("nobody reacted," "she said nothing"). Here
  "did not look at the block" is a deliberate physical CHOICE by the actor, with a
  rendered cause (his failing eyes, ch38's presbyopia) and a concrete payoff (the
  inventor who can no longer read close, who no longer needs to). It is action, not
  implied-silence. It also lands the inventor-to-tender theme through behavior rather
  than thesis - exactly right. Keep it. (Minor: "spoil my own pleasure in not needing
  to" is a hair abstract for a closing clause; if tightening, consider ending on the
  physical - the keen edge under his thumb, the boy's seven printed true - but this is
  taste, not a fault.)
