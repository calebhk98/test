## Non-blocking notes

VOICE (passes):
- Genuinely Roman epistolary register throughout: opens with the salutation
  formula ("GAIUS NORBANUS RUFUS TO HIS FRIEND SEXTUS CAECILIUS PRISCUS,
  GREETING"), periodic and allusive ("A library, properly understood, is the
  conversation of the great dead, conducted in the silence they have earned,"
  L7), Pliny-manner supercilious self-awareness ("You will say I am a snob, and
  you will be right," L9). No slip into Daniel's modern register or modern
  knowledge anywhere. The "second mile" (L9) is a milestone/mile-marker, not a
  clock unit; no minutes/seconds/okay; no anachronism.
- The clerk-honesty point ("a clerk three streets off could check my sum without
  trusting my honesty," L9) is the audit/anti-fraud virtue of the numerals
  expressed in period-plausible terms, not modern bookkeeping jargon. Clean.

DRAMATIC IRONY - architectural, not declared (passes; this was the load-bearing check):
- Rufus is fully confident throughout and never winks. He repeats every road-lie
  AS FACT in period-plausible wrongness: Thule as a real frozen nursery (L13),
  the western-sea scouts as a thing that "always made me set down my cup" yet is
  still reported as the tale (L13), "He made fire fly. That much is past dispute"
  (L15). The reader supplies the truth; the writer never hints he might be wrong.
- The buried black-glass tablet (L21) is handled exactly to brief: misread as "a
  savage's charm... a dark mirror these northern peoples carry to frighten off
  the dead," laughed at ("must take his idol down into the dark with him like a
  Pharaoh"), NOT understood, NOT dug up. The phone-detail cuts hard because the
  reader knows precisely what black glass the size of a palm that "took no
  inscription" is. The citizenship "bronze plate" buried alongside is consistent
  with ch49.
- RULING on the watched line (L23): "the thing he made does not need him to be
  remembered rightly in order to go on." This reads as an IN-CHARACTER CYNICAL
  ASIDE, not theme-declaration. It is framed as a worldly commonplace ("A lie
  outlives the liar; we have always known it"), extended to truth only as a
  detached, faintly surprised epigram ("It appears a true thing may do the
  same"), and immediately buried under the corked-wine close. Rufus tosses it off
  without grasping its weight; he does not address the reader or signal a hidden
  meaning. Architectural. Acceptable as written - no recast required.
- The ch27 "reason" (the knowing was never his own; every rung set down before
  him) is reported as the freedman's hearsay AND as Rufus's explicit disbelief
  ("I do not believe he believed it," L23), framed as convenient piety, not as a
  thesis. Correct.

- DATE: "the day before the Ides of June, in the consulship of Clarus and
  Cethegus" (L29) = 12 June AD 170 (C. Erucius Clarus and M. Gavius Cornelius
  Cethegus were the ordinary consuls of 170). A correct, real historical anchor;
  lands squarely in the briefed ~165-175 window. Not a fake detail.

## New canon facts that should be logged
- [ch51] INTERLUDE, dated 12 June AD 170 (consulship of Clarus and Cethegus),
  ~15 years after Daniel's death. A letter from GAIUS NORBANUS RUFUS (cultured
  man of letters, Pliny manner; has a grandson; wife TERTIA) to his friend
  SEXTUS CAECILIUS PRISCUS. New interlude-only names; no clash with principals.
- [ch51] The printed library in the portico off the Argiletum is public, chained,
  and heavily used by the low/working/literate-learning classes (apprentices, a
  fuller's boy, a Syrian, a girl reading with her finger) - the partial Latin copy
  (ch49 copy #4) is in active circulation; numerals are "everywhere now, cut in the
  milestone at the second mile," in private wine-tallies, etc.
- [ch51] Tyche (unnamed, "very old," "his clerk fifty years") is ALIVE ~170 AD,
  still keeping the books, and has bought a slave-girl and begun teaching her the
  cipher/saying; the girl is teaching two more (keeper-chain fanning down; seeds
  ch52). Confirms the ch48/49 keeper-chain persisting past Daniel's death.
- [ch51] The buried black-glass tablet (the phone) and the bronze citizenship
  plate are, in received Roman legend, misread as a "savage's charm"/"dark mirror"
  - i.e. the grave is known of but its meaning is lost and the objects are NOT
  recovered. Consistent with ch49 burial.
- [ch51] Daniel's ch27 "reason" (the knowing was never his own; every rung set
  down before him; he only carried the fire back) survives ~170 AD as a remembered
  saying attributed to him via Tyche, treated by the cultured class as convenient
  founder-piety, not believed.
