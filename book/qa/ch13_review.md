# QA — Chapter 13

VERDICT: REVISE

## Blocking issues (must fix)
- [ZERO-TOLERANCE correctio / "not X but Y"] (line 43) "I tied a long light
  line to the basket anyway, not to hold him, the wind would have that line for
  breakfast, but so that when he came down we'd have something to run to, a
  thread to follow into the brush." — This is a textbook "not X ... but Y"
  construction: the line's purpose is defined by negation and then pivoted with
  "but." Per the QA brief this is always blocking, no borderline pass.
  Concrete fix: state the purpose directly and put the wind aside as its own
  beat. e.g. "I tied a long light line to the basket anyway. Not to hold him;
  the wind would have that line for breakfast. It was so that when he came down
  we'd have something to run to, a thread to follow into the brush." (Splitting
  the clauses removes the rhetorical pivot while keeping every word.) An even
  cleaner option: "I tied a long light line to the basket anyway, knowing the
  wind would have it for breakfast, so that when he came down we'd have a thread
  to follow into the brush."

## Non-blocking notes
- Retrospective lines: all four are PERMITTED close-retrospect, not banned
  future-annotation. None spoils a plot beat or arrives from the future to
  assign weight to a coming event:
  - (L35) "I have hated him and envied him... more times than I can count" —
    memoir coloring. Keep.
  - (L47) "which I have never forgiven the army for letting a man do" — present
    memoir judgment, no plot spoil. Keep. (This is the line the canon VOICE
    WATCH already sanctions.)
  - (L47) "and I have them still" (the children's names) — memoir, no spoil.
    Keep.
  - (L101) "that smell I will not get out of the back of my throat as long as I
    live" — memoir, lands on a concrete image, no spoil. Keep.
  - Frequency within this chapter is acceptable. Heed the canon VOICE WATCH:
    "I have / I never / as long as I live" must not become a per-chapter tic in
    later chapters.
- Negative narration around the body (L77) "no alive, he's alive to hold up in
  front of myself this time, nothing to bind in wet linen, nothing for Heras's
  clean hands" — NOT a banned negation. It states a substantive concrete fact
  (the fall killed him outright; there is no wound to treat) and renders
  Daniel's useless reflex toward the ch11 treatment that worked on Naso. It is
  immediately grounded in what IS there: "The fall had done it. The fire was
  just the fire." Justified. Keep.
- Naso (L83) "He didn't say I told you to make him keep his seat. He didn't say
  anything." — Borderline negative narration, but defensible: the specific thing
  he withholds carries weight (he warned, he earned the right to say it), and it
  is immediately anchored in rendered physical action (the one good hand over
  Daniel's fist). Reads as character-restraint, not empty "nobody reacted."
  Keep, but watch: if a leaner cut is wanted, the first sentence alone ("He
  didn't say I told you to make him keep his seat") carries it; the second is
  near-redundant.
- Telegraphing: (L63) "I knew with my whole body what was coming" and (L15) "I
  had heard it coming" are Daniel's in-the-moment, technically-grounded dread
  (he predicted the wind failure), not narrator-from-the-future omniscience.
  The death is never named before it happens. Acceptable.
- Em dashes: none. Beat-tag clichés (nodded/sighed/chuckled/shrugged/etc.):
  none.
- Scene ending lands on a concrete image (the rag in the fire, the grease
  catching, the smell), not a restated takeaway. Good.
