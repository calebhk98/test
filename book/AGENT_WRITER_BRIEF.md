# Writer Brief — standing instructions for any chapter-writing subagent

You are writing ONE chapter of a long novel. You will be told the chapter number
and given a specific brief. Other people wrote the chapters around yours; stay in
the lines so it all fits together.

## Read first, in this order (only these unless told otherwise)
1. `/home/user/test/book/AGENT_RULES.md` - the writing law. Non-negotiable.
2. `/home/user/test/book/bible/06_style_guide.md` - voice, POV, tense, mechanics.
3. `/home/user/test/book/bible/00_premise.md` - what the book is about.
4. `/home/user/test/book/bible/08_canon_log.md` - everything already established
   in the prose. DO NOT contradict it. DO NOT re-explain what it already covers.
5. The specific bible files named in your chapter brief (characters, world,
   tech, timeline, glossary - usually only the relevant ones).
6. The "previous chapter recap" given in your brief (a few lines). Continue
   cleanly from it. You generally will NOT be given the full previous chapter;
   trust the recap and the canon log.

## Your job
- Write the chapter as finished prose. Render every scene. Never summarize a
  scene, skip hard material, or write a placeholder. That is cheating and will
  be rejected.
- Hit the beats in your brief, but you own the execution: the lines, the
  blocking, the texture, the order within the chapter.
- Target 2,500-4,000 words unless the brief says otherwise.
- Plant only the hints/foreshadowing your brief asks for. Don't resolve threads
  that belong to later chapters.
- Stay inside Daniel's knowledge and the material reality of the era. If the
  brief asks for an invention, show the cost and the failure points; don't let
  things just work.

## Hard "don'ts"
- Don't break any rule in AGENT_RULES.md. Re-read the banned-moves list before
  you finalize. Especially: no em dashes, no "not X but Y" constructions, no
  narrator stepping outside time, no telegraphing, no beat-tag clichés.
- Don't restate dynamics or backstory the reader already has (check the canon
  log).
- Don't make Daniel smoothly competent or a genius. He's a normal smart teen.

## Deliver
- Write the chapter to the exact path given in your brief (e.g.
  `/home/user/test/book/chapters/ch07.md`).
- Start the file with a single H1 line: `# Chapter N — <short title>`.
- After writing, reply to the coordinator in under 120 words with: (a) a 3-5
  sentence recap of what happens (for the next writer's continuity), and (b) any
  NEW canon facts you established that must be logged (names, dates, places,
  inventions' states, promises made, who knows what). Be precise.
