                      ONE PERSON, AND EVERYTHING THEY KNOW                    
==============================================================================

   You are one person, dropped into a pre-industrial society, carrying the
   knowledge of how modern technology works and none of the industry that makes
   it. Knowing how a thing works is free. Building it is not: it costs your own
   hours, other people's hours, money, materials, and years you do not get
   back.

   You arrive alone. No employees, no slaves, nobody who owes you anything, and
   about enough money to eat for a few months.

   3) Scandinavia in the Viking age, 900

      nobody will stop you, and nobody can fund you either.
      1,500,000 people   state capacity 0.15   prices 1.40x Rome

   4) England under Edward I, 1300
      Watermills and windmills everywhere, a real legal system, chartered towns,

      and about to be hit by the worst demographic catastrophe in European
      history.
      4,500,000 people   state capacity 0.60   prices 1.10x Rome

   5) The Mexica Triple Alliance, 1500
      A large, dense, literate, well-administered state with monumental
      construction, chinampa agriculture that outyields anything in Europe, and no
      draught animals, no iron, no wheel in practical use.
      5,000,000 people   state capacity 0.80   prices 0.80x Rome

   Which one? [1-5, or q to leave] 
==============================================================================
   THE ROMAN EMPIRE UNDER TRAJAN, 100
==============================================================================

   You come to on the Ostia road in the eighteenth year of Domitian's calendar,
   except Domitian is four years dead and nobody has corrected the milestone.
   It is the second consulship of Trajan. Rome is at its greatest extent and
   its most confident: the roads are the best in the world, the water arrives
   on schedule, the courts sit, the grain fleet comes in from Alexandria, and
   sixty-five million people are governed by men who genuinely believe the
   arrangement is permanent.

   WHAT YOU CAN SEE
   Concrete that sets under water. Glass windows. Surgical instruments a modern
   surgeon would recognise. Water-mills in banks of sixteen. A postal service
   that outruns anything Europe will have again for fourteen hundred years. And
   no mechanical clock, no printing, no spectacles, no chimney, no stirrup, no
   zero.

   WHAT IS MISSING
   Not the ideas. The instruments. Nobody can measure a temperature, hold a
   tolerance, sustain a vacuum, or purify anything. Every single thing you know
   how to build waits behind those four.

   WHAT IS COMING, AND ONLY YOU KNOW IT
   Sixty-five good years, then the Antonine plague. A century after that, the
   currency is debased to a tenth and the frontier stops holding. You know the
   dates. Nobody else does.
