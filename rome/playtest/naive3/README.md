# Third naive round: the Mexica, 1500

Rome, Han China and the Norse have been played. This is Tenochtitlan: a quarter
of a million people on a lake, chinampa agriculture that outyields anything in
Europe, no draught animal anywhere in the hemisphere, no iron, and nineteen
years before the ships come.

Same rules as before. The testers are told the launch line, where to write, and
their role. No command list, no protocol document, no examples of what to look
for.

One thing is new. They are asked to write down, AS THEY PLAY, what they think a
thing will unlock or do before they build it — and then what actually happened.
Fog of war means the game is a model-building exercise, and the useful question
is not only "did it work" but "could a careful player form a correct picture of
it from what the game says". A prediction that turns out wrong is a finding
about the game, not about the tester.
