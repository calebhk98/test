# Han China playtest notes (naive15)

Session started 2026-09-12. Playing via `python3 rome/sim/simulator.py`, menu mode, tmux session "hangame" for persistent interactive play.

## Setup choices
- New game -> civ 1) The Later Han Empire, 100 AD
- Fog of war: left ON (default Y) - wanted the "first time" experience, not the planner view.
- Mortality: left OFF (default N) - founder does not age. The prompt explicitly says the premise of the whole game is that mortality SHOULD be on, but also that default is off "to measure the tree". Went with default.
- Goal: left default (1) "Grown and alloy junction transistors" - the transistor, closure 168, floor 142 years. This is clearly the "real" top-level goal of the game (title of the intro screen literally sets up "carrying the knowledge of how modern technology works").
- Starting wealth: left default "poor_scholar" (400 den in Rome prices -> 300 cash here since Han prices are 0.75x Rome).
- Horizon: left default (2) Standard, 500 years.

Save file: /root/.rome-saves/han_china_100ad_252.json (game tells you this so you can resume later - nice touch, auto-saves after every command apparently).

## First impressions (100 AD, turn 0)
Status line format: `[100 AD | 300 cash | you:2000 hr | sch 1 art 1 | rep 5] >`
- 300 cash
- "you:2000 hr" - presumably my own labor-hours available, per year? 2000 hours/year is roughly a full work-year (40hr*50weeks), so I'm guessing this is an annual labor budget that refills.
- "sch 1 art 1" - unclear yet. Maybe "scholarship 1, artisan-skill 1"? Or could be building/institution counts. Need to check with 'state' or 'help'.
- "rep 5" - reputation, probably 0-10 or some scale.

Game explicitly suggests starting commands: state, available, why <name>, start <name>, step. Plus 'stuck' and 'help'.


## Years 100-104: first real mistake (debt spiral)
At turn 0 I started 3 things at once: the two free "flavor" techs (lnd_cursus_publicus, sea_pharos_lighthouse - cost 0, turned out to be one-off knowledge with "nothing else rests on this; worth having for itself" - basically achievement/flavor items) plus scientific_method (157.5 cash, "almost everything rests on this").

Year 101: after both free items + scientific_method completed their calendar floors, I greedily also started arithmetic_positional (360 cash, described in its own flavor text as "Highest return on personal hours in the entire tree") and units_standards (402 cash, "almost everything rests on this"). Both looked good individually by their own "why" text, but doing them simultaneously on top of an already-committed scientific_method tipped me deep into debt: cash went from 242 to -513.8 in one step, with 59% of my 856 credit limit used and interest on arrears alone at 60.4/yr. NET RECURRING went negative (-35/yr).

This is the first real trap of the game: each item's "why" screen only tells you about itself - cost, risk, payoff - not about what happens when you stack several. There's no visible "total cash outlay this year across everything in hand" warning before you commit; you have to add it up yourself (cost items were 360+402=762, I had 242 cash, so immediately ~520 went to credit at 12%/yr). 'start' DOES print a credit forecast per item when you start it, including "you would borrow: 160" etc, but it's easy to not add up multiple such forecasts in your head, especially with information overload. This is a thing I'd tell the designers: show a running total / warn loudly when you're about to exceed cash on hand across everything in flight, not just per-item.

Discovered the 'work <trade> <hours>' command as the fix: spending my own idle founder-hours as ordinary wage labour (scholar trade) earns MORE than my passive medical practice revenue for the same hours - e.g. working all 2000 hours as a scholar for one year earned 340.1 cash gross, of which 194.5 was "taken from" what the passive practice would have earned anyway, netting +145.6 versus doing nothing. So once a project's calendar floor is just ticking (waiting on time, not on your hours), the right move is to sell ALL your idle hours via 'work' rather than let them go to waste - the game says this explicitly ("2,000 founder-hours this year are going into nothing") but it took me a few turns to realize 'work' was the lever, since it's not one of the 5 starter commands.

Used three successive 'work scholar' calls (500, 1000, 500, then 2000 hours) across two turns to climb back from -513.8 to -29.7 cash.

Then in year 103->104, scientific_method FAILED its first attempt (the stated 20% risk hit): lost 60 cash (40% of cost) and 140 of 350 hours, but "what went wrong is now understood" - risk drops to 15% on attempt 2, and 1.3 of its 2-year calendar floor already carries over. So failure is not a full restart, it's a penalized retry with the risk curve improving - this matches what 'why' told me upfront ("IT CAN FAIL MORE THAN ONCE"). Good, it was honest about this risk in advance; I just didn't expect to actually eat it turn 3.

Lesson for future turns: don't start more than one or two paid projects at once until there's a cash cushion; use 'work' aggressively whenever founder-hours are idle and a project is only waiting on the calendar.


## Year 111-113: staff hiring - a genuinely confusing/possibly-broken bit of behaviour
Wanted a second scholar to satisfy "STAFF NEEDED: 2 scholars" gates on atomic_theory / calculus / logarithms (with only myself, I'm "1.0 scholars"). Used 'hire scholar 1'. Cost: 328-354 cash as "a finder's fee and the first year in advance", deducted immediately. Prompt correctly showed "sch 2 art 1" right after hiring, and 'labour' confirmed "ON YOUR STAFF: scholar 1".

FIRST ATTEMPT (year 111->112): hired the scholar, then used 'work scholar 2000' (spent all my own hours on wage work), then 'step 1'. Result: in year 112 the hired scholar was GONE. 'labour' showed "ON YOUR STAFF: nobody", "Total employed: 0", EMPLOY back to "0 people, 0 cash/yr in wages", prompt back to "sch 1 art 1". The 'log' command showed NO entry at all for a firing, death, resignation, or anything else between the hiring and this point - the newest log line was still the hiring itself. No message printed during the 'step' about losing anyone either. Cash was positive when this happened (160.8), so it wasn't obviously an affordability auto-fire. This looks like either a bug or a very poorly-signposted rule.

SECOND ATTEMPT (year 112->113), to see if it was reproducible: hired another scholar (same finder's-fee pattern), confirmed via 'labour' they were on staff, but this time immediately 'start atomic_theory' (which needs 2 scholars) BEFORE stepping, then stepped without doing 'work' first. This time the scholar SURVIVED the step - year 113 shows "EMPLOY: 1 people, 337.5 cash/yr in wages", "scholar 1" listed.

So the working hypothesis (not confirmed, and I did not look at source to check): a hired specialist who is not actually claimed by a started project in the same year they're hired can quietly disappear by the next step, with zero log entry and zero warning, while one who's immediately put to use on a project sticks around. If that's really the rule, it is not explained anywhere I've found (not in 'help labour', not in the hire confirmation text, not in 'policy'). It cost me 328 cash for nothing on the first attempt and I would not have understood why without deliberately re-testing it. This is the single most "looks broken" thing I've hit so far - I'm flagging it and moving on rather than digging into the code to confirm the mechanism, per the ground rules for this playtest.

Also noted in passing: hiring a second scholar does NOT increase how many hours my own 'work' command can sell (still capped at my 2,000 founder-hours/yr either way) - the extra scholar's purpose seems to be purely (a) satisfying STAFF NEEDED headcount gates and (b) presumably contributing to a project's separately-purchased "HIRED LABOUR" cost line, not to my personal 'work' throughput.


## Year 112-123: atomic_theory, the debt spiral round two, and a stale status label
Went for atomic_theory (825 cash, 1000 of my hours, 2yr floor, "worth roughly 150 years of chemistry") right after hiring the second scholar, without rebuilding a cash cushion first - same mistake pattern as turn 0, and it bit again: cash swung to -556 at the worst point (year 114), net recurring -371/yr with the scholar's wage stacked on top of arrears interest. Fired the scholar (see above) to stop the wage bleed, and spent about 8 years alternating 'work scholar <hours>' (all-in when the project just needed cash) and 'work scholar <hours minus project's remaining need>' (once the project's OWN debt hit 0 and it was "waiting on your hours" specifically) with 'step 1', grinding the arrears down. There's a genuinely nice piece of UI here I hadn't seen before: once you've been in arrears a while, the game prints an unprompted warning block telling you exactly how to dig out ("YOU HAVE BEEN IN ARREARS 8 YEARS AND YOU LOSE 7 CASH A YEAR... work as a scholar: 2000 of your own hours are left this year and would bring in about 340..."). That's good, proactive design - it should probably show up much earlier than 8 years in, though.

Also: once atomic_theory hit "100% of your hours spent, 0 still owed", the status line kept saying "waiting on your hours" for THREE MORE STEPS (years 121, 122, 123) while I was in fact feeding it 0 additional hours (I was working all 2000 for wages each of those turns, correctly assuming the project itself had nothing left to take). It only actually needed the 2-year calendar floor to run out, the same as e.g. lnd_cursus_publicus's "waiting on the calendar" earlier - but the label never switched to that phrase, it just sat on "waiting on your hours" (which by its own numbers, 100%/0, was already false) right up until it silently completed. Minor, but a real "the status text contradicts the numbers on the same line" issue I'd flag to the designers - it cost me a bit of confused re-checking (ran 'why atomic_theory' three separate times expecting to find some hidden remaining requirement, there wasn't one).

Total elapsed for atomic_theory: started year 112, completed year 123 - 11 calendar years against a stated 2-year floor, almost entirely because of my own cash mismanagement rather than bad dice (no failures this time, 10% risk never landed). Lesson reinforced: don't start anything expensive right after a big recurring cost (a new hire) without a cushion.

Scandal has been climbing with each foundational/orthodoxy-challenging completion (scientific_method, atomic_theory): 0.44 -> ... -> 0.98 by year 124, "rising 0.90 last year, crosses the line (25) in about 27 years" at the current rate. Not urgent yet but clearly a long-run pressure I'll need to manage (bribe, or slower down the controversial techs) rather than ignore forever.


## Year 125: the history/risk system - genuinely impressive
'risk' turned out to be one of the best parts of the game so far. It lists real, named historical events on a timeline specific to Han China (the regency cycle and Partisan Prohibitions, Yellow Turban rebellion 184-205, Three Kingdoms fragmentation, the Yongjia disaster/fall of Luoyang, the An Lushan rebellion, etc.), each with a short, well-written explanation of what actually happened historically and WHY it matters to my position (e.g. the bureaucracy I depend on hollows out when the court fragments; a "millenarian Daoist religious movement" hardens the state against tolerating unlicensed technical/alchemical work, which is exactly the category my own research falls into). Each entry gives a concrete year-by-year sack/output-loss percentage and a currently-live "what would help" (land and power of my own not depending on trade a war can cut; walls, firearms, powerful friends, copies of my work kept elsewhere). The "regency cycle and Partisan Prohibitions" window is HAPPENING NOW at year 125, which the game flagged unprompted in 'state' ("HAPPENING NOW: ..."). This is the single most surprising-in-a-good-way piece of design I've hit: it's not a generic "random event" system, it's the actual history of the setting, dated, explained, and tied concretely to mechanics (which of my techs are exposed, what fraction I'd lose, what I could build today to blunt it). I'd tell the designers this is a highlight, not just a good idea in principle - the writing quality holds up.


## Year 127-138: engineers, training, "work <trade>" at a better wage, and abandoning a project
Trained my first specialist trade via 'train engineer 1' (450 of my own hours + 177 cash, ready in 2 years) to unlock a cluster of engineer-gated techs (sc2_geometry_descriptive, sc2_notation_dimension, mfg_control_chart, mfg_sampling_plan). Discovered 'work <trade>' isn't limited to trades I "am" - once the "engineer" trade existed in society, 'work engineer <hours>' let me sell MY OWN founder-hours at the (higher) engineer wage rate (428 for 2000 hours vs ~340 for scholar), for a further net gain. Worth remembering: once a trade exists, always work the highest-waged one available.

The engineer round-trip clarified the mystery from year 111-114 (my earlier "hired staff silently vanish" note): staff loss is a REAL, modelled, random annual event ("you lose 1 engineer to death and to better offers"), not something you do - it can happen to a staff member in their very first year. This time it WAS logged clearly. So my working theory now is: it's genuine random attrition, occasionally not well-logged (the very first scholar-vanishing case had zero log line at all, this one did) - possibly because that first case triggered some other, differently-worded path. I'm noting the discrepancy rather than chasing it into the code.

Losing the engineer this way put a running project (sc2_geometry_descriptive) on a "3 years, then abandoned, everything lost" countdown. Retrained a replacement engineer in time (2 more years, 450 hours, 177 cash) and the project survived - but then, having paid off the engineer's HIRED LABOUR requirement (0% owed on that line), I fired the engineer again to stop the 410/yr wage bleed, assuming the project no longer needed them since 'why' said "waiting on: money" (not "waiting on: engineer"). It did: firing immediately reissued "sc2_geometry_descriptive cannot go on: no engineer here", contradicting what "waiting on: money" alone would suggest. Lesson: 'why'/'state' only ever surface the SINGLE current bottleneck, not the whole live set of things a project still depends on - you can safely remove what looks like a satisfied requirement and discover it wasn't. This is worth flagging to the designers as a genuine surprise, not obviously "working as intended" from the player's seat, even if it turns out to be deliberate (an ongoing engineer might genuinely be needed to keep "absorbing" the yearly cash instalment, which is plausible in hindsight but was never stated).

Given the project's own 'why' said "HOW MUCH RESTS ON THIS: nothing else; this is worth having for itself" (i.e. not on any critical path I can see, just a standalone unlock), and it had cost me two separate engineer-training cycles and repeated debt spirals to chase, I cut losses with 'stop sc2_geometry_descriptive' - lost the sunk hours, kept the 96 cash already paid "to my credit ... if you begin again". Good, honest wording on what stopping actually preserves.

Overall running theme by year 138: my single biggest recurring mistake is committing to something (a hire, a training, an expensive project) right when cash is already tight, rather than banking a cushion first. The game is quite forgiving of this (arrears just slow things down and add interest, they don't end the run) but it costs a lot of wasted years grinding 'work' to dig back out. A newcomer's natural instinct - "this looks good, start it" - needs to be tempered hard against "can I actually carry this AND its ongoing costs for the next several years," which nothing in the UI surfaces as a single combined check before you commit.


## Year 143-144: a few more quick mechanics before stopping
- 'score' shows a live, weighted breakdown even mid-run ("provisional - what you would be optimising if you reached [goal]"): literacy, workforce, economy, institutions, resilience, standing, and a withheld technology-coverage share. At year 143 my workforce component was flat 0 (I've fired every hire to control costs, so I have no standing employees at all right now) - a concrete signal that the debt-averse "solo founder working every idle hour" strategy I fell into has a real opportunity cost against the scoring model, not just a vibe.
- 'quote mine coal 500' previewed a mining venture without committing: sink cost, annual holding cost, years to first output, affordability, and geological ceiling, plus a warning that mothballing a mine is not a free reverse (floods, crew disperses, 85% of book price to reopen). Nice, honest cost preview.
- Confirmed the "one thing at a time, leave a cash cushion first" strategy is stable once actually followed: picked civ_arch_roman (276.9 cash, "some" rests on it) with a healthy 679-cash buffer built up first via 'work', and it completed cleanly on the first attempt with no debt at all.
