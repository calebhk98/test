# Module 60: Mathematics, Physics and the Scientific Method

This is the highest return-on-effort module in the entire corpus. Every
other module describes a machine, a material, or a process: it helps the
people who build that specific thing, in that specific place, for as long
as that copy survives. This module describes how to think and how to
teach, and its output is not a machine but a multiplier applied to every
hour spent on every other module, by everyone who ever studies this
corpus, for as long as the teaching lineage survives. It costs almost
nothing in material terms (wax tablets, papyrus, your own and your
students' hours) and it is the one investment whose payoff compounds
across generations instead of depreciating like a tool. Write it first, or
as close to first as the sequencing below allows.

Two honesty notes. First, "invention" here mostly means "transmission
ahead of schedule." Almost everything below has real roots already in
100 AD: an abacus, Euclid, Archimedes, Hipparchus, Lucretius, Hero of
Alexandria, and a genuine Roman contemporary, Menelaus of Alexandria, doing
live spherical trigonometry. You are compressing timelines, not conjuring
things from nothing, and each entry states how far the compression
reaches. Second, several entries (scientific method, education) are social
engineering, not physics. The physics and math below are HIGH confidence,
textbook material that already built the modern world. The social strategy
for getting Romans to accept it is a genuine judgment call, and each entry
says so.

### arithmetic_positional - place-value numbers, zero, negatives, decimals (no Latin or Greek term exists; this notation has not been invented anywhere your students can reach in 100 AD)

**What it is / why you want it.** Ten digit-shapes, including one for "an
empty column," used so a digit's position tells you its size, letting
anyone add, subtract, multiply, and divide long numbers by a fixed written
procedure. It also gives negative numbers and decimal fractions written
the same way as whole numbers.

**Why you would never guess this.** Try multiplying MCMXLVII by LXXXIII on
a tablet using Roman numerals and ordinary multiplication rules: you
cannot, there is no column to carry into, since M, C, X, L, V, I encode
value but never position. Romans already solve this on an abacus
(*calculi* on a grooved board), whose columns are already positional; the
numerals are a recording notation for a finished answer, never a working
notation. The step you are taking is writing down what the abacus columns
already do, including a mark for an empty column, so the written form
becomes calculable on its own.

**Prerequisites.** Literacy; the numerate habits any Roman already has from
coinage and land measurement.

**What Roman thought already provides.** The abacus and hand-counting
board, already daily equipment for merchants and tax collectors. Roman
coinage (*as*, *sestertius*, *denarius*) is already a mixed-base system in
practice, a bridge to "value depends on which column." Babylonian
sexagesimal fractions already circulate in the live astronomical tradition,
so fractional place value is not a foreign idea, only its base and its
universal use are new. For skeptics, present the nine digit-shapes as
recovered from Indian sources: this is not even a lie, the underlying
Brahmi numerals are already centuries old, and Brahmagupta's full positional
system with zero is five hundred years in your future, drawing on a still
older tradition.

**Teaching sequence.** (1) Demonstrate addition and subtraction on the
abacus the student already trusts. (2) Introduce the nine digit shapes plus
a tenth for "no counters here," framed mechanically, not philosophically,
to dodge arguments about the void. (3) Map abacus columns onto written
digits, right to left. (4) Drill written addition and subtraction as a
direct mirror of abacus moves. (5) Teach multiplication by the lattice
(grid) method, self-checking and easy to verify cell by cell. (6) Teach
long division as the mirror procedure. (7) Extend columns past the units
place, marked by a point, for decimal fractions. (8) Introduce negative
numbers through a ledger a Roman banker (*argentarius*) already half-uses:
owed versus held, with a mark distinguishing the two.

**How you know it has been transmitted.** Two independent students, one
using the written procedure alone, one using an abacus, multiply the same
four-digit pair without consulting each other mid-calculation. The answers
must agree, repeated across several pairs, and the written method should
win on speed once fluent.

**Failure modes.** Students argue the zero-mark's philosophical meaning
instead of using it mechanically. Students write final answers back in
Roman numerals from habit, defeating the benefit for later calculation.
Similar-looking digit shapes cause copying errors under fast wax-tablet
writing; drill a standardized hand before arithmetic begins.

**Cost & labour.** ESTIMATED (basis: modern curricula reach four-operation
fluency in roughly 200-300 classroom hours; an adult already numerate via
coinage and abacus should need less): 80-150 personal hours for the four
operations and decimals, plus roughly 20 more for negative numbers. Cost
is wax tablets and a stylus.

**Danger.** None physical. Socially trivial, at worst dismissed as a
foreign merchant's trick.

**Confidence: HIGH.** Notation and its superiority for calculation are not
in question; only labour-hour figures are estimated.

### algebra_symbolic - symbols for unknowns, the equals sign, systematic equation-solving (no term exists; "algebra" is an Arabic loanword, centuries in your future)

**What it is / why you want it.** A letter for an unknown, a symbol for
"equals," and a fixed set of legal moves (do the same thing to both sides)
that turn a stated problem into a solved one mechanically.

**Why you would never guess this.** In the history you remember, Diophantus
reaches "syncopated" algebra (abbreviations, not full symbols) around
250 AD, and full symbolic algebra with an equals sign waits for Viete
(1591) and Descartes (1637), roughly 1350-1400 years past even Diophantus.
The leap is treating an unknown as an object you manipulate by rule rather
than a missing number hunted by trial or proportion, which is how every
ancient tradition, Roman included, actually solves "find the number."

**Prerequisites.** arithmetic_positional.

**What Roman thought already provides.** Euclid's *Elements*, already
complete (circa 300 BC), contains "geometric algebra" in Book II, area
proofs that are exactly our algebraic identities proved by cutting up
rectangles, and a second toolkit in Book V's theory of proportion. Hero of
Alexandria, a real contemporary in 100 AD, routinely solves "find the
unknown" problems in his *Metrica* using Greek prose with an abbreviation
for the unknown, a genuine live rhetorical-algebra tradition. The logic
already exists; you are introducing compressed notation for logic Romans
already accept.

**Teaching sequence.** (1) Translate a known Book II proof into symbolic
form, showing it says the same thing. (2) Introduce a single mark for the
unknown; use a Greek letter, never I, V, X, L, C, D, M, which already mean
fixed numbers. (3) Introduce a new mark, two short parallel strokes, for
"equals," stated plainly as a new invention, not a recovered ancient
symbol. (4) Teach the balance-scale metaphor using a real *statera*.
(5) Progress from a single linear equation to unknowns on both sides, to
quadratics via Book II's method in symbols, to two simultaneous linear
equations by substitution. (6) Defer cubics and quartics; if ever wanted,
note Cardano's 1545 solution as the further jump required.

**How you know it has been transmitted.** A student solves a linear
equation with the unknown on both sides purely symbolically and checks by
substitution. Separately, a student sets up and solves a Roman
inheritance-division problem as an equation and compares against the old
rhetorical solution.

**Failure modes.** Students revert to descriptive prose mid-solution under
pressure, losing the compression benefit. A symbol resembling a numeral
causes real reading errors.

**Cost & labour.** ESTIMATED, similar order to arithmetic_positional but
higher: 100-180 personal hours to fluency with linear and simple quadratic
equations, less if arithmetic_positional is already solid.

**Danger.** Minimal; at worst dismissed as an abstract puzzle, nowhere
near as fraught as anything touching cosmology.

**Confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH.** Logic is solid (HIGH); symbol choices are
your own invention rather than an attested fastest path, so the sequence
and labour estimate carry more judgment.

### geometry_analytic - coordinates and trigonometric tables (*geometria* already exists generally; the coordinate-algebra fusion and true sine tables do not)

**What it is / why you want it.** Describing any point by two signed
distances from perpendicular reference lines, so a curve becomes the set
of points satisfying an algebraic equation, plus trigonometric tables
relating an angle directly to a ratio of sides.

**Why you would never guess this.** Coordinates look obvious only in
hindsight. The real step, why it waited for Descartes in 1637 even in a
Europe with strong algebra and geometry separately, is realizing an
algebraic relation between two numbers can BE a shape, letting each
field's toolkit attack the other's problems.

**Prerequisites.** algebra_symbolic, arithmetic_positional, standard
Euclidean geometry.

**What Roman thought already provides.** Roman surveyors (*agrimensores*,
per the *Corpus Agrimensorum*) already lay out land on a grid referenced
from two perpendicular baselines (*decumanus* and *cardo*), using a
purpose-built instrument, the *groma*, for right angles, already daily
provincial practice. Hipparchus already built a chord table for astronomy,
and Menelaus of Alexandria, an actual contemporary active around 100 AD, is
doing spherical trigonometry right now; Ptolemy's chord-table synthesis
follows within decades. Trigonometric tables land on prepared ground.

**Teaching sequence.** (1) Start from centuriation practice: perpendicular
reference lines, distances from each. (2) Formalize as signed coordinate
axes, using negative numbers already taught. (3) Show a straight boundary's
points satisfy a linear equation. (4) Extend to the circle equation via
Euclid I.47, already known. (5) Build a chord table in the Hipparchus
style, then relabel it as the more usable sine (half-chord of double the
angle), presented as repackaging the existing tradition. (6) Fill in
intermediate values by bisection and addition/half-angle relations from
Euclidean geometry. (7) Adopt the 360-division convention already standard
in the live astronomical tradition. (8) Publish the combined toolkit for
surveying, artillery, and astronomy together.

**How you know it has been transmitted.** An apprentice computes distance
and bearing between two points from coordinates alone, then lays the same
line out physically with groma and cord; agreement within a stated
tolerance (ESTIMATED from the surveyors' own attainable precision, not
invented here) over a stated baseline confirms transmission.

**Failure modes.** Sign errors across a reference line; angle-unit
confusion without consistent drilling; confusing the older chord table
with the new sine table when consulting older records.

**Cost & labour.** ESTIMATED, roughly 60-100 hours to coordinate fluency;
a first useful trigonometric table needs several hundred team
computation-hours, comparable to the labour discussed under logarithms.

**Danger.** Essentially none; reads as an extension of respected surveying
and astronomical craft.

**Confidence: HIGH**, given the direct mapping onto centuriation and the
live contemporary chord-table tradition.

### logarithms - tables that turn multiplication into addition, and the slide rule that reads them cheaply (no term exists)

**What it is / why you want it.** A table pairing each number with an
"exponent" such that multiplying two numbers reduces to adding their
exponents and looking up the sum. Once built, two rulers marked with a
matching non-uniform scale, slid against each other, multiply and divide
by mechanical addition of lengths, with no lookup needed per use.

**Why you would never guess this.** The insight that an arithmetic
progression paired against a geometric one can replace multiplication with
addition is deep, and John Napier reached it only after decades of solitary
computation before publishing in 1614.

**Prerequisites.** arithmetic_positional; algebra_symbolic helps but is not
required for the doubling-table method below.

**What Roman thought already provides.** No direct precedent for the
trick, but the discipline of laborious, checked hand tables is thoroughly
familiar from Hipparchus's chord tables and from compound-interest
calculation, already done step by step by Roman bankers, itself a
geometric progression computed the slow way.

**Teaching sequence.** (1) Demonstrate the core identity with a small hand
table of a geometric progression close to 1 (repeated multiplication by
1.0001), paired against its step count, checkable by direct multiplication.
(2) Show multiplication reduces to finding two step-counts, adding, and
looking up the sum. (3) State the real labour honestly: Napier's roughly
two decades (MEASURED, cited for calibration) and Henry Briggs's later
team recomputation to base ten; this is multi-person, multi-year work.
(4) Organize a computing office: divide the number range into blocks, have
independent pairs compute each block separately, compare, flag
disagreements for a third check. (5) Fix a stated decimal precision
(five digits is plausible) and hold every entry to it. (6) Once the table
exists, mark rulers with position proportional to logarithm, giving a
cheap, table-free multiplication device.

**How you know it has been transmitted.** A trained computer's table
lookup and a second computer's direct long multiplication agree within the
table's stated precision; a slide-rule user reads a product within a
stated tolerance faster than hand multiplication.

**Failure modes.** Interpolation error between entries; accumulated
rounding in long chains; misreading the compressed high end of the scale;
forgetting the slide rule gives only significant digits, not magnitude.

**Cost & labour.** Napier's roughly twenty years is MEASURED, a real
historical figure used only for calibration. A Roman-scale project (a
coarser five-digit table, a trained team of ten to twenty computers using
the shortcuts above) is ESTIMATED at two to four years of team labour,
loosely by analogy to Briggs's team's throughput in the 1620s; revise
against real observed throughput once running.

**Danger.** No physical danger; the real risk is opportunity cost, this
locks up many literate people for years. Treat it as a deliberate one-time
capital investment.

**Confidence: MEDIUM.** Mathematics and payoff are HIGH confidence; the
labour estimate for a Roman team's throughput is a clearly flagged guess.

### calculus - limits, derivatives, integrals (*calculus* already exists as a Latin word for a counting pebble, a pleasant accident, not a claim Romans have the mathematics)

**What it is / why you want it.** Rigorous handling of instantaneous rate
of change (the derivative) and accumulated quantity (the integral), plus
the fundamental theorem showing the two undo each other.

**Why you would never guess this.** The kernel is that infinitely many
infinitely small pieces can be handled rigorously through the limit
concept rather than waved past as paradox. This took genius-level insight
from Newton and Leibniz even in a Europe that already had symbolic algebra
and coordinate geometry.

**Prerequisites.** algebra_symbolic, geometry_analytic.

**What Roman thought already provides.** Archimedes' method of exhaustion
(*On the Measurement of the Circle*, *On the Sphere and Cylinder*, *On
Conoids and Spheroids*, circa 250 BC) already computes exact areas and
volumes by bounding a shape above and below with ever-finer known shapes
and closing the gap by contradiction, the genuine historical ancestor of
the integral, done rigorously, by a figure any educated Roman already
respects. Archimedes' own *Method* additionally describes his private
heuristic of weighing infinitesimal strips on a mental balance scale
against a known shape before proving the result rigorously. Present
calculus explicitly as finishing what Archimedes started: he had the
mechanical intuition and the rigorous method per shape, but lacked the
coordinate algebra to make either general.

**Teaching sequence.** (1) Re-derive Archimedes' own parabolic-segment
area using exhaustion, on ground the student half-knows. (2) Present the
balance-scale heuristic from the *Method* as the intuitive seed, using a
real balance. (3) Formalize the limit using Archimedes' own phrasing,
"smaller than any assigned quantity," rather than modern notation.
(4) Define the derivative as the slope of the line through (x, f(x)) and
(x+h, f(x+h)) as h shrinks, landing on the power rule by binomial
expansion, itself accessible via figured-number patterns already known.
(5) Define the integral as the limit of an Archimedes-style exhaustion
sum, for polynomials first. (6) Prove the fundamental theorem for
polynomial cases using coordinate tools, narrated as closing the loop
Archimedes could not close. (7) Apply to a checkable case: compute an
amphora's volume by integration, check against a water-fill measurement.

**How you know it has been transmitted.** A student derives a parabola's
area both by exhaustion and by the integral and gets matching answers.
Separately, a derivative-computed instantaneous speed (tying to
newtonian_mechanics) matches the trend of shrinking-interval average
speeds measured directly.

**Failure modes.** Treating "infinitesimal" as a literal fixed tiny number
leads to division-by-zero contradictions; conflating average and
instantaneous rate; sign errors for area below the axis.

**Cost & labour.** ESTIMATED: modern first-year calculus runs roughly
100-150 contact hours for a student who already has algebra and coordinate
geometry; a Roman scholar without existing textbooks to draw on should
plan for more, 300-600 personal study hours to reach fluency with
polynomial differentiation and integration.

**Danger.** None physical; near-neutral on its own, read as advanced
geometry. The real social cost arrives only when applied against
Aristotelian physics (see newtonian_mechanics).

**Confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH.** Mathematics is HIGH confidence; the
Archimedes bridge and labour estimate are judgment calls.

### scientific_method - controlled experiment, hypothesis, replication, publication, peer criticism (no term exists as a unified concept)

**What it is / why you want it.** State a hypothesis and what would prove
it wrong before testing; vary one factor while holding others fixed; compare
against an untreated control group; have an independent party repeat the
test; publish the method and results; treat public correction of your own
claims as normal, not shameful.

**Why you would never guess this.** The kernel is not "test things,"
Romans already test things practically. The invention is the
INSTITUTIONAL package: pre-registering an expectation before looking,
deliberately trying to disprove your own idea, and building a norm where
contradicting a famous man's claim with evidence is honorable, not
insolent. That social technology, not the logic, is the hard part.

**Prerequisites.** statistics_basic helps directly; publication depends on
notation_and_writing; building a community that can do peer criticism
depends on education_pedagogy.

**What Roman thought already provides.** Roman siege engineers already
test and adjust artillery. Columella's *De Re Rustica* already advises
comparing seed varieties and methods in the field, a real ancestor of the
controlled trial. Greek medicine's Empiric school, active into the Roman
period, already argues clinical experience should outrank theoretical
reasoning about hidden causes, against the Dogmatist school; the Empirics
are natural first allies, since their whole position is already "test, don't
theorize."

**The obstacle, stated plainly.** Aristotelian authority: natural
philosophy in 100 AD is a settled, prestigious literary tradition, and
disagreeing with Aristotle (say, that heavier objects fall faster) reads as
ignorance of the classics, not as evidence. Elite contempt for manual work:
Cicero, *De Officiis* 1.150-151, ranks hired labour, retail trade, and
craft work as *sordidus*, unbecoming a free man, allowing only large-scale
agriculture and grudgingly commerce as respectable; Seneca, *Epistulae
Morales* 90, separately mocks crediting philosophers with inventing
practical devices, insisting the wise man's business is virtue, not
gadgetry. Together, the people with leisure and standing to do research
consider hands-on work beneath them, and the people who do it, slaves and
artisans, are not considered capable of producing knowledge worth citing.
No institution rewards truth over eloquence: Roman higher education trains
persuasion, not self-correction, and there is no journal, no priority
dispute mechanism, no replication culture, no research post independent of
a patron's favor.

**Tactics, one per obstacle.** Against authority: frame new claims as
completing respected figures rather than attacking them, and open with a
dramatic, cheap, safe, repeatable demonstration where Aristotle is
verifiably wrong, two different weights dropped simultaneously hit the
ground together, from any upper floor. Against contempt for manual work:
train freedmen and slaves as the actual experimenters, publish under a
sponsoring patron's name where needed for a hearing while privately keeping
an accurate authorship record for later correction, and build a parallel
prestige ladder (a demonstration circuit, a research establishment
explicitly modeled on the already-existing Museum and Library of
Alexandria) that rewards verified results over birth. Against the missing
institution: build it deliberately, require a written, dated,
pre-registered prediction before any test, require independent replication
before a claim enters the corpus, and keep a dated archive including failed
submissions, so being wrong becomes routine and unremarkable rather than
rare and humiliating.

**Teaching sequence.** (1) Open with the falling-weights demonstration.
(2) Teach isolated variables via Columella's own comparative method,
formalized. (3) Require written pre-registration before results are known.
(4) Require independent replication by an unconnected team. (5) Introduce
the formal control group (below). (6) Introduce publication norms
including publishing negative results. (7) Introduce graded peer criticism
inside the school before risking it in public.

**How you know it has been transmitted.** A submitted claim carries a
pre-registered prediction, is independently replicated within a stated
tolerance, survives an assigned critique session, and is replicated again
by an outside non-participant scholar.

**Failure modes.** Post-hoc hypotheses faked as pre-registered; unequal
"control" conditions; replication by the same biased team; critique
sessions degrading into status contests; patrons suppressing inconvenient
results.

**Cost & labour.** ESTIMATED, mostly institutional: the demonstration
itself costs hours; a durable school-wide norm of pre-registration and
replication realistically takes years and multiple cohorts, ESTIMATED as
multi-decade infrastructure, loosely by analogy to how long the Royal
Society's own norms took to stabilize after 1660.

**Danger.** Real and specific: publicly contradicting Aristotle can read
as arrogance or impiety; going further, into astrology or the emperor, is
sharper still (see "What NOT to teach"). Keep early demonstrations safe
and narrowly technical, and frame generously ("Aristotle was refining an
unfinished picture," never "a fool").

**Confidence: MEDIUM.** The logic is correct epistemology, HIGH on its own
terms; the institutional engineering against Ciceronian and Senecan class
prejudice is a genuine gamble with no guaranteed playbook, the highest
uncertainty entry in the module alongside education_pedagogy.

### statistics_basic - mean, variance, sampling, the control group (no term exists)

**What it is / why you want it.** The mean, variance (typical scatter
around the mean), sampling (why a properly chosen subset stands in for a
population), and the control group as applied practice.

**Why you would never guess this.** Scatter itself is measurable and
meaningful, not noise to ignore, and comparing two GROUPS rather than two
individuals is what separates a real effect from ordinary variation
between people. Romans already compare one patient before and after; the
group-level comparison is the invention.

**Prerequisites.** arithmetic_positional; pairs with scientific_method.

**What Roman thought already provides.** The census and grain-distribution
(*annona*) rolls already collect large-scale numeric population records as
routine administration, so the raw material and habit of big datasets
already exist; summarizing and testing with them does not.

**Teaching sequence.** (1) Use a dataset Romans already keep, harvest yield
per *iugerum* across many farms, or a legion's roll. (2) Compute the mean
by hand. (3) Define variance as typical scatter, computed for a small
dataset. (4) Show sampling: a properly chosen small subset's mean tracks
the full dataset's mean, demonstrated on a case where both are available.
(5) Introduce the control group through medicine: two matched patient
groups, treat only one, compare outcomes, teaching explicitly that a
single recovery proves nothing since many patients recover regardless
(spontaneous remission). (6) State plainly why medicine specifically
cannot progress by anecdote: variable natural disease courses,
heterogeneous patients, and a hopeful physician's own bias will otherwise
manufacture false confidence in whatever was tried before an already-likely
recovery.

**How you know it has been transmitted.** A student correctly computes
mean and spread for a new dataset, and given a fabricated recovery
anecdote, correctly states what a proper control-group test would require
before belief.

**Failure modes.** Self-selected control groups; samples too small so
scatter is mistaken for effect; correlation mistaken for causation.

**Cost & labour.** ESTIMATED, modest: tens of hours once
arithmetic_positional and scientific_method exist, though hand computation
on real-sized datasets is tedious and motivates the same computing pool
used for logarithms.

**Danger.** Minimal physically; real social friction in medicine, since a
formal trial means some patients knowingly go untreated. Build credibility
first on non-fatal conditions or agricultural yield.

**Confidence: HIGH** for the mathematics; **MEDIUM** for how readily
patients and patrons accept a withheld-treatment control group.

### newtonian_mechanics - the laws of motion, universal gravitation, public prediction (*mechanica* already exists generally)

**What it is / why you want it.** Three laws (inertia; force equals mass
times acceleration; equal and opposite reaction) plus gravitation
(attraction falling off as the square of distance), letting the same rule
set predict motion from a dropped stone to a planet.

**Why you would never guess this.** The kernel is that the SAME rule
governs a falling apple and the Moon's orbit, directly against the
Aristotelian division of the cosmos into a changeable sublunar realm and a
perfect celestial one. That unification is the scandal.

**Prerequisites.** algebra_symbolic and geometry_analytic for the basic
laws; calculus for Newton's own general proofs, though algebra alone gives
useful predictions sooner; scientific_method, since public advance
prediction is the whole strategy.

**What Roman thought already provides.** Existing positional astronomical
data and predictive geometric models mean the observations needed to
check your predictions already exist or are being actively collected.
Roman artillery engineers, per Vitruvius Book X, already care intensely
about projectile behavior, a receptive, non-philosophical audience for
trajectory predictions regardless of any view on Aristotle.

**Teaching sequence.** (1) Establish the laws qualitatively with cheap
demonstrations (carts on a smooth track, colliding weights). (2) Add the
algebra (F = ma, constant-acceleration kinematics) for real quantitative
prediction. (3) Predict, publicly and in advance, a pendulum's period from
its length alone, cheap and repeatable, tying directly to the
seconds-pendulum length standard under units_standards. (4) Predict,
publicly and in advance, artillery range for a given projectile, and hand
the formula to ballista crews to test against their own empirical tables.
(5) Only after credibility is built, extend to gravitation: predict a
planet's future position from inverse-square gravitation plus existing
astronomical data, published years ahead and checked by the existing
astronomer community. (6) Once calculus exists, give the general
derivation, noting the simple cases above needed only algebra and
geometry.

**How you know it has been transmitted.** A published, dated prediction
(pendulum period, artillery range, planetary position) is checked
afterward by an independent party and matches within a tolerance
appropriate to the measurement.

**Failure modes.** Air resistance and friction make naive projectile
predictions systematically wrong until accounted for qualitatively;
failing to disclose this damages credibility more than an honest
approximation would.

**Cost & labour.** ESTIMATED: the pendulum rig and a controlled track are
cheap, days of carpentry. A planetary prediction needs existing
astronomical records plus substantial computation, tying into the
logarithms computing-office infrastructure, ESTIMATED at months of
dedicated work for one planet's near-term position.

**Danger.** As stated up front, this is the expensive part. Publicly
contradicting Aristotelian cosmology, taught for over four centuries by
100 AD and entangled with patron-connected philosophical schools, invites
accusations of impiety. Separately and more dangerously, any public
celestial prediction risks being reframed as astrological prophecy (see
"What NOT to teach"). Frame every celestial prediction strictly as physics
of position, never as portent, and build credibility on pendulums and
artillery before ever touching planets in public.

**Confidence: HIGH** on the physics; **MEDIUM** on the social strategy and
timeline.

### thermodynamics_theory - heat as energy, the gas laws, entropy, efficiency limits (no term exists)

**What it is / why you want it.** Heat as a form of energy rather than a
fluid; the gas laws relating pressure, volume, and temperature; entropy,
capturing that heat flows spontaneously hot to cold and never the reverse;
and the resulting hard ceiling on any heat engine's efficiency.

**Why you would never guess this.** It is entirely possible to build and
run working heat engines for decades on a wrong theory of heat, so getting
the theory right is a separate achievement from getting a machine to run;
even in the world you remember this waited for Carnot's 1824 work despite
Europe already having working steam engines.

**Prerequisites.** units_standards, not optional (see below);
arithmetic_positional, algebra_symbolic.

**Why you must have the thermometer first.** Every statement here, the gas
laws, the efficiency limit, is a relation between numbers: pressure,
volume, temperature. Without a reproducible, calibrated instrument giving
two people in two cities the same reading, "heat is energy" is a
plausible-sounding story, not physics. Build and standardize a thermometer
(a sealed graduated tube, calibrated against melting ice and boiling water
at a stated condition) and publish its calibration as part of
units_standards BEFORE writing a single gas-law number here.

**What Roman thought already provides.** Hero of Alexandria's
*Pneumatica*, a contemporary text, already documents heated air and steam
doing mechanical work (the aeolipile, temple-door mechanisms), so the raw
phenomenon of heat producing motion is already observed and monetized,
purely empirically. The hook: "you have already built machines that do
this; we can now tell you why, and the why sets the hard limit on how good
such a machine can ever become."

**Teaching sequence.** (1) Build and calibrate the thermometer first,
publish it as a units_standards artifact. (2) Demonstrate the gas laws
with a sealed volume of air and a movable piston or mercury column,
recording paired pressure-temperature and volume-temperature numbers.
(3) State the ideal gas relation as the algebraic fit to the recorded
data. (4) Introduce heat as energy: measured mechanical work (stirring in
an insulated water bath) produces a measured temperature rise, contradicting
any "caloric fluid" story. (5) Introduce entropy qualitatively: heat flows
hot to cold and never spontaneously reverses, demonstrated repeatedly.
(6) Derive the efficiency limit conceptually from the impossibility of a
self-powered heat engine. (7) Apply to Hero's own devices: predict how
efficiency should scale with temperature difference, then test.

**How you know it has been transmitted.** A student, given only measured
temperatures, correctly predicts which of two designs should be more
efficient, and a built comparison confirms the ranking even if the exact
efficiency figure is off.

**Failure modes.** Skipping calibration gives numbers that disagree
between workshops; conflating temperature (intensity) with heat quantity
(amount); assuming a hot-to-cold process can run backward for free.

**Cost & labour.** ESTIMATED: thermometer development, weeks to months of
glassblowing and testing; establishing the gas laws from scratch, months
of apparatus-building and data collection by a small team.

**Danger.** Minimal social danger, reads as engineering. Real physical
danger from sealed heated vessels, burst risk and burns; require safety
margins and observe from a safe distance.

**Confidence: MEDIUM.** Physics is HIGH confidence; the Hero bridge and
labour estimates are judgment calls.

### atomic_theory - atoms, elements, atomic weights, stoichiometry, the periodic table (*atomus*, from Greek *atomos*, already exists, used by Lucretius)

**What it is / why you want it.** Matter as atoms; an element as one
atom-type; atomic weight as relative mass against a reference;
stoichiometry as whole-number atom ratios predicting reaction quantities;
the periodic table organizing elements by recurring behavior.

**Why you would never guess this.** The kernel is not "matter is made of
tiny pieces," Roman thought already has this. It is the experimental fact
that reactions always occur in fixed, small whole-number ratios by weight
(Dalton, circa 1803), which is what forces atoms to be real, countable
objects rather than a philosophical guess with no testable content.

**Prerequisites.** statistics_basic, arithmetic_positional; an analytical
balance (see the relevant instrument-making module) before any of this is
operational rather than trivia.

**What Roman thought already provides.** Lucretius's *De Rerum Natura*, a
widely circulated Latin poem, already popularizes Epicurean and
Democritean atomism as a respectable, contested position: indivisible
particles (*atomi*, *primordia rerum*) moving through void. "Atoms exist"
is not a new idea in 100 AD, only a new idea to make quantitative.

**State plainly.** You can write the full periodic table from memory,
element names, approximate atomic weights, group and period structure, and
doing so is worth roughly a century and a half of historical chemistry,
Dalton 1803 to Mendeleev 1869 to a settled table by the early twentieth
century (ESTIMATED as a round span). But the table is inert trivia until
Romans can weigh reactants and products precisely enough to see the
ratios; the bottleneck is the balance, not the element names.

**Teaching sequence.** (1) Open from Lucretius to establish atomism is not
foreign. (2) Build or acquire an analytical balance with enough precision
to reveal clean small-integer ratios. (3) Run simple, clean reactions
Romans can already produce (metal with sulfur or a vitriol; calcining a
carbonate) and weigh reactants and products precisely. (4) Show the
weight ratios come out as small whole numbers or simple fractions,
consistently, using replication and averaging from statistics_basic to
handle balance error. (5) Build a relative atomic weight scale from
Roman-measured data, pick a reference substance, assign it a round number,
compute others' relative weights, even though you privately already know
the modern answer, so the corpus is internally justified. (6) Introduce
the periodic pattern as an empirical discovery students can partly
rediscover by sorting their own measured elements, before filling gaps
from memory. (7) Apply immediately to stoichiometric prediction: predict a
product's mass from a reactant's mass, then check.

**How you know it has been transmitted.** A student predicts product mass
from a stated reactant mass using a small whole-number ratio, runs the
reaction, and the measured mass matches within the balance's stated
precision.

**Failure modes.** Impure Roman-era reagents give "off" ratios until
purification improves, an expected limitation, not a theory failure;
insufficient balance precision produces false conclusions, meaning balance
quality, not theory, is the true rate-limiter.

**Cost & labour.** ESTIMATED: writing the table from memory, a day or two
of careful transcription. Building and validating the balance, weeks to
months of skilled instrument-making. Establishing relative weights for a
first dozen elements with replication, months to a few years of dedicated
lab work.

**Danger.** Real physical danger: toxic fumes (mercury, arsenic, sulfur
compounds), corrosive vitriols, heat; require ventilation and explicit
per-reaction warnings, detailed in the individual chemistry modules. Social
danger minimal.

**Confidence: HIGH** that the theory is correct and the table can be
reconstructed from memory; **MEDIUM-LOW** on labour estimates for reaching
operational precision.

### units_standards - defined, published, reproducible standards for length, mass, time, temperature (*mensura* already exists generally)

**What it is / why you want it.** A formally defined, published standard
for each quantity, backed by both a physical artifact and a
natural-phenomenon method for rebuilding it from scratch if every artifact
is lost.

**Why you would never guess this.** A number is worthless across time and
distance without a fixed reference. Romans already have local units, the
*pes*, the *libra*, but these vary by city and period and are defined only
by a physical object kept in one place, with no natural-phenomenon backup.
You are adding the layer Roman units never had: a written recipe for
remaking the standard from nature if every physical copy is destroyed.

**Prerequisites.** None technical; this is why it belongs in the FIRST
year, before any other number in the corpus means anything to a reader in
another province or century.

**What Roman thought already provides.** Existing units to anchor
immediate usability, the *pes* and *libra*, and existing water-clock
craft, so this only fixes one precise reference version of each and adds a
nature-based backup, not a replacement.

**Argue the first-year priority.** Every later module states masses,
ratios, temperatures, times. If those numbers are only meaningful relative
to a *libra* that drifts by province and century, no later reader can
reliably reproduce a recipe. The cost of doing this first is weeks, not
years, and it is the one piece of infrastructure every other module
depends on, arguably the single highest-leverage first move in the corpus,
even ahead of arithmetic_positional in strict dependency terms, though
arithmetic is what makes recording the standards' values usable.

**Concrete proposal.** Length: define it via a seconds pendulum (the
length whose full swing takes one defined second, at a stated reference
latitude, Rome itself), giving a from-nature recipe reproducible anywhere:
build a pendulum, time it against a stated fraction of a day, adjust length
until the period matches. Cast a bronze artifact bar (durable, corrosion
resistant, well-understood casting), a small number of exact duplicates,
ESTIMATED at three to five, cast in the same pour to minimize copy error.
Mass: define it as the mass of a stated water volume (itself defined via
the length standard) at a reproducible reference condition, ice just
visibly floating and not yet melted, usable before any thermometer exists;
cast a matching bronze mass artifact. Time: define the second as a fixed
fraction of the mean solar day (a definition, needing no ESTIMATED flag
itself, only the water clock's realized precision), calibrated against
day-length observations averaged over a stated period to smooth real
seasonal variation, an honestly flagged imprecision; publish this
definition first, since length depends on it. Temperature: defer the full
numeric scale to the thermometer module, but publish the two fixed points
now, melting ice and boiling water at a stated condition, noting boiling
point shifts with elevation and weather. Deposit one full set in Rome
(propose the Temple of Saturn, already the state treasury) and a second
full set in a genuinely distant city (propose Alexandria, for its existing
scholarly institutions), so loss of one set does not erase the standard and
the two cities can cross-check. Publish the whole definition as the first
document in the corpus, per notation_and_writing's rules, since every
other module implicitly cites it.

**How you know it has been transmitted.** An independent team elsewhere,
given only the written procedure, builds its own pendulum and water-mass
sample, and the reproduction agrees with the deposited artifact within a
stated, small tolerance, neither team having handled the original.

**Failure modes.** Local gravity varies with latitude and elevation, a
pendulum built far from Rome's latitude drifts slightly if the reference
latitude is ignored, a known, small, flagged error. Water purity affects
mass precision. Day-length averaged over too short a period disagrees with
a full-year average.

**Cost & labour.** ESTIMATED: design and first casting, days to a couple
of weeks of skilled bronzework. A robust year-averaged time standard
inherently takes a year, though a usable provisional version publishes
within weeks. Overall: a first usable published package within a single
year by one dedicated person plus artisan support.

**Danger.** Essentially none physically. Mild social risk of a private
individual presuming to declare official standards, mitigated by seeking a
patron's or magistrate's sponsorship for the deposit as a benefaction
(*euergetism*), a well-understood Roman social form.

**Confidence: HIGH** on the method itself; **MEDIUM** on the specific
labour and timeline estimates, explicitly flagged.

### education_pedagogy - how to actually teach all of this to Romans (*disciplina*, *paedagogia* already exist generally)

**What it is / why you want it.** Curriculum order, apprenticeship versus
lecture, textbook design, and examination, the concrete system for
teaching everything above reliably across generations.

**Why you would never guess this.** Your most valuable students will not
be aristocrats with leisure for a philosopher's lectures, but slaves and
freedmen already in trades that reward precision (surveying, accounting,
glassblowing, medicine). Roman elite education optimizes for rhetoric and
status display, not competence, while the servile and freedman population
has real economic motive and, crucially, no status to protect by accepting
correction, better raw material for the norm that being wrong in public is
fine.

**Prerequisites.** notation_and_writing (textbooks need to be written, or
close to it, first); scientific_method (for examination design).

**Concrete curriculum order.** units_standards and arithmetic_positional
first, since nothing else can be stated precisely without them; then
geometry_analytic and algebra_symbolic together; then statistics_basic and
scientific_method together; then logarithms once arithmetic is solid; then
calculus and newtonian_mechanics as the advanced track;
thermodynamics_theory and atomic_theory in parallel once their instrument
prerequisites (thermometer, balance) are ready.

**Apprenticeship versus lecture.** Weight toward apprenticeship, since Rome
already has a strong apprenticeship tradition to graft onto directly,
whereas a lecture-hall rhetoric format trains talking about a subject, not
doing it. Mornings: apprenticeship-style problem sets under a more advanced
student, small groups, immediate correction. Afternoons: shorter lecture
and demonstration sessions for new material and public demonstrations.

**The status problem, stated plainly.** A slave's or freedman's discoveries
may not be believed regardless of correctness; pair training with a
patronage structure, a respected free citizen who directly benefits (an
engineer's patron who profits from better aqueducts) sponsors and vouches
for demonstrations publicly, while the corpus privately records true
authorship for later correction. Manumission should be an explicit, stated
reward for defined competence milestones, a real prize Romans already
respect, and it converts your best-trained minds into free operators who
run their own workshops and propagate the system.

**Textbook design.** Cross-reference notation_and_writing for the writing
rules. Every textbook needs worked examples with real numbers,
progressively harder problem sets with separately provided answers for
self-study, explicit statements of what a student should be able to DO
after each section, and a glossary of new symbols and terms.

**Examinations.** A two-part exam mirroring the scientific method's own
logic: a written component matching the textbook's answer key, and a
demonstration component, laying out a right angle with a groma, or
replicating a stated experiment within tolerance. Include at least one
examiner the student does not study under, to catch favoritism.

**How you know it has been transmitted.** A second-generation cohort,
students taught by your first cohort of students, not by you personally,
reaches the same benchmarks on the same exams.

**Failure modes.** Drift back toward a rhetorical, lecture-only format,
the higher-status default; aristocratic students certifying without the
demonstration component; freedman graduates barred from teaching citizens
in some contexts, requiring the patronage workaround.

**Cost & labour.** ESTIMATED: a single teacher runs a cohort of roughly
ten to twenty students through the early curriculum over two to three
years; advanced tracks need further dedicated years per student.

**Danger.** Social danger centers on status friction: training slaves and
freedmen past freeborn competence can read as subversive. Mitigate through
patronage and credit structure, without challenging the status hierarchy
itself, only using its existing tools.

**Confidence: MEDIUM.** Curriculum order and apprenticeship structure are
HIGH confidence, grounded in existing Roman craft training; the social
engineering around status and credit is a genuine judgment call, among the
least certain parts of the module alongside scientific_method.

### notation_and_writing - how to write the corpus so it survives (no single term covers this)

**What it is / why you want it.** Plain declarative language, explicit
numbers with stated units, no allegory standing in for a mechanism, worked
numeric examples, diagrams, and deliberate redundancy, the rules for
writing the whole corpus so it survives transmission across centuries.

**Why you would never guess this.** The opposite strategy, deliberate
obscurity, is the historically dominant premodern choice for protecting
status or guild secrecy, and it is a proven corpus-destroyer. Alchemical
texts across a thousand years used deliberately cryptic, allegorical
language ("the green lion," "the marriage of sun and moon") to keep
outsiders out, and the well-attested result was that even the authors
often lost track of what earlier obscure recipes meant, later readers
wasted enormous effort on wrong interpretations, and centuries of real
chemical knowledge were strangled by the secrecy meant to protect it. You
are choosing the opposite on purpose, which will feel unnatural, since
guarding trade secrets is exactly what Roman guild-adjacent craft culture
already does.

**Prerequisites.** units_standards (numbers need units); education_pedagogy
(textbooks need to be usable, not merely correct in principle).

**What Roman thought already provides.** A strong existing tradition of
clear technical prose: Vitruvius's *De Architectura* and Frontinus's *De
Aquaeductu* are both genuinely plain, structured, numbered technical
writing already circulating and respected in this period, a native Latin
model to point to. Contrast directly with the allegorical tradition,
visible in some religious, mystery, and medical-magical texts, as the model
to avoid.

**Teaching sequence (the rule set itself).** (1) State every number with
its unit from units_standards; a bare number means nothing. (2) Never let
a metaphor stand as the ONLY statement of a mechanism; add one for
intuition only after the plain statement. (3) Follow every abstract rule
with at least one fully worked numeric example from start to finish.
(4) Give every procedure of more than about three steps a diagram,
cross-referenced by a figure label, since a picture survives copying
errors better than prose. (5) State the same critical fact in more than
one place and form, prose, table, worked example, so damage to one copy
does not erase the fact from the corpus. (6) Flag uncertainty with the
corpus's own convention, MEASURED, DERIVED, ESTIMATED, rather than
presenting a guess with false confidence. (7) Avoid inventing vocabulary
where an existing Latin or Greek word can be repurposed; where new terms
are unavoidable, define them on first use and keep a running glossary.

**How you know it has been transmitted.** Hand a finished module to a
reader who was not present for its writing and was not personally tutored;
they should execute a described procedure and reach the stated result
without a clarifying question. A text needing the author's presence to be
understood has failed regardless of correctness.

**Failure modes.** Writers under time pressure reach for allegory or
compressed jargon because it is faster, exactly the failure mode that
destroyed alchemical transmission. Copyists introduce numeric errors,
especially in tables, which the redundancy rule is designed to catch.

**Cost & labour.** ESTIMATED: the redundancy requirement increases
copying labour by roughly a fifth to a third versus a terse version, a
cost worth paying given how much real technical knowledge in the world you
remember was lost to exactly this failure.

**Danger.** Minimal physical danger. Plain writing may read to
rhetorically trained elites as unsophisticated, the same status prejudice
from Cicero and Seneca reappearing here. Mitigate with sponsor framing and
by pointing to Vitruvius and Frontinus as already-respected models.

**Confidence: HIGH.** Supported by clean, unambiguous evidence in both
directions, alchemy's obscurity failure and Vitruvius's and Frontinus's
plainness success.

## The multiplier argument

A machine built with 3000 hours of personal effort, a better water mill, an
improved furnace, helps the people who use that specific copy, in that
specific place, for as long as that copy physically lasts, plausibly years
to a few decades (ESTIMATED, basis: typical working lifetime of Roman-era
wooden and metal machinery before major rebuild). Its benefit does not
reach a second location or generation unless someone separately spends
further hours copying it there.

3000 hours on this module instead produces no machine at all. It produces
a multiplier applied to every future hour spent by everyone in the
programme, including every other machine-building module in the corpus.

Positional arithmetic alone plausibly cuts the time cost of any
calculation-heavy task, bookkeeping, engineering design, artillery
tables, by a large factor compared with numeral-based reckoning (DERIVED
loosely from the plain fact that Roman numerals admit no fixed written
multiplication procedure at all, while the positional method does; no
controlled measurement of the exact factor exists yet, only the
qualitative direction is certain). The scientific method, once it takes
hold, does not make one experiment better, it makes every experiment run
afterward, by everyone, self-correcting. Education_pedagogy is explicitly
a self-replication mechanism: a trained teacher trains a cohort, that
cohort trains further cohorts. Over the roughly two centuries this module
targets, and taking a generation as roughly 25-30 years (ESTIMATED,
ordinary demographic figure), that is on the order of six to eight
teaching generations of compounding reach. A single machine has no
equivalent property; it multiplies the output of whoever operates that one
copy, nothing else.

This module is nearly zero-capital, no ore, no charcoal, only teaching
hours and wax tablets, so its entire cost really is close to the 3000
hours in the question. A machine's 3000 hours also consumes real capital,
so per hour of your own effort, this module is cheaper to attempt and
carries no material failure mode, a bad batch of iron wastes ore and fuel,
a badly taught lecture just gets re-taught at no material cost.

Most directly, this module is upstream of every other module's confidence
level. Better notation means every other module's numbers survive
copying. Standard units mean every other module's numbers mean the same
thing in every province and century. The scientific method means every
other module's claims get tested and corrected rather than trusted on
authority. A machine module returns none of this. A machine is a lever;
this module is what makes every future lever, including ones not yet
designed, faster to build, better tested, and more reliably passed on.
That is the case for 3000 hours here over 3000 hours on any single
machine, and why this module belongs as close to first in the whole
programme as units_standards' own first-year argument allows.

## What NOT to teach, and why

**Astrology.** Roman law under multiple emperors treats astrologers
(*mathematici*) casting a horoscope for, or predicting the fate of, the
emperor as a capital matter under the broad treason law (*maiestas*);
repeated documented expulsions and prosecutions of astrologers from Rome
occur across the first century, and by 100 AD, under Trajan, this pattern
is already a live political hazard, not a historical curiosity. The exact
tools taught under geometry_analytic and newtonian_mechanics, planetary
position tables, predictive calculation, are trivially repurposable by a
student, rival, or informer into "casting a horoscope," even if you never
intend that use. Teach position prediction strictly as physics of where a
point of light will appear, never publish anything assigning meaning to a
celestial configuration, and train students to refuse any request to
interpret a configuration as fate or personal prediction, as a standing
rule, never a case-by-case judgment.

**Anything that reads as prophecy.** Do not predict specific future human
events, even mundane ones like a harvest's outcome, framed as fate rather
than as an openly probabilistic estimate with stated uncertainty (per
statistics_basic). Being perceived as a prophet invites competition with
state-sanctioned religious prediction (the College of Pontiffs, public
augury), reading as impiety or rivalry, and suspicion of secret power
(*maleficium*, harmful magic), its own distinct legal danger.

**Predicting the emperor's death.** The sharpest danger in the entire
corpus, and a blanket prohibition, not a case-by-case judgment. Never
calculate, publish, or privately discuss anything readable as predicting
the emperor's death or a succession, regardless of framing, astrology,
omen-reading, or even a carefully caveated actuarial argument applied to a
named living emperor. Roman audiences under social pressure will not
reliably distinguish a statistical caveat from a prophecy, and under the
*maiestas* tradition even indirect or private predictions about an
emperor's fate have historically triggered prosecution. A student pressured
into "just this once" is exactly the failure mode that gets someone
executed, so the rule is absolute, stated plainly in the school's own
written code, with no authorized exception for any teacher or student to
grant.
