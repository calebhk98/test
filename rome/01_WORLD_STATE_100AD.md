# 01 - World State: The Roman Empire, c. 100 AD

*Read this before you plan anything. Half of all bootstrap plans fail because
they assume Rome has something it does not, or laboriously reinvent something
Rome already sells in a market stall.*

Every factual claim below carries a confidence tag:
**[A]** well attested / textbook, **[B]** probable, scholarly consensus but
contested in detail, **[C]** my estimate or inference, treat as a working number.

---

## 1. When and where you are

- **Year:** c. 100 AD. **Emperor:** Trajan (r. 98-117), at the start of a reign
  that is about to be the territorial and fiscal high-water mark of the Empire. **[A]**
- **This is the best possible arrival window.** The Pax Romana is real, the
  currency is still sound, the road and sea network is at maximum extent, the
  state is solvent and building, and Trajan is personally an engineer-emperor
  who funds enormous public works (Forum of Trajan, Portus, Via Traiana, the
  Danube bridge at Drobeta). An emperor who will pay Apollodorus of Damascus to
  bridge the Danube will pay you. **[A]**
- **Population:** roughly 60-70 million in the Empire; Rome itself c. 1 million.
  Alexandria and Antioch c. 200,000-500,000 each. **[B]**
- **Your clock is running.** Three dated catastrophes are coming and they define
  the whole strategy:
  | Event | Date | Effect on you |
  |---|---|---|
  | Antonine Plague | 165-180 AD | Kills perhaps 10-25% of the Empire. Will gut any workshop and any school. **[B]** |
  | Plague of Cyprian | 249-262 AD | Same again. **[B]** |
  | Crisis of the Third Century | 235-284 AD | Currency collapse, ~50 emperors, civil war, invasion, breakdown of long-distance trade. This is the event that destroys institutions. **[A]** |
  | Currency debasement | begins under Nero, accelerates from Commodus, catastrophic after 230 | Any wealth stored as coin evaporates. **[A]** |
  You know these dates and nobody else does. That foreknowledge is worth more
  than any single machine you can build. See `03_SOCIAL_POLITICS.md`.

## 2. What Rome ALREADY HAS (do not waste a single hour reinventing these)

**Materials and chemistry**
- Glassblowing, including deliberately decolourised near-colourless glass.
  Roman glass is genuinely good and cheap. **[A]**
- Hydraulic concrete (*opus caementicium*) with Puteoli pozzolana, better in
  seawater than 19th-century Portland cement. **[A]**
- Lime, gypsum plaster, alum (Melos), natron (Wadi Natrun), sulfur, pyrolusite
  (MnO2, used as glass decolouriser), cinnabar and metallic mercury (Almadén,
  an imperial monopoly), litharge, red lead, white lead, verdigris, orpiment
  and realgar (arsenic sulfides), stibnite, borax-free but boric acid springs
  exist in Tuscany. **[A/B]**
- Vitriols: *chalcanthum* (copper sulfate), *misy*, *sory*, *melanteria*
  (iron sulfates and mixed weathering products of pyrite). They have the
  feedstock for sulfuric acid and do not know what it is for. **[A]**
- Brass, by cementation of copper with calamine (they never see zinc metal). **[A]**
- Soap exists as a Gallic/Germanic hair pomade (*sapo*, Pliny NH 28.191) and is
  **not** used as a cleaning agent. Fullers use urine and fuller's earth. **[A]**

**Metals and machines**
- Bloomery iron on a large scale (Noricum steel is famous), case-hardening,
  quenching, pattern welding, soldering, brazing, lost-wax and sand casting of
  bronze. **[A]**
- Water mills, including the Barbegal complex of 16 overshot wheels. Undershot,
  overshot and (later) horizontal wheels. **[A]**
- The screw, the screw press, the Archimedes screw, force pumps with valves,
  siphons, geared mechanisms of astonishing quality (the Antikythera mechanism
  is ~150-100 BC and its gear-cutting is better than anything in Europe until
  the 14th century). **[A]**
- The lathe (bow and pole driven), the drill, the file, the saw, the plane. **[A]**
- Cranes with compound pulleys and treadwheels, lifting tens of tonnes. **[A]**
- Surveying instruments: the groma, the chorobates, the dioptra. Hero of
  Alexandria's *Dioptra* and *Pneumatica* are roughly contemporary. **[A]**

**Institutions**
- Roads, a state post (*cursus publicus*), a unified currency, a common legal
  system, sea lanes, banking and credit (*argentarii*), contracts, and the
  *collegia* (trade associations). Alexandria has a functioning research
  institution, the Mouseion and Library. Literacy among the elite is high.
  Greek is the language of learning. **[A]**

## 3. What Rome DOES NOT HAVE (the actual gap list)

This is the list that matters. Ranked roughly by how badly you will want it.

| Missing thing | Why it matters | Difficulty for you |
|---|---|---|
| **Potassium nitrate (saltpetre)** | Gate on nitric acid, gunpowder, most energetics. Roman *nitrum* is **sodium carbonate**, not saltpetre. Nitre beds take 12-24 months, so start in month one. | Low tech, long lead time |
| **Zinc metal** | Gate on the voltaic pile, hence on all electricity. Zinc boils (907 C) below the temperature needed to reduce it, so it escapes and reburns; you need a sealed downward-condensing retort. | Medium |
| **Cast iron / blast furnace** | Rome uses bloomeries only. China has had cast iron for 500 years by now. Tall shaft + water-blown continuous blast + limestone flux. Biggest single metallurgical jump available. | Medium |
| **Mineral acids** | Sulfuric, nitric, hydrochloric, hydrofluoric. Nothing in chemistry happens without them. | Medium |
| **The concept of zero and positional notation** | Roman numerals make arithmetic a specialist craft. This costs you nothing but teaching hours and multiplies everything. | Trivial cost, huge payoff |
| **Symbolic algebra, the equals sign, decimal fractions, logarithms, calculus** | Same argument. You carry all of it in your head. | Trivial cost, huge payoff |
| **The controlled experiment and quantitative method** | Rome has natural philosophy but not systematic experiment. Aristotle's authority actively suppresses measurement. | Cultural, hard |
| **Paper** | Cai Lun's paper is 105 AD in China and will not reach the West for centuries. Papyrus is an Egyptian monopoly and parchment is dear. Rag paper from linen is easy and is the substrate of printing and of knowledge redundancy. | Low |
| **Printing** | Movable type needs paper, oil-based ink, a screw press (Rome has the press!) and punchcutting. | Low-medium |
| **Horse collar, whippletree, nailed horseshoe, stirrup** | Free draught power and free cavalry. Politically safe, instantly popular. | Trivial |
| **Crank and connecting rod** | Not attested in the Roman world until the Hierapolis sawmill relief, c. 3rd century. Converts rotary to reciprocating; unlocks sawmills, trip hammers, pumps, and eventually engines. | Trivial |
| **The drawplate** | Rome makes wire by cutting strip and swaging it. Drawn wire is cheaper, longer and rounder, and every electrical device needs kilometres of it. | Trivial |
| **Distilled spirits and an efficient condenser** | Alexandrian alchemists have crude stills; nobody has the worm condenser. Alcohol is a solvent, a medicine, an antiseptic and a luxury with an obscene margin. | Low |
| **Germ theory, antisepsis, variolation** | Will save more Roman lives than every machine in this document combined, and will save *your institution* from the Antonine Plague. | Trivial cost |
| **Precision as a concept** | No surface plate, no lead screw, no micrometer, no interchangeable parts, no tolerance. This, not any single invention, is the real wall between Rome and machinery. | Medium, and unavoidable |

**Materials you simply cannot get, at any price:**
natural rubber, gutta percha, quinine (cinchona), Chile saltpetre, potato,
maize, tomato, tobacco, vanilla, cocoa, platinum in usable quantity.
The Americas are out of reach and will stay out of reach for most of your life. **[A]**

**Materials reachable but ruinously expensive** (Silk Road / Indian Ocean trade,
both fully functional in 100 AD via Palmyra, and via Berenice-Muziris with the
monsoon route): silk, lac/shellac, camphor, cassia, indigo, wootz steel ingots,
pepper, Chinese iron goods. Budget 10-100x local prices and 1-3 year lead
times. **[B]** Shellac matters: it is your only good wire varnish for a long time.

## 5. Money and prices

**Coinage [A]**
- 1 *aureus* = 25 *denarii* = 100 *sestertii* (HS) = 400 *asses*.
- Denarius at 100 AD: c. 3.4 g at roughly 93% fine, so c. 3.15 g fine silver.
  Trajan reduces the fineness to about 89% in his reform of 107 AD, so this
  number is already sliding while you watch. **[B]**
- Aureus: 1/45 of a Roman pound, c. 7.27 g, worth 25 denarii. Implied
  gold:silver ratio about 11:1. **[A]**

**Wages [B]**
| Role | Per day | Per year (250 working days) |
|---|---|---|
| Unskilled labourer | 2-4 HS | 125-250 den |
| Skilled artisan (smith, carpenter) | 4-8 HS | 250-500 den |
| Master craftsman / glassblower | 8-16 HS | 500-1000 den |
| Legionary (Domitian's rate, unchanged) | - | 300 den gross, less deductions |
| Literate secretary / grammaticus | - | 300-1000 den |

**Status thresholds [A]** - these tell you what you must be to be listened to.
| Rank | Property qualification |
|---|---|
| Decurion (town councillor) | 100,000 HS = 25,000 den |
| Equestrian (*eques*) | 400,000 HS = 100,000 den |
| Senator | 1,000,000 HS = 250,000 den |

**Transport [A/B]** - the single most important economic fact in the Empire.
Sea freight costs roughly one-fortieth to one-sixtieth of land freight per
tonne-kilometre. Moving grain 500 km overland roughly doubles its price. **Every
facility you build must be on navigable water.** A furnace 40 km inland from a
river is a furnace you cannot supply.

## 6. Technology of thought: what Rome believes

- **Authority beats observation.** Aristotle, Galen and Ptolemy will be treated
  as settled. Contradicting them is a social act, not a scientific one.
- **Manual work is degrading.** Cicero, *De Officiis* 1.150-151, ranks the crafts
  as sordid; Seneca, *Epistulae* 90, sneers at mechanical invention as beneath
  philosophy. Plutarch has Archimedes disdain his own machines. **[A]**
  Consequence: **never present yourself as an inventor or a manufacturer.**
  Present as a philosopher whose *conclusions* happen to have uses, and let
  freedmen run the workshops in your name.
- **Novelty is suspect, antiquity is authoritative.** The winning rhetorical
  move is not "I have discovered" but "I have recovered": an older, purer
  Egyptian or Chaldean or Pythagorean teaching. This is a lie you should tell.
- **Labour-saving machinery has an actively hostile precedent.** Suetonius,
  *Vespasian* 18: an engineer offers a cheap machine for hauling columns to the
  Capitol; Vespasian rewards him but declines to use it, saying he must be
  allowed to feed his poor commons. **[A]** Take this seriously. Frame every
  machine as *enabling something impossible*, never as *replacing men*.
- **Magic is a capital crime.** The *Lex Cornelia de sicariis et veneficis* is
  used against *malefici*. Astrologers and magicians are expelled from Rome
  repeatedly (Tiberius, Vitellius, Vespasian, Domitian). Casting a horoscope of
  the emperor's death is treason. **[A]** Your chemistry demonstrations look
  exactly like magic. See `03_SOCIAL_POLITICS.md` for the mitigation.
