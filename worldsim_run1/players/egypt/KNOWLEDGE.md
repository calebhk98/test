# EGYPT — KNOWLEDGE (what Egypt believes; may be wrong or incomplete)

This is Egypt's own view of the world. It does NOT know how many other powers exist.

## What we know
- We are the wealthiest and most learned kingdom we know of.
- Rivals: the Seleucid kingdom to our east (ongoing Syrian Wars) and other Hellenistic
  successor states.
- Carthage: a rich trading power in the west.
- Rome: a rising western city-state, currently locked in a war with Carthage. Distant,
  not yet our concern.
- To our south: Nubia/Kush.
- Across the Red Sea & via Arabian traders: spices and goods come from a vast wealthy
  land far to the east ("India"). We trade indirectly; details are hazy.

## Update — 240 BC
- The eastern spice-land ("India") is confirmed real and very large, a source of spices,
  fine cotton, gems and excellent iron/steel — but everything reaches us through Arabian
  middlemen. We have never seen its heartland and do not know its true size or strength.
- Rome has apparently prevailed in its long war with Carthage in the west and now holds
  Sicily. A rising power worth watching, though far away.
- Our southern frontier (Nubia/Kush) is now more firmly under our influence.

## Update — 220 BC
- The eastern spice-land ("India") is a SINGLE vast empire — populous, rich, famed for a
  huge war-elephant army and excellent steel, ruled from a great inland capital. Its envoys
  came to us by sea. We do not know its true military strength, its borders, or what lies on
  the land route between us. Our own mission travels east to learn more.
- The lean Nile years have stirred discontent among native Egyptians over grain prices —
  a matter to watch.
- Rome (west) now also holds Sardinia and Corsica and is pressing northward; still friendly
  to us through trade.

## Update — 190 BC
- India and the Seleucids: the eastern empire lies beyond the Seleucids by land — and the
  Seleucids are OUR enemy too. A shared rival. Worth pondering.
- Some Indian steel reaches us through Arabian middlemen as a costly import; we do not produce
  it ourselves.
- Rome has won a great war against Carthage and now dominates the western sea and holds Iberia.
  Powerful, but far, and friendly to us.
- Internally: the native revolt is suppressed but the grievance (bread, taxes, native vs Greek)
  is not gone. Our reliance on native troops is now permanent.

## Update — 90 BC
- WE BELIEVE ROME IS NOW OUR ENEMY. A Roman general invaded us by sea with his legions and we
  destroyed him at the Delta. We assume this was Rome's will and that Rome means to take us — so
  we have cut off papyrus to Rome and now fund Rome's rivals and the pirates to keep it weak.
  [NOTE — our belief, possibly OVERSTATED: we do not actually know whether this was sent by the
  Roman state or by one ambitious general acting for himself. We treat all Rome as the threat.]
- Our victory proved our navy and combined army can hold the homeland. Rome's manpower is vast;
  we must keep it divided and distracted.
