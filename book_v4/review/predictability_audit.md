## 2. Analysis: Is the Book Over-Telegraphed?

**The two-CORRECT threshold is not met.** No test scored outright CORRECT; the results are PARTIAL / PARTIAL / WRONG / WRONG. By the stated rule, that does not trigger the over-telegraphed flag.

However, this surface result conceals a more important finding: the two WRONG scores are not failures of telegraphing — they are failures of triage between multiple, simultaneously telegraphed threads. In both ch21 and ch36, the agent correctly identified a real, well-prepared turn (Vibenius/Crispus legal threat; Apollodorus's fall), but the novel had prepared a competing turn (the cannon demonstration; the Ocean Gambit) with equal or greater structural care. The WRONG verdicts arise because the book had too many live wires, not too few.

The two PARTIAL scores show a different pattern: the core mechanical turn was correctly predicted in both cases (engineering failure in ch06, fatal manned flight in ch13), but the consequences and emotional register were not foreseeable. This is healthy telegraphing — the "what" is prepared, the "how it lands" is not.

**Summary verdict:** The book is not globally over-telegraphed in the sense of making its outcomes obvious. It is, however, over-loaded with simultaneously live threads in its middle-to-late sections (roughly chs 14–36), where any given chapter could plausibly discharge any of two or three well-prepared arcs. This is a distinct and meaningful problem: readers who track setup carefully will find multiple competing predictions all feeling valid, which dilutes tension and can produce a sense of narrative sprawl or deferred payoff even when individual turns land well.

## 3. Flag List: Specific Over-Telegraphed Beats

### Flagged as requiring attention

1. **Ch13 — Manned flight death (HIGH)**
   The fatal mechanism (fire-runs-up-greased-seam) is shown, named, and burned into Daniel physically across three separate chapters (06, 07, 11) before it kills Sabinus in ch13. The Naso accident in ch11 is a near-identical rehearsal with the exact same physics. The sequence from ch11's final line ("it works... we'll do it again") to ch12's closing image (Celer climbing the hill) to ch13's disaster is among the most step-by-step prepared turns in the manuscript. Consider whether one of the intermediate rehearsals (ch07 or ch11) can be trimmed or obscured.

2. **Ch21 — Gunpowder vow-break (MEDIUM-HIGH)**
   The powder-and-white-salt setup is planted in ch02 (Daniel's cell notes), named explicitly in ch14 as a thing Daniel will withhold, and built toward across chs 15, 18, and 19. By ch20, any attentive reader has the cannon in their peripheral vision. The vow-break itself is emotionally effective, but its mechanics are over-prepared. The ch14 interior monologue naming the powder specifically may be doing too much work.

3. **Ch36 — Ocean Gambit / map reveal (MEDIUM)**
   Ch35 closes on Marcia's question "Did you keep the map in the box" as the chapter's final line — this is a direct pointer forward. Combined with the two-chapter rehearsal of the gambit's exact framing (chs 32, 34), the map reveal in ch36 arrives having been announced. The ch35 final line in particular removes almost all uncertainty about what ch36's central event will be.

4. **Vibenius/Crispus legal threat (MEDIUM — unresolved problem)**
   This thread is seeded in ch07–08, escalated in chs 15–16, and then not discharged in chs 21 or 36 (the two chapters where agents predicted it would land). It is a well-built threat that the novel keeps deferring. This is not over-telegraphing in the traditional sense, but the long-running explicit setup ("the graver word the law knows") without payoff risks becoming a credibility problem if the thread is never resolved, or a pacing problem if it resolves very late.

5. **Apollodorus's fall (MEDIUM — unresolved problem, same pattern as above)**
   Seeded ch07, escalated ch35 (Daniel recognizes the emperor is the young Spaniard, cannot warn Apollodorus). The threat is vivid and emotionally loaded, making it a strong candidate for the "next chapter's turn" at every point from ch35 onward. Like the Vibenius thread, it is a well-prepared danger that the novel is holding in reserve — effective as dread, but accumulating reader anticipation that will need a precise release point.

### Not flagged (healthy preparation)

- **Ch06 engineering failures**: Prepared but consequences (circular economic trap) are not foreseeable. Healthy.
- **Ch13 aftermath (Celer's honesty, Naso's grace note)**: Entirely unprepared; the emotional grace arrives as a genuine surprise inside a predicted structure. Healthy.
- **Ch21 internal emotional register** (Daniel at Geta's bedside, not erasing the ratio): Not telegraphed. Healthy.
