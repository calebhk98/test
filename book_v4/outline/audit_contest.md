## STRUCTURAL ELEMENTS MISSING

**From the contest-launch description (ch19):**

1. **Two-round structure not described at launch.** The ch19 outline entry says "Contest launched with simple public structure — prize money, submission deadline, all classes eligible." It explicitly defers the 1,000-entry culling system to "later," and says Daniel "reads everything himself" in year one. This is canonically correct for the *first* cycle. However, the outline never establishes the chapter or phase at which the full two-round structure (Round 1 paid-judge culling → 20 finalists → Round 2 public readings with token voting) is first on-page. V2_CONTEST_STRUCTURE §2 says this structure matures in Part IV (chs 25–31). No outline entry in chs 25–31 names this transition.

2. **Paid-judge-as-labor-funding mechanic not placed.** The detail that judges are paid workers (literate freedmen, low-status scribes, teachers without patrons) and that this deliberately funds literacy as labor is a structural feature of the contest's design. It does not appear in any chapter entry in the outline.

3. **Token-voting / public-reading mechanics not placed.** The outline confirms public readings happen (ch31 references "the press is covering the controversy") but never places the chapter where the Round 2 reading format (taverns, forum, theater; token voting or raised-hands voting; oral performance quality affecting public vote) is first shown on the page.

4. **Anonymous submission track not placed.** V2_CONTEST_STRUCTURE §4 requires an anonymous submission track (sealed-envelope system for slaves and lower-class entrants) introduced after Daniel notices fictitious Roman names on entries. The outline assigns the formal endowment to the Beat H gap (see above), but the anonymous track's introduction — including the controversy among judges and Daniel's editorial response — is not placed in any chapter.

5. **Dual-prize press narrative as annual cultural event not placed.** V2_CONTEST_STRUCTURE §5 requires the People's Prize vs. Judges' Prize disagreement to be established as a recurring press story. The outline's ch31 entry confirms it is happening ("the press is covering the controversy"), but no earlier chapter establishes when this pattern first begins. V2_CONTEST_STRUCTURE says it is available as a texture beat from Part IV onward; it should first appear in chs 25–31 per the reference document.

6. **Class tension at the lower-status winner moment (Beat E, ch26) — friction detail absent.** The outline places Beat E in ch26 but describes only that "a dockworker or freedman's story wins the People's vote." V2_CONTEST_STRUCTURE §10 requires specific friction: some judges protest, at least one questions whether someone from this background could have written work of this quality. The outline does not specify whether this friction is present in the ch26 beat.

## QUESTIONS

1. **Beat C / Felix's first entry:** The ch19 outline entry calls Felix's submission a "steam-ship entry." V2_CONTEST_STRUCTURE Beat C says Felix's early entries are "not good — earnest and imaginative in the wrong ways" and that he does not win anything for at least four contest cycles. A steam-ship entry in ch19 is a specific and inventive idea — does the outline intend Felix's ch19 entry to be a *losing* entry (consistent with Beat C), or is this meant to imply an early-cycle win? This needs clarification.

2. **Beat F / "Daniel realizes science fiction is inspiring real craftsmen":** The brief listed in the task header describes Beat F this way, but V2_CONTEST_STRUCTURE §6 Part IV does not label a beat "F" for this. V2_CONTEST_STRUCTURE's Beat F in Part IV is actually "Protégé goes wrong (seeded here, full beat in Part V)." The "Daniel realizes tech inspiration is working" beat is described in Loop 3 with Part VI ch41/ch44 placements, and partially in the ch29 "unexpected prize winner solves something" note. Is Beat F as described in the task header an additional beat distinct from the V2_CONTEST_STRUCTURE Beat F, or is it Loop 3 given a separate label? The outline does not resolve this.

3. **Beat H vs. Beat I labeling mismatch:** The task header warns "Beat H: Protégé goes wrong — may be labeled Beat I in some files." V2_CONTEST_STRUCTURE uses Beat H for the endowment formalization and Beat I for the protégé-goes-wrong beat. The outline uses "Beat I" for protégé-goes-wrong (ch39), consistent with V2_CONTEST_STRUCTURE. The endowment beat (V2_CONTEST_STRUCTURE's Beat H) is unassigned in the outline. Writers should use V2_CONTEST_STRUCTURE's labeling (H = endowment, I = protégé) as authoritative.

4. **Ch26 Beat E — is the friction on-page?** The outline entry for ch26 says Beat E involves "the class tension is on-page" but does not specify whether the judges' protest moment (V2_CONTEST_STRUCTURE's required friction element) is included. The writing agent for ch26 needs explicit guidance on this.

5. **Mercator Thulensis pseudonym establishment:** At what chapter does Daniel first use the Mercator Thulensis pseudonym? The outline never names this. If his first story submission goes in under a pseudonym (V2_CONTEST_STRUCTURE §7 says most entries use this pseudonym), the pseudonym needs to be established before it can appear in the prose.

6. **Contest categories evolution:** V2_CONTEST_STRUCTURE §4 defines three stages of category development (ch19: one category; Part IV: three categories; Part VI: four + anonymous track). The outline does not place the category expansion moments in any chapter. The three-category transition (Part IV, chs 25–31) needs a chapter assignment.
