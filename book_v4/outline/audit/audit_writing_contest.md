## SECTION 1: WHAT THE PLANNING DOCS SAY

Sources: V2_CONTEST_STRUCTURE.md (primary), V2_STORY_PLOT_NOTES.md §7, master_outline.md flywheel section.

### Operational Structure

The contest is a formal recurring literary competition (annual or semi-annual). It runs in two rounds:

- **Round 1:** Paid judges read all submissions and cull to 20 finalists. The payment is the key mechanic — Daniel funds literacy by paying educated readers as professional labor.
- **Round 2:** Public readings of finalists at taverns, the forum, and the theater. Audience members vote with tokens.

### Dual Prize

Both a **Judges' Prize** and a **People's Prize** are awarded each cycle. They can and regularly do go to different stories. This disagreement is a recurring press story: the class tension between educated judges and the crowd drives public argument, controversy, and therefore press coverage.

### 9 Flywheel Loops (V2_CONTEST_STRUCTURE.md and V2_STORY_PLOT_NOTES.md §7)

1. Gets more people writing fiction (reward money: "it's easy money, just write a story")
2. More stories → more readers → increases literacy rates
3. Gives Daniel inspiration on future tech (science follows science fiction — Loop 3 requires a specific beat where a craftsman's fiction accidentally describes a future technology)
4. Makes his books more valuable
5. Makes him more culturally central (Rome's Hollywood)
6. Makes his libraries better stocked
7. Makes his printing press more useful
8. Makes his paper more useful
9. Daniel submits his own stories — Romeo and Juliet, Trip to the Moon, etc.

**Loop 3 specific beat:** V2_CONTEST_STRUCTURE.md names a Gallic craftsman (or provisioner's clerk) who enters a story that accidentally describes one of Daniel's actual future technical plans. This is placed at ch41. Daniel must decide whether to let it win (giving the craftsman credit) or suppress it. This is labeled Beat K (Accidental Parallel).

## SECTION 3: DISCREPANCIES

### D1 — Romeo and Juliet Prize Year: Direct Contradiction

**Planning says:** V2_CONTEST_STRUCTURE.md states Romeo and Juliet wins the Judges' Prize "in the year it enters."

**Outline shows:** Ch19 explicitly states "It does not win in year one. The judges find it melodramatic."

**No chapter is assigned for its eventual Judges' Prize win.**

This is a hard contradiction between the two planning documents and between the planning and the outline. V2_CONTEST_STRUCTURE.md also describes the western ocean story winning the Judges' Prize "three contest cycles later, in a different entry year, after he revises it" — which is consistent with ch29. If Romeo and Juliet also wins a Judges' Prize, the two wins need to be in different years, but neither the prize year for Romeo and Juliet nor the chapter where it wins is currently defined.

**Resolution needed:** Either the planning doc's "wins in the year it enters" language is wrong (and Romeo and Juliet wins later, in a chapter that must be assigned), or ch19 must be changed so it wins immediately. The current outline has it losing in year one with no follow-up win scheduled.

### D2 — Women Writers Beat: Conditional vs. Required

**Planning says:** Required beat, no conditional language. "A story entered by a young woman takes the prize. Daniel makes the award public. The cultural friction is real; the press covers it; the precedent is set."

**Outline shows:** Ch44 entry says "One woman wins a contest prize before the book ends (per V2_STORY_PLOT_NOTES §7). If not yet on the page, ch44 is a strong candidate." The word "if" makes this conditional. No chapter definitively owns this beat.

### D3 — Women on Judge Panel: Completely Absent

**Planning says:** Women serve on the standing judge panel. Daniel expected trouble. He did not get any. The beat is noted as a counterpoint to his modern assumptions about ancient Rome.

**Outline shows:** Not in any chapter entry, including ch38 where the standing panel is formalized.

### D4 — Two-Round Structure Never Placed

**Outline shows:** Ch19 notes Daniel reads everything himself in year one (consistent with pre-scale). 3. Resolve the Romeo and Juliet prize contradiction: either change ch19 so it wins immediately (consistent with "wins in the year it enters") or confirm it loses in year one and assign a later chapter for its eventual Judges' Prize win.

### Ch26 — Fix Required

1. Specify judge protest at Beat E: the outline says class tension is on-page but does not say the judges protest the dockworker winner's background. Add this specific friction. The judges questioning whether the winner deserves the prize is what generates the press story and the public argument.

### Ch28–31 — New Beats Needed (choose one chapter)

1. First on-page placement of the two-round structure: paid judges in Round 1, public readings with token voting in Round 2. By ch31 the contest has hundreds of entries, so the two-round format must already exist. Assign a chapter in this range to show it operating.
2. Paid-judge-as-literacy-funding mechanic: a character (Marcia, or Daniel in reflection) articulates that paying readers as judges creates professional demand for literacy. This is Flywheel Loop 2's specific on-page moment.
3. Category expansion (1 → 3 categories): a chapter entry should note when the second and third categories were introduced and why. The Natural Philosophy category exists by ch22, so the expansion likely happened in the ch19–22 range — assign it explicitly.

### Ch34–38 — New Beats Needed

### Ch38 — Additions Needed
