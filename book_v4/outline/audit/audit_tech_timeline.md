## SECTION 1: WHAT THE PLANNING DOCS SAY

### Coal
- **KEY DECISIONS**: Phase A (first 5-10 years), not an afterthought. Romans already use coal
  (Vindolanda fort: "lignum fossile"). Daniel recognizes "burning stone" IMMEDIATELY. The
  self-reinforcing loop (coal → hotter forge → better iron → Newcomen pump → pump drains flooded
  mines → more coal) begins here as a plan.
- **04_tech_schedule.md**: Coal explicitly labeled [PHASE A-B, EASY WIN]. "Action in Phase A:
  Daniel asks about 'burning stone' and locates British coal sources via Macer's trade network."
- **V2_TECH_DEEP_DIVE.md**: No explicit coal section but confirms the Newcomen bootstrapping loop
  depends on coal availability established early.
- **Authoritative position**: Coal recognition at ~99 AD, Phase A. The loop is named at first
  recognition, not when the pump is built.

### Optics / Spyglass
- **KEY DECISIONS**: Working spyglass by 103-105 AD is achievable. V1 treated it as "blocked" —
  that was WRONG. Some Roman glass (especially Alexandrian) already clear. 5-8x monocular
  achievable; binoculars harder. A "useful refracting telescope" remains correctly blocked (Roman
  glass striae/bubbles prevent it).
- **V2_TECH_DEEP_DIVE.md**: Timeline table:
  - Prize competition: 100-103 AD
  - First rough 3-5x spyglass: 103-106 AD
  - Reliable 8-12x monocular: 106-112 AD
  - Useful refracting telescope: CORRECTLY BLOCKED
  - HUMBLING BEAT entry: "Useful optics — blurry 2-3x toy; the atom-seeing microscope is a lie."
- **04_tech_schedule.md**: Glass clarity prize seeded Phase A. First working spyglass Phase B-C.
  Refracting telescope blocked.
- **Authoritative position**: Prize competition by ~100-103 AD; first rough spyglass 103-106 AD
  (i.e., no later than end of Phase C); high-power telescope blocked throughout. HUMBLING BEAT
  is that early optics are disappointing toys, not that spyglasses fail entirely.

### Steam Pump
- **KEY DECISIONS**: Newcomen-type atmospheric pump achievable 104-108 AD. Low precision bar
  (Newcomen's engines leaked badly and ran at 1% efficiency and still worked). Mine drainage =
  enormous wealth.
- **V2_TECH_DEEP_DIVE.md**: Steam timeline:
  - Small-scale working pump (30cm bore): 104-108 AD
  - Full-scale pump (70cm bore), mine application: 108-115 AD
  - Priority: MAXIMUM
- **04_tech_schedule.md**: "Atmospheric steam pump [Newcomen-class — V1 WRONGLY BLOCKED ALL
  STEAM]... PLAUSIBLE IN 15-25 YEARS from workshop establishment — achievable by ~120 AD."
- **Internal tension in reference docs**: V2_TECH_DEEP_DIVE says 104-108 AD (small scale) and
  108-115 AD (mine scale). 04_tech_schedule says ~120 AD. KEY DECISIONS align with
  V2_TECH_DEEP_DIVE's more optimistic timeline (104-108 AD first working unit). The master
  outline's Phase D note "steam pump (~118 AD Hispania)" is a middle position.
- **Authoritative position** (per KEY DECISIONS): First working small-scale pump 104-108 AD.
  First mine-scale application 108-115 AD. Commercial mine drainage ~115-120 AD. NOT a
  Phase E development.

### Pendulum Clock
- **KEY DECISIONS**: Does NOT need spring steel — uses falling weights and swinging pendulum. Hard
  part is precision escapement. Good to ~5 min/day achievable by Phase D (~115-125 AD). Marine
  chronometer needs spring steel and stays harder.
- **V2_TECH_DEEP_DIVE.md**: Contradicts itself. Clock timeline table says "Working pendulum clock
  ±10 min/day" achievable by 108-115 AD. But HUMBLING BEATS section says: "Mechanical clock —
  the escapement never clicks in his memory; he builds excellent water clocks instead." This is
  an internal planning document inconsistency: the timeline table and the HUMBLING BEATS list
  disagree.
- **04_tech_schedule.md**: HUMBLING BEATS: "Mechanical clock — the escapement never clicks in his
  memory." This matches V2_TECH_DEEP_DIVE's HUMBLING BEATS but contradicts V2_TECH_DEEP_DIVE's
  own timeline table.
- **KEY DECISIONS** resolve the contradiction: Pendulum clock achievable by Phase D (~115-125 AD).
  The HUMBLING BEAT entry in the reference docs must be superseded by the KEY DECISIONS ruling.
- **Authoritative position**: Phase D working clock (±5 min/day) is the target. The escapement
  is hard for DANIEL specifically — he does not solve it himself — but the PRIZE mechanism means
  someone else achieves it. Daniel's personal failure does not prevent the tech from existing.

### Prize / DARPA Model
- **KEY DECISIONS**: Daniel specifies OUTCOMES not methods. Core R&D mechanism throughout. How the
  Longitude Prize worked (1714).
- **V2_TECH_DEEP_DIVE.md**: Detailed prize beat sequence. Prize Beats 1-6 identified:
  1. First glass clarity prize posted (Phase A, ~100-103 AD)
  2. Glabrio first contact — pointed questions about spec (Phase B-C)
  3. Glabrio nearly solves with different approach (Phase C)
  4. Farrier pyrometry chain — aggregate of separate solvers (Phase C-D)
  5. Crispus figures out the aggregate / system emerges (Phase D)
  6. Technique reaches wrong hands / unintended competitor (Phase D)
- **master_outline.md**: Prize/innovation model listed as a continuous subplot across all parts.
- **Authoritative position**: Prize model is present from ch07 through the end. Six distinct beats
  must appear in the narrative arc.

### Optical Semaphore
- **KEY DECISIONS**: Chappe telegraph system (1790s France). Signal tower every 10-15 miles from
  Rome to Rhine = 1,200 miles in hours. Achievable Phase B-C. Directly addresses Rome's
  communication lag.
- **master_outline.md**: "By 130 AD, experimental optical semaphore line runs from Rome to
  Mogontiacum (~1,200 miles)."
- **V2_REVISED_OUTLINE.md**: Cross-chapter continuity table: "Optical semaphore: sketched ch23,
  Rome-to-Adriatic by ch40, Rhine line ch49."

  The Rome-to-Rhine line should be operational by ~130 AD. The outline's own continuity table
  shows Rhine line only at ch49 legacy (~153-155 AD), which contradicts master_outline.md's
  own statement of 130 AD.

## SECTION 3: DISCREPANCIES

### DISCREPANCY 1 — Steam Pump: 20-Year Gap, Wrong Phase (CRITICAL)
- **What planning docs say**: First small-scale Newcomen pump by 104-108 AD (V2_TECH_DEEP_DIVE);
  mine-scale pump 108-115 AD; commercial drainage ~115-120 AD.
- **What outline shows**: No on-page steam work until ch31 passing reference (~118 AD); first
  on-page scene ch38 (~128 AD).
- **Gap**: 20-24 years of off-page development. The outline treats the steam pump as a Phase E
  reward instead of a Phase C-D milestone.
- **Specific problems**:
  - The coal loop is named at ch07 but nothing in Phase B or Phase C (ch08-ch22) shows any
    steam component work — cylinder casting, piston fit, boiler construction, or test.
  - Ch31's "approximately 118 AD" is a passing mention, not a chapter beat.
  - Ch38's first scene arrives at the commercial-ready phase without showing any of the
    iterative failure process that makes Newcomen-class plausible.
  - The low-precision bar that makes Newcomen achievable (leaky pistons are OK) is a KEY
    DECISION point; the outline never dramatizes this insight.

### DISCREPANCY 2 — Optical Semaphore: Rhine Line 20+ Years Late (SIGNIFICANT)
- **What planning docs say**: Achievable Phase B-C (KEY DECISIONS); "By 130 AD, experimental
  optical semaphore line runs from Rome to Mogontiacum" (master_outline.md).
- **What outline shows**: Rome-to-Adriatic by ch40 (~130 AD) is on time. But Rhine line only
  appears in ch49 legacy inventory (~153-155 AD).
- **Gap**: Rhine line is 23-25 years late versus master_outline.md's own 130 AD target.
- **Specific problem**: V2_REVISED_OUTLINE.md's continuity table says "Rhine line ch49" —
  this is a discrepancy within the V2 documents themselves. The continuity table contradicts
  master_outline.md's stated 130 AD date. One of these must be wrong; per KEY DECISIONS and
  master_outline.md, 130 AD is the target.
- **Secondary problem**: From sketch (ch23, ~109 AD) to first operational segment (ch40,
  ~130 AD) = 21 years off-page. At least one chapter in Phase C-D should show tower
  construction, codebook trials, or the first two-tower test.

### DISCREPANCY 3 — Pendulum Clock: Phase D Working Result Off-Page (MODERATE)
- **What planning docs say**: Working clock ±5 min/day achievable Phase D (~115-125 AD) via
  prize mechanism. Daniel doesn't solve it himself but the prize produces a result. (KEY
  DECISIONS supersede the HUMBLING BEATS entries in V2_TECH_DEEP_DIVE and 04_tech_schedule,
  which are now outdated on this point.)
- **What outline shows**: Failed attempt ch23. Daniel gives up permanently ch47. "Pendulum
  clocks in public buildings" in ch49 legacy inventory with no prior chapter showing this
  result. Posthumous solve by Procula, ch52.
- **Internal outline contradiction**: Ch49 claims working clocks exist as part of Daniel's
  legacy, but ch47 shows him giving up and ch52 shows the problem unsolved until after his
  death. If the clock was NOT solved in Daniel's lifetime, ch49 cannot list it as his legacy.
  The ch49 line must be either: (a) removed/changed to acknowledge the problem was unsolved,
  or (b) a chapter must be added showing a prizewinner solves the escapement in Phase D,
  after which Daniel commissions public clocks using the result.
- **Planning doc internal inconsistency (flagged)**: V2_TECH_DEEP_DIVE.md simultaneously
  lists the pendulum clock as achievable in the timeline table AND as a HUMBLING BEAT (never
  solved). KEY DECISIONS resolve this: achievable by Phase D via prize, not by Daniel's own
  hand. The reference docs need updating, but that is separate from the outline fix.

### DISCREPANCY 4 — Spyglass: First Result 1-4 Years Late (MINOR)
- **What planning docs say**: First rough 3-5x spyglass 103-106 AD (V2_TECH_DEEP_DIVE).
- **What outline shows**: First on-page test ch18 (~106-107 AD). This is at the far edge of
  the reference range and 1-4 years after KEY DECISIONS' stated target of 103-105 AD.
- **Severity**: Minor, especially since prize competition is seeded ch07 (~99 AD) and ch18
  is within a year of the outer bound. However, if the prize is posted at ~100-103 AD and
  first results by 103-106 AD, there is no specific chapter between ch07 and ch18 that shows
  any prize submission, partial result, or iteration step. The payoff arrives without any
  visible development.
- **Secondary problem**: No chapter shows the "blurry 2-3x toy" HUMBLING BEAT that the
  reference docs specify as the first disappointing result. Ch18 goes straight from "prize
  posted" to "it works approximately." The intermediate failures are off-page.

### DISCREPANCY 5 — Prize Beat 5 (Aggregate Moment) Not Staged (MODERATE)
- **What planning docs say**: Prize Beat 5 is the "Crispus moment" — someone figures out the
  aggregate of separate solvers produces a result no single solver could. This is a key
  intellectual pivot showing the prize model's emergent power. It belongs in Phase D.
- **What outline shows**: No chapter in the Phase D range (roughly ch24-ch36) is identified
  as staging this beat. The prize model is active but the aggregate-insight scene is absent.
- **Note**: This is distinct from Beat 6 (technique reaches wrong hands). Beat 5 is about
  Daniel's own understanding of what the prize model is doing; Beat 6 is about loss of control.

### DISCREPANCY 6 — No On-Page Steam Development Path (CRITICAL — same as Discrepancy 1, structural dimension)
- The coal loop introduced at ch07 implies a development chain. The outline shows no Phase B
  chapter addressing: (a) what fuel supply looks like at scale, (b) any attempt to cast a
  cylinder or fit a piston, (c) any boiler or steam-pressure test. The first steam content
  after ch07 is ch31's passing mention at ~118 AD. This means 19 years of the most important
  tech development in the novel happens entirely off-page.
- The KEY DECISIONS argument that Newcomen-class is achievable precisely BECAUSE it tolerates
  low precision (leaky piston = atmospheric pressure still pushes down adequately) is a
  narrative beat that must be shown, not asserted. Without a chapter showing Daniel reasoning
  through why leaky is OK, the steam pump's appearance in ch38 lacks earned credibility.

### DISCREPANCY 7 — Coal Loop Never Closes On-Page Before Phase E (MODERATE)
- **What planning docs say**: The self-reinforcing loop (coal → better iron → Newcomen pump →
  more coal) is named at Phase A as a plan. The loop closing — i.e., the pump actually draining
  mines that produce more coal — is the financial and technological payoff.
- **What outline shows**: The loop is named at ch07. It begins to close at ch38-39 (~128 AD).
  The gap is ~29 years. No Phase B, C, or D chapter shows any link in the chain being forged:
  better iron quality from coal-fired forge, cylinder castings, boiler construction. The chain
  is invisible until the pump appears fully formed in ch38.
