## GAP 1: FOOD AS MOTIVATION FOR THE ATLANTIC CROSSING

**STATUS: PARTIAL**

**What the outline shows:**

The food arc is consistently tracked across the outline. The master_outline's Part IV food
section contains the closest thing to what the planning context is asking for:

> "Note: Daniel pushed for the Atlantic crossing partly because he knew what was growing
> there. He never said this aloud."

This is a single parenthetical sentence at the end of the tomato-sauce passage in the
master_outline's Part IV food arc section. It acknowledges the motivation exists but frames
it as something never voiced — which is presented as a feature, not a bug.

The V2_REVISED_OUTLINE's ch25 "Three Faces" entry adds: "he starts thinking about ships,
routes, the specific crops he wants." This is a brief beat in Daniel's private interior.

The V2_REVISED_OUTLINE's ch26 entry ("The Forum") notes expedition organization is
underway and citrus is "specified in the provisioning inventory (on-page detail)."

The ch27 entry ("The Accusation") shows Daniel sending a captain with detailed
instructions but does not list crops explicitly.

The ch28 entry ("East") shows the expedition's return dispatch with "corn, sweet potato,
cacao, rubber" documented.

**What's missing:**

The planning context asks for food to appear as one of Daniel's *personal* motivations
stated in his interiority — "I want real food" — not just as strategic resources. The
outline treats the food motivation as subtext ("he never said this aloud") when the
planning context wants it as a beat the reader feels directly.

Equally missing: explicit on-page crop-finding instructions to the expedition. The ch27
departure paragraph specifies navigation instructions ("southwest to latitude of the Canary
Islands, catch the westward winds...") but does not list specific crops Daniel has told
the captain to look for. The captain's return dispatch mentions crops, but whether Daniel
named them before departure is not established.

**What's needed:**

1. One brief interior beat — probably in ch25 or ch26 — where food is explicitly listed
   alongside strategic motivations. Not a speech; a private thought in which tomatoes,
   potatoes, and chocolate are named as part of what he is going for. The current framing
   ("he never said this aloud") is correct for his public voice but shouldn't mean it's
   absent from his interiority.
2. In the ch27 departure planning or ch26 expedition-organization beat, add a line showing
   Daniel giving the captain a specific crop list: sweet potato, maize, cacao especially.
   This is a practical expedition instruction that serves the plot even without emotional
   weight.

**Suggested chapter(s):** Ch25 (private interior planning beat) and ch26 or ch27
(crop-finding instruction to the captain).

## GAP 2: CITRUS / SCURVY KNOWLEDGE ON THE EXPEDITION

**STATUS: PRESENT (partial — the beat exists but is distributed and sometimes vague)**

**What the outline shows:**

The citrus beat appears in multiple places:

- V2_REVISED_OUTLINE ch22 ("Marcia"): "Citrus provisioning planned — Daniel begins
  specifying requirements for the Atlantic expedition he is still years away from launching.
  Citrus listed explicitly in his private notes for the crossing's provisions. He knows why
  (he doesn't explain it; it is a line in an inventory)."
- V2_REVISED_OUTLINE ch26 ("The Forum"): "Citrus specified in the provisioning inventory
  (on-page detail)."
- master_outline Part V: "Daniel specifies citrus in the provisioning list and explains why,
  naming the symptom — teeth falling out, bleeding gums, weakness — even though he cannot
  name the vitamin. He phrases it in terms the Roman naval mind understands: 'sailors who
  lose their teeth cannot work the lines.'"
- updated_ch37_53.md ch47 ("Old"): "He is specifying: the compass, the latitude gnomon,
  the hull design, the citrus provisions (naming the symptoms — teeth loosening, bleeding
  gums, weakness — even though he cannot name the vitamin)."

**What's missing:**

The beat exists and is well-specified. The weakness is that it is spread across at least
four chapters without any one chapter clearly owning the "this is the scurvy beat" moment.
Ch22 plants it silently (private notes, no explanation). Ch26 confirms it in inventory.
The master_outline places the full symptom explanation in Part V expedition planning. Ch47
places the symptom detail in the ~139-145 AD planning for the 149 AD departure.

There is a mild inconsistency: ch22 says Daniel "knows why (he doesn't explain it; it is a
line in an inventory)" — meaning the reader doesn't get the symptoms. The master_outline
and ch47 say he does name symptoms. These need to be reconciled: either he names symptoms
when explaining to a captain or shipwright (making it on-page), or it stays as a private
notation (keeping it as subtext). The planning context asks for the latter to be on the
page — "he would insist on this before any ship departs."

**What's needed:**

One on-page beat (not just an inventory line) in the expedition preparation chapters
(ch26-27 or the ch47 planning range) where Daniel explains the symptom logic to a captain
or shipwright. The "sailors who lose their teeth cannot work the lines" phrasing from the
master_outline is good and should be anchored to a specific chapter.

**Suggested chapter(s):** Ch26 or ch27 for the first expedition; the master_outline already
places the fully explained version in Part V, which maps to the serious expedition planning
around ch47.

## GAP 3: BALLOONS + CANNONS ON THE ATLANTIC EXPEDITION

**STATUS: PARTIAL (balloons yes; cannons ambiguous)**

**What the outline shows:**

**Balloons:** Multiple references confirm balloon equipment on Atlantic expeditions.
- master_outline "Atlantic expeditions (Part V onward): balloon rigs standard equipment
  on ocean voyages."
- master_outline Part V second organized Atlantic expedition: "The expeditions now carry
  balloons, crossbow-armed soldiers, printed maps, and medical protocols including citrus
  provisions."
- The CROSS-CHAPTER CONTINUITY section: "Manned balloon archers operational by ch18.
  Military doctrine established."

**Cannons:** The outline is ambiguous. The outline establishes that a working bronze cannon
never exists in Daniel's lifetime:

- V2_REVISED_OUTLINE ch29 (Overreach): ch45's text confirms "there is no gun in the world,
  no cannon a gunner can trust, the thing I am most often credited with and have most
  completely failed to make."
- ch45 prose (read directly) states this explicitly: "The steel to make a tube that does
  not burst has never come... So no one threw a stone at a wall in Judaea out of a tube of
  mine."

So the planning context's reference to "early cannon technology" on the expedition ships
conflicts with the established canon that cannon never works in Daniel's lifetime.

The expeditions carry crossbow-armed soldiers and balloon archers, but not functional
cannon.

**What's missing:**

The first-contact military dynamics are not detailed in the outline. The master_outline
Part V notes the expeditions "carry balloons, crossbow-armed soldiers" but does not show
how this changes first contact with indigenous peoples relative to the historical Columbian
Exchange. The outline mentions a returned indigenous person (ch33) but the military context
of that first contact is not dramatized.

**What's needed:**

1. A clarification note that cannon is NOT part of the Atlantic expeditions (per established
   canon) — this prevents a continuity error if the planning context document is used
   without the ch45 canon check.
2. A beat (probably in ch28 or ch29's Atlantic planning material, or in the ch33 indigenous
   contact scene) showing how balloon capability specifically changes first contact dynamics:
   a balloon overhead changes the encounter's power differential significantly even without
   cannon. The ch33 scene as outlined (a quiet, complicated meeting with an interpreter) does
   not address the military context of initial landing.

**Suggested chapter(s):** Ch26-27 (expedition preparation — specify balloon equipment
carried) and ch33 (indigenous contact — note what the expedition's aerial capability meant
for the encounter). A note to the canon log clarifying cannon-never-works is advisable.

## GAP 4: BLOCKER-SOLVING PATTERN IN THE TECH ARCS

**STATUS: PARTIAL (pattern present but not consistently rendered with all three steps)**

**What the outline shows:**

The planning context's three-step pattern (identify root blocker → targeted solution →
prize/specialist/direct experiment) appears in several places but not consistently at all
three steps:

**Glass/telescope:** The strongest example. V2_PRIZE_INNOVATION §Phase B documents the
glass clarity prize posted through a guild contact, the Brundisium glassmaker winning with
an unexpected technique, and Daniel's recalibration of what the prize model actually does.
V2_REVISED_OUTLINE ch07 notes the first glass clarity prize as his first prize-model
deployment. Ch15 shows a near-miss result arriving from Alexandria. Ch18 shows the spyglass
payoff when it finally works. Root blocker (glass purity) → prize competition → specific
result. This three-step pattern is fully rendered.

**Steel:** Partially rendered. The steel wall is a running motif. Ch15 states "Steel gates
everything." The cannon chapter (ch21) shows Daniel building a bronze cannon knowing it
will burst, as a demonstration argument for better steel. But the *root diagnostic* step —
Daniel explicitly identifying what specifically is wrong with Roman steel (carbon atmosphere
control, not just temperature) — appears in prose rules ("Write the wall honestly") without
the diagnostic scene itself being specified as a required beat.

**Clock:** The escapement failure is rendered as a humbling beat (he forgot the mechanism),
not as a diagnostic-then-solve pattern. The outline says he failed to reconstruct the
escapement and wrote "begin where I failed" in the encyclopedia. There is no "root
blocker → prize competition for this specific sub-problem" beat for the clock.

**Steam:** The "precision bootstrapping loop" is documented in V2_TECH_DEEP_DIVE (referenced
in ch37, ch38, ch39 entries). This is the closest thing to the full three-step diagnostic
pattern: thermocouple chain → better steel → better boring bar → tighter cylinder. But it
is described as a chain Daniel is "aware of and frustrated by," not as a sequence of
individual root-blocker diagnoses followed by targeted competitions.

**What's missing:**

The diagnostic step ("identifying the specific root problem") is present for glass but
absent or vague for steel and steam. The outline shows Daniel *hitting walls* and *knowing
walls exist* but the explicit moment of diagnostic clarity — "the root blocker is X, not
the surface problem I thought it was" — is only well-rendered for glass.

**What's needed:**

1. In the steel arc (probably ch15 or ch21), a brief interiority beat where Daniel diagnoses
   specifically what is wrong: "not the temperature — the temperature I can guess at; it is
   the carbon atmosphere in the crucible, the thing I cannot measure." This is the diagnosis
   step.
2. In the steam arc (ch38 area), a beat where Daniel explicitly identifies the tightest
   bottleneck in the precision bootstrapping loop and names the next targeted prize spec
   that addresses it.
3. The clock can remain a pure humbling beat (he cannot bootstrap from forgotten memory) —
   this is its correct function in the story and the diagnostic pattern doesn't apply to
   a memory failure.

**Suggested chapter(s):** Ch15 or ch21 (steel diagnosis), ch38 (steam loop diagnosis).

## GAP 5: DINOSAURS FOR THE CHILDREN

**STATUS: MISSING**

**What the outline shows:**

The Ulpia education arc is extensively documented across both outlines and the updated
chapter entries. Beats include: English from birth (ch36), English visible in household
(ch38), astronomy and maps (ch40), sleet-word revelation (ch41), full truth-telling (ch43),
encyclopedia editing (ch44), survey instruction (ch46), public English (ch47), final session
(ch49).

The content of Daniel's teaching is specified: the cipher, English alphabet, the ladder of
knowledge (arithmetic through algebra through geometry), astronomy, the western continents,
science fiction (Jules Verne, Star Wars), moon landings, internet, cars (ch43 truth-telling
scene). The ch43 entry also specifies cacao as a food beat shared with Ulpia or Marcia.

None of these entries mention dinosaurs, prehistoric creatures, or the category of "fun
modern knowledge with no strategic utility." The closest thing is the ch43 truth-telling
where Daniel tells Ulpia about science fiction as "stories from his world."

The master_outline's food arc, tech arc, and Ulpia education arc are all detailed, but the
"parental connection through modern trivia" beat — the kind of thing a father tells children
purely because it delights them — is not present.

**What's missing:**

The entire category of "things Daniel shares with his kids purely because they're a parent."
The planning context specifically names dinosaurs as the exemplar of this: standard
children's knowledge, no strategic value, pure parental connection. The outline consistently
frames Daniel's knowledge-sharing as strategic (the ladder, the cipher, the encyclopedia)
or as emotional truth-telling (the ch43 session). The parental-delight register — "did you
know there were giant lizards that ruled the world before people?" — is absent.

**What's needed:**

One brief scene (100-150 words maximum) in the Lucanus or Ulpia chapters where Daniel
shares something purely for the child's delight, with no strategic purpose. Dinosaurs are
the obvious choice. The planning context suggests this belongs in Ulpia's early education
arc or in Lucanus's childhood chapters. It should feel tonally different from the cipher
sessions and the encyclopedia work — more like a bedtime story.

**Suggested chapter(s):** Ch38 (Ulpia at 9x table, Daniel aging, the playground visible —
a natural domestic scene) or ch40 (Ulpia astronomy and maps session — dinosaurs could
come out of a tangent when Ulpia asks about the deep past of the earth). Could also fit
in ch41 or ch43.

## GAP 6: READER WEALTH TRACKING

**STATUS: PARTIAL (spot-checked chapters vary significantly)**

**What the five chapters show:**

**Ch10 (workshop founding, Phase A/B):** Strong. The chapter opens with the Pamphilus
purchase: "four thousand sesterces." The free-women piecework references "a few asses."
The peculium system is explained ("Macer's coins that I was permitted to spend as if my
hand were his hand"). The reader gets: a man costs 4,000 HS; a seam costs a few small
coins; Daniel has no independent wealth but manages Macer's money. No comparative context
is given (the finances document says a soldier earned 5,000-15,000 HS/year, so 4,000 HS
is roughly a soldier's annual wage — but this context is in the planning documents, not
the chapter). Grade: **Good grounding, needs one comparator.**

**Ch18 (Second Dacian War, Phase B/C):** The chapter opens with the Pamphilus purchase
price repeated ("four thousand sesterces"). The freeing scene is emotionally grounded in
the cost but the economic stakes are not given: we don't know what Daniel is worth at this
point. The narrative jumps to the war without establishing his financial position. The
quartermaster figures work in "columns of grain and fodder" without amounts. Grade:
**Weak — financial position at this stage (Phase B transition) not established.**

**Ch25 (Three Faces, Phase C/D border):** The chapter has strong implicit wealth signals.
Scaeva arrives with "eight bearers" (the narrator notes this is "the precise number, the
senatorial number"). The bulla is "gold because we could afford gold now." Daniel has
"a household... and a yard full of people on my wages." But no specific numbers. The
finances document says he is 3-6 million HS at this point, but the chapter gives no
anchors. Grade: **Atmospheric but no numbers — reader cannot track scale.**

**Ch35 (Hadrian audience, Phase D):** Hadrian says Daniel is "rich" and that paying him
in coin would be unnecessary. No numbers. The chapter is about the political relationship,
not the financial one, and this is appropriate — but the reader who wants to track wealth
has no anchor in this chapter. The senator bribe (400,000 HS) that appears in the finances
document around this era is not mentioned. Grade: **Intentionally sparse — appropriate
for the chapter's purpose but contributes nothing to wealth tracking.**

**Ch45 (Bar Kokhba, Phase E):** The slave-price column is the chapter's central financial
image and it works powerfully — but it works as a moral horror, not as a wealth-tracking
tool. The Captives column shows prices falling during wartime supply glut. The chapter
gives no sense of Daniel's own wealth position in Phase E. Grade: **Strong moral-financial
image; zero personal wealth context.**

**Overall assessment:**

The outline's financial context entries are detailed and specific (the finances document
is excellent), but this information is not consistently reaching the actual chapter prose.
The major financial milestones — the First Balloon Contract (800,000 HS total), the
paper/press licensing deal (500,000 HS/year guaranteed), the Mine Drainage Contract (144,000
HS from one mine in year one), the senator bribe (400,000 HS) — are documented in
V2_DANIEL_FINANCES.md but do not appear in the chapters reviewed.

Ch10 does the best job: "four thousand sesterces" for Pamphilus, immediately legible because
the reader has been given the daily-wage baseline. Ch18, ch25, ch35, and ch45 do not provide
equivalent anchors.

**What's needed:**

1. In ch18 (end of Second Dacian War, when Daniel is coming into first-fortune territory):
   one brief financial anchor establishing his changed position. Even a sentence noting that
   the balloon contract had paid out and he now has coin of his own would let the reader
   track the scale.
2. In ch25 (peak danger, Phase C wealth): one specific number or comparator. The bulla being
   "gold because we could afford gold now" is good but incomplete. Something like "I had
   paid more for one afternoon of a senator's attention than Pamphilus cost me" would give
   the reader a number they already know.
3. In ch35 (Hadrian audience): this chapter probably doesn't need explicit wealth tracking —
   Hadrian's remark about Daniel being "rich" is appropriate context for its purpose.
4. In ch45: the slave-price column already functions as a financial image. It could be
   sharpened by noting the price in the new figures and giving the reader a comparator
   (soldier's wage, skilled craftsman) to understand what the falling price means.

**Suggested chapter(s) for fixes:** Ch18 (first fortune anchor), ch25 (Phase C wealth
comparator). Ch45's treatment is appropriate for its purpose and probably should not be
changed.

## GAP 7: BALLOON MILITARY DEMAND POST-FIRST

**STATUS: PARTIAL (military demand implied but supply tension and production scene absent)**

**What the outline shows:**

The military adoption of balloons is well-documented:
- Ch06 ("Cheap, Mine, Undeniable"): "Celer introduced watching the military possibilities"
- Ch08 ("Fire That Flies"): Celer asks about signaling from the basket
- Ch11 ("The Army's Eye"): "Celer's signaling interest from ch08 paid: he asks specifically
  about fire-relay stations"
- Ch13 ("The Fall"): Sabinus's death; the vow
- Ch17 ("Citizenship"): "Manned balloon program resumes — stated on-page... manned balloon
  archers are operational"
- Ch18 ("The Siege"): "Manned balloon archers deployed in the siege — their utility
  confirmed; this is military doctrine now, not an experiment"
- master_outline Part III: By the Second Dacian War (105-106 AD), six trained operators
  with 50+ hours tethered flight time
- master_outline Part IV: "By 113 AD, manned balloon crossbowmen are standard formation
  attachments (two rigs per legion on campaign)"

The military demand trajectory is clear. What the outline does NOT show:

The *production and supply pressure* that would naturally follow from Trajan wanting two
rigs per legion. Who is building these rigs? How fast can Daniel's workshop produce them?
Does the military demand crowd out civilian uses? The outline jumps from "military doctrine
now" (ch18) to "standard formation attachments" (by 113 AD per ch28) without showing the
production/supply chain in between.

The ch19 through ch26 range (roughly 107-112 AD) documents the contest, casino, printing,
and Scaeva/Crispus/Vibenius conflicts — but balloon production scaling during this same
period is invisible.

**What's missing:**

1. A production/supply scene showing Daniel managing balloon production at scale — who
   builds the envelopes, how the training program works, whether there are supply shortfalls.
2. Any tension between military demand (which now has institutional backing and will not
   be refused) and Daniel's other uses for balloons — reconnaissance for non-military
   purposes, demonstration work, the civilian applications he might prefer.
3. The moment where Daniel becomes, in some sense, a *supplier* to the military rather than
   a lone inventor — when the balloon stops being "his" technology and becomes a Roman
   military asset with its own production chain.

**What's needed:**

One scene (probably in the ch19-ch22 range, as the transition from Phase B to Phase C)
showing balloon production at scale: a workshop or contractor producing envelopes, trained
operators being certified, Daniel reviewing the military's request for more rigs than he
can quickly supply. The tension between "the military wants ten more" and "Daniel can
supervise two production runs at once" is a real constraint that would give this arc
texture.

**Suggested chapter(s):** Ch19 or ch20 (the period of peak institutional momentum, before
the manned archers are fully established as doctrine in ch18 — note: ch18 is the siege,
~106-107 AD; the volume demand would come after). Alternatively, a brief beat in ch22
("Marcia") when Marcia is taking on the workshop management — she would be managing this
supply chain.

## GAP 8: PRIZE MODEL AS ONE OF SEVERAL R&D METHODS

**STATUS: PARTIAL (multiple methods documented in planning docs; "too many prizes" subplot
entirely absent)**

**What the outline shows:**

V2_PRIZE_INNOVATION.md documents the prize model thoroughly and does acknowledge other
methods in passing:

- Phase D section: "he quietly funds all four craftsmen to work together afterward on the
  next precision target. This is outside the formal prize structure and he does not
  advertise it."
- Phase E section: Glabrio receives "a direct consulting arrangement — not a prize but a
  retainer."
- Section 2 ("Why it works better than hiring one craftsman"): The comparison implies
  hiring does happen — it just explains why prizes are better in some cases.

The ch39 entry mentions "Felix returning as a former contest winner" and a direct consulting
arrangement with the workshop, which is hire-not-prize. The master_outline's Part II food
arc mentions "a verbal arrangement with Gnaeus" for food, which is a direct deal.

The ch10 chapter (read directly) shows Daniel personally doing the work himself — the
block-cutting, the printing experiments — which is the "hands-on" method.

So all three non-prize methods (direct hire, in-house staff, personal experimentation) are
present in the outline and chapters. They are not, however, framed explicitly as "methods
alongside the prize model." The prize model gets a dedicated planning document
(V2_PRIZE_INNOVATION.md) and multiple chapter beats. The other methods are mentioned
incidentally.

**The "too many prizes" subplot: ABSENT.**

There is no mention anywhere in the outline, master_outline, updated chapter entries,
V2_DANIEL_FINANCES.md, or V2_PRIZE_INNOVATION.md of the "too many prizes active at once →
multiple winners show up → near-depletes finances" scenario described in the planning
context. This is entirely missing.

The closest approximation is a note in V2_DANIEL_FINANCES Phase C: "Can absorb a 500,000
HS failure without threatening his position" — which implies financial resilience, the
opposite of the subplot.

**What's missing:**

1. An explicit framing moment (probably in ch19 or ch24, when the prize model is becoming
   formalized) where Daniel or the narration shows the prize model as *one tool among
   several* rather than the primary method — a scene where he chooses direct hire over a
   prize, or where he rolls up his sleeves and personally experiments, and the narration
   shows him aware of the tradeoffs.

2. The "too many prizes" subplot: a period (probably Phase C, ch22-ch26 range, when
   multiple prizes are running simultaneously) where several winners show up in close
   succession before the next revenue wave has fully materialized. The outline's Phase C
   finance notes show 1.5-3M HS/year annual income, but the prize costs are not
   individually tracked against cash position. A good candidate: the lens-grinding prize
   (runs nine years, won in Phase D, ch27-29 range per V2_PRIZE_INNOVATION) collides
   with other prize payouts and a slow revenue quarter during the impiety crisis (ch27,
   which already costs Daniel politically and financially). This would give the subplot a
   natural home.

**What's needed:**

1. One scene (ch19 or ch22) showing Daniel choosing direct hire or personal experiment for
   a specific problem rather than a prize, with brief interiority on why this one needs a
   different approach.

2. One brief scene (ch27 range, during the impiety crisis and its financial damage) where
   multiple prize payouts cluster in a bad quarter. This can be one paragraph — Daniel
   reviewing the prize-pool ledger and noting the collision. It serves double duty: shows
   the prize model's real cost, and adds financial pressure to the crisis chapter.

**Suggested chapter(s):** Ch19 or ch22 (method diversity beat), ch27 (too-many-prizes
collision, timed to the impiety crisis financial damage).

## CROSS-GAP NOTES

**Gap 1 and Gap 3 interact:** If the expedition instructions (Gap 1) include an explicit
crop list, that same scene can establish what equipment the ships carry (Gap 3 — balloon
rigs). One scene in ch26-27 can address both.

**Gap 4 and Gap 8 interact:** The blocker-solving pattern (Gap 4) is closely related to
the prize model being one tool among several (Gap 8). A scene showing Daniel diagnosing a
root blocker and then choosing a prize competition as the targeted solution demonstrates
both simultaneously. The steel diagnosis beat in ch15 or ch21 could accomplish both goals
if written carefully.

**Gap 6 and Gap 7 interact:** The balloon production scaling (Gap 7) should be set against
Daniel's Phase B/C financial position (Gap 6). A production scene that also anchors his
wealth position kills two gaps with one beat.

**Gap 5 (dinosaurs) is the only gap that requires entirely new material with no existing
outline scaffold to build from.** All other gaps have partial anchors in the outline that
need expansion or clarification.

**The "too many prizes" subplot (Gap 8)** is the most structurally significant absent beat
because it would add a character-revealing moment (his genius and his limits simultaneously)
that currently has no equivalent. The ch27 crisis is the natural home for it, since that
chapter already costs Daniel politically and financially.
