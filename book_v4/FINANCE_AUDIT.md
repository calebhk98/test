# Finance Reconstructability Audit

**Goal:** a dedicated reader (the "YouTube deep-dive" reader) should be able to piece
together Daniel's wealth per chapter — **with difficulty**. This audits the financial
breadcrumbs actually present in the prose (full 53-chapter sweep), reconstructs the
trajectory they imply, flags the chapters where the trail goes cold, and checks the
result against `V2_DANIEL_FINANCES.md`.

Design verdict up front: the breadcrumbs are mostly **relative/comparative** (a denarius
a load, "most of everything I had," "could afford gold now," a total "I counted twice"),
rarely a clean net-worth number. That is exactly the right texture for "piece it together
with difficulty" — keep it. The fixes below are about (a) closing the few dark stretches
where the trail vanishes for many chapters, and (b) one trajectory inconsistency.

## Reconstructed trajectory (what the breadcrumbs let a reader triangulate)

- **Enslaved (ch01–16):** effectively zero personal wealth; a peculium that "is not yours."
  Best clue: the **120,000 HS balloon contract (ch11)** flows mostly to Macer; Daniel's
  40,000 "direct" share is real but controlled.
- **Newly freed (ch17–20, ~age 25–28):** **~40–50,000 HS total** ("40,000 was most of
  everything I had"), most of it immediately spent freeing Tyche (**38,000**) and Pamphilus
  (**4,000**). This is the cleanest anchor in the book.
- **Rising workshop owner (ch21–34, ~30s):** "more money than I knew how to hold"; press
  throws off a quarterly total "counted twice"; owns mine + wharf + press + yard; absorbs an
  **8,000 HS** loss from his own purse with interest; can afford gold and bribes. Implies
  steady growth into the low millions.
- **Institutional wealth (ch38–49, ~45–74):** endows perpetual rent-shares, a Minerva gift,
  a craft-failure patronage fund, 40 salaried physicians, paper-mills, the ocean program; the
  estate needs a legal "fortress" deed. Consistent with **tens of millions HS**.
- **Legacy (ch51–53):** self-sustaining institutions on tied rents; an unclaimed prize fund
  "grown fat."

## Cross-check vs `V2_DANIEL_FINANCES.md` — ONE inconsistency to resolve

`V2_DANIEL_FINANCES.md` states: **age-21 ≈ 200,000–300,000 HS**; age-36 ≈ 15–25M HS;
age-60+ ≈ 50–100M HS. The mid/late figures square with the prose (institutional endowments,
the fortress deed → tens of millions). **But the early figure does not:** at **ch20 (~age
26–28)** Daniel says **40,000 HS "was most of everything I had."** A reader reconstructing
from the prose lands ~40–50K at ~26, while the doc claims 200–300K at 21. A YouTuber *would*
catch this.

**Recommended fix (pick one):**
1. **Lower the doc's early figure** to match the leaner prose (~40–60K liquid by his
   mid-20s, growing fast after the press + casino + mine compound) — simplest, and the prose
   is the reader-facing source of truth.
2. **Reframe the doc's 200–300K as total notional asset value** (press, yard, equipment,
   contracts) vs. the ch20 line being *liquid/spendable* wealth — then add one prose
   breadcrumb near ch20–22 distinguishing "what I could put my hand on" from "what the whole
   thing was worth," so the two numbers are reconcilable rather than contradictory.

(My recommendation: option 1 — it keeps the prose honest and the rags-to-institution arc
steeper and more satisfying.)

## Gaps — where the trail goes cold (seed a breadcrumb if you want full reconstructability)

The longest dark stretch is **ch26→ch30** (status/debt only, almost no quantities) bleeding
into **ch37** and **ch40** — i.e., across the Trajan-to-Hadrian transition and the early
institution-building, a reconstructor loses the thread for a long run. Also fully dark:
**ch16, ch29, ch50** (ch50 fine — death). Recommended minimal seeds (keep them indirect):

- **ch16** — one comparative line (press/contest income vs. a year ago) to anchor the
  pre-freedom commercial scale.
- **ch26–28** — one quantity: the cost of fitting out the first western probe, or the
  customs-man's annual "bite," or the wharf/house purchase price — so the reader sees the
  jump from "freed with 40K" to "rich man with a wharf."
- **ch30–31** — quantify the Hispanian mine (purchase or the pump's output value) — it's his
  first big industrial asset and currently appears only as a name.
- **ch37 / ch40** — one line each tying the province-auditing work or the press to a number
  (even "the press cleared in a month what the yard cleared in a year").
- **ch44** — the western-yard buy-in cost (it's a major capital commitment shown only as
  "bought into").

Keep every seed **comparative or oblique** — never a tidy "I was worth X." The goal is a
trail a determined reader can follow, not a balance sheet.

---

## How to keep the "with difficulty" feel

- Hard sums appear roughly **once per 4–5 chapters** (ch11, ch18–20, ch34, ch38, ch43, ch46)
  — that cadence is good; preserve it. The gaps above just lengthen one or two spans too far.
- Prefer **units a reader must convert** (denarii vs sesterces vs asses; "⅓ a legionary's
  yearly pay"; "a season's mules"; "most of everything I had") over round modern-feeling
  totals. The mixed units are themselves part of the puzzle.
- Let **scale signals** (payroll counts, bearer numbers, perpetual rent-shares, the fortress
  deed) carry the late-book wealth instead of a stated net worth — they already do this well.
