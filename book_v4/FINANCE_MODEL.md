# Finance Model — Per-Stream Economics & Wealth Trajectory

**Purpose:** the proposed, internally-consistent economic model for Daniel's enterprises, so a
determined reader can reconstruct his finances "with difficulty." Synthesized from three design
passes (balloon+hydrogen; paper/press/contest; gunpowder/cannon + steam/mine) and sum-checked
against the canon anchors. **This is the one place to tune the numbers** — change a figure here
and the breadcrumbs below get seeded to match. Nothing is seeded into the outlines yet.

Roman money used throughout: 1 denarius = 4 HS; 1 HS = 4 asses; skilled wage ≈ 1,000–1,500 HS/yr;
legionary ≈ 1,200 HS/yr; equestrian census 400,000 HS; senator 1,000,000 HS. Motif preserved:
**Daniel does not track his own profit; Marcia/Tyche do; totals stay oblique or withheld.**

|------|--------|-------------|----------------------------|
| 1 — hidden engine | **Mine drainage (steam pumps)** | bulk of the late fortune; hidden via Caepio's shipping invoices; moat = no one else can drain it | **~600k–1M+/yr** (8+ pumps, ~97% margin) |
| 1 — visible engine | **Paper + press + contest** | the famous public earner; moat = zero copy-cost + standard-form network effect + imperial paper floor | **~150–300k+/yr** |
| 2 | **Kerosene lamp-fuel** | mass consumer energy good sold through existing oil merchants; moat = secret distillation | **~80–200k/yr** |
| 2 | **Steam power for hire** (grain / fulling / saw mills) | same engine platform as the mine pumps, different customers, ~0 incremental R&D | **~80–200k/yr** |
| 2 | **Eyeglasses** (fixed-strength reading lenses) | recurring luxury consumable for every aging literate professional; moat = grinding-curve spec + clear-glass supply; **low political exposure** | **~80–200k/yr** |
| 2 — secret monopoly | **Gunpowder + cannon** | sole vendor of cannon + *consumable* powder; recipe/ingredients secret (profit + counter-espionage); lucrative **and** dangerous (what Scaeva wants to seize) | **~100–400k/yr in war years**, less in peace |
| 2 — recurring | **Balloon program** | many rigs + replacements + ocean rigs + hydrogen-upgrade wave; moat = envelope/gas know-how | **~50–150k/yr** |
| 2/3 | **Coal-gas street lighting** | municipal utility contract; converts coke-byproduct **waste** into income; moat = the coke process | **~60–150k/yr** |
| 3 | **Hydrogen** (torch lease + lift) | scales once the generator zeroes marginal cost; overlaps the balloon upgrade | ~70–130k/yr |
| 3 | **Lottery** (state-franchise) | modest income, but a big **press-demand multiplier** (mass printed tickets, no literacy needed) | ~20–80k/yr |
| 3 | **Industrial acids; premium steel tools; watermill-upgrade royalties; phonograph; semaphore codebook lock-in** | a constellation of ~15–80k streams; several fund their own programs | ~15–80k/yr each |
| negligible | gambling, games, kites, barometer, typewriter, public clocks, insurance (failed), photography/calculator (blocked), electroplating (avoid) | ambient or correctly small | small / none |

The craft point: **the press is famous because it's visible; the mine is bigger because Marcia made it invisible.** Several streams are *monopolies protected by secrecy* (powder/cannon recipe, kerosene distillation, eyeglass grinding-curve, balloon/hydrogen know-how, coke→gas) — which is why customers keep paying Daniel instead of replicating him. And the structure has a strong recurring theme: **Daniel's industrial ecology turns waste into feedstock** — coke byproduct → coal-gas lighting; coal-distillation ammonia → refrigeration; crude oil → kerosene (+ lubricant); the one engine → pumps *and* mills; the one glass-clarity prize → telescope *and* eyeglasses *and* microscope. The systematic engineer monetizes the leftovers. (These figures are designed fresh and need NOT match the current draft — the prose will be revised to the model.)

## 2. Per-stream model (condensed)

**Paper + Press + Contest (linked system).** Paper from near-free linen rags (vs papyrus 5–30 HS/sheet); printed sheets sell "an as or two"; contest is a **loss-leader** (~3–6% of press revenue) that builds brand + a free R&D pipeline (pay only on success). The **imperial paper deal** (seeded ch26, formalized ch34) is the capital anchor: a guaranteed floor (~400–600k HS/yr in purchases) — Daniel keeps the **sizing step** as a trade secret so they're buyers, not licensees. Net: marginal at launch (ch19); **~55–70k/yr by ch33**; **~200k/yr by ch38**; **~250–400k/yr by ch44** as mills + deal scale. Three compounding moats: zero marginal copy cost, the standard-form network effect (his figures become *the* standard), and the imperial floor.

**Mine drainage (steam pumps).** Daniel buys the **flooded, abandoned Hispanian gallery for almost nothing** (worthless after 11 years underwater), drains it with the pump as his price of entry, then runs a **service-contract model** (10–15% of gross ore, ~97% margin). One gallery ≈ 100–300k HS/yr to him; **8 pumps across the empire by ~128 AD** ⇒ ~600k–1M+/yr. Rubber gaskets (from the Atlantic program, ch46) ~double pump uptime and widen the viable market. Routed through Caepio's as shipping income to stay invisible to a confiscating prefect.

**Balloon (recurring, not a one-off).** The ch11 contract is just the *first* sale: **80k to Macer + 40k direct to Daniel + a 10k holding fee** (~120–130k total; he opened at 12k when the army would pay 40k+ — *the leverage lesson*). After that it is a **standing manufacturing/supply business**, because the army does not field one balloon — doctrine is ~2 rigs per legion across ~25–30 legions (≈50–60 rigs), the treated-linen envelopes **wear out and are replaced every 2–3 seasons**, every ocean expedition carries rigs (the M4 standing note: 2 per ship), and then the whole fleet is **re-bought as hydrogen-lift conversions**. The army can train its own operators, but Daniel's workshop keeps the **envelope treatment and (later) the hydrogen apparatus** as trade know-how, so the recurring spend flows to him. Realistic ongoing ~**50k–150k/yr**, spiking during the hydrogen-upgrade wave and active wars; cheaper workshop linen (looms) widens the margin. Tier 2, recurring — not the plateau I first modeled.

**Gunpowder + cannon (secret state monopoly — a profit center, not a cost).** The early program *is* a sunk cost (Geta's wages, 2 yrs of niter beds, failed casts). But once the reliable bronze siege bombard works (~118–122), the economics flip: **Rome pays to break walls, and cannot make its own**, because Daniel withholds the powder recipe and quietly controls the saltpeter/sulfur sourcing — secrecy that serves *both* profit (he's the sole vendor) *and* counter-espionage (no formula for a Parthian spy to carry off). So the state pays him: per cast bombard (an expensive bronze piece), and — the recurring part — per **keg of powder**, a consumable every siege and campaign burns through. During active wars (Parthian, Bar Kokhba, frontier sieges) this is a large munitions contract; in peace it's stockpiling + a trickle of quarry-blasting powder. Estimated ~**100k–400k/yr in war years**, lower in peace. The catch is the catch the book already has: a private monopoly on the empire's strongest weapon makes Daniel indispensable *and* a target — which is precisely the Scaeva pressure ("point the powder"), now with a clear economic engine under it.

**Hydrogen.** Oxy-hydrogen torch leased by the service-day (~40–80 HS/day); ~11–20k/yr early, ~70–130k/yr at mature Phase E once the **generator** makes current ~free. Hydrogen-lift balloons need a gas-tight envelope — solved by **Atlantic rubber** (closes the ch24 prize). Theme: the torch is *sold* (needs his operators + consumables) while the water filter is *given away* (anyone can carry it) — a clean character line.

*(Gunpowder/cannon is modeled above, between Paper and Balloon — moved up because it is now a monopoly profit center, not a cost line.)* The R&D still buys two things beyond the later munitions revenue: **political insurance** (too strategically valuable for Trajan/Hadrian/Antoninus to discard) and the **100-denarii boring prize** that simultaneously unlocked the cannon bore, the **steam-pump cylinder**, and the clock gears — the one prize that opened three doors.

### 2b. Additional streams (from the revenue sweep)

**Kerosene lamp-fuel (Phase C–D on).** Refine the kerosene fraction from Mesopotamian crude (factor sourcing); sell as a branded superior lamp oil through the same merchants/amphorae that move olive oil. Cleaner/brighter than tallow, frees olive oil for food. Moat = the secret temperature-staged distillation (Romans know crude burns; not that it can be cleaned/separated). Mass market — *every lamp in the empire*. ~80–200k/yr at multi-city wholesale. Caveat: crude transport (1,500–2,500 km) needs sealed lead/pitched vessels — establish that cost on the page first.

**Steam power for hire (Phase E).** Once the mine pump is proven, the same engine drives a rotary shaft → grain mills, **fulling mills** (year-round wool work, no seasonal peak — ideal), sawmills. Service/lease model like the pumps; ~8–20k/yr per installation × ~10 ⇒ ~80–200k/yr, near-zero incremental R&D, different customer geography (mills are everywhere; mines are in Hispania/Dacia/Britain). Must come *after* the mine proves the device.

**Eyeglasses (Phase E, gated on clear glass).** Fixed-strength reading lenses (3–4 strengths, not custom prescriptions — period-honest) for presbyopia: every literate professional past ~45. Recurring consumable disguised as a durable. Moat = grinding-curve spec + his glass supply (a competitor's lens from striated glass corrects nothing). Sell the pair, keep the curve. ~80–200k/yr at modest penetration; high margin, **low political exposure** — the opposite risk profile to the powder.

**Coal-gas street lighting (Phase D–E).** The coke retorts (run for steel/cannon) already vent combustible gas; pipe it to forum/colonnade burners under a municipal contract. Civic prestige competition = willing counterparties (a provincial capital first, not political Rome). ~60–150k/yr; converts a waste stream to income. Caveat: leak/explosion risk → first demo open-air, cautious in dense centres.

**Lottery (Phase C, state-franchise).** Printed serialized tickets (his press) + the probability framework + imperial imprimatur + state revenue-share. ~20–80k/yr direct — but the real value is as a **press-demand multiplier**: the first mass print product that needs no literacy, justifying bigger mills. Requires Hadrian-era political capital + anti-fraud serialization.

**Industrial acids (Phase D).** Sell excess sulfuric/hydrochloric/nitric to trusted dyers/goldsmiths/bronze-casters in lead-lined vessels ("sell the product, never the process"). ~15–40k/yr; funds the chemistry program. Moat erodes in ~5–10 yrs as buyers reverse-engineer — model it as declining, not permanent.

**Smaller/secondary:** premium coke-steel tools (~20–50k/yr, byproduct of the metallurgy program); overshot-watermill upgrade royalties (~30–70k/yr, Phase B–C, low moat); ball bearings + standardized screws (~10–30k/yr, value is in *setting the standard*); phonograph (~30–80k/yr Phase E, cutting-head secret, but wax cylinders degrade in Roman summers — a "perishable archive"); optical-semaphore contract (one-time ~80–160k + small recurring **codebook lock-in** printed only by his press — mostly *political* lock-in, not big money). **Avoid:** electroplating (would debase coinage → political dynamite). **Net cost / COGS:** oil extraction is the supply cost for kerosene, not a stream of its own.

## 3. Reconciled trajectory (the streams summed)

| Age (ch) | Liquid / position | Annual net income | Driver |
|----------|-------------------|-------------------|--------|
| ~21 (ch11) | ~10–15k liquid (peculium era, controlled) | — | balloon contract money mostly Macer's |
| ~26 (ch20) | **~40–50k — "most of everything I had,"** spent on Tyche (38k) + Pamphilus (4k) | small | sportsbook + early press |
| ~37 (ch33) | total capital ~0.5–1M | **press ~55–70k/yr net** ("the quarter I counted twice" ≈ 15–18k/quarter) | press compounding; mine just starting |
| ~46 (ch38) | total ~2–4M | **press ~200k/yr net** + mine ramping | imperial paper deal + mills + first pumps |
| ~57 (ch44) | total ~8–15M (Pliny-tier) | press ~250–400k + **mine ~600k–1M** | mills at scale + 8-pump mine empire |
| ~74 (death) | **50–100M** incl. endowments, property, mill value, mine contracts | — | matches the V2_DANIEL_FINANCES terminal figure |

This is steep (40k → millions in ~15 yrs) but defensible: a printing near-monopoly + a drained-mine service monopoly are exactly the kinds of engines that compound that fast — and on top of those, the **secret munitions monopoly** (powder/cannon) and the **recurring balloon-supply business** add large spikes in the war years (Parthian ~115–117, Bar Kokhba ~132–135), which is also when Daniel is most politically exposed. Four monopolies, three of them protected by secrecy or know-how, one (the mine) hidden by Marcia's bookkeeping.
