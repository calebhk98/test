## 2. Banned Pattern Violations

### 2a. Correctio / "not X, it's Y" (ZERO TOLERANCE)
- "They told it as a victory and their faces told it as something else" — not a correctio; a contrast observation.
- "The leaning back was a smell before it was anything else." — This is the banned "not X, it's Y" pivot applied to a sensory sequence: defines the leaning back by what it was BEFORE it was fully itself ("a smell before it was anything else"). Compare to the banned form: "It was not [the full thing]. It was [the early sign of it]." Borderline. The construction "X before it was Y" is in the same rhetorical family as the correctio — it names what a thing was NOT yet (anything else) before saying what it was (a smell). FLAGGED as correctio-adjacent.
- "The army was a fist that had punched all the way through and now could not pull its arm back out." — Pure metaphor, not a correctio.
- "I was not surprised when they brought him to me. I had been half waiting to be surprised by it for weeks, which is a different and worse way to wait." — "I was not surprised... which is a different and worse way to wait." Defines Daniel's state by negation ("not surprised") then pivots to the different thing his actual state was (half-waiting-to-be-surprised). This is a correctio structure. FLAGGED.
- "He was awake. That was the first thing, and I let it be a good thing for the space of one breath before I made myself look properly." — "I let it be a good thing for the space of one breath" — not a correctio; narrative, in-scene.
- "There was no last word, because the wound that kills you that way does not leave you a word, it takes the words first and the breath after." — "no last word... it takes the words first and the breath after" — correctio-adjacent: defines the thing by what it is NOT (a last word) then pivots to what happens instead. FLAGGED as correctio-adjacent.
