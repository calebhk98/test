## 4. Monologue / Info-Dump Check

Celer's speech in the command tent (Scene 1): He lays out the tactical situation — valley, the Loaf, column, three days — with a wax tablet. This is multi-sentence but interspersed with Daniel's responses and physical description. Not a 4+ consecutive uninterrupted lines violation.

Apollodorus's speech examining the cloth (Scene 2): "You want to put a soldier on a fire under a sack of greased cloth, and let the wind have him… A clever toy that does one thing. I told you that at the bridge. The one thing is not this." — The speech is 5-6 lines but broken by Daniel's interjections ("I don't want to," "I know"). No full 4+ uninterrupted block. Clean.

Celer's speech after the death (final crouching scene): "'You said it,' he said… 'He volunteered… It's a good thing you made,' he said, 'and it's a butcher…'" — This is a significant monologue: Celer speaks approximately 6 lines without physical beat or interruption. This is the most extended single-character speech in the chapter. However, each "sentence" is short and clipped (Celer's register), and the content is dramatically necessary (his ledger-entry moral accounting). Borderline. The one place where a physical beat between "That's mine" and "He volunteered" would break it appropriately without damaging the scene.

## 5. Voice & Character Issues

**Celer's voice:** Consistently clipped, flat, ledger-register throughout. The post-death speech is somewhat longer than his typical register but remains in character because it is delivered as bookkeeping facts, not sentiment. "I let it go anyway. That's mine" is exactly right. His final line — "It's a good thing you made, and it's a butcher, and now it's killed a man, and you're going to have to decide what kind of man builds a thing like that" — is perhaps one sentence too long for his usual clipped style, but thematically the only place in the book where this moral question is put directly and it is given to the right character. Tolerable.

**Naso post-accident:** The shift in his characterization after the ch11 burn — "His face when he said it was not a young face anymore" — is well-handled. His line "Make him keep his seat" is quoted directly and in the right register (flat, imperative, no self-pity). Good.

**Apollodorus:** Correct voice throughout; especially "The one thing is not this."

**Pamphilus muting:** Pamphilus appears at the river sequence and sits with Daniel without speaking. The description "Pamphilus had a riveted collar around his own neck that I could not take off and he knew better than anyone in that camp what it was to build the thing your owner told you to build" is narrated, not dramatized through dialogue. For this specific scene — grief, silence by the river — the silence is appropriate and powerful. Not every Pamphilus scene requires speech. His required V2 minimum of 4 spoken lines across the book means this is a scene where silence is the correct choice.
