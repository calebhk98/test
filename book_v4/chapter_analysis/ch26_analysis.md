## 3. Scene-Ending Audit

The chapter has no section breaks ("---") — it is a continuous narrative with only one small break around the litter arrival. Treating major narrative pivots as section-ends:

- **Post-Apollodorus / bearded-man confrontation**: "A man who softens his true things to suit the listener is building on sand. You of all men should know what water does to sand." → WISDOM BUTTON. Apollodorus's last line is a packaged aphorism. It is in-character for him (Tier 2 educated Greek) — he gets chiastic closure-cadence. However, it closes the Apollodorus section with a portable epigram. Given that Apollodorus is specifically one of the three characters who are permitted polished epigrams (see CHARACTER_VOICE_GUIDE Section 7: "polished philosophical epigrams belong to Heras, Apollodorus, and Hadrian"), this instance is technically permitted for the character. It should be noted but is not a violation.

- **Chapter ending**: "...outside on the slope the river went on telling the wharf the same flat thing it told every stone it passed, which was nothing, which was that it had found its level and would keep it, and did not care in the least whose walls were thin." → IMAGE CUT (good). The river-finding-its-level image is Apollodorus's idea about water made physical; it closes the chapter on a sensory image without a gnomic summary. The "which was nothing" and "it had found its level and would keep it" add a small interpretive layer, but it is the river doing the speaking, not the narrator. Clean.

**Wisdom-button count for this chapter:** 1 (Apollodorus's "building on sand" line, permitted for his character). Chapter ending is clean. Within limit.
