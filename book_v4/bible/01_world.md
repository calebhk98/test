# World Canon (writer-facing) — Rome, 98-138 AD

Load-bearing facts writers must not get wrong. For depth, see
`research/history_facts.md`. If you need a detail not here, check there before
inventing.

## Time and place
- Daniel arrives in **98 AD**, the year **Nerva dies (late January)** and
  **Trajan** becomes emperor. CRITICAL: Trajan is on the **Rhine/Danube
  frontier** in 98 and does **not enter Rome until 99**. The throne is
  physically absent the first year. Daniel arrives in the countryside near Rome
  in **early-to-mid 98**.
- The city: ~**1,000,000** people, the largest on earth. Stinking, loud,
  crowded, vertical (insulae up to 5-7 storeys), unlit and dangerous at night,
  carts banned by day so deliveries roar all night. The **Colosseum** is 18
  years old, intact, gleaming. Nine aqueducts, public fountains, communal
  latrines, the Cloaca Maxima. Forum, Subura (poor/loud), Palatine (rich/palace),
  Campus Martius, Tiber, Trans Tiberim (immigrants).

## How power works (Daniel must learn this the hard way)
- **Emperor (princeps)**: autocrat backed by the army; also Pontifex Maximus.
- **Senate**: ~600 men, **membership for LIFE** (not a term). Do not write
  senators "up for re-election."
- **Cursus honorum**: quaestor -> aedile/tribune -> praetor -> consul -> governor.
- **Praetorian Guard**: only army in Italy, at the Castra Praetoria; king-makers.
- **Equestrians** (400,000 sesterces) below **senators** (1,000,000). Then
  citizens, freedmen, slaves, and at the very bottom **peregrini** (non-citizen
  foreigners) - which is what Daniel is. He **cannot** sue, vote, marry a
  citizen, hold the grain dole, or own legal standing. He can trade, own
  property, and earn money. To rise he MUST get a **patron**; citizenship is a
  gift of the emperor or earned. This dependency is the engine of his story.
- **Patronage/clientela**: the spine of everything. The dawn **salutatio**
  (clients queue at a patron's door) runs society.

## Money
- as (copper) -> sestertius (4 asses) -> denarius (4 sest., silver) -> aureus
  (25 den., gold). Bread ~1-2 asses. Unskilled day-wage ~1 denarius. Legionary
  ~300 denarii/yr. Senatorial census 1,000,000 sesterces. Coins are heavy metal,
  bitten to test, no notes or credit.

## Daily texture
- **Clothing announces rank.** Tunic universal; **toga** = citizen-male formal
  wear (Daniel may not wear it). Trousers (*bracae*) = barbarian. Daniel's
  jeans/T-shirt/sneakers read as impossibly fine barbarian witch-cloth (no
  machine knit, no elastic, no rubber, no zippers).
- **Wine watered, all day**, even for kids; neat wine = barbarian drunk; plain
  water distrusted. **Garum** (fermented fish sauce) in everything. No sugar,
  potatoes, tomatoes, coffee, tea, citrus, distilled spirits.
- **Writing**: wax tablets + stylus (everyday); papyrus scrolls (permanent);
  ink of soot/gum; reed pen. **No paper, no printing.** Books hand-copied,
  costly. **Silent reading is remarked on as strange.**
- **Literacy** maybe 10-20%, higher among urban men, elite, and notably skilled
  **slaves/freedmen** (literacy was often a slave's job).
- **Language**: Latin on the street/in the army; **Greek** is the language of
  medicine, philosophy, the educated, and the whole eastern Med. Educated Romans
  are bilingual. Plus immigrant tongues everywhere.
- **Time**: no clocks. Sundials, water-clocks. The 12 daylight "hours" stretch
  in summer, shrink in winter.
- **Measurement**: Roman foot ~29.6 cm; mile = 1,000 paces ~1.48 km; libra
  (pound) ~327 g, 12 unciae.

## Slavery (the book's quiet war — keep it in events, never speeches)
- Pervasive; maybe 1 in 3 in Italy. Slaves are **property (res)**, beaten,
  worked, used sexually with impunity, testify only under torture. **Manumission
  is common**; a freed slave of a citizen usually becomes a citizen (barred from
  office; the freeborn son can rise) and owes his ex-master (now **patron**)
  duties. Freedmen can grow rich and powerful but carry the social stain.
- Visibility is total: litter-bearers, shop slaves, chained gangs, the
  collared/branded, people sold naked on platforms with placards. The horror is
  that **no one questions it.**

## Religion (friction source)
- Polytheist, ritual-first (correct performance over belief). Emperor is Pontifex
  Maximus. **Augury** (birds) and **haruspicy** (entrails/liver) precede every
  major act. Priestly colleges are elite political honors. **Imperial cult**:
  dead emperors deified; honoring the living emperor's genius is a loyalty test;
  impiety/blasphemy/magic are dangerous.
- **NO germ theory.** Disease is explained by **miasma** (bad air), **humors**
  (Hippocratic/Galenic imbalance), or **divine wrath/curses**. A claim of "tiny
  invisible living creatures cause illness" maps onto NOTHING and risks reading
  as demon-talk or magic. The one fragile hook: atomist philosophers
  (Epicurus/Lucretius) and Varro's guess about tiny swamp creatures. This is a
  major danger/friction source, not an easy win.

## The army (Daniel's route to power and protection)
- ~28-30 legions (~5,000 citizen heavy infantry each) + roughly equal
  auxiliaries (non-citizen, earn citizenship after 25 yrs). Legion -> cohorts ->
  centuries of ~80 under **centurions**. Specialist auxilia: Syrian/Cretan
  archers, slingers. Siege gear: ballistae, onagers, rams, towers, testudo.
  **Dysentery/camp disease kills more than battle.** The army values rugged
  reliability, logistics edges, medical/morale edges, artillery range. It
  distrusts the fragile and unproven.

## Geography Rome knows
- Mediterranean ("Mare Nostrum") well; outward vaguer. North Africa Roman, Sahara
  and beyond = rumor. **Parthia** the great eastern rival. **India** real and
  traded with via Red Sea monsoon routes (pepper, spices). **China = "Seres,"**
  the silk people, near-mythical eastern edge; no direct contact. **The Atlantic
  ("Oceanus")** is the fearsome encircling edge of the world; the **Americas are
  unknown**; a few (Seneca) speculated about land across the western ocean.
- **THULE / Ultima Thule** (Daniel's claimed homeland): the legendary far-north
  land beyond Britain, reported by Pytheas, cited by Pliny, but **Strabo called
  Pytheas a liar**; most treat Thule as semi-mythical, the edge of the world.
  A "from Thule" claim is **unfalsifiable and exotic** because no Roman has been
  there, but a Strabo-reading skeptic may scoff it isn't real. Use this.

## Real historical figures available (use carefully — see characters file)
Trajan, Apollodorus of Damascus (Trajan's engineer/architect), Hadrian
(Trajan's ward, succeeds 117), Plotina (Trajan's wife), Attianus (Praetorian
prefect), Pliny the Younger (senator/letter-writer, governor of Bithynia
~110-112). Keep the famous mostly offstage; personal audiences are set-pieces.
