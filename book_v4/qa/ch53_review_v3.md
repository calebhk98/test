The outline explicitly says "no mechanical justification" required for this vague-whisper chapter. **SHOULD FIX (low priority):** Consider changing "a weight no bigger than a plum fell down a brass thread" to "a coiled spring" or "a coiled brass spring" to align with Harrison-type marine chronometer physics. ### Revise items (should fix)

3. **Para 11 close — "No one did yet."** — This sentence closes the paragraph with a near-wisdom-button cadence. It is factually accurate and not extractable as a clean aphorism, but the paragraph already shows (not states) the pilot's interpretive restraint through her action ("She did not write what it meant"). The declarative "No one did yet" states what the action already showed. Consider: end Para 11 at "She did not know what it meant." Cut "No one did yet." The reader infers the collective ignorance from the singular instance. The cut tightens and removes the only touch of commentary in an otherwise action-pure passage.

4. **Final clause — "and would now keep being asked."** — The book's last four words interpret the image ("a coast that had never had an hour told to it before") rather than letting it stand. The clause is not portable as an aphorism, but it reaches slightly toward meaning. The preceding physical image is complete without it. Consider ending at: "...telling the hour of a yard a year behind them to a coast that had never had an hour told to it before." Full stop. The silence after "before" is the correct ending for a book about things that don't arrive in a man's lifetime. "Would now keep being asked" is a good sentence; it is a slightly worse final sentence than the one it follows.

5. **Sea-clock drive mechanism — weight vs. spring.** Change "a weight no bigger than a plum fell down a brass thread the length of a man's forearm" to something like "a coiled brass spring, no bigger than a closed fist, drove the works." A spring drive is required for a practical marine chronometer (cf. Harrison H4); a weight drive on a gimbaled mount is functional but technically weaker. The outline explicitly grants "no mechanical justification" latitude, so this is low-priority — but a one-word fix closes a real engineering gap.

### Final image assessment

The book's closing image — unnamed pilot at the rail, looking west at the shore, gimbaled clock knocking below in the dark — **largely lands.** The physical image is precise and earned (the pilot has literally only seen this shore on a chart; the simile "the way you look at a thing you have only ever drawn" is accurate, not decorative).
