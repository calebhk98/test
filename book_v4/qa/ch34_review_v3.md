## 3. Prose Tics — Zero-Tolerance Sweep

### 3a. Correctio / "Not X, it's Y" (ZERO allowed)

- [x] **FOUND: L117** — Tyche's dialogue: `"Not a better way to add. He wants to see the size of a thing too big for one head."`
  - Construction: "Not [X]. [Y]." — canonical zero-tolerance form, occurring in a character's mouth. The ban applies to dialogue as well as narration; PROSE_PATTERNS_TO_AVOID.md states "Any construction that cancels its premise by naming what a thing is NOT before naming what it IS. All variants are banned."
  - Fix: Remove the negating opener. Tyche should state Eudemus's want directly: `"He'll want the purpose under the marks — the size of a thing too big for one head. Not arithmetic. Scope."` Or simply cut "Not a better way to add." The clause following it is self-sufficient.

No other correctio instances found. The V1 L71 "Not warmth..." is correctly gone.

### 3b. Em dashes (ZERO allowed)

- [x] None found. Chapter uses hyphens, commas, and full stops throughout. Clean.

### 3c. Meta-disclaimers / throat-clearing (ZERO allowed)

- [x] **Borderline: L63** — Heras's dialogue: `"I will not pretend I am glad to do this."`
  - This is a character's own speech, not Daniel's narration. The meta-disclaimer ban targets the narrator prefacing scenes with "I want to be honest about..." The Heras line is character voice, not authorial throat-clearing. It fits Heras's register (oblique, self-aware, mock-formal). **RULING: PERMITTED.** Heras is not the narrator; this is in-character hedging, not authorial apology.
- [x] No narrator-level meta-disclaimers found.

### 3d. Narrator stepping outside time / future-vantage (ZERO allowed)

- [x] **FOUND: L77** — `"In Greek. A test, I understood later."`
  - Construction: "I understood later" = narrator arriving from a future vantage to assign cognitive significance to a moment. The style guide prohibits "any sentence that tells the reader the future emotional state of the narrator as a way of assigning weight to the present moment." This is exactly that: the narrator comes from after the meeting to characterize what was happening at the time. Compare the **permitted** form: "He was speaking Greek, and I read it as a test" (reporting what Daniel read at the time). The **banned** form is the "I understood later" temporal marker.
  - Severity note: This does not telegraph dramatic weight or foreshadow consequences. It is a mild instance — a cognitive note rather than a dramatic-irony announcement. However, the rule is zero-tolerance.
  - Fix: `"In Greek. The language of a man who wanted to know how far my Greek went."` — reports what Daniel registered at the time. The reader understands this was a test without the narrator declaring it retrospectively.

- [x] **Borderline: L63** — `"it was the most valuable sentence anyone had said to me in a year"`
  - Close retrospect assigning comparative significance. The permitted/banned line is: "reports retrospective knowledge = fine" vs. "announces what mattered = fine only if it reports what Daniel knew at the time." Daniel knows, as he hears it, that it is valuable — this reads as present-tense recognition rendered in past tense, not as the narrator arriving from the future. **RULING: PERMITTED.** The narration is reporting his reaction in the moment, not announcing future significance.

No other future-vantage instances found.
