## 3. Prose Tics — Zero-Tolerance Sweep

### 3a. Correctio / "Not X, it's Y" (ZERO allowed)

Two instances found. One is a clear violation; one is a borderline case requiring a ruling.

- **[BLOCKER] L81:** `"He looked well. Not the strained-looking Crispus of the salons who prosecuted each argument as if the alternative were execution, but a man who had arrived somewhere and set down the bag."`
  Pattern: "Not [X], but [Y]." This is the banned correctio construction in its standard "Not X, but Y" form. It defines the character by first naming what he is not.
  Fix: State the positive directly. "He looked like a man who had arrived somewhere and set down the bag" followed by a specific physical detail. The salon version of Crispus is already established across prior chapters; the reader does not need the negation.

- **[BORDERLINE — ruling: PERMITTED]** L49: `"...which is as close to friendship as we ever came, and was not friendship."`
  This is the banned "Not X, it's Y" architecture reversed (positive stated, then negated), but the construction serves a specific function here: it names a closeness and then precisely limits it. Structurally it is the inverse of the banned form (the sentence does not define by negation-first). The meaning could not be expressed more precisely in direct statement without two separate sentences. The emotional weight depends on the reversal. This is a judgment call; the reviewer rules it PERMITTED as a rare inversion that does work the direct form cannot. However, the writer should be aware it sits close to the line.

**Zero-tolerance count for correctio: 1 clear violation (L81). 1 borderline ruled permitted.**

### 3d. Narrator stepping outside time / future-vantage (ZERO allowed)

- **[BLOCKER] L35:** `"...and standing under it I understood for the first time what the difference was between a thing that happened and a thing that is remembered..."`
  This is the TIC V13 frozen-epiphany variant combined with a potential future-vantage concern. "For the first time" is an absolute claim — the narrator, writing retrospectively, claims this was the exact moment of first understanding. That framing assigns retroactive significance to this moment as the origin of a lasting insight. The style guide permits close-retrospect ("I can still see it") but prohibits declaring a moment the first time of understanding something that will shape the rest of the narrator's life — this is the banned "I did not know then what that meeting would cost him" pattern in positive form. The narrator is arriving from the future to crown this instant. **
  Fix:** Remove "for the first time." Let the understanding arrive in the text without being formally installed as an epiphany. "...and standing under it I understood what the difference was between a thing that happened and a thing that is remembered..." The drop of five words removes the retrospective crowning without changing the observation itself.

- **[PERMITTED]** L51: `"He had said that to me on the Danube eleven years before..."` — This is permitted close-retrospect: reporting what happened, not telescoping significance.

- **[PERMITTED]** L55: `"Vanity is the door into a proud man and I had learned where his hinges were."` — Retrospective knowledge of what Daniel had learned by this point. Reports knowledge, does not announce significance.
