- [ ] Found: L29 — "...the one small bronze argument that every clock for the next two thousand years would be built around. It existed. It would exist. Men not yet born would file it in their sleep."

This is a future-vantage passage. The narrator declares significance from a future vantage point, predicting what "men not yet born" will do. Per the ban: "The narrator does not announce significance from the future." The close retrospect is permitted ("I know now..."), but this goes further — it is the narrator declaring the historical importance of the escapement to unborn craftsmen.

**Ruling: Borderline / Near-banned.** The passage could be argued as Daniel's present-tense interiority (he knows the escapement will matter because he comes from the future) rather than the narrator performing dramatic irony. Unlike the banned examples ("I did not know then what that meeting would cost him"), this is Daniel KNOWING what the future holds, not a narrator lamenting what the character didn't know. Given that Daniel literally is from the future, his knowledge of the escapement's significance is part of his character — not the same as the narrator stepping out. Ruling: **Permitted, as close-retrospect of Daniel's time-traveler interiority.** However, the phrase "Men not yet born would file it in their sleep" is at the edge — it reads more like narrator commentary than character thought. Flag for tightening: "It would exist. A man not yet born, maybe more than one, would file it in his sleep and never know he was doing something extraordinary." vs. cutting the declaration entirely.
