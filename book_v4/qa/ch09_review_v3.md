## 7. Verdict

**REVISE**

### Blocking issues (must fix before next use)

1. **Macer's crude oath absent [L77-89, outline ch09 requirement]** — V2 notes and the character bible both require one genuine crude Roman oath from Macer in this chapter (his first major scene). None is present. The character feels mean-funny throughout but never reaches the volume/register the brief requires. Fix: add one crude oath in genuine anger — the most natural moment is when Daniel asks for freedom (L81-87), where Macer could react with a single crude interjection before settling into the patient "it is not mine to give" dismissal. Alternatively, place it when Macer hears the news about the senator's freedman at the door (L27), which is the moment most likely to produce a genuine money-anger response.

2. **"The word" at L73 lacks a clear antecedent** — "He used the word like he owned it, which he did" refers to Macer having just said "balloon," but Macer's preceding speech (L71) does not visibly contain the word "balloon" on the page. The reader cannot track "the word." Fix: add the word "balloon" explicitly into Macer's L71 speech ("He knows what he is. That is the dangerous kind... Back to me, louder: 'So that is your little knife. The only one who knows how to get my balloon back up.'"). Then L73 ("He used the word like he owned it") is earned.

### Revise items (should fix)

1. **L79 — wisdom-button at mid-scene** — "I did not understand all the words. I understood the logic well enough: a cage so well made it looked from the inside like a workshop." This crystallizes the chapter's meaning in a portable metaphor delivered mid-narration. The cage logic is already visible in the deal structure; naming it explicitly reduces the reader's discovery. The first sentence stands; cut or revise the second: "I did not understand all the words. The logic was plain enough." The image of the workshop-as-cage can stay if the phrasing is less epigram-like, but in its current form it reads as a portable takeaway. Recommend cutting "a cage so well made it looked from the inside like a workshop" and ending the paragraph on the logic-tally paragraph below (L65) which already makes the point through Daniel's accounting.

2. **L49 — double "thing"** — "it was the first thing in the room that had felt like a thing a person actually did." The doubling dulls the sentence. Revise: "it was the first act in the room that felt human" or similar. A specific noun replaces the second "thing."
