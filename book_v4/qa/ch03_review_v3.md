## 3. Prose Tics — Zero-Tolerance Sweep

### 3a. Correctio / "Not X, it's Y" (ZERO allowed)

Two candidates found; ruling on each:

- **L29:** "the one asset I had that nobody else in this room had wasn't something you say. It was something you show."
  — RULING: **CORRECTIO VIOLATION.** Classic banned form: "it wasn't X [something you say]. It was Y [something you show]." Two consecutive sentences define the thing by first saying what it is not. Direct fix: "The one asset nobody else in this room had was something you show, not something you say." Or simply: "I had one thing none of them had, and it didn't need words."

- **L81:** "Not as a strategic move. As something more like hunger."
  — RULING: **CORRECTIO VIOLATION.** This is the banned "Not X. Y." pivot form explicitly listed in PROSE_PATTERNS_TO_AVOID.md §1.1. The construction cancels "strategic move" to pivot to "hunger." Direct fix: "I'd drawn those shapes the way a hungry man reaches — not because I'd planned it." Or simply: "The hunger in me was older than any plan." The meaning is already earned by what precedes it; the negation frame is unnecessary and banned.

- **L39:** "not as facts you recall but as a pattern your hand already knows"
  — RULING: **BORDERLINE / NOT A BLOCKING CORRECTIO.** This "not as X but as Y" appears inside a simile ("the way you have the alphabet in you, not as facts..."), not as the primary claim. It refines a comparison rather than defining a thing by negation. The style guide prohibits the construction when it "cancels its premise by naming what something is NOT before naming what it IS" — here the premise (knowing coastlines) is already positively stated; the "not as" clause specifies the mechanism of knowing, not what knowing is. Flag for writer attention but not a blocking violation. If the chapter is sent to REVISE for L29 and L81, the writer can make a judgment call on this instance.

**Count of blocking correctio violations: 2 (L29, L81)**

### 3d. Narrator stepping outside time / future-vantage (ZERO allowed)

Three candidates found:

- **L89:** "a single word that I would learn, later, was a kind of yes, the army yes"
  — RULING: **PERMITTED.** This is close-retrospect: the narrator reports what he eventually learned (the word's meaning), not what significance the moment carried. It is the permitted form: "I know now that X" (reports retrospective knowledge). It does not perform dramatic irony by announcing emotional weight. No action required.

- **L93:** "I picked up, much later, that he'd said two things about the man he was sending for... one of them I would eventually understand was just a slur for foreigner"
  — RULING: **PERMITTED.** Same form as L89. The retrospective frame licenses knowing what words meant; it does not telegraph significance or emotional stakes. The narrator is simply translating delayed comprehension. No action required.

- **L79:** "There was nothing across that ocean for them. Not yet. Not for fourteen hundred years."
  — RULING: **BORDERLINE — PERMITTED, but watch.** This is the narrator, writing from old age, correctly applying retrospective knowledge to the scene. The style guide's permitted form is "I know now that X" — this is that form (he knows the Americas won't be reached). The banned form is declaring that a moment mattered from a future vantage; this is not doing that — it is reporting a fact the narrator knows. However, the phrase "Not yet. Not for fourteen hundred years" has a rhetorical cadence (two staccato fragments, dramatic pause) that could slide into future-vantage annotation. The content is permitted; the presentation is slightly overperformed. Flag for writer awareness, not a blocking violation.

**Zero-tolerance future-vantage violations: 0 blocking.** Two borderline cases noted.
